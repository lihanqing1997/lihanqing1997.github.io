VC dimension measures combinatorial regularity. Smoothness, compactness, and shape restrictions provide a different source of small entropy: nearby domain points constrain nearby function values. The exponent in the entropy rate records how many local degrees of freedom remain after those analytic constraints are imposed.

Parametric Lipschitz families

Proposition. Let \(\Theta\subset\mathbb R^q\) have diameter at most \(D\), and suppose \[ |f_\theta(x)-f_\eta(x)| \leq L(x)\|\theta-\eta\|_2. \] Then, for every \(Q\) with \(\|L\|_{Q,2}<\infty\), \[ N\!\left( \varepsilon\|L\|_{Q,2}, \{f_\theta:\theta\in\Theta\},L_2(Q) \right) \leq\left(1+\frac{2D}{\varepsilon}\right)^q. \]

Proof. An \(\varepsilon\)-net \(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_N\) of \(\Theta\) induces a net \(f_{\theta_1},\ldots,f_{\theta_N}\), because \[ \|f_\theta-f_{\theta_j}\|_{Q,2} \leq\|L\|_{Q,2}\|\theta-\theta_j\|_2. \] Apply the Euclidean volume bound from the preceding page. \(\square\)

Thus a \(q\)-parameter model has logarithmic entropy \[ \log N(\varepsilon)\lesssim q\log(1/\varepsilon). \] This is qualitatively smaller than the polynomial entropy of an infinite-dimensional smoothness ball.

Hölder balls

Definition. Write \(s=m+\alpha\), where \(m\in\mathbb N_0\) and \(0<\alpha\leq1\). The Hölder norm on \([0,1]^d\) is \[ \|f\|_{C^s} = \max_{|\beta|\leq m}\|D^\beta f\|_\infty + \max_{|\beta|=m} \sup_{x\ne y} \frac{|D^\beta f(x)-D^\beta f(y)|}{\|x-y\|_2^\alpha}. \] Let \(C_M^s([0,1]^d)=\{f:\|f\|_{C^s}\leq M\}\).

For the lower bound, place disjoint \(s\)-smooth bumps of height \(\varepsilon\) on a grid of spacing \(h\), and use binary choices of their signs. Corollary (Donsker threshold). Since \(L_2(P)\)-distance is bounded by sup-norm distance, \[ \int_0^1 \sqrt{\log N(\varepsilon,C_M^s,L_2(P))} \,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon \lesssim \int_0^1\varepsilon^{-d/(2s)}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon. \] The integral is finite exactly when \(s>d/2\). Subject to measurability, a uniformly bounded Hölder ball is therefore universally Donsker when \(s>d/2\).

The threshold is not merely decorative. Below it, the class contains too many small-scale oscillations for a supremum-norm Gaussian limit under arbitrary \(P\). It is the same smoothness-versus-dimension balance that appears in nonparametric rates.

Sobolev balls and compact embeddings

Theorem. On a bounded regular domain \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d\), the unit ball of \(W^{s,2}(\Omega)\) satisfies \[ \log N(\varepsilon,B_{W^{s,2}},L_2(\Omega)) \asymp\varepsilon^{-d/s}. \] In particular, the embedding \(W^{s,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_2(\Omega)\) is compact for \(s>0\).

One way to see the rate is spectral. Expanding in eigenfunctions of an elliptic operator turns the Sobolev ball into an ellipsoid with semiaxes \(a_j\asymp j^{-s/d}\). Covering the first \(m\asymp\varepsilon^{-d/s}\) coordinates and bounding the remaining tail yields the upper rate; packing sign vectors in the first \(m\) suitably scaled coordinates yields the lower rate.

Shape-constrained classes

Theorem (monotone functions). For the class \(\mathcal M\) of nondecreasing functions \([0,1]\to[0,1]\), \[ \log N_{[]}(\varepsilon,\mathcal M,L_2(P)) \lesssim\varepsilon^{-1} \] uniformly over probability laws \(P\). Hence \(\mathcal M\) is \(P\)-Donsker for every \(P\).

A monotone function may jump and need not be smooth, but it can spend its total variation only once. The entropy exponent \(1\) records this order restriction. Convexity likewise restricts how slopes may change. For uniformly bounded convex functions on a compact convex subset of \(\mathbb R^d\), the characteristic interior sup-norm entropy rate is \[ \log N(\varepsilon)\asymp\varepsilon^{-d/2}, \] with boundary behavior requiring care when no Lipschitz bound is imposed.

Sequence ellipsoids

Example. In \(\ell^2\), let \[ \mathcal E(a)= \left\{\theta:\sum_{j\geq1}\frac{\theta_j^2}{a_j^2}\leq1\right\}, \qquad a_1\geq a_2\geq\cdots\downarrow0. \] Coordinates with \(a_j\ll\varepsilon\) are invisible at resolution \(\varepsilon\). A useful entropy approximation is \[ \log N(\varepsilon,\mathcal E(a),\ell^2) \asymp \sum_{j:a_j\gtrsim\varepsilon} \log\!\left(\frac{a_j}{\varepsilon}\right). \] If \(a_j\asymp j^{-s/d}\), this sum has order \(\varepsilon^{-d/s}\), recovering the Sobolev rate.

Entropy exponents and statistical regimes

If a local class satisfies \[ \log N(\varepsilon)\asymp\varepsilon^{-\alpha}, \] then its Dudley integral near zero behaves as \[ \int_0^\delta\varepsilon^{-\alpha/2}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon. \] It is finite at zero when \(\alpha<2\), logarithmically critical at \(\alpha=2\), and divergent when \(\alpha>2\). Divergence of this particular integral does not prove that no useful limit or rate exists; it says that global Donsker control by this entropy route is too coarse. Localization, a weaker topology, or problem-specific cancellation may still succeed.
