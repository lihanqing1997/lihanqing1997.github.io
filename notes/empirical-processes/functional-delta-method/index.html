The functional delta method transports weak convergence through maps between infinite-dimensional spaces. Its differentiability notion must be uniform along perturbing directions because the empirical process itself is random and only converges, rather than approaching the target along one fixed line.

Hadamard differentiability

Definition. Let \(\mathbb D,\mathbb E\) be normed spaces, \(\phi:\mathbb D_\phi\subset\mathbb D\to\mathbb E\), and \(\theta\in\mathbb D_\phi\). The map \(\phi\) is Hadamard differentiable at \(\theta\), tangentially to \(\mathbb D_0\subset\mathbb D\), if a continuous linear map \[ \phi'_\theta:\mathbb D_0\to\mathbb E \] exists such that, whenever \(t_n\to0\), \(h_n\to h\in\mathbb D_0\), and \(\theta+t_nh_n\in\mathbb D_\phi\), \[ \left\| \frac{\phi(\theta+t_nh_n)-\phi(\theta)}{t_n} -\phi'_\theta(h) \right\|_{\mathbb E}\longrightarrow0. \]

Gâteaux differentiability tests only fixed \(h\). Hadamard differentiability permits \(h_n\to h\), which is the stability needed when \(h_n=r_n(T_n-\theta)\) is random. Fréchet differentiability is stronger: it requires a remainder uniformly small over all sufficiently small perturbations in the ambient norm.

The functional delta theorem

Theorem. Suppose \[ r_n(T_n-\theta)\rightsquigarrow T \] in \(\mathbb D\), where \(r_n\to\infty\), \(T\) is separable, and \(T\in\mathbb D_0\) almost surely. If \(\phi\) is Hadamard differentiable at \(\theta\) tangentially to \(\mathbb D_0\), then \[ r_n\{\phi(T_n)-\phi(\theta)\} \rightsquigarrow\phi'_\theta(T). \] Moreover, \[ r_n\{\phi(T_n)-\phi(\theta)\} -\phi'_\theta\{r_n(T_n-\theta)\} \xrightarrow{p}0. \]

Proof. Define \[ g_n(h)=r_n\{\phi(\theta+r_n^{-1}h)-\phi(\theta)\} \] on the admissible directions. Hadamard differentiability says that \(g_n(h_n)\to\phi'_\theta(h)\) whenever \(h_n\to h\in\mathbb D_0\). This is the sequential condition in the extended continuous-mapping theorem. Apply it to \(h_n=r_n(T_n-\theta)\rightsquigarrow T\). The same argument applied to \(g_n(h)-\phi'_\theta(h)\), whose limit is zero, gives the asymptotic linear remainder. \(\square\)

Proposition (chain rule). If \(\phi:\mathbb D\to\mathbb E\) is Hadamard differentiable at \(\theta\) tangentially to \(\mathbb D_0\), and \(\psi:\mathbb E\to\mathbb F\) is Hadamard differentiable at \(\phi(\theta)\) tangentially to \(\phi'_\theta(\mathbb D_0)\), then \[ (\psi\circ\phi)'_\theta = \psi'_{\phi(\theta)}\circ\phi'_\theta. \]

Elementary statistical functionals

Example (ratio). For \(\phi(a,b)=a/b\), \(b\ne0\), \[ \phi'_{(a,b)}(h_1,h_2) =\frac{h_1}{b}-\frac{a h_2}{b^2}. \] If \(a=Pg\) and \(b=Ph\), the plug-in estimator \(P_ng/P_nh\) therefore has influence function \[ \frac{g-Pg}{Ph} -\frac{Pg}{(Ph)^2}(h-Ph). \]

Example (integral functional). Let \[ \phi(F)=\int\varphi(x)\,\mathrm{d}F(x). \] On signed measures for which the integral is continuous, \[ \phi'_F(H)=\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}H. \] The delta method reduces to the ordinary central limit theorem for the sample average.

The inverse and quantile maps

Theorem (quantile derivative). Let \(F\) be continuously differentiable on a neighborhood of \[ q_p=F^{-1}(p)=\inf\{x:F(x)\geq p\}, \] with density \(f(q_p)>0\). Then the quantile map \(\phi_p(G)=G^{-1}(p)\) is Hadamard differentiable at \(F\), tangentially to functions continuous at \(q_p\), with \[ \phi'_{p,F}(h)=-\frac{h(q_p)}{f(q_p)}. \]

Proof. Let \(t_n\to0\), \(h_n\to h\) uniformly, \(F_n=F+t_nh_n\), and \(q_n=F_n^{-1}(p)\). Monotonicity and local strict increase imply \(q_n\to q_p\). Up to a term \(o(t_n)\) caused by the generalized inverse, \[ 0=F_n(q_n)-F(q_p) =F(q_n)-F(q_p)+t_nh_n(q_n). \] By differentiability of \(F\), \[ F(q_n)-F(q_p) =f(q_p)(q_n-q_p)+o(|q_n-q_p|). \] The equation first yields \(q_n-q_p=O(t_n)\); divide by \(t_n\), use \(h_n(q_n)\to h(q_p)\), and solve for the limit. \(\square\)

Corollary (sample quantile). If \(F\) is continuous and \(f(q_p)>0\), then \[ \sqrt n(\hat q_p-q_p) = -\frac{\mathbb G_n1_{(-\infty,q_p]}}{f(q_p)} +o_p(1) \rightsquigarrow N\!\left(0,\frac{p(1-p)}{f(q_p)^2}\right). \]

For a compact interval \(p\in[a,b]\subset(0,1)\), if \(f\) is continuous and bounded away from zero on the corresponding quantile range, the inverse map is Hadamard differentiable as a map into \(\ell^\infty[a,b]\). This gives a quantile-process limit and simultaneous bands.

Distributional transformations

Example (composition). Let \(\Phi(F,G)=F\circ G^{-1}\). Under smoothness and positive density of \(G\), the chain rule combines evaluation, inversion, and composition: \[ \Phi'_{(F,G)}(h_F,h_G)(u) = h_F(G^{-1}(u)) -\frac{f(G^{-1}(u))}{g(G^{-1}(u))} h_G(G^{-1}(u)). \] Such derivatives underlie Q–Q processes, ROC curves, and distributional treatment-effect functionals.

Directional derivatives and nonregular maps

Definition. A map is Hadamard directionally differentiable if the same sequential limit exists, but \(\phi'_\theta\) need only be continuous and positively homogeneous, not linear.

Example (maximum). For \(\phi(x)=\max_{1\leq j\leq k}x_j\), let \(I(x)=\{j:x_j=\phi(x)\}\). Then \[ \phi'_x(h)=\max_{j\in I(x)}h_j. \] At a unique maximizer this derivative is linear; at a tie it is nonlinear. The directional delta method still gives \[ r_n\{\phi(T_n)-\phi(\theta)\} \rightsquigarrow\max_{j\in I(\theta)}T_j, \] but the ordinary bootstrap may fail because it differentiates at the random \(T_n\), whose active set need not stabilize.

The practical hierarchy is therefore: identify the process limit, determine the strongest valid differentiability of the final map, apply the corresponding delta theorem, and only then decide whether an ordinary bootstrap consistently reproduces that derivative.
