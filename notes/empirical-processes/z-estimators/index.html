A \(Z\)-estimator is defined by making an empirical map approximately zero. The central issue is inversion: uniform convergence locates a root, while differentiability and empirical-process equicontinuity convert the residual equation into an asymptotic linear representation.

Approximate roots

Definition. Let \(\psi_\theta:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^p\), \[ \Psi(\theta)=P\psi_\theta, \qquad \Psi_n(\theta)=P_n\psi_\theta. \] An approximate \(Z\)-estimator \(\hat\theta_n\) satisfies \[ \|\Psi_n(\hat\theta_n)\| \leq \inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\|\Psi_n(\theta)\|+o_p(1), \] or, for root-\(n\) linearization, \[ \Psi_n(\hat\theta_n)=o_p(n^{-1/2}). \]

Consistency of roots

Theorem. Suppose:

  1. \(\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|\Psi_n(\theta)-\Psi(\theta)\|\to_p0\);
  2. \(\Psi(\theta_0)=0\), and for every \(\varepsilon>0\), \[ \inf_{d(\theta,\theta_0)\geq\varepsilon} \|\Psi(\theta)\|>0; \]
  3. \(\|\Psi_n(\hat\theta_n)\|=o_p(1)\).

Then \(\hat\theta_n\to_p\theta_0\).

Proof. On \(d(\hat\theta_n,\theta_0)\geq\varepsilon\), \[ \inf_{d(\theta,\theta_0)\geq\varepsilon}\|\Psi(\theta)\| \leq \|\Psi(\hat\theta_n)-\Psi_n(\hat\theta_n)\| +\|\Psi_n(\hat\theta_n)\|. \] The right side is bounded by the uniform discrepancy plus \(o_p(1)\), and therefore tends to zero in probability, contradicting the positive deterministic lower bound. \(\square\)

The separation condition allows multiple finite-sample roots but requires the population root to be isolated. On noncompact parameter spaces, it is enough to prove the condition on a compact set that contains \(\hat\theta_n\) with probability tending to one.

Root-\(n\) linearization

Theorem. Let \(\hat\theta_n\to_p\theta_0\) and \(\Psi_n(\hat\theta_n)=o_p(n^{-1/2})\). Assume:

  1. for a nonsingular matrix \(A\), \[ \Psi(\theta)-\Psi(\theta_0) =A(\theta-\theta_0)+o(\|\theta-\theta_0\|); \]
  2. \[ \sup_{\|\theta-\theta_0\|\leq\delta_n} \frac{ |\mathbb G_n(\psi_\theta-\psi_{\theta_0})| }{ 1+\sqrt n\|\theta-\theta_0\| } \xrightarrow{p}0 \] for every deterministic \(\delta_n\downarrow0\);
  3. \(P\|\psi_{\theta_0}\|^2<\infty\).

Then \[ \sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta_0) = -A^{-1}\mathbb G_n\psi_{\theta_0}+o_p(1). \]

Proof. Expand the estimating equation: \[ \begin{aligned} o_p(1) &=\sqrt n\,\Psi_n(\hat\theta_n)\\ &=\mathbb G_n\psi_{\theta_0} +\mathbb G_n(\psi_{\hat\theta_n}-\psi_{\theta_0}) +\sqrt n\{\Psi(\hat\theta_n)-\Psi(\theta_0)\}. \end{aligned} \] The stochastic differentiability condition makes the second term \(o_p(1+\sqrt n\|\hat\theta_n-\theta_0\|)\). Population differentiability gives \[ \sqrt n\{\Psi(\hat\theta_n)-\Psi(\theta_0)\} = A\sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta_0) +o_p(\sqrt n\|\hat\theta_n-\theta_0\|). \] Nonsingularity of \(A\) first implies root-\(n\) tightness, after which both relative remainders become \(o_p(1)\). Rearrangement proves the expansion. \(\square\)

The equicontinuity theorem on page 9 gives a simpler sufficient condition: \[ \mathbb G_n(\psi_{\hat\theta_n}-\psi_{\theta_0})=o_p(1). \] The ratio formulation above is useful because it can establish root-\(n\) tightness and linearization simultaneously.

Generalized method of moments

Example. Let \(g(X,\theta)\in\mathbb R^q\), \(q\geq p\), and suppose \(Pg(X,\theta_0)=0\). The GMM estimator minimizes \[ Q_n(\theta)= P_ng_\theta^\top W_nP_ng_\theta. \] Its first-order equation is \[ \dot G_n(\theta)^\top W_nP_ng_\theta=0. \] If \(G=P\partial_\theta g_{\theta_0}\) has full column rank and \(W_n\to_pW\), then \[ \sqrt n(\hat\theta-\theta_0) = -(G^\top WG)^{-1}G^\top W\mathbb G_ng_{\theta_0} +o_p(1). \] With \(\Omega=\operatorname{Var}(g_{\theta_0})\), the choice \(W=\Omega^{-1}\) gives covariance \((G^\top\Omega^{-1}G)^{-1}\).

Nonsmooth estimating equations

Example (quantile). A sample \(p\)-quantile approximately solves \[ P_n\{1(X\leq\theta)-p\}=0. \] The map \(\theta\mapsto1(X\leq\theta)\) is not pointwise differentiable, but its expectation is: \[ P\{1(X\leq\theta)-p\} =F(\theta)-p =f(q_p)(\theta-q_p)+o(|\theta-q_p|). \] Donsker equicontinuity of the indicator class gives \[ \sqrt n(\hat q_p-q_p) = -\frac{\mathbb G_n\{1(X\leq q_p)-p\}}{f(q_p)} +o_p(1). \] Thus differentiability of individual criterion functions is unnecessary; differentiability of the population map and stochastic equicontinuity are the relevant properties.

Banach-space roots

Theorem (implicit functional delta method). Let \(\Psi:\mathbb D\times\Theta\to\mathbb E\) satisfy \(\Psi(P,\theta_0)=0\). If \(\Psi\) is Hadamard differentiable in both arguments and the derivative \[ A=\partial_\theta\Psi(P,\theta_0):\Theta_0\to\mathbb E_0 \] is continuously invertible, then the local solution map \(P\mapsto\theta(P)\) has derivative \[ \theta'_P(h) =-A^{-1}\partial_P\Psi(P,\theta_0)[h]. \]

This implicit-map formulation covers estimating equations whose unknown is a function, such as cumulative hazards or integral-equation estimators. Proving that \(A^{-1}\) exists and is continuous is the functional-analytic part; controlling the empirical map uniformly is the empirical-process part.
