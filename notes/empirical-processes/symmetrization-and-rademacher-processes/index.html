The centered empirical process contains the unknown mean \(Pf\). Symmetrization removes it by comparing the sample with an independent copy and then replacing that difference by random signs. Conditional on the observations, the resulting process has explicit sub-Gaussian increments in the empirical \(L_2\) metric.

Ghost samples and random signs

Definition. Let \(X_1',\ldots,X_n'\) be an independent copy of the sample, and let \(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_n\) be independent Rademacher variables, \[ P(\varepsilon_i=1)=P(\varepsilon_i=-1)=\tfrac12, \] independent of both samples. The conditional Rademacher process is \[ \mathbb G_n^\varepsilon f =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i f(X_i). \]

Theorem (symmetrization). For every class \(\mathcal F\) for which the outer expectations are defined, \[ E^*\|\mathbb G_n\|_{\mathcal F} \leq 2E^*\|\mathbb G_n^\varepsilon\|_{\mathcal F}. \] More generally, for every nondecreasing convex function \(\Phi:\mathbb R_+\to\mathbb R_+\), \[ E^*\Phi\!\left(\frac12\|\mathbb G_n\|_{\mathcal F}\right) \leq E^*\Phi(\|\mathbb G_n^\varepsilon\|_{\mathcal F}). \]

Proof. Conditional Jensen inequality with respect to the ghost sample gives \[ \begin{aligned} E\Phi\!\left(\left\|\frac1{\sqrt n} \sum_{i=1}^n\{f(X_i)-Pf\}\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right) &\leq EE'\Phi\!\left(\left\|\frac1{\sqrt n} \sum_{i=1}^n\{f(X_i)-f(X_i')\}\right\|_{\mathcal F}\right). \end{aligned} \] Each pair \((X_i,X_i')\) is exchangeable, so the joint law is unchanged when its difference is multiplied by an independent sign: \[ \sum_i\{f(X_i)-f(X_i')\} \overset d=\sum_i\varepsilon_i\{f(X_i)-f(X_i')\}. \] By the triangle inequality, \[ \left\|\sum_i\varepsilon_i\{f(X_i)-f(X_i')\}\right\|_{\mathcal F} \leq \left\|\sum_i\varepsilon_if(X_i)\right\|_{\mathcal F} \left\|\sum_i\varepsilon_if(X_i')\right\|_{\mathcal F}. \] Convexity, \(\Phi((a+b)/2)\leq\{\Phi(a)+\Phi(b)\}/2\), and identical distribution of the two terms prove the general inequality. Taking \(\Phi(t)=t\) yields the first statement. \(\square\)

Conditional sub-Gaussian increments

Proposition. Conditional on \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\), for every \(f,g\in\mathcal F\) and \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), \[ E_\varepsilon \exp\{\lambda(\mathbb G_n^\varepsilon f-\mathbb G_n^\varepsilon g)\} \leq \exp\!\left\{\frac{\lambda^2}{2}P_n(f-g)^2\right\}. \] Thus \(\mathbb G_n^\varepsilon\) is sub-Gaussian for the random semimetric \(d_n(f,g)=\|f-g\|_{P_n,2}\).

Proof. Independence of the signs and \(\cosh u\leq e^{u^2/2}\) imply \[ \begin{aligned} E_\varepsilon e^{\lambda n^{-1/2}\sum_i\varepsilon_i(f-g)(X_i)} &=\prod_{i=1}^n \cosh\!\left(\frac{\lambda(f-g)(X_i)}{\sqrt n}\right)\\ &\leq \exp\!\left\{\frac{\lambda^2}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^n(f-g)^2(X_i)\right\}. \end{aligned} \] \(\square\)

Finite classes

Lemma (finite-class maximal inequality). Let \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_N\) be centered random variables satisfying \(E e^{\lambda Z_j}\leq e^{\lambda^2\sigma^2/2}\) for all \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\). Then \[ E\max_{1\leq j\leq N}|Z_j| \leq \sigma\sqrt{2\log(2N)}. \]

Proof. For \(\lambda>0\), \[ e^{\lambda E\max_j|Z_j|} \leq E e^{\lambda\max_j|Z_j|} \leq\sum_{j=1}^N E(e^{\lambda Z_j}+e^{-\lambda Z_j}) \leq2Ne^{\lambda^2\sigma^2/2}. \] Therefore \[ E\max_j|Z_j| \leq\frac{\log(2N)}{\lambda}+\frac{\lambda\sigma^2}{2}. \] Optimize at \(\lambda=\sqrt{2\log(2N)}/\sigma\). \(\square\)

Corollary. If \(\mathcal F=\{f_1,\ldots,f_N\}\), then conditionally on the sample, \[ E_\varepsilon\|\mathbb G_n^\varepsilon\|_{\mathcal F} \leq \max_{1\leq j\leq N}\|f_j\|_{P_n,2} \sqrt{2\log(2N)}. \] If \(|f|\leq b\) for all \(f\in\mathcal F\), then \[ E\|\mathbb G_n\|_{\mathcal F} \leq 2b\sqrt{2\log(2N)}. \]

The \(\sqrt{\log N}\) term is the atom from which chaining is built: at each resolution one applies this finite maximum bound to all increments between two successive nets.

Contraction

Theorem (Rademacher contraction). Let \(\phi_i:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) satisfy \(\phi_i(0)=0\) and \[ |\phi_i(s)-\phi_i(t)|\leq L|s-t|. \] For any \(T\subset\mathbb R^n\), \[ E_\varepsilon\sup_{t\in T} \left|\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_i\phi_i(t_i)\right| \leq 2L E_\varepsilon\sup_{t\in T} \left|\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_it_i\right|. \]

The factor \(2\) disappears in several one-sided formulations. The theorem says that a Lipschitz loss cannot make a class substantially more complex than its predictions. For example, if \(|y|,|f(x)|\leq B\), then \(u\mapsto(y-u)^2\) is \(4B\)-Lipschitz on \([-B,B]\), so the Rademacher complexity of squared losses is controlled by that of the regression class.

Rademacher complexity and generalization

Definition. The conditional and expected Rademacher complexities are \[ \widehat{\mathfrak R}_n(\mathcal F) =E_\varepsilon\sup_{f\in\mathcal F} \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_if(X_i), \qquad \mathfrak R_n(\mathcal F) =E\widehat{\mathfrak R}_n(\mathcal F). \]

Corollary. If \(\mathcal F\) is symmetric, meaning \(f\in\mathcal F\) implies \(-f\in\mathcal F\), then \[ E\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}|(P_n-P)f| \leq 2\mathfrak R_n(\mathcal F). \]

Example (linear predictors). Suppose \(\|x\|_2\leq R\) and \[ \mathcal F=\{x\mapsto w^\top x:\|w\|_2\leq B\}. \] Then \[ \begin{aligned} \widehat{\mathfrak R}_n(\mathcal F) &=\frac Bn E_\varepsilon \left\|\sum_{i=1}^n\varepsilon_iX_i\right\|_2\\ &\leq\frac Bn \left(\sum_{i=1}^n\|X_i\|_2^2\right)^{1/2} \leq\frac{BR}{\sqrt n}. \end{aligned} \] The first equality is Euclidean duality and the inequality is Jensen combined with cancellation of cross terms. The bound depends on norm geometry rather than the ambient dimension.

Symmetrization is a reduction, not the end of the proof. The remaining problem is geometric: bound a sub-Gaussian supremum in the random metric \(L_2(P_n)\). VC entropy, analytic entropy, and chaining provide three complementary answers.
