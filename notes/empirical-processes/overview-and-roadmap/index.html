Empirical-process theory replaces one test function in the law of large numbers or central limit theorem by an entire class. The resulting random function records all empirical averages simultaneously, making uniform consistency, confidence bands, goodness-of-fit statistics, bootstrap processes, and estimator remainders instances of one theory.

Empirical measures and indexed fluctuations

Definition. Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) be i.i.d. with law \(P\) on \((\mathcal X,\mathcal A)\). The empirical measure is \[ P_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{X_i}, \qquad P_nf=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nf(X_i). \] For a class \(\mathcal F\) of measurable real functions, define \[ \|Q\|_{\mathcal F}=\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}|Qf|, \qquad \mathbb G_nf=\sqrt n(P_n-P)f. \] The map \(f\mapsto\mathbb G_nf\) is the empirical process indexed by \(\mathcal F\).

For a fixed \(f\in L^2(P)\), \(\mathbb G_nf\) is governed by the ordinary central limit theorem. The difficulty is uniformity: an infinite class can contain a data-dependent function that follows random noise even though every fixed coordinate is well behaved.

Glivenko-Cantelli and Donsker classes

Definition. A class \(\mathcal F\) is \(P\)-Glivenko-Cantelli if \[ \|P_n-P\|_{\mathcal F}\to0 \] in outer probability. It is \(P\)-Donsker if \[ \mathbb G_n\rightsquigarrow\mathbb G_P \quad\text{in }\ell^\infty(\mathcal F), \] where \(\mathbb G_P\) is a tight centered Gaussian process with covariance \[ \operatorname{Cov}\{\mathbb G_Pf,\mathbb G_Pg\} =P\{(f-Pf)(g-Pg)\}. \]

Donsker implies coordinatewise finite variances and, because \(n^{-1/2}\|\mathbb G_n\|_{\mathcal F}\to_p0\), typically implies Glivenko-Cantelli under an integrable envelope. The converse is false: uniform convergence at law-of-large-numbers scale need not yield a tight root-\(n\) process.

The canonical semimetric

Definition. The intrinsic semimetric of the \(P\)-Brownian bridge is \[ \rho_P(f,g) =\left[ P\{(f-g)-P(f-g)\}^2 \right]^{1/2}. \] The larger but convenient metric \[ d_P(f,g)=\|f-g\|_{P,2} \] satisfies \(\rho_P(f,g)\le d_P(f,g)\).

Proof of the comparison. Variance is bounded by second moment: \[ \rho_P(f,g)^2 =P(f-g)^2-\{P(f-g)\}^2 \le P(f-g)^2=d_P(f,g)^2. \]

Functions at zero \(\rho_P\)-distance define the same Gaussian coordinate. Tight Gaussian limits have sample paths that are uniformly continuous for a suitable totally bounded version of this semimetric. Entropy and chaining quantify how many distinguishable functions remain at each scale.

Five recurring proof problems

  1. Measurability: the supremum over an uncountable class may not be measurable, which requires outer probability or a separability reduction.
  2. Finite-dimensional convergence: ordinary multivariate limit theorems identify candidate coordinate laws.
  3. Tightness and equicontinuity: these prevent oscillation between finitely many inspected coordinates.
  4. Complexity: covering, bracketing, VC dimension, symmetrization, and chaining turn class size into numerical bounds.
  5. Localization: statistical estimators need suprema over shrinking, often random, neighborhoods rather than a fixed global class.

Indicator, loss, and score classes

Example (empirical distribution function). For \[ \mathcal F=\{1_{(-\infty,t]}:t\in\mathbb R\}, \] \[ P_nf=F_n(t),\qquad Pf=F(t),\qquad \mathbb G_nf=\sqrt n\{F_n(t)-F(t)\}. \] Glivenko-Cantelli becomes uniform convergence of \(F_n\), and Donsker convergence becomes the Brownian-bridge limit.

Example (M-estimation). If \[ \hat\theta_n\in\arg\max_{\theta\in\Theta}P_nm_\theta, \] consistency follows from uniform control of \[ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|(P_n-P)m_\theta|. \] Rates depend on the local oscillation \[ \sup_{d(\theta,\theta_0)\le\delta} |\mathbb G_n(m_\theta-m_{\theta_0})| \] balanced against curvature of \(P(m_\theta-m_{\theta_0})\).

Example (estimated nuisance functions). A semiparametric expansion contains \[ \mathbb G_n(\phi_{\hat\eta}-\phi_{\eta_0}). \] If \(\hat\eta\) lies in a Donsker class and converges in \(L^2(P)\), asymptotic equicontinuity can make this term \(o_p(1)\). Cross-fitting provides another route by conditionally fixing \(\hat\eta\) on evaluation folds.

A deterministic-stochastic decomposition

Reference-function decomposition. For any functions \(f,f_0\), \[ P_nf-Pf_0 =(P_n-P)(f-f_0) +(P_n-P)f_0 +P(f-f_0). \] Thus a data-dependent \(f\) is handled by a local empirical-process increment, one fixed-coordinate fluctuation, and deterministic approximation.

Proof. Add and subtract \(P_nf_0\) and \(Pf\), then group terms: \[ P_n(f-f_0)+Pf-Pf_0 =(P_n-P)(f-f_0)+(P_n-P)f_0+P(f-f_0). \]

The remainder of the note develops the conditions that control each term: weak convergence and measurability first, complexity and maximal inequalities next, refined bootstrap and sequential processes after that, and estimator applications last.
