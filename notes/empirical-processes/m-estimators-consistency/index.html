An \(M\)-estimator is defined by optimization rather than an equation. Uniform laws of large numbers ensure that the random objective has the same global shape as its population limit; identification then forces every approximate maximizer into the target neighborhood.

Exact and approximate maximizers

Definition. Let \[ M_n(\theta)=P_nm_\theta, \qquad M(\theta)=Pm_\theta. \] A sequence \(\hat\theta_n\) is an approximate \(M\)-estimator if \[ M_n(\hat\theta_n) \geq\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}M_n(\theta)-o_p(1). \]

The definition accommodates numerical error, nonattainment of the supremum, and measurable approximate selectors. For rates, the optimization error must be smaller than the criterion gap at the target rate.

The argmax consistency theorem

Theorem. Let \((\Theta,d)\) be a semimetric space. Suppose:

  1. \[ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|M_n(\theta)-M(\theta)| \xrightarrow{p}0; \]
  2. for every \(\varepsilon>0\), \[ M(\theta_0)> \sup_{d(\theta,\theta_0)\geq\varepsilon}M(\theta); \]
  3. \(\hat\theta_n\) approximately maximizes \(M_n\).

Then \(d(\hat\theta_n,\theta_0)\to_p0\).

Proof. Fix \(\varepsilon>0\) and define the positive gap \[ \Delta_\varepsilon = M(\theta_0) -\sup_{d(\theta,\theta_0)\geq\varepsilon}M(\theta)>0. \] On the event \(\|M_n-M\|_\Theta<\Delta_\varepsilon/3\), every \(\theta\) outside the \(\varepsilon\)-ball satisfies \[ M_n(\theta) \leq M(\theta_0)-2\Delta_\varepsilon/3 \leq M_n(\theta_0)-\Delta_\varepsilon/3. \] If the optimization error is smaller than \(\Delta_\varepsilon/3\), an approximate maximizer cannot lie outside that ball. Both events have probability tending to one. \(\square\)

Obtaining the uniform law

Proposition (compact Lipschitz criterion). Suppose \(\Theta\subset\mathbb R^d\) is compact, \[ |m_\theta(x)-m_\eta(x)| \leq L(x)\|\theta-\eta\|, \qquad PL<\infty, \] and \(P|m_{\theta_0}|<\infty\) for one \(\theta_0\). Then \[ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|P_nm_\theta-Pm_\theta| \to0 \quad\text{almost surely}. \]

Proof. Cover \(\Theta\) by finitely many balls of radius \(\delta\), centered at \(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_N\). For every \(\theta\) in the ball around \(\theta_j\), \[ |(P_n-P)(m_\theta-m_{\theta_j})| \leq\delta(P_nL+PL). \] Therefore \[ \sup_\theta|(P_n-P)m_\theta| \leq\max_j|(P_n-P)m_{\theta_j}| +\delta(P_nL+PL). \] The finite maximum tends to zero and \(P_nL\to PL\) almost surely. Let \(\delta\downarrow0\). \(\square\)

For nonparametric criteria, one instead verifies that \(\{m_\theta:\theta\in\Theta\}\) is Glivenko–Cantelli by bracketing, VC structure, monotonicity, or an envelope-and-continuity theorem.

Noncompact parameter spaces

Proposition. Global compactness can be replaced by:

  1. for every \(\eta>0\), a compact \(K_\eta\subset\Theta\) such that \(P(\hat\theta_n\in K_\eta)\geq1-\eta+o(1)\);
  2. uniform convergence on each \(K_\eta\);
  3. separation of \(\theta_0\) within \(K_\eta\).

Then \(\hat\theta_n\to_p\theta_0\).

Tightness of \(\hat\theta_n\) often follows from coercivity: \[ \sup_{d(\theta,\theta_0)>R}M(\theta) \longrightarrow-\infty \quad\text{as }R\to\infty, \] together with a uniform stochastic bound on outer shells.

Maximum likelihood under correct specification

Example. Let \(p_\theta\) be densities and \(m_\theta(x)=\log p_\theta(x)\). Under \(P=P_{\theta_0}\), \[ M(\theta)-M(\theta_0) = -D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{\theta_0}\|P_\theta) \leq0. \] Equality holds only when \(P_\theta=P_{\theta_0}\). Thus model identifiability supplies the population separation, while a uniform law for log-likelihoods supplies stochastic approximation.

Misspecified criteria and pseudo-truth

Example. If \(P\) is outside the likelihood model, the same estimator converges, under suitable conditions, to \[ \theta^\dagger \in\arg\max_\theta P\log p_\theta =\arg\min_\theta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|P_\theta). \] Consistency is then relative to a pseudo-true projection, not to a claim that the model is correct. If the minimizer is unique and separated, the argmax theorem is unchanged.

Least squares

Example. For regression functions \(g_\theta\), minimizing \(P_n(Y-g_\theta(X))^2\) targets \[ \theta_0\in\arg\min_\theta P(Y-g_\theta(X))^2. \] If \(g_0(x)=E(Y\mid X=x)\) lies in the class, then \[ P(Y-g_\theta(X))^2-P(Y-g_0(X))^2 =P\{g_\theta(X)-g_0(X)\}^2. \] The population criterion gap is exactly squared \(L_2(P_X)\)-distance, so identification and curvature are transparent.

Set identification

Proposition. If the population maximizer set \[ \Theta_0=\arg\max_{\theta\in\Theta}M(\theta) \] is not a singleton, uniform convergence and separation from \(\Theta_0\) imply \[ d(\hat\theta_n,\Theta_0)\to_p0, \] but do not determine a unique limit point within \(\Theta_0\).

Consistency is therefore logically separate from asymptotic normality. The next page adds local curvature and a stochastic modulus to determine how rapidly an \(M\)-estimator approaches the identified set or point.
