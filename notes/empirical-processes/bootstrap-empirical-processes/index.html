The nonparametric bootstrap treats the empirical law \(P_n\) as a surrogate population. Process-level bootstrap consistency asks whether sampling from \(P_n\) reproduces, conditionally on the data, the same uniform fluctuation that sampling from \(P\) produces.

Multinomial representation

Definition. Given \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\), draw \(X_1^*,\ldots,X_n^*\) independently from \(P_n\), and write \[ P_n^*=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\delta_{X_j^*}, \qquad \mathbb G_n^*=\sqrt n(P_n^*-P_n). \] Equivalently, if \((W_{n1},\ldots,W_{nn})\sim \operatorname{Multinomial}(n;1/n,\ldots,1/n)\), then \[ \mathbb G_n^*f =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n(W_{ni}-1)f(X_i). \]

Conditional centering is exact because \(E_*(W_{ni}-1)=0\). The multinomial weights are exchangeable and dependent, with \[ \operatorname{Var}_*(W_{ni})=1-\frac1n, \qquad \operatorname{Cov}_*(W_{ni},W_{nj})=-\frac1n \quad(i\ne j). \]

Proposition. Conditional on the data, \[ E_*(\mathbb G_n^*f\,\mathbb G_n^*g) = P_n\{(f-P_nf)(g-P_ng)\}. \]

Proof. Since a bootstrap draw \(X^*\) has law \(P_n\), the conditional covariance of \(n^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^n\{f(X_j^*)-P_nf\}\) is the covariance of one draw: \[ P_n(fg)-P_nf\,P_ng. \] \(\square\)

The bootstrap Donsker theorem

Theorem. Let \(\mathcal F\) be a suitably measurable \(P\)-Donsker class. Then, conditionally on the observations, \[ \mathbb G_n^*\rightsquigarrow\mathbb G_P \qquad\text{in }\ell^\infty(\mathcal F) \] in \(P\)-probability. Equivalently, \[ \sup_{h\in BL_1} \left| E_*h(\mathbb G_n^*)-Eh(\mathbb G_P) \right|\xrightarrow{p}0. \]

Proof outline. Conditional finite-dimensional convergence follows from the multinomial central limit theorem and the empirical covariance convergence. For tightness, Poissonization or exchangeable-weight inequalities compare the bootstrap process with a multiplier process. The Donsker entropy or asymptotic-equicontinuity bounds remain valid after replacing \(P\) by \(P_n\), while multinomial weight tails satisfy the required moment conditions. Conditional asymptotic equicontinuity and the finite-dimensional limit yield the result. \(\square\)

The theorem is process-valued: one resample simultaneously approximates the joint law of every coordinate in \(\mathcal F\). This is what makes it suitable for suprema, bands, and other non-scalar functionals.

Exchangeably weighted bootstraps

Definition. An exchangeably weighted bootstrap has \[ \mathbb G_n^Wf = \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n(W_{ni}-\bar W_n)f(X_i), \qquad \bar W_n=n^{-1}\sum_iW_{ni}, \] where the weight vector is exchangeable and independent of the sample.

Multinomial, Bayesian-bootstrap exponential, and several subsampling schemes fit this representation after normalization. First-order validity requires an asymptotic weight variance of one, negligible maximal weight at \(\sqrt n\) scale, and an appropriate uniform tail integral.

The bootstrap functional delta method

Theorem. Let \(\phi:\mathbb D_\phi\subset\mathbb D\to\mathbb E\) be Hadamard differentiable at \(P\), tangentially to a set \(\mathbb D_0\), with continuous linear derivative \(\phi'_P\). If \[ \sqrt n(P_n-P)\rightsquigarrow G\in\mathbb D_0 \] and, conditionally, \[ \sqrt n(P_n^*-P_n)\rightsquigarrow G, \] then \[ \sqrt n\{\phi(P_n^*)-\phi(P_n)\} \rightsquigarrow \phi'_P(G) \] conditionally in probability, the same limit as \(\sqrt n\{\phi(P_n)-\phi(P)\}\).

Proof. Hadamard differentiability gives, uniformly along convergent tangent sequences, \[ \frac{\phi(P+t_nh_n)-\phi(P)}{t_n} \to\phi'_P(h). \] Apply this expansion jointly to \(P_n-P\) and \(P_n^*-P=(P_n-P)+(P_n^*-P_n)\). Subtract the expansion for \(\phi(P_n)-\phi(P)\). The conditional continuous-mapping theorem and linearity of \(\phi'_P\) leave \(\phi'_P\{\sqrt n(P_n^*-P_n)\}+o_{P^*}(1)\). \(\square\)

This two-step architecture is the process foundation of the bootstrap methods in Statistical inference, §20.

Failure for nonregular functionals

Example (sample maximum). Let \(X_i\sim U[0,1]\), \(M_n=\max_iX_i\). Then \[ n(1-M_n)\rightsquigarrow\operatorname{Exp}(1). \] In a bootstrap sample, the original maximum \(M_n\) is omitted with conditional probability \[ (1-1/n)^n\to e^{-1}, \] but whenever it is selected, \(M_n^*=M_n\). Hence the conditional law of \(n(M_n-M_n^*)\) has an asymptotic atom at zero, unlike the continuous target law. The ordinary \(n\)-out-of-\(n\) bootstrap is inconsistent.

The empirical process itself may bootstrap correctly while the final functional does not. Maxima, boundary parameters, cube-root estimators, and merely directionally differentiable maps require separate analysis. Remedies include \(m\)-out-of-\(n\) bootstrap with \(m/n\to0\), subsampling, smoothing, or a bootstrap tailored to the directional derivative.

From conditional laws to confidence sets

Proposition. If a real statistic \(T_n\rightsquigarrow T\) and its bootstrap version satisfies \[ \sup_x\left|P_*(T_n^*\leq x)-P(T\leq x)\right|\to_p0, \] with continuous strictly increasing limit distribution at its \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile \(c_{1-\alpha}\), then the conditional bootstrap quantile \(c_{n,1-\alpha}^*\to_pc_{1-\alpha}\). Consequently, \(P(T_n\leq c_{n,1-\alpha}^*)\to1-\alpha\).

For process statistics such as \(\|\mathbb G_n\|_{\mathcal F}\), continuity of the supremum map is automatic in the supremum norm, but continuity of the limit distribution at the selected quantile must still be checked.
