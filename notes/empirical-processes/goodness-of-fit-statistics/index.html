Goodness-of-fit statistics measure a distance between an empirical law and a null model. Their null distributions are not separate miracles: they are continuous or quadratic functionals of the empirical distribution process. The process viewpoint also shows exactly why estimating null parameters changes the reference law.

A fully specified continuous null

Let \(H_0:F=F_0\), where \(F_0\) is continuous. The probability integral transform reduces the empirical process to \[ \sqrt n\{F_n(F_0^{-1}(u))-u\}\rightsquigarrow B(u), \qquad0\leq u\leq1, \] where \(B\) is a standard Brownian bridge.

Definition. The classical EDF statistics are \[ D_n=\sqrt n\sup_x|F_n(x)-F_0(x)|, \] \[ W_n^2=n\int\{F_n(x)-F_0(x)\}^2\,\mathrm{d}F_0(x), \] and \[ A_n^2=n\int \frac{\{F_n(x)-F_0(x)\}^2} {F_0(x)\{1-F_0(x)\}} \,\mathrm{d}F_0(x). \]

Kolmogorov–Smirnov uses the uniform metric. Cramér–von Mises uses unweighted \(L_2(F_0)\). Anderson–Darling magnifies tail discrepancies through the singular weight \(\{u(1-u)\}^{-1}\), so its convergence requires a weighted topology rather than the ordinary supremum-norm continuous-mapping theorem alone.

Theorem. Under a fully specified continuous null, \[ D_n\rightsquigarrow\sup_{0\leq u\leq1}|B(u)|, \qquad W_n^2\rightsquigarrow\int_0^1B(u)^2\,\mathrm{d}u. \]

Proof. The maps \[ z\mapsto\|z\|_\infty, \qquad z\mapsto\int_0^1z(u)^2\,\mathrm{d}u \] are continuous on \(C[0,1]\) under the supremum norm. Apply the continuous-mapping theorem to the uniform empirical-process limit. \(\square\)

The two classical limit laws

Proposition (Kolmogorov distribution). For \(x>0\), \[ P\left\{\sup_{0\leq u\leq1}|B(u)|\leq x\right\} = 1-2\sum_{k=1}^\infty(-1)^{k-1}e^{-2k^2x^2}. \]

The reflection principle applied repeatedly between the two barriers \(\pm x\) gives this alternating image sum. It is distribution-free because the continuous \(F_0\) disappears under the probability integral transform.

Proposition (Cramér–von Mises expansion). If \(Z_1,Z_2,\ldots\) are independent \(N(0,1)\), then \[ \int_0^1B(u)^2\,\mathrm{d}u \overset d= \sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{Z_k^2}{\pi^2k^2}. \]

Proof. The Brownian-bridge covariance operator on \(L_2[0,1]\) has kernel \(K(s,t)=s\wedge t-st\). Its eigenfunctions and eigenvalues are \[ e_k(t)=\sqrt2\sin(\pi kt), \qquad \lambda_k=(\pi^2k^2)^{-1}. \] The Karhunen–Loève expansion is \(B=\sum_k\sqrt{\lambda_k}Z_ke_k\) in \(L_2\). Parseval's identity gives the result. \(\square\)

Composite parametric nulls

Now let \(H_0:F=F_\theta\) for some \(\theta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb R^p\), and estimate \(\theta\) from the same sample. Suppose \[ \sqrt n(\hat\theta-\theta_0) =\mathbb G_n\psi_{\theta_0}+o_p(1) \] and \(F_\theta(x)\) is differentiable with derivative \(\dot F_{\theta_0}(x)\in\mathbb R^p\).

Theorem (fitted empirical process). Uniformly in \(x\), \[ \sqrt n\{F_n(x)-F_{\hat\theta}(x)\} = \mathbb G_n \left[ 1_{(-\infty,x]} -\dot F_{\theta_0}(x)^\top\psi_{\theta_0} \right] +o_p(1). \] Hence the limit is a centered Gaussian process with covariance obtained from the corrected functions inside the brackets.

Proof. Add and subtract \(F_{\theta_0}\): \[ \sqrt n(F_n-F_{\hat\theta}) =\mathbb G_n1_{(-\infty,x]} -\sqrt n(F_{\hat\theta}-F_{\theta_0}). \] Uniform differentiability gives \[ \sqrt n\{F_{\hat\theta}(x)-F_{\theta_0}(x)\} = \dot F_{\theta_0}(x)^\top \sqrt n(\hat\theta-\theta_0)+o_p(1). \] Insert the asymptotic linear representation and combine the two empirical-process terms. \(\square\)

For an efficient maximum-likelihood estimator, \[ \psi_{\theta_0}=I_{\theta_0}^{-1}\dot\ell_{\theta_0}. \] The correction subtracts the component of the raw bridge explained by movement along the fitted parametric model. Geometrically, it is a projection away from the score tangent space. The null law is therefore model dependent and is not the Kolmogorov distribution.

Parametric bootstrap calibration

Proposition. Under regularity and consistent estimation, the following parametric bootstrap reproduces the fitted-process limit:

  1. draw \(X_1^*,\ldots,X_n^*\) from \(F_{\hat\theta}\);
  2. re-estimate \(\hat\theta^*\) in the bootstrap sample;
  3. compute the statistic from \(F_n^*-F_{\hat\theta^*}\).

Re-estimation is essential. Comparing \(F_n^*\) only with the fixed \(F_{\hat\theta}\) would simulate an unprojected bridge and therefore calibrate the wrong null law.

Pearson chi-square as a finite empirical process

Theorem. Let \(A_1,\ldots,A_k\) partition the sample space, with \(p_j=P_0(A_j)>0\). Under \(P=P_0\), \[ X_n^2 = n\sum_{j=1}^k\frac{\{P_n(A_j)-p_j\}^2}{p_j} \rightsquigarrow\chi_{k-1}^2. \]

Proof. The multinomial central limit theorem gives \[ \sqrt n(P_n(A_1)-p_1,\ldots,P_n(A_k)-p_k)^\top \rightsquigarrow N_k(0,\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top). \] After standardizing by \(\operatorname{diag}(p)^{-1/2}\), the covariance is the orthogonal projection onto the \((k-1)\)-dimensional complement of \((\sqrt{p_1},\ldots,\sqrt{p_k})^\top\). The squared Euclidean norm of the projected standard normal is \(\chi_{k-1}^2\). \(\square\)

Estimating \(r\) regular parameters typically removes \(r\) further tangent directions and changes the degrees of freedom to \(k-1-r\).

Local alternatives

Proposition. If a sequence of alternatives satisfies \[ \sqrt n\{F_n^{(0)}(x)-F_0(x)\}\to\Delta(x) \] uniformly and the centered empirical fluctuation still converges to the null bridge, then \[ \sqrt n\{\widehat F_n-F_0\}\rightsquigarrow B\circ F_0+\Delta. \] Consequently, \[ D_n\rightsquigarrow\|B\circ F_0+\Delta\|_\infty, \qquad W_n^2\rightsquigarrow \int(B\circ F_0+\Delta)^2\,\mathrm{d}F_0. \]

The deterministic drift determines local power. Supremum tests respond to the largest standardized discrepancy, integrated-square tests aggregate diffuse departures, and Anderson–Darling gives greater leverage to tail drift. The choice of functional should therefore reflect the alternatives one wants the test to detect, not only the availability of a tabulated null law.
