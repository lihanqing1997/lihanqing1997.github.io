A function class is infinite, but an empirical process can only resolve it to finite accuracy. Covering and bracketing numbers count the representatives needed at each resolution. Their logarithms are called entropies because they measure the information required to encode an index up to a prescribed statistical error.

Coverings, packings, and total boundedness

Definition. Let \((T,d)\) be a semimetric space.

  1. An \(\varepsilon\)-net is a finite set \(T_\varepsilon\subset T\) such that every \(t\in T\) satisfies \(d(t,T_\varepsilon)\leq\varepsilon\).
  2. The covering number \(N(\varepsilon,T,d)\) is the least cardinality of an \(\varepsilon\)-net.
  3. An \(\varepsilon\)-packing is a set whose distinct points are more than \(\varepsilon\) apart. The packing number \(M(\varepsilon,T,d)\) is its largest possible cardinality.
  4. The metric entropy is \(H(\varepsilon,T,d)=\log N(\varepsilon,T,d)\).

Proposition. For every \(\varepsilon>0\), \[ M(2\varepsilon,T,d)\leq N(\varepsilon,T,d)\leq M(\varepsilon,T,d). \] Consequently, \(T\) is totally bounded if and only if \(N(\varepsilon,T,d)<\infty\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\).

Proof. An \(\varepsilon\)-ball contains at most one point of a \(2\varepsilon\)-packing, proving the first inequality. For the second, take a maximal \(\varepsilon\)-packing. If some \(t\in T\) were farther than \(\varepsilon\) from every packing point, adjoining \(t\) would produce a larger packing. Hence a maximal packing is an \(\varepsilon\)-net. \(\square\)

For function classes the semimetric is usually \(\|f-g\|_{Q,r}\), where \(Q\) may be the population law, the empirical measure, or an arbitrary finitely supported probability measure. Entropy is therefore not intrinsic to a set of formulas alone; it depends on which regions of the sample space the metric regards as important.

Brackets

Definition. For measurable functions \(l\leq u\), the bracket \[ [l,u]=\{f:l(x)\leq f(x)\leq u(x)\text{ for every }x\} \] has \(L_r(P)\)-width \(\|u-l\|_{P,r}\). The bracketing number \(N_{[]}(\varepsilon,\mathcal F,L_r(P))\) is the least number of brackets of width at most \(\varepsilon\) whose union contains \(\mathcal F\).

A bracket may be pointwise wide on a rare set and still be narrow in \(L_r(P)\). This is precisely why bracketing is adapted to probability: approximation error is charged according to how often it is observed.

Proposition. If every covering bracket contains an element of \(\mathcal F\), then \[ N(\varepsilon,\mathcal F,L_r(P)) \leq N_{[]}(\varepsilon,\mathcal F,L_r(P)). \] There is no converse bound without additional structure.

Proof. Select \(f_j\in\mathcal F\cap[l_j,u_j]\) from every nonempty bracket. If \(f\in[l_j,u_j]\), then pointwise \[ |f-f_j|\leq u_j-l_j, \] so \(\|f-f_j\|_{P,r}\leq\varepsilon\). Thus the selected functions form an \(\varepsilon\)-net. A metric ball imposes no pointwise order, so it need not fit into any comparably narrow bracket. \(\square\)

Uniform entropy

Definition. If \(F\) is an envelope of \(\mathcal F\), the normalized uniform entropy is \[ H_U(\varepsilon,\mathcal F,F) = \sup_Q \log N\!\left( \varepsilon\|F\|_{Q,2}, \mathcal F,L_2(Q) \right), \] where \(Q\) ranges over finitely supported probability measures with \(\|F\|_{Q,2}>0\).

The supremum over \(Q\) is what makes the bound usable after conditioning on \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\): one may set \(Q=P_n\). Distribution-specific bracketing can be sharper, whereas uniform covering is particularly natural for symmetrization and VC classes.

Entropy calculus

Lemma. Let \(d\) be induced by a norm.

  1. If \(\mathcal F\subset\mathcal G\), then \(N(\varepsilon,\mathcal F,d)\leq N(\varepsilon,\mathcal G,d)\).
  2. For \(\mathcal F+\mathcal G=\{f+g\}\), \[ N(\varepsilon+\delta,\mathcal F+\mathcal G,d) \leq N(\varepsilon,\mathcal F,d)N(\delta,\mathcal G,d). \]
  3. If \(\Phi\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz, then \[ N(L\varepsilon,\Phi(\mathcal F),d) \leq N(\varepsilon,\mathcal F,d). \]
  4. If \(|f|,|g|\leq B\), then \[ N(2B\varepsilon,\mathcal F\mathcal G,L_2(Q)) \leq N(\varepsilon,\mathcal F,L_2(Q)) N(\varepsilon,\mathcal G,L_2(Q)). \]

Proof. The first assertion is immediate. For sums, add a center from an \(\varepsilon\)-net of \(\mathcal F\) to a center from a \(\delta\)-net of \(\mathcal G\), then use the triangle inequality. The Lipschitz assertion follows from \(d(\Phi f,\Phi g)\leq Ld(f,g)\). Finally, \[ |fg-f'g'|\leq |g||f-f'|+|f'||g-g'| \leq B|f-f'|+B|g-g'|, \] and the triangle inequality proves the product bound. \(\square\)

Euclidean and ordered examples

Example (Euclidean ball). Volume comparison gives, for the radius-\(R\) ball \(B_2^d(R)\), \[ \left(\frac{R}{\varepsilon}\right)^d \lesssim N(\varepsilon,B_2^d(R),\|\cdot\|_2) \leq \left(1+\frac{2R}{\varepsilon}\right)^d. \] Hence its entropy is of order \(d\log(R/\varepsilon)\), not \(\varepsilon^{-d}\). A compact \(d\)-parameter Lipschitz model inherits this logarithmic entropy.

Proof of the upper bound. Take a maximal \(\varepsilon\)-packing \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\). The disjoint balls \(B(x_j,\varepsilon/2)\) lie inside \(B(0,R+\varepsilon/2)\). Comparing volumes, \[ M(\varepsilon/2)^d\leq (R+\varepsilon/2)^d. \] Thus \(M\leq(1+2R/\varepsilon)^d\), and maximality makes the packing an \(\varepsilon\)-net. \(\square\)

Example (half-line indicators). Let \(\mathcal F=\{1_{(-\infty,t]}:t\in\mathbb R\}\). Choose quantiles \(-\infty=t_0\leq t_1\leq\cdots\leq t_m=\infty\) so that \(P(t_{j-1},t_j]\leq\varepsilon^2\), with \(m\leq\lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\rceil\). For \(t\in[t_{j-1},t_j]\), \[ 1_{(-\infty,t_{j-1}]}\leq1_{(-\infty,t]}\leq1_{(-\infty,t_j]}, \] and the bracket has \(L_2(P)\)-width at most \(\varepsilon\). Therefore \[ N_{[]}(\varepsilon,\mathcal F,L_2(P)) \leq \lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\rceil. \] Its square-root log entropy is integrable at zero, which is the analytic core of the classical Donsker theorem.

Example (bounded monotone functions). Let \(\mathcal M\) be the nondecreasing functions \([0,1]\to[0,1]\). For every probability law \(P\), \[ \log N_{[]}(\varepsilon,\mathcal M,L_2(P)) \lesssim \varepsilon^{-1}. \] The entropy is infinite-dimensional but its square-root integral is finite because \(\int_0^1\varepsilon^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon<\infty\). Thus bounded monotonicity alone can produce a Donsker class.

Which entropy condition is needed?

The level of convergence determines how entropy is used:

\[ \begin{array}{c|c} \text{objective} & \text{typical complexity requirement}\\ \hline \text{finite approximation / total boundedness} &N(\varepsilon)<\infty\text{ for every }\varepsilon>0,\\[2mm] \text{uniform law of large numbers} &\text{finite bracketing at every fixed scale},\\[2mm] \text{uniform central limit theorem} &\displaystyle\int_0^1\sqrt{\log N(\varepsilon)}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon<\infty,\\[3mm] \text{estimator rates} &\text{a localized entropy integral at the unknown rate.} \end{array} \]

These are sufficient templates, not equivalences. The geometry of a class may permit sharper arguments than a global entropy integral, and a poor choice of metric can conceal useful structure.
