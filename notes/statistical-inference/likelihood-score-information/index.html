Likelihood compares parameter values after the data have been observed. Its first derivative is the random local direction in which the data favor moving the parameter; its quadratic variation is Fisher information. These objects become reliable asymptotic coordinates only under explicit regularity conditions.

Likelihood and score

Definition. In a dominated model with density \(p_\theta\), the likelihood at \(x\) is \(L(\theta;x)=p_\theta(x)\). On \(\{p_\theta(x)>0\}\), define \[ \ell(\theta;x)=\log p_\theta(x),\qquad s_\theta(x)=\nabla_\theta\ell(\theta;x),\qquad J_\theta(x)=-\nabla_\theta^2\ell(\theta;x). \] Here \(s_\theta\) is the score and \(J_\theta\) is observed information. For an i.i.d. sample, \[ \ell_n(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^n\ell(\theta;X_i),\quad S_n(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^ns_\theta(X_i),\quad J_n(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^nJ_\theta(X_i). \]

Derivatives of \(\log p_\theta\) are meaningful only where the support is locally stable. The uniform model \(U[0,\theta]\) violates this condition: the moving boundary contains first-order information that the ordinary score misses.

Fisher information identities

Definition (Fisher information). If \(s_\theta\in L^2(P_\theta)^d\), the information matrix is \[ I(\theta)=\mathbb E_\theta[s_\theta(X)s_\theta(X)^\top]. \] It is positive semidefinite because \(a^\top I(\theta)a=\mathbb E_\theta(a^\top s_\theta)^2\ge0\).

Theorem (first and second information identities). Suppose \(\Theta\) is open, the support does not depend on \(\theta\), \(p_\theta(x)\) is twice differentiable, and differentiation can be passed under the integral sign. Then \[ \mathbb E_\theta s_\theta(X)=0,\qquad I(\theta)=\mathbb E_\theta J_\theta(X). \]

Proof. Componentwise, \[ \mathbb E_\theta s_{\theta,j} =\int\frac{\partial_jp_\theta}{p_\theta}p_\theta\,\mathrm{d}\mu =\partial_j\int p_\theta\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0. \] Differentiate this zero identity with respect to \(\theta_k\): \[ 0=\int\{\partial_ks_{\theta,j} +s_{\theta,j}s_{\theta,k}\}p_\theta\,\mathrm{d}\mu. \] Since \(\partial_ks_{\theta,j}=\partial_{kj}^2\ell_\theta=-J_{\theta,jk}\), rearrangement gives \(\mathbb E_\theta J_{\theta,jk}=\mathbb E_\theta(s_{\theta,j}s_{\theta,k})\).

Corollary (information additivity). For \(n\) independent observations with information matrices \(I_i(\theta)\), \[ I_n(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^n I_i(\theta). \] In particular, an i.i.d. sample has information \(nI(\theta)\).

Proof. The joint score is \(\sum_i s_{\theta,i}\). The cross terms have expectation zero because independence and score centering give \(\mathbb E(s_{\theta,i}s_{\theta,j}^\top)=0\) for \(i\ne j\).

Normal location-scale calculation

Example. If \(X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)\) and \(\theta=(\mu,\sigma)^\top\), then \[ s_\mu(X)=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma^2},\qquad s_\sigma(X)=-\frac1\sigma+\frac{(X-\mu)^2}{\sigma^3}. \] Writing \(Z=(X-\mu)/\sigma\sim N(0,1)\) gives \[ I(\mu,\sigma) = \begin{pmatrix} \sigma^{-2}&0\\ 0&2\sigma^{-2} \end{pmatrix}. \] The zero off-diagonal term follows from \(\mathbb EZ^3=0\): mean and scale are information-orthogonal in this parametrization.

Reparametrization

Proposition. Let \(\eta=g(\theta)\) be a \(C^1\) one-to-one reparametrization with inverse \(\theta=h(\eta)\) and Jacobian \(D h(\eta)\). Then \[ s_\eta=(D h(\eta))^\top s_\theta,\qquad I_\eta(\eta)=(D h(\eta))^\top I_\theta(\theta)D h(\eta). \]

Proof. The score identity is the multivariate chain rule applied to \(\ell(h(\eta);x)\). Taking the second moment of that identity gives the information transformation law.

This covariance law is why Fisher information behaves as a Riemannian metric rather than as an ordinary matrix attached to a coordinate system; the geometric interpretation is developed in information geometry.

Differentiability in quadratic mean

Pointwise differentiability of densities is stronger than necessary and can be unstable under changes of versions. The intrinsic regularity condition differentiates square-root densities in \(L^2(\mu)\).

Definition (quadratic-mean differentiability). The model is differentiable in quadratic mean at \(\theta\) if a measurable vector \(\dot\ell_\theta\in L^2(P_\theta)^d\) exists such that, as \(h\to0\), \[ \int\left[ \sqrt{p_{\theta+h}}-\sqrt{p_\theta} -\frac12h^\top\dot\ell_\theta\sqrt{p_\theta} \right]^2\mathrm{d}\mu=o(\|h\|^2). \] The function \(\dot\ell_\theta\) is the quadratic-mean score and \(I(\theta)=P_\theta\dot\ell_\theta\dot\ell_\theta^\top\).

Proposition. Quadratic-mean differentiability implies \[ P_\theta\dot\ell_\theta=0 \quad\text{and}\quad H^2(P_{\theta+h},P_\theta) =\frac14h^\top I(\theta)h+o(\|h\|^2), \] where \(H^2(P,Q)=\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu\).

Proof. Let \(r_h\) denote the \(L^2(\mu)\) remainder. Because both square-root densities have norm one, \[ 0=\|{\sqrt{p_{\theta+h}}}\|_2^2-\|\sqrt{p_\theta}\|_2^2 =h^\top P_\theta\dot\ell_\theta+o(\|h\|), \] so \(P_\theta\dot\ell_\theta=0\). The defining expansion then gives \[ \|\sqrt{p_{\theta+h}}-\sqrt{p_\theta}\|_2^2 =\left\|\frac12h^\top\dot\ell_\theta\sqrt{p_\theta}+r_h\right\|_2^2 =\frac14h^\top I(\theta)h+o(\|h\|^2). \]

Local Kullback-Leibler curvature

Proposition. Under sufficient third-order domination in a regular model, \[ \operatorname{KL}(P_\theta\|P_{\theta+h}) =\frac12h^\top I(\theta)h+o(\|h\|^2). \]

Proof. Taylor expansion gives \[ \ell(\theta+h;X)-\ell(\theta;X) =h^\top s_\theta(X)-\frac12h^\top J_\theta(X)h+R_h(X), \] with \(P_\theta|R_h|=o(\|h\|^2)\). Negating and taking \(P_\theta\)-expectations, then using the two information identities, yields the result.

Nuisance parameters and efficient information

Partition \(\theta=(\psi^\top,\lambda^\top)^\top\) and the information matrix as \[ I= \begin{pmatrix} I_{\psi\psi}&I_{\psi\lambda}\\ I_{\lambda\psi}&I_{\lambda\lambda} \end{pmatrix}. \] Projecting the target score onto the orthocomplement of the nuisance-score span gives \[ s_\psi^{\mathrm{eff}} =s_\psi-I_{\psi\lambda}I_{\lambda\lambda}^{-1}s_\lambda, \qquad I_{\psi\cdot\lambda} =I_{\psi\psi}-I_{\psi\lambda}I_{\lambda\lambda}^{-1}I_{\lambda\psi}. \] The Schur complement \(I_{\psi\cdot\lambda}\) is the information remaining after the nuisance parameter must also be learned. Its infinite-dimensional analogue is the central construction of semiparametric efficiency theory.

Verification. The proposed residual is orthogonal to \(s_\lambda\) because \[ \mathbb E(s_\psi^{\mathrm{eff}}s_\lambda^\top) =I_{\psi\lambda}-I_{\psi\lambda}I_{\lambda\lambda}^{-1}I_{\lambda\lambda}=0. \] Expanding its covariance gives the Schur complement.
