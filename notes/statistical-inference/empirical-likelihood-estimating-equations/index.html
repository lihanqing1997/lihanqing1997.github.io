Empirical likelihood assigns probabilities directly to the observed sample points and chooses them by entropy maximization subject to estimating equations. It produces likelihood-ratio-shaped confidence regions without specifying a full parametric density, but its feasibility and chi-square calibration depend on the geometry and rank of the moment restrictions.

Moment restrictions and identification

Definition. Let \[ g(x,\theta)\in\mathbb R^r,\qquad \theta\in\Theta\subseteq\mathbb R^p, \] satisfy \[ \mathbb E g(X,\theta_0)=0. \] The parameter is locally identified if the Jacobian \[ G=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^\top} \mathbb E g(X,\theta)\right|_{\theta=\theta_0} \] has column rank \(p\). The model is just identified when \(r=p\) and overidentified when \(r>p\).

Examples include a mean restriction \(g(x,\mu)=x-\mu\), instrumental-variable moments \(g((y,x,z),\beta)=z(y-x^\top\beta)\), and score equations. The moment model contains every law \(P\) satisfying \(Pg_{\theta_0}=0\); it does not otherwise prescribe the distribution.

Empirical likelihood and feasibility

Definition. For fixed \(\theta\), write \(g_i(\theta)=g(X_i,\theta)\). The empirical likelihood is \[ L_n(\theta) =\max\left\{ \prod_{i=1}^np_i: p_i\ge0,\ \sum_{i=1}^np_i=1,\ \sum_{i=1}^np_ig_i(\theta)=0 \right\}. \] Relative to the unconstrained maximum \(n^{-n}\), define \[ R_n(\theta)=n^nL_n(\theta)\in[0,1]. \]

Convex-hull criterion. Feasible weights exist if and only if \[ 0\in\operatorname{conv}\{g_1(\theta),\ldots,g_n(\theta)\}. \] Strictly positive weights exist if zero lies in the relative interior of this convex hull.

Proof. The constraint \(\sum_i p_i g_i=0\) with \(p_i\ge0\) and \(\sum_i p_i=1\) is exactly a representation of zero as a convex combination. All coefficients can be positive precisely for relative-interior points of the finite convex hull.

This geometric condition is a genuine finite-sample limitation. If all observed moment vectors lie on one side of a separating hyperplane, empirical likelihood at that \(\theta\) is zero even when the population moment condition is valid.

Lagrange multiplier representation

Theorem. Suppose zero lies in the interior of the convex hull and the vectors \(g_i(\theta)\) span \(\mathbb R^r\). The unique maximizing weights are \[ p_i(\theta) =\frac1{n\{1+\lambda(\theta)^\top g_i(\theta)\}}, \] where \(\lambda(\theta)\) is the unique solution in \[ \Lambda_\theta =\{\lambda:1+\lambda^\top g_i(\theta)>0,\ i=1,\ldots,n\} \] of \[ \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{g_i(\theta)} {1+\lambda^\top g_i(\theta)}=0. \] Consequently, \[ -2\log R_n(\theta) =2\sum_{i=1}^n \log\{1+\lambda(\theta)^\top g_i(\theta)\}. \]

Proof. Maximize \(\sum_i\log p_i\) under the equality constraints. With multipliers \(\alpha\in\mathbb R\) and \(\gamma\in\mathbb R^r\), stationarity gives \[ p_i^{-1}-\alpha-\gamma^\top g_i=0. \] Multiply by \(p_i\), sum over \(i\), and use both constraints to obtain \(\alpha=n\). Put \(\lambda=\gamma/n\), which gives the stated weights; the moment constraint gives the equation for \(\lambda\). Strict concavity of \(\sum_i\log p_i\) gives uniqueness. Substitution into \(R_n=n^n\prod_i p_i\) gives the ratio formula.

Wilks expansion for a fixed moment restriction

Empirical Wilks theorem. Suppose \(\mathbb Eg(X,\theta_0)=0\), \[ \Omega=\mathbb E[g(X,\theta_0)g(X,\theta_0)^\top] \] is positive definite, and \(\mathbb E\|g(X,\theta_0)\|^{2+\delta}\lt\infty\) for some \(\delta>0\). Then \[ -2\log R_n(\theta_0)\rightsquigarrow\chi_r^2. \]

Proof. Write \(g_i=g(X_i,\theta_0)\), \(\bar g=P_ng\), and \(\hat\Omega=P_n(gg^\top)\). The moment condition for \(\lambda\) and the expansion \((1+u)^{-1}=1-u+O(u^2)\) give \[ 0=\bar g-\hat\Omega\lambda +P_n\!\left[ g_i\frac{(\lambda^\top g_i)^2}{1+\lambda^\top g_i} \right]. \] The moment assumption implies \(\max_i\|g_i\|=o_p(\sqrt n)\), \(\bar g=O_p(n^{-1/2})\), and \(\hat\Omega\to_p\Omega\). Convexity of the dual criterion then yields \[ \lambda=\hat\Omega^{-1}\bar g+o_p(n^{-1/2}), \qquad \max_i|\lambda^\top g_i|=o_p(1). \] Expanding \(\log(1+u)=u-u^2/2+O(|u|^3)\), \[ -2\log R_n(\theta_0) =2n\lambda^\top\bar g -n\lambda^\top\hat\Omega\lambda+o_p(1) =n\bar g^\top\hat\Omega^{-1}\bar g+o_p(1). \] Finally, \(\sqrt n\,\bar g\rightsquigarrow N(0,\Omega)\) and \(\hat\Omega\to_p\Omega\), so the quadratic form converges to \(\chi_r^2\).

Example (the population mean). For \(g_i(\mu)=X_i-\mu\), the profile empirical likelihood for \(\mu\) uses weights \[ p_i(\mu)=\frac1{n\{1+\lambda(X_i-\mu)\}}. \] It is feasible exactly when \(\mu\) lies between the sample minimum and maximum. At the true mean, the empirical likelihood ratio converges to \(\chi_1^2\) without separately inserting a variance estimator.

Profiling over the parameter

When \(r\ge p\), define \[ \hat\theta_{\mathrm{EL}}\in\arg\max_{\theta\in\Theta}R_n(\theta). \] Under smooth identification, \[ \sqrt n(\hat\theta_{\mathrm{EL}}-\theta_0) \rightsquigarrow N\!\left(0,(G^\top\Omega^{-1}G)^{-1}\right). \] For testing \(q\) smooth restrictions \(a(\theta)=0\), the difference between constrained and unconstrained empirical log-likelihood ratios converges to \(\chi_q^2\). Testing the overidentifying restrictions themselves produces \(r-p\) degrees of freedom.

Proof outline. Uniformly for \(\theta=\theta_0+h/\sqrt n\), \[ \sqrt n\,P_ng_\theta =\sqrt n\,P_ng_{\theta_0}+Gh+o_p(1). \] The fixed-\(\theta\) expansion makes the empirical log-likelihood locally equivalent to \[ (\sqrt n\,P_ng_{\theta_0}+Gh)^\top \Omega^{-1} (\sqrt n\,P_ng_{\theta_0}+Gh). \] Minimizing this quadratic form yields \[ \sqrt n(\hat\theta_{\mathrm{EL}}-\theta_0) =-(G^\top\Omega^{-1}G)^{-1} G^\top\Omega^{-1}\sqrt n\,P_ng_{\theta_0}+o_p(1). \] Orthogonal projection in the \(\Omega^{-1}\) inner product gives the chi-square degrees of freedom for constrained differences.

Relation to GMM and exponential tilting

The local empirical-likelihood criterion \[ n(P_ng_\theta)^\top\hat\Omega_\theta^{-1}(P_ng_\theta) \] is the optimally weighted generalized method-of-moments criterion. Empirical likelihood differs globally by choosing weights through reverse Kullback-Leibler divergence from the uniform empirical weights. Exponential tilting instead uses \[ p_i(\theta)\propto e^{\lambda^\top g_i(\theta)}, \] corresponding to the opposite entropy orientation. These generalized empirical likelihood methods share first-order efficiency but differ in higher-order bias and feasibility behavior.

Misspecification, boundaries, and growing moments

If no \(\theta\) satisfies \(Pg_\theta=0\), the Lagrange multiplier need not be \(O_p(n^{-1/2})\), and the central chi-square expansion fails. Boundary points can make the convex-hull event nonuniform. If \(r=r_n\) grows with \(n\), remainder control requires dimension-dependent rates, and a fixed-\(r\) Wilks theorem is not valid. Bootstrap or adjusted empirical likelihood can improve calibration, but only after the corresponding high-dimensional or misspecified limit has been established.
