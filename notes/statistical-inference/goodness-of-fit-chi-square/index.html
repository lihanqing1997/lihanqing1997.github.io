Goodness-of-fit tests compare an entire observed distribution with a null model. Finite partitions lead to multinomial chi-square geometry; continuous distribution functions lead to empirical-process functionals. Estimating null parameters changes the tangent space and therefore changes the limiting law.

Multinomial sampling

Let \(N=(N_1,\ldots,N_k)^\top\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(n,p)\), where every \(p_j>0\) and \(\sum_jp_j=1\). Put \(\hat p=N/n\) and \(D_p=\operatorname{diag}(p_1,\ldots,p_k)\).

Multinomial central limit theorem. \[ \sqrt n(\hat p-p) \rightsquigarrow N(0,\Sigma_p), \qquad \Sigma_p=D_p-pp^\top. \] The covariance has rank \(k-1\) and null vector \(\mathbf1\).

Proof. Represent each observation by a one-hot vector \(Z_i\) with \(P(Z_i=e_j)=p_j\). Then \(\hat p=n^{-1}\sum_iZ_i\), \[ \mathbb EZ_i=p,\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(Z_i)=D_p-pp^\top. \] Apply the multivariate central limit theorem. Since \(\mathbf1^\top(\hat p-p)=0\), the limit lies in the \(k-1\)-dimensional contrast space.

Pearson statistic for a simple null

Definition. For \(H_0:p=p^0\), Pearson's statistic is \[ X^2 =\sum_{j=1}^k\frac{(N_j-np_j^0)^2}{np_j^0} =n(\hat p-p^0)^\top D_{p^0}^{-1}(\hat p-p^0). \]

Pearson theorem. Under \(H_0\), \[ X^2\rightsquigarrow\chi_{k-1}^2. \]

Proof. Let \[ Z_n=\sqrt nD_p^{-1/2}(\hat p-p). \] Its limiting covariance is \[ D_p^{-1/2}\Sigma_pD_p^{-1/2} =I-\sqrt p\,\sqrt p^\top, \] the orthogonal projection onto the complement of \(\sqrt p\), of rank \(k-1\). Therefore \(Z_n\rightsquigarrow\Pi Z\) for \(Z\sim N(0,I_k)\), and \[ X^2=\|Z_n\|^2\rightsquigarrow\|\Pi Z\|^2 \sim\chi_{k-1}^2. \]

Estimated cell probabilities

Composite Pearson theorem. Let \(p(\theta)\) be a smooth \(d\)-dimensional multinomial model with full-rank tangent matrix \(\dot p(\theta_0)\), and let \(\hat\theta\) be asymptotically efficient under the multinomial model. Then \[ X^2(\hat\theta) =n\{\hat p-p(\hat\theta)\}^\top D_{p(\hat\theta)}^{-1} \{\hat p-p(\hat\theta)\} \rightsquigarrow\chi_{k-1-d}^2. \]

Proof. Standardize the multinomial fluctuation by \(D_p^{-1/2}\). The total-probability constraint already removes the direction \(\sqrt p\). Fitting \(\theta\) projects the standardized fluctuation onto the \(d\)-dimensional model tangent space \[ \mathcal T =\operatorname{span}\{D_p^{-1/2}\dot p(\theta_0)\}, \] which lies in \(\sqrt p^\perp\) because \(\mathbf1^\top\dot p=0\). The residual projection has rank \(k-1-d\). Pearson's statistic is its squared norm plus \(o_p(1)\).

Likelihood-ratio deviance

Pearson-deviance equivalence. The multinomial likelihood-ratio statistic \[ G^2=2\sum_{j:N_j>0} N_j\log\frac{N_j}{np_j^0} \] satisfies \[ G^2=X^2+o_p(1) \] under a fixed null with all \(p_j^0>0\).

Proof. Put \(u_j=(N_j-np_j^0)/(np_j^0)=O_p(n^{-1/2})\). Since \[ 2(1+u)\log(1+u) =2u+u^2+O(u^3), \] summing \(np_j^0\) times this expansion makes the linear term \(2\sum_j(N_j-np_j^0)=0\). The quadratic term is \(X^2\), and the finite sum of cubic remainders is \(o_p(1)\).

Small expected counts

The fixed-\(k\) theorem assumes \(np_j\to\infty\) for every cell. Rules such as “all expected counts exceed five” are heuristics, not theorem statements. With sparse tables, growing \(k\), structural zeros, or estimated boundaries, the chi-square approximation can fail. Exact conditional tests, parametric bootstrap, cell combination justified before inspection, or high-dimensional multinomial theory are alternatives.

Empirical distribution-function tests

For a fully specified continuous null \(F_0\), define \[ \alpha_n(x)=\sqrt n\{F_n(x)-F_0(x)\}. \] The probability integral transform reduces the problem to uniforms: \[ \alpha_n(F_0^{-1}(t)) \rightsquigarrow B(t),\qquad 0\le t\le1, \] where \(B\) is a Brownian bridge with covariance \[ \operatorname{Cov}\{B(s),B(t)\} =\min(s,t)-st. \]

Definition. The principal statistics are \[ \begin{aligned} D_n&=\sup_x|F_n(x)-F_0(x)|,\\ W_n^2&=n\int\{F_n(x)-F_0(x)\}^2\,\mathrm{d}F_0(x),\\ A_n^2&=n\int \frac{\{F_n(x)-F_0(x)\}^2} {F_0(x)\{1-F_0(x)\}} \,\mathrm{d}F_0(x). \end{aligned} \] These are the Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Cramer-von Mises, and Anderson-Darling statistics.

Null limits. Under a continuous fully specified \(F_0\), \[ \sqrt nD_n\rightsquigarrow\sup_{0\le t\le1}|B(t)|, \] \[ W_n^2\rightsquigarrow\int_0^1B(t)^2\,\mathrm{d}t, \qquad A_n^2\rightsquigarrow \int_0^1\frac{B(t)^2}{t(1-t)}\,\mathrm{d}t. \]

Proof outline. Donsker's theorem gives weak convergence of the uniform empirical process in \(\ell^\infty[0,1]\). The supremum and unweighted \(L^2\) maps are continuous at continuous paths, giving the first two limits by continuous mapping. The Anderson-Darling weight is singular at the endpoints; weighted empirical-process tightness and the Brownian-bridge endpoint rate justify truncation at \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\) followed by \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

Kolmogorov-Smirnov emphasizes the largest discrepancy, Cramer-von Mises aggregates squared discrepancy, and Anderson-Darling upweights tails. No one statistic is uniformly most powerful against every distributional departure.

Composite continuous nulls

If \(F_0=F_{\hat\theta}\) with parameters estimated from the same data, then \[ \sqrt n\{F_n-F_{\hat\theta}\} \] equals the Brownian bridge minus its projection onto the model's score-induced tangent functions. Its law depends on the family and estimator; using the fully specified Kolmogorov critical values is generally wrong. A parametric bootstrap that refits \(\theta\) in every sample reproduces this projection under regularity.

Consistency against fixed alternatives

Consistency of Kolmogorov-Smirnov. If \(F\ne F_0\), then \[ D_n\to\|F-F_0\|_\infty>0 \quad\text{almost surely}, \] and any test rejecting for \(\sqrt nD_n>c_\alpha\) has power tending to one.

Proof. Glivenko-Cantelli gives \(\|F_n-F\|_\infty\to0\) almost surely. The reverse triangle inequality yields \[ |D_n-\|F-F_0\|_\infty| \le\|F_n-F\|_\infty\to0. \] Multiplication by \(\sqrt n\) then diverges.

The empirical-process results used here, including Brownian bridges, Donsker classes, and bootstrap analogues, are developed in empirical processes.
