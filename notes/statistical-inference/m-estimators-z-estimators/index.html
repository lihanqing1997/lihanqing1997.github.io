M-estimation describes estimators obtained from empirical optimization; Z-estimation describes roots of empirical equations. Smooth interior M-estimators are Z-estimators through their first-order conditions, but the broader formulations also cover nonsmooth losses, constrained optima, quantiles, robust estimators, generalized estimating equations, and semiparametric procedures.

Definitions and population targets

Definition. Given criteria \(m_\theta:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R\), an M-estimator satisfies \[ P_nm_{\hat\theta_n} \ge\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}P_nm_\theta-o_p(1). \] Given estimating functions \(\psi_\theta:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R^d\), a Z-estimator satisfies \[ \|P_n\psi_{\hat\theta_n}\|=o_p(n^{-1/2}) \] for distribution theory, or merely \(o_p(1)\) for consistency. The population targets maximize \(Pm_\theta\) or solve \(P\psi_\theta=0\).

The distinction between an empirical definition and a population target is essential under misspecification. An estimator can consistently estimate a well-defined pseudo-true root even when no member of a working parametric family generated the data.

Consistency of M-estimators

Consistency theorem. Let \((\Theta,d)\) be a metric space. Suppose \[ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|P_nm_\theta-Pm_\theta|\to_p0, \] and, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), \[ Pm_{\theta_0}> \sup_{d(\theta,\theta_0)\ge\varepsilon}Pm_\theta. \] Every approximate M-estimator with optimization error \(o_p(1)\) then satisfies \(\hat\theta_n\to_p\theta_0\).

Proof. This is the argmax theorem applied to \(M_n(\theta)=P_nm_\theta\) and \(M(\theta)=Pm_\theta\). For a fixed \(\varepsilon\), the population separation gives a positive gap. Uniform convergence and the vanishing optimization error preserve that gap with probability tending to one, excluding all approximate maximizers outside the \(\varepsilon\)-ball.

A compact-parametric uniform law. Suppose \(\Theta\) is compact, \(\theta\mapsto m_\theta(x)\) is continuous for almost every \(x\), and \[ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|m_\theta(x)|\le M(x), \qquad PM\lt\infty. \] Then \[ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|P_nm_\theta-Pm_\theta|\to0 \quad\text{almost surely}. \]

Proof outline. For each \(\delta>0\), compactness supplies a finite parameter net. Pointwise continuity and dominated convergence make the \(L^1(P)\) oscillation over sufficiently small net cells arbitrarily small. Apply the strong law to the finitely many net representatives and to an integrable envelope for the local oscillations, then let the mesh tend to zero.

Z-estimator linearization

Asymptotic linearity of a Z-estimator. Suppose \(\hat\theta_n\to_p\theta_0\), \(P\psi_{\theta_0}=0\), and \[ P\psi_\theta =A(\theta-\theta_0)+o(\|\theta-\theta_0\|) \] with nonsingular \(A\). Assume stochastic equicontinuity in the form \[ \sqrt n(P_n-P)(\psi_{\hat\theta_n}-\psi_{\theta_0}) =o_p\{1+\sqrt n\|\hat\theta_n-\theta_0\|\}, \] and \(\sqrt nP_n\psi_{\hat\theta_n}=o_p(1)\). Then \[ \sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta_0) =-A^{-1}\frac1{\sqrt n} \sum_{i=1}^n\psi_{\theta_0}(X_i)+o_p(1). \]

Proof. Decompose \[ P_n\psi_{\hat\theta_n} =P_n\psi_{\theta_0} +P(\psi_{\hat\theta_n}-\psi_{\theta_0}) +(P_n-P)(\psi_{\hat\theta_n}-\psi_{\theta_0}). \] The population expansion and stochastic equicontinuity yield \[ o_p(n^{-1/2}) =P_n\psi_{\theta_0} +A(\hat\theta_n-\theta_0) +o_p(\|\hat\theta_n-\theta_0\|) +o_p\{n^{-1/2}+\|\hat\theta_n-\theta_0\|\}. \] Nonsingularity of \(A\) first implies \(\hat\theta_n-\theta_0=O_p(n^{-1/2})\); substituting that rate back and solving gives the stated expansion.

Corollary (sandwich limit). If \[ B=P(\psi_{\theta_0}\psi_{\theta_0}^\top)\lt\infty, \] then \[ \sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta_0) \rightsquigarrow N(0,A^{-1}BA^{-\top}). \]

The matrices have distinct meanings: \(A\) is sensitivity of the population equation to the parameter, while \(B\) is variability of its empirical fluctuation. Equality \(B=-A\) is the likelihood information identity and generally fails for arbitrary equations or misspecified likelihoods.

Smooth M-estimator expansion

Quadratic M-estimator theorem. Suppose, uniformly for bounded \(h\), \[ n\{P_nm_{\theta_0+h/\sqrt n}-P_nm_{\theta_0}\} =h^\top Z_n-\frac12h^\top Vh+o_p(1), \] where \(V\) is positive definite and \(Z_n\rightsquigarrow Z\). If \(\sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta_0)=O_p(1)\) and \(\hat\theta_n\) approximately maximizes the criterion to \(o_p(n^{-1})\), then \[ \sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta_0) =V^{-1}Z_n+o_p(1) \rightsquigarrow V^{-1}Z. \]

Proof. Complete the square: \[ h^\top Z_n-\frac12h^\top Vh =\frac12Z_n^\top V^{-1}Z_n -\frac12(h-V^{-1}Z_n)^\top V(h-V^{-1}Z_n). \] Positive definiteness makes the maximizer unique at \(V^{-1}Z_n\). Uniform control of the remainder on every compact set and tightness of the empirical maximizer transfer this separation to the random criterion.

Sample quantiles as nonsmooth M- and Z-estimators

Example. For \(p\in(0,1)\), define the check loss \[ \rho_p(u)=u\{p-1_{\{u\lt0\}}\}. \] Any empirical \(p\)-quantile minimizes \[ \theta\mapsto P_n\rho_p(X-\theta) \] and solves, in a generalized sense, \[ P_n\{1_{\{X\le\theta\}}-p\}=0. \] If \(F(q_p)=p\) and \(f(q_p)>0\), then \[ \sqrt n(\hat q_p-q_p) =\frac1{f(q_p)} \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n \{p-1_{\{X_i\le q_p\}}\}+o_p(1), \] so its asymptotic variance is \(p(1-p)/f(q_p)^2\).

The loss is not differentiable at zero, so a literal Hessian argument is invalid. The expansion follows instead from local linearization of \(F(\theta)-p\) and stochastic equicontinuity of the empirical distribution function.

One-step estimators

One-step correction. Suppose a preliminary estimator \(\tilde\theta_n\) is \(n^{1/4}\)-consistent, \(\|\tilde\theta_n-\theta_0\|=o_p(n^{-1/4})\), and the estimating equation is twice smoothly expandable with derivative \(A\). If \(\hat A_n\to_pA\), define \[ \theta_n^{(1)} =\tilde\theta_n-\hat A_n^{-1}P_n\psi_{\tilde\theta_n}. \] Then, under the Z-estimator regularity conditions, \[ \sqrt n(\theta_n^{(1)}-\theta_0) =-A^{-1}\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n \psi_{\theta_0}(X_i)+o_p(1). \]

Proof. A second-order expansion gives \[ P_n\psi_{\tilde\theta_n} =P_n\psi_{\theta_0} +A(\tilde\theta_n-\theta_0) +O_p(\|\tilde\theta_n-\theta_0\|^2) +o_p(n^{-1/2}). \] The squared preliminary error is \(o_p(n^{-1/2})\). Substitute into the one-step definition: the term \(\tilde\theta_n-\theta_0\) cancels, and replacing \(A\) by \(\hat A_n\) costs \(o_p(n^{-1/2})\).

Estimated nuisance functions

If \(\psi_{\theta,\eta}\) depends on a nuisance function \(\eta\), then \[ P_n\psi_{\hat\theta,\hat\eta}=0 \] contains both an empirical-process term and a nuisance-estimation bias. Neyman orthogonality, \[ \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t} P\psi_{\theta_0,\eta_0+t h}\right|_{t=0}=0, \] removes the first-order nuisance effect. Cross-fitting can then decouple empirical evaluation from nuisance fitting. This is the estimating-equation foundation of modern debiased and double-machine-learning procedures.
