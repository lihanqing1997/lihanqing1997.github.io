Robust statistics studies stability under departures from an ideal law. Three notions should be separated: local sensitivity to an infinitesimal contaminating mass, resistance to a finite fraction of arbitrary replacements, and uniform performance over an explicit neighborhood of distributions.

Contamination neighborhoods

Definition (gross-error neighborhood). Around an ideal distribution \(P\), the \(\varepsilon\)-contamination neighborhood is \[ \mathcal U_\varepsilon(P) =\{(1-\varepsilon)P+\varepsilon Q: Q\text{ any probability law}\}. \] A procedure is locally robust if its target and sampling law change slowly as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), uniformly over contaminating \(Q\).

Heavy-tail robustness and contamination robustness are related but not identical. A procedure can tolerate a specified heavy-tailed family yet have unbounded response to a single arbitrarily placed point.

Influence functions

Definition. For a functional \(T\), the influence function at \(P\) is \[ \operatorname{IF}(x;T,P) =\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon} T\{(1-\varepsilon)P+\varepsilon\delta_x\} \right|_{\varepsilon=0+}, \] when the directional derivative exists.

Centering. If \(T\) is differentiable along signed perturbations of total mass zero and its derivative is represented by \(\operatorname{IF}(\cdot;T,P)\), then \[ P\operatorname{IF}(\cdot;T,P)=0. \]

Proof. Integrating the point-mass direction \(\delta_x-P\) over \(x\sim P\) gives the zero signed measure. Linearity of the derivative therefore gives \[ 0=\int \dot T_P(\delta_x-P)\,P(\mathrm{d}x) =P\operatorname{IF}(\cdot;T,P). \]

Asymptotic law of a smooth plug-in functional. If \[ T(P_n)-T(P) =(P_n-P)\operatorname{IF}(\cdot;T,P) +o_p(n^{-1/2}) \] and \(P\operatorname{IF}^2\lt\infty\), then \[ \sqrt n\{T(P_n)-T(P)\} \rightsquigarrow N\{0,P\operatorname{IF}^2\}. \]

Proof. Centering makes the leading term \[ \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n \operatorname{IF}(X_i;T,P). \] Apply the central limit theorem and Slutsky's theorem.

Mean and median

Example (mean). For \(T(P)=Px\), \[ T\{(1-\varepsilon)P+\varepsilon\delta_x\} =(1-\varepsilon)Px+\varepsilon x, \] so \[ \operatorname{IF}(x;T,P)=x-\mu. \] It is unbounded, and one distant observation can move the empirical mean arbitrarily far.

Influence function of a quantile. Let \(q_p=F^{-1}(p)\), and suppose \(F\) has a continuous positive density \(f(q_p)\). Then \[ \operatorname{IF}(x;q_p,F) =\frac{p-1_{\{x\le q_p\}}}{f(q_p)}. \] In particular, the median has bounded influence \[ \operatorname{IF}(x;q_{1/2},F) =\frac{\operatorname{sign}(x-q_{1/2})}{2f(q_{1/2})} \] away from the immaterial equality point.

Proof. Let \(F_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)F+\varepsilon1_{\{x\le\cdot\}}\) and \(q_\varepsilon=q_p+\varepsilon a+o(\varepsilon)\). The equation \(F_\varepsilon(q_\varepsilon)=p\) gives \[ p+\varepsilon\{a f(q_p)-p+1_{\{x\le q_p\}}\} +o(\varepsilon)=p. \] Solving for \(a\) gives the formula.

M-functionals

Definition. A location M-functional \(T(P)\) solves \[ P\psi(X-T(P))=0, \] where \(\psi=\rho'\) is typically odd and nondecreasing. The empirical estimator minimizes \[ \sum_{i=1}^n\rho(X_i-\theta) \] or solves \(\sum_i\psi(X_i-\theta)=0\).

Influence function of a location M-functional. Suppose \(P\psi(X-\theta_0)=0\) and \[ A=P\psi'(X-\theta_0)>0. \] Then \[ \operatorname{IF}(x;T,P) =\frac{\psi(x-\theta_0)}{A}. \]

Proof. Put \(P_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)P+\varepsilon\delta_x\) and \(T(P_\varepsilon)=\theta_0+\varepsilon a+o(\varepsilon)\). Expanding \[ 0=P_\varepsilon\psi\{X-T(P_\varepsilon)\} \] to first order gives \[ 0=-aP\psi'(X-\theta_0)+\psi(x-\theta_0), \] because \(P\psi(X-\theta_0)=0\). Hence \(a=\psi(x-\theta_0)/A\).

Example (Huber score). For \(c>0\), \[ \psi_c(u)= \begin{cases} -c,&u\lt-c,\\ u,&|u|\le c,\\ c,&u>c. \end{cases} \] Its bounded influence limits the effect of extreme residuals. As \(c\to\infty\), it approaches the mean score \(u\); as \(c\downarrow0\) after scaling, it approaches the median sign score.

Asymptotic variance. Under the preceding conditions, \[ \sqrt n(\hat\theta-\theta_0) \rightsquigarrow N\!\left( 0,\frac{P\psi(X-\theta_0)^2} {\{P\psi'(X-\theta_0)\}^2} \right). \]

Gross-error sensitivity and rejection

Definition. The gross-error sensitivity is \[ \gamma^\star(T,P) =\sup_x|\operatorname{IF}(x;T,P)|. \] It is finite exactly when the influence function is bounded. A redescending score additionally satisfies \(\psi(u)\to0\) as \(|u|\to\infty\), so extremely remote observations receive vanishing first-order weight.

Redescending objectives are nonconvex and can have multiple local minima. Bounded influence therefore addresses statistical sensitivity but introduces an optimization problem that must be handled explicitly.

Finite-sample breakdown

Definition. For data \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\), the replacement breakdown fraction of an estimator \(T_n\) is \[ \varepsilon_n^\star(T_n;x) =\frac1n\min\left\{ m:\sup_{x':\,d_H(x,x')\le m} |T_n(x')|=\infty \right\}, \] where \(d_H\) counts replaced coordinates.

Mean and median breakdown. The sample mean has replacement breakdown \(1/n\). The sample median has breakdown \[ \frac{\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor}{n}, \] approaching \(1/2\).

Proof. Replacing one observation by an arbitrarily large value sends the mean to infinity, proving \(1/n\). Fewer than half of the observations cannot move the central order statistic beyond the range of the unreplaced majority. Once at least half are replaced, they can be placed arbitrarily far on one side and force the median there.

Breakdown and influence answer different scales of perturbation. The median has both bounded influence and high breakdown; some high-breakdown estimators can nevertheless have rough local behavior, while a bounded-influence estimator need not attain maximal breakdown in regression.

Robust standard errors

For a regression M-estimator solving \[ \sum_{i=1}^nX_i\psi\!\left( \frac{Y_i-X_i^\top\beta}{s}\right)=0, \] the asymptotic covariance is a sandwich built from the derivative of the estimating equation and the variance of \(X_i\psi(\varepsilon_i/s)\). A robust point estimate paired with model-based Gaussian standard errors is only half a robust procedure. Scale estimation, leverage, clustering, and dependence must also be reflected in the covariance.
