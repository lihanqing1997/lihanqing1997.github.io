A working model can be useful without being literally true. Misspecification theory identifies the population functional estimated by a likelihood or estimating equation and derives uncertainty for that functional under the actual law. A robust covariance estimator repairs variance calibration; it does not make a scientifically inappropriate target meaningful.

Kullback-Leibler projection

Definition. Let \(P_0\) be the true law and \(\{P_\theta:\theta\in\Theta\}\) a dominated working model. A pseudo-true parameter is \[ \theta^\star\in\arg\max_{\theta\in\Theta} P_0\ell_\theta =\arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta} \operatorname{KL}(P_0\|P_\theta), \] whenever these quantities are finite.

Population score equation. If \(\theta^\star\) is an interior differentiability point and differentiation can pass through \(P_0\), then \[ P_0s_{\theta^\star}=0. \] This centering is with respect to \(P_0\), although \(s_{\theta^\star}\) was constructed from the working model.

Proof. The differentiable map \(\theta\mapsto P_0\ell_\theta\) has an interior maximum at \(\theta^\star\), so its gradient vanishes: \[ 0=\nabla_\theta P_0\ell_\theta|_{\theta^\star} =P_0s_{\theta^\star}. \]

Misspecified likelihood limit

Quasi-MLE theorem. Suppose \(\hat\theta_n\to_p\theta^\star\), the empirical score admits a uniform first-order expansion, and \[ A=-P_0\nabla_\theta s_{\theta^\star} \quad\text{is nonsingular},\qquad B=P_0(s_{\theta^\star}s_{\theta^\star}^\top)\lt\infty. \] Then \[ \sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta^\star) =A^{-1}\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n s_{\theta^\star}(X_i)+o_p(1) \rightsquigarrow N(0,A^{-1}BA^{-\top}). \]

Proof. Taylor-expand the empirical score: \[ 0=P_ns_{\hat\theta_n} =P_ns_{\theta^\star} +P_0\nabla s_{\theta^\star} (\hat\theta_n-\theta^\star) +o_p(n^{-1/2}). \] The population score is zero, so \[ \sqrt nP_ns_{\theta^\star} =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^ns_{\theta^\star}(X_i) \rightsquigarrow N(0,B). \] Solving the expansion and applying Slutsky gives the result.

Under correct specification, the information identity gives \(A=B=I(\theta_0)\), and the covariance reduces to \(I^{-1}\). Under misspecification, curvature \(A\) and score variability \(B\) answer different questions and must remain separate.

Estimating the sandwich

Plug-in sandwich covariance. Define \[ \hat A=-P_n\nabla_\theta s_{\hat\theta_n}, \qquad \hat B=P_n(s_{\hat\theta_n}s_{\hat\theta_n}^\top). \] If \(\hat A\to_pA\) and \(\hat B\to_pB\), then \[ \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat\theta_n) =\frac1n\hat A^{-1}\hat B\hat A^{-\top} \] is consistent for the asymptotic covariance divided by \(n\).

Proof. Matrix inversion and multiplication are continuous at nonsingular \(A\). The continuous mapping theorem gives \[ \hat A^{-1}\hat B\hat A^{-\top} \to_pA^{-1}BA^{-\top}. \]

Heteroskedastic linear regression

Let \(Y_i=X_i^\top\beta^\star+\varepsilon_i\), where \(X_i\in\mathbb R^p\) and \[ \mathbb E(X_i\varepsilon_i)=0, \] but \(\mathbb E(\varepsilon_i^2\mid X_i)\) need not be constant. The OLS normal equation is \[ P_n\{X(Y-X^\top\hat\beta)\}=0. \] Thus \[ A=\mathbb E(XX^\top),\qquad B=\mathbb E(XX^\top\varepsilon^2), \] and \[ \sqrt n(\hat\beta-\beta^\star) \rightsquigarrow N(0,A^{-1}BA^{-1}). \]

Derivation. The exact identity \[ \hat\beta-\beta^\star =\left(\frac1n\sum_iX_iX_i^\top\right)^{-1} \frac1n\sum_iX_i\varepsilon_i \] reduces the result to a law of large numbers for the Gram matrix and a multivariate central limit theorem for \(X_i\varepsilon_i\).

Writing \(X\) for the design matrix and \(\hat e_i=Y_i-X_i^\top\hat\beta\), the HC0 covariance estimator is \[ (X^\top X)^{-1} \left(\sum_{i=1}^n\hat e_i^2X_iX_i^\top\right) (X^\top X)^{-1}. \] HC1 multiplies the middle term by \(n/(n-p)\). HC2 and HC3 inflate residuals using leverage \(h_{ii}\), replacing \(\hat e_i^2\) by \(\hat e_i^2/(1-h_{ii})\) or \(\hat e_i^2/(1-h_{ii})^2\), respectively. These corrections target finite-sample leverage bias; they share the same fixed-\(p\) limit under standard conditions.

Clustered observations

Suppose clusters \(c=1,\ldots,G\) are independent but observations within a cluster may be arbitrarily dependent. If the estimating contribution is \(\psi_{ci}(\theta)\), define the cluster score \[ \Psi_c(\theta)=\sum_{i\in c}\psi_{ci}(\theta). \] The variance meat is estimated by \[ \hat B_{\mathrm{cl}} =\frac1G\sum_{c=1}^G \Psi_c(\hat\theta)\Psi_c(\hat\theta)^\top, \] with normalization matched to the chosen \(A\). The asymptotic sample size is the number of independent clusters \(G\), not the number of rows. Few-cluster inference needs degrees-of-freedom or randomization methods beyond the large-\(G\) sandwich.

Serial dependence and long-run variance

For a stationary dependent score sequence \(\psi_t\), the central limit variance is the long-run covariance \[ B_{\mathrm{LR}} =\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} \operatorname{Cov}(\psi_0,\psi_k). \] Heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-consistent estimators replace this series by a kernel-weighted sample sum \[ \hat B_{\mathrm{HAC}} =\hat\Gamma_0+ \sum_{k=1}^{L}w(k/L) (\hat\Gamma_k+\hat\Gamma_k^\top). \] Bandwidth \(L\) must increase slowly enough to control variance while capturing dependence. An i.i.d. sandwich is not robust to serial correlation.

Targets and robust tests

Under a misspecified conditional mean model, OLS targets the best linear projection \[ \beta^\star =\arg\min_\beta\mathbb E(Y-X^\top\beta)^2, \] not necessarily a structural slope or a conditional derivative. Sandwich standard errors give valid inference for this projection under the moment assumptions; they do not validate causal interpretation.

A sandwich-Wald statistic for \(H_0:r(\theta^\star)=0\) is \[ n\,r(\hat\theta)^\top \{R(\hat\theta)\hat V R(\hat\theta)^\top\}^{-1} r(\hat\theta), \qquad \hat V=\hat A^{-1}\hat B\hat A^{-\top}, \] and converges to \(\chi_q^2\) under regularity. A naive model-based Wald test generally has the wrong null scale when \(A\ne B\).
