A point estimator replaces an unknown functional of the data-generating law by a measurable function of the sample. No estimator is intrinsically “best”: its quality is indexed by a model, a target, a loss, and either a finite-sample or asymptotic comparison.

Loss, risk, bias, and dispersion

Definition. Let \(\psi:\Theta\to\mathcal A\) be the target and \(\delta:\mathcal X\to\mathcal A\) an estimator. For a loss \(L:\Theta\times\mathcal A\to[0,\infty]\), its risk is \[ R(\theta,\delta)=\mathbb E_\theta L(\theta,\delta(X)). \] For a scalar target and squared-error loss, \[ \operatorname{Bias}_\theta(\delta) =\mathbb E_\theta\delta-\psi(\theta),\qquad \operatorname{MSE}_\theta(\delta) =\mathbb E_\theta\{\delta-\psi(\theta)\}^2. \]

Bias-variance decomposition. If \(\delta\in L^2(P_\theta)\), then \[ \operatorname{MSE}_\theta(\delta) =\operatorname{Var}_\theta(\delta) +\operatorname{Bias}_\theta(\delta)^2. \]

Proof. Write \[ \delta-\psi=(\delta-\mathbb E_\theta\delta) +(\mathbb E_\theta\delta-\psi). \] The cross term has expectation zero because \(\mathbb E_\theta(\delta-\mathbb E_\theta\delta)=0\). Taking expectations after squaring gives the identity.

Unbiasedness controls one summand but may increase the other. Shrinkage estimators deliberately introduce bias when the variance reduction is larger. Exact admissibility, Bayes risk, and minimaxity are developed in statistical decision theory.

Example (a bias-variance tradeoff). If \(X\sim N(\theta,\sigma^2)\) and \(\delta_a=aX\), then \[ R(\theta,\delta_a) =a^2\sigma^2+(1-a)^2\theta^2. \] For a fixed bounded parameter set \(|\theta|\le M\), choosing \(a=M^2/(M^2+\sigma^2)\) minimizes the worst-case upper envelope \(a^2\sigma^2+(1-a)^2M^2\), and improves on \(X\) near zero.

Consistency and rates

Definition. An estimator \(T_n\) is weakly consistent for \(\psi(\theta)\) if \[ T_n\longrightarrow\psi(\theta)\quad\text{in }P_\theta\text{-probability} \] for every fixed \(\theta\); it is strongly consistent if convergence is almost sure. It has rate \(r_n\to\infty\) if \[ r_n\{T_n-\psi(\theta)\}=O_{P_\theta}(1). \] A uniform rate over \(\Theta_0\) requires \[ \lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty} \sup_{\theta\in\Theta_0} P_\theta\!\left(r_n\|T_n-\psi(\theta)\|>M\right)=0. \]

Pointwise and uniform statements answer different questions. Pointwise consistency permits the sample size needed for accuracy to depend arbitrarily on \(\theta\); honest confidence procedures usually require uniform control over a stated class.

Mean-square consistency criterion. If \[ \operatorname{Bias}_\theta(T_n)\to0 \quad\text{and}\quad \operatorname{Var}_\theta(T_n)\to0, \] then \(T_n\to\psi(\theta)\) in probability.

Proof. The bias-variance decomposition gives \(\mathbb E_\theta(T_n-\psi)^2\to0\). Markov's inequality then yields \[ P_\theta(|T_n-\psi|>\varepsilon) \le\varepsilon^{-2}\mathbb E_\theta(T_n-\psi)^2\to0. \]

Method of moments

Suppose \(m(\theta)=\mathbb E_\theta h(X)\in\mathbb R^d\) is one-to-one. The method-of-moments estimator solves \[ m(\hat\theta_n)=P_nh,\qquad P_nh=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nh(X_i). \] When \(m^{-1}\) is continuous at \(m(\theta_0)\), the law of large numbers and continuous mapping theorem give consistency.

Asymptotic distribution of a just-identified moment estimator. Suppose \(D m(\theta_0)\) is nonsingular and \(\operatorname{Var}_{\theta_0}h(X)=\Sigma\lt\infty\). Then \[ \sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta_0) \rightsquigarrow N\!\left(0,\, Dm(\theta_0)^{-1}\Sigma Dm(\theta_0)^{-\top}\right). \]

Proof. The multivariate central limit theorem gives \[ \sqrt n\{P_nh-m(\theta_0)\}\rightsquigarrow N(0,\Sigma). \] Since \(m(\hat\theta_n)=P_nh\), differentiability of \(m^{-1}\) and the inverse-function theorem give \[ \sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta_0) =Dm(\theta_0)^{-1}\sqrt n\{P_nh-m(\theta_0)\}+o_p(1). \] Slutsky's theorem completes the proof.

Example (Gamma moments). For \(X\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(\alpha,\beta)\) in shape-rate parametrization, \[ \mathbb EX=\frac{\alpha}{\beta},\qquad \operatorname{Var}(X)=\frac{\alpha}{\beta^2}. \] Equating these to \(\bar X\) and the empirical second central moment gives \[ \hat\alpha=\frac{\bar X^2}{S_n^2},\qquad \hat\beta=\frac{\bar X}{S_n^2}. \] Their joint asymptotic law follows by applying the delta method to the empirical first two raw moments.

Plug-in estimation and statistical functionals

Definition. Let \(T:\mathcal P\to\mathbb R^k\) be a functional of a probability law. The empirical plug-in estimator is \(T(P_n)\), where \[ P_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{X_i}. \] Examples include \(P_nf\) for a mean, \(F_n^{-1}(p)\) for a quantile, and smooth functions of empirical moments.

Continuity of \(T\) under a topology in which \(P_n\to P\) gives consistency. Differentiability of \(T\) gives a first-order expansion. If \[ T(P_n)-T(P)=P_n\phi_P+o_p(n^{-1/2}), \qquad P\phi_P=0, \] then \(\phi_P\) is an influence function and the central limit theorem supplies uncertainty quantification.

Example (variance functional). For \[ T(P)=P x^2-(Px)^2, \] direct algebra gives \[ T(P_n)-T(P) =(P_n-P)\{(x-\mu)^2\} -\{(P_n-P)x\}^2, \] where \(\mu=Px\). Thus \[ \phi_P(x)=(x-\mu)^2-\operatorname{Var}_P(X) \] and the squared empirical-mean remainder is \(O_p(n^{-1})\) when \(PX^2\lt\infty\).

Equivariance

Definition. Suppose a group \(G\) acts on the sample space and parameter space so that \(P_{g\theta}\) is the image of \(P_\theta\) under \(x\mapsto gx\). An estimator \(\delta\) is equivariant if \[ \delta(gx)=g\delta(x). \]

For a location family, translation equivariance means \(\delta(x_1+a,\ldots,x_n+a)=\delta(x_1,\ldots,x_n)+a\). For a scale family, scale equivariance means \(\delta(cx)=c\delta(x)\). Equivariance reduces arbitrary coordinate dependence, but it does not by itself imply low risk or robustness.

Asymptotic comparison

Definition (asymptotic relative efficiency). If \[ \sqrt n(T_n-\theta)\rightsquigarrow N(0,V_T), \qquad \sqrt n(S_n-\theta)\rightsquigarrow N(0,V_S) \] for scalar estimators, the asymptotic relative efficiency of \(T\) to \(S\) is \(V_S/V_T\). In several dimensions no single ordering exists unless one covariance dominates the other in Loewner order or a scalar loss is specified.

Rates and limit variances describe local large-sample behavior. They do not replace finite-sample risk, robustness under contamination, or uniformity near boundaries and singular points.
