Resampling replaces an unknown sampling law by a data-dependent law and recomputes the statistic. The central mathematical question is conditional consistency: does the resampled distribution approximate the original distribution in probability? Repeating a procedure many times does not by itself answer that question.

Nonparametric bootstrap law

Definition. Given \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\), let \[ P_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{X_i}. \] Conditionally on the data, draw \(X_1^\star,\ldots,X_n^\star\) i.i.d. from \(P_n\), and define \[ P_n^\star=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{X_i^\star}. \] Conditional probability and expectation are denoted \(P^\star(\cdot)=P(\cdot\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)\) and \(\mathbb E^\star\).

Definition (bootstrap consistency). For a statistic \(T_n\) centered at \(\theta(P)\) with scale \(r_n\), the bootstrap is consistent if, for a metric \(d\) that metrizes weak convergence, \[ d\!\left( \mathcal L^\star\{r_n(T_n^\star-T_n)\}, \mathcal L_P\{r_n(T_n-\theta(P))\} \right)\to_p0. \]

Bootstrap central limit theorem for the mean

Theorem. If \(PX^2\lt\infty\) and \(\sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}_P(X)>0\), then \[ \sup_t\left| P^\star\!\left\{ \frac{\sqrt n(\bar X^\star-\bar X)}{S_n}\le t \right\}-\Phi(t)\right|\to_p0, \] where \(S_n^2=P_n(X-\bar X)^2\).

Proof. Conditionally on the data, \(X_i^\star-\bar X\) are i.i.d. with mean zero and variance \(S_n^2\). The conditional Lindeberg condition is \[ \frac1{nS_n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n (X_i-\bar X)^2 1_{\{|X_i-\bar X|>\varepsilon\sqrt nS_n\}} \to_p0. \] Since \(S_n^2\to_p\sigma^2\), truncation plus \(PX^2\lt\infty\) proves this condition. The conditional Lindeberg-Feller theorem gives asymptotic normality in probability. Continuity of \(\Phi\) upgrades pointwise convergence to the displayed uniform convergence.

Functional bootstrap delta method

Bootstrap delta theorem. Suppose \[ \sqrt n(P_n-P)\rightsquigarrow\mathbb G_P \] in a normed function space and, conditionally in probability, \[ \sqrt n(P_n^\star-P_n)\rightsquigarrow_P\mathbb G_P. \] If \(T\) is Hadamard differentiable at \(P\), tangentially to the support of \(\mathbb G_P\), then \[ \sqrt n\{T(P_n^\star)-T(P_n)\} \rightsquigarrow_P T_P'\mathbb G_P, \] the same limit as \(\sqrt n\{T(P_n)-T(P)\}\).

Proof outline. The ordinary functional delta method expands \[ \sqrt n\{T(P+n^{-1/2}h_n)-T(P)\} =T_P'h_n+o(1) \] uniformly along convergent tangential sequences. Apply this expansion conditionally to \(P_n^\star-P_n\), while using consistency \(P_n\to P\) to replace the derivative at \(P_n\) by that at \(P\). The conditional continuous-mapping theorem then transfers the bootstrap empirical-process limit through \(T_P'\).

Bootstrap confidence intervals

Let \(\hat\theta=T_n\), and let \(q_\gamma^\star\) denote the conditional \(\gamma\)-quantile of \(\hat\theta^\star-\hat\theta\). The basic interval is \[ \left[ \hat\theta-q_{1-\alpha/2}^\star,\, \hat\theta-q_{\alpha/2}^\star \right]. \] If \(a_\gamma^\star\) is the conditional quantile of \(\hat\theta^\star\) itself, the percentile interval is \[ [a_{\alpha/2}^\star,a_{1-\alpha/2}^\star]. \] They coincide only under special symmetry or transformation structures.

For a studentized statistic \[ Z_n=\frac{\hat\theta-\theta}{\hat s}, \qquad Z_n^\star=\frac{\hat\theta^\star-\hat\theta}{\hat s^\star}, \] the bootstrap-\(t\) interval is \[ \left[ \hat\theta-q_{1-\alpha/2}^{Z,\star}\hat s,\, \hat\theta-q_{\alpha/2}^{Z,\star}\hat s \right]. \] Studentization can make the limiting law pivotal and often improves coverage order. Bias-corrected and accelerated intervals alter percentile levels using a median-bias constant and a jackknife acceleration estimate; they require smoothness assumptions beyond first-order bootstrap consistency.

Parametric and model-based bootstraps

In a parametric bootstrap, draw \(X_i^\star\sim P_{\hat\theta}\) and refit the procedure. This can be more accurate under a correct parametric model, but inherits its misspecification. In regression, a residual bootstrap presumes an appropriate residual invariance structure; a wild bootstrap multiplies residuals by mean-zero unit-variance weights and is designed to preserve heteroskedastic conditional variance. Cluster and block bootstraps resample independent clusters or contiguous blocks rather than individual rows.

Jackknife pseudovalues

Definition. Let \(\hat\theta=T_n(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\) and \(\hat\theta_{(-i)}\) be the estimate after deleting observation \(i\). Put \[ \bar\theta_{(-\cdot)}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\hat\theta_{(-i)}, \qquad J_i=n\hat\theta-(n-1)\hat\theta_{(-i)}. \] The \(J_i\) are jackknife pseudovalues.

Jackknife bias and variance estimates. Define \[ \widehat{\operatorname{bias}}_{\mathrm{jack}} =(n-1)(\bar\theta_{(-\cdot)}-\hat\theta) \] and \[ \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}_{\mathrm{jack}}(\hat\theta) =\frac{n-1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (\hat\theta_{(-i)}-\bar\theta_{(-\cdot)})^2 =\frac1{n(n-1)}\sum_{i=1}^n(J_i-\bar J)^2. \]

Jackknife consistency for asymptotically linear estimators. If \[ \hat\theta-\theta =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\phi(X_i)+r_n, \qquad r_n=o_p(n^{-1/2}), \] and the expansion remains uniformly valid after one deletion, then \[ J_i=\theta+\phi(X_i)+o_p(1) \] in average squared error, and \[ n\,\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}_{\mathrm{jack}}(\hat\theta) \to_pP\phi^2. \]

Proof. The leave-one-out expansion is \[ \hat\theta_{(-i)}-\theta =\frac1{n-1}\sum_{j\ne i}\phi(X_j)+r_{n,-i}. \] Substitution into \(J_i=n\hat\theta-(n-1)\hat\theta_{(-i)}\) cancels every \(\phi(X_j)\) except \(\phi(X_i)\), leaving \(\theta+\phi(X_i)\) plus the uniform remainder. The sample variance of the pseudovalues therefore converges to \(P\phi^2\).

When the ordinary bootstrap fails

Example (sample maximum). If \(X_i\sim U[0,1]\), then \[ n(1-X_{n:n})\rightsquigarrow\operatorname{Exp}(1). \] Conditionally, every bootstrap observation is at most \(X_{n:n}\), and \[ P^\star(X_{n:n}^\star=X_{n:n}) =1-(1-1/n)^n\to1-e^{-1}. \] Thus the bootstrap distribution has an asymptotic atom at zero for \(n(X_{n:n}-X_{n:n}^\star)\), unlike the target exponential law. The ordinary \(n\)-out-of-\(n\) bootstrap is inconsistent.

Other failures occur for some boundary parameters, cube-root estimators, post-model-selection laws, infinite-variance means, and degenerate U-statistics under an alternative centering. Remedies are problem-specific: \(m\)-out-of-\(n\) bootstrap, subsampling, multiplier or wild bootstrap, null-imposed resampling, block bootstrap, or analytic nonregular limits. The resampling scheme must reproduce the mechanism that generates the leading stochastic term.
