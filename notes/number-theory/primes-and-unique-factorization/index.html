Prime numbers are the irreducible building blocks of the integers. Unique factorization rests on one decisive property: a prime dividing a product must divide one factor.

Irreducibles and primes

Definition. An integer \(p>1\) is prime if its only positive divisors are \(1,p\). An integer \(n>1\) is composite otherwise.

Euclid's lemma. If \(p\) is prime and \(p\mid ab\), then \(p\mid a\) or \(p\mid b\).

Proof. If \(p\nmid a\), then \(\gcd(p,a)=1\), so Bezout gives \(px+ay=1\). Multiplying by \(b\) yields \(pbx+aby=b\). Both terms on the left are divisible by \(p\), hence \(p\mid b\).

Perfect powers and divisor counts

If \(n=\prod p_i^{\alpha_i}\), then every divisor has the unique form \(\prod p_i^{\beta_i}\) with \(0\le\beta_i\le\alpha_i\). Hence \[ \tau(n)=\prod_i(\alpha_i+1),\qquad \sigma(n)=\prod_i\frac{p_i^{\alpha_i+1}-1}{p_i-1}. \] An integer is a square exactly when every \(v_p(n)\) is even. These simple consequences illustrate the central principle: multiplicative questions become additive or coordinatewise after prime factorization.

Example. \(360=2^3\cdot3^2\cdot5\), so \[ \tau(360)=4\cdot3\cdot2=24,\qquad \sigma(360)=15\cdot13\cdot6=1170. \]
