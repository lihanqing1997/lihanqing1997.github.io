Derive the lambda-return, its limiting cases, and the precise fixed-value forward–backward identity.

Longer returns and their limits

Fix a bounded value vector \(V\) throughout the calculation, and assume bounded rewards and \(\gamma<1\). Define \[ G_t^{(n)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\gamma^kR_{t+k+1}+\gamma^nV(S_{t+n}),\qquad G_t^\lambda=(1-\lambda)\sum_{n=1}^\infty\lambda^{n-1}G_t^{(n)} \] for \(0\le\lambda<1\). At \(\lambda=0\), interpret the weights as unit mass at \(n=1\).

Proposition (controlled Monte Carlo limit). Let \(C=\|V\|_\infty+R_{\max}/(1-\gamma)\). Then \[ |G_t^{(n)}-G_t|\le C\gamma^n,\qquad |G_t^\lambda-G_t|\le C\frac{\gamma(1-\lambda)}{1-\gamma\lambda}. \] Consequently \(G_t^\lambda\to G_t\) uniformly over paths, and hence in \(L^1\), as \(\lambda\uparrow1\).

Proof. Subtract the infinite return, leaving a bootstrap term and a discounted reward tail: \[ G_t^{(n)}-G_t =\gamma^nV(S_{t+n})-\sum_{k=n}^\infty\gamma^kR_{t+k+1}. \] Their absolute values sum to at most \(C\gamma^n\). The lambda weights are nonnegative and sum to one, so \[ \begin{aligned} |G_t^\lambda-G_t| &\le(1-\lambda)\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda^{n-1}|G_t^{(n)}-G_t|\\ &\le C(1-\lambda)\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda^{n-1}\gamma^n =C\frac{\gamma(1-\lambda)}{1-\gamma\lambda}. \end{aligned} \] The denominator tends to \(1-\gamma>0\). The deterministic upper bound proves both pathwise and \(L^1\) convergence, without an unspecified Abelian-limit argument.

The multistep Bellman operator

Proposition (lambda resolvent). For a fixed stationary policy, \[ T_\lambda^\pi V=(1-\lambda)\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda^{n-1}(T^\pi)^nV =V+(I-\gamma\lambda P_\pi)^{-1}(T^\pi V-V). \] Its fixed point is \(V^\pi\), and its sup-norm Lipschitz constant is at most \(\kappa_\lambda=\gamma(1-\lambda)/(1-\gamma\lambda)<1\).

Proof. Induction on \(n\) gives \[ (T^\pi)^nV-V =\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\gamma^kP_\pi^k(T^\pi V-V). \] The weighted double sum of norms is bounded by \[ (1-\lambda)\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda^{n-1} \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\gamma^k\|T^\pi V-V\|_\infty \le\frac{\|T^\pi V-V\|_\infty}{1-\gamma}. \] Thus rearrangement is justified by absolute convergence. For a fixed \(k\), \((1-\lambda)\sum_{n=k+1}^\infty\lambda^{n-1}=\lambda^k\). Hence \[ T_\lambda^\pi V-V =\sum_{k\ge0}(\gamma\lambda)^kP_\pi^k(T^\pi V-V). \] This is the resolvent formula. Taking differences cancels rewards: \[ T_\lambda^\pi V-T_\lambda^\pi W =(1-\lambda)\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda^{n-1}\gamma^nP_\pi^n(V-W). \] Its norm is at most \(\kappa_\lambda\|V-W\|_\infty\). Substituting \(V^\pi\) in the resolvent yields a fixed point; contraction proves uniqueness.

Forward and backward credit assignment

Proposition (TD-error expansion). For fixed \(V\), put \(\delta_t=R_{t+1}+\gamma V(S_{t+1})-V(S_t)\). Then \[ G_t^\lambda-V(S_t)=\sum_{k=0}^\infty(\gamma\lambda)^k\delta_{t+k}. \]

Proof. For each finite \(n\), expand and cancel the intermediate value terms: \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\gamma^k\delta_{t+k} &=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\gamma^kR_{t+k+1} +\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\gamma^{k+1}V(S_{t+k+1}) -\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\gamma^kV(S_{t+k})\\ &=G_t^{(n)}-V(S_t). \end{aligned} \] Since \(|\delta_t|\le D:=R_{\max}+(1+\gamma)\|V\|_\infty\), the weighted sum of absolute terms is at most \(D/(1-\gamma)\). Weight by \((1-\lambda)\lambda^{n-1}\), rearrange the absolutely convergent double sum, and use the same weight identity: \[ (1-\lambda)\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\gamma^k\delta_{t+k} =\sum_{k\ge0}(\gamma\lambda)^k\delta_{t+k}. \]

Theorem (offline forward–backward equivalence). For a finite episode ending at time \(H\), take terminal value zero and extend rewards and values by zero thereafter. Hold \(V\) fixed during the episode. Let features be \(\phi_t=\phi(S_t)\) and \(z_{-1}=0,\ z_t=\gamma\lambda z_{t-1}+\phi_t\). With a common step \(\alpha\), the total forward and backward updates agree: \[ \alpha\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}[G_t^\lambda-V(S_t)]\phi_t =\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\delta_k z_k. \]

Proof. The preceding identity terminates at \(H-1\) because all later TD errors are zero. Expanding the trace recursively gives \[ z_k=\sum_{t=0}^k(\gamma\lambda)^{k-t}\phi_t. \] All sums are finite, so their order can be exchanged directly: \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{t=0}^{H-1}[G_t^\lambda-V(S_t)]\phi_t &=\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\sum_{k=t}^{H-1} (\gamma\lambda)^{k-t}\delta_k\phi_t\\ &=\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\delta_k \sum_{t=0}^k(\gamma\lambda)^{k-t}\phi_t =\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\delta_kz_k. \end{aligned} \] If \(V\) changes within the episode, the telescoping calculation no longer uses one fixed function; ordinary online accumulating traces are then not exactly this offline forward view. True-online TD uses a corrected trace to obtain a corresponding online identity.

Worked example (two rewards and two credits). Let an episode have rewards \(R_1=0,R_2=1\), terminal state at time 2, \(V=0\), and \(\gamma=\lambda=1/2\). Then \(\delta_0=0,\delta_1=1\), and \[ G_0^\lambda=(\gamma\lambda)\delta_1=\tfrac14,\qquad G_1^\lambda=1. \] For distinct state indicators \(e_0,e_1\), the last trace is \(z_1=\tfrac14e_0+e_1\). The backward update \(\alpha\delta_1z_1\) therefore assigns exactly the same credits as the two forward errors.

Off-policy traces

Importance ratios \(\rho_t=\pi(A_t\mid S_t)/\mu(A_t\mid S_t)\) correct the target policy, but their products can have large variance. Retrace and V-trace truncate some coefficients. Their fixed points depend on which ratios are clipped: clipping trace propagation need not introduce the same bias as clipping the policy correction in the TD residual. Specify the entire operator before claiming either exactness or bias. The unweighted on-policy proof above does not establish an off-policy theorem.

Exercises

  1. Compute \(\kappa_\lambda\) at \(\gamma=0.9\) for \(\lambda=0,1/2,0.9\).

    Hint and solution

    The values are \(0.9\), \(0.45/0.55=9/11\), and \(0.09/0.19=9/19\). Longer expected backups contract more strongly, although sampled returns can have more noise.

  2. In the two-step example, replace \(\lambda\) by 1. What are the forward credits?

    Hint and solution

    Define the endpoint by the Monte Carlo return. The credits are \(G_0=1/2\) and \(G_1=1\); the trace is \(z_1=(1/2)e_0+e_1\). The finite-episode identity remains valid at \(\lambda=1\).
