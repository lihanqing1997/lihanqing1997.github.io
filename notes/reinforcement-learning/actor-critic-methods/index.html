Connect critic error to actor bias, derive compatible features, and distinguish a local policy surrogate from a global improvement guarantee.

Which distribution does the actor need?

For \(J_\rho(\theta)=\rho^\top V^{\pi_\theta}\), the gradient uses discounted occupancy \(d_\rho^{\pi_\theta}\), not an arbitrary replay or stationary distribution. An exact sampling construction is to draw \(T\), independently of a trajectory from \(\rho\), with \(\Pr(T=t)=(1-\gamma)\gamma^t\). Then \[ \Pr(S_T=s,A_T=a)=\sum_{t\ge0}(1-\gamma)\gamma^t \Pr(S_t=s,A_t=a)=d_\rho^{\pi_\theta}(s,a). \] Equivalently one can weight all trajectory terms by \(\gamma^t\). A continuing-chain actor sampled from the stationary law generally optimizes a different expression unless a correction is supplied.

A TD actor uses \(g_t=z_\theta(S_t,A_t)\delta_t^{\widetilde V}/(1-\gamma)\). If the critic is fixed for this fresh sample, with error \(e=\widetilde V-V^\pi\), then \[ \mathbb E[g_t]-\nabla J_\rho =\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\mathbb E_{(S,A)\sim d_\rho^\pi} [z_\theta(S,A)(P e)(S,A)]. \] Indeed, \(\mathbb E[\delta^{\widetilde V}\mid s,a]=A^\pi(s,a)+\gamma Pe(s,a)-e(s)\), and \(\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)z(s,a)e(s)=0\). Hence the bias norm is at most \(\gamma L\|e\|_\infty/(1-\gamma)\) when \(\|z\|\le L\). Training and evaluation on a dependent sample require conditioning on the full history; this fixed-critic identity cannot silently treat a fitted random critic as independent.

Compatible function approximation

Proposition (compatible least squares). Fix \(\theta\), let \(F=\mathbb E_d[zz^\top]\), and choose any minimizer \(w\) of \(\mathbb E_d[(Q^\pi(s,a)-b(s)-z(s,a)^\top w)^2]\). Then \[ Fw=\mathbb E_d[zQ^\pi]=(1-\gamma)\nabla J_\rho. \] Thus the fitted advantage gives the exact population gradient, even if its pointwise approximation is imperfect.

Proof. The loss is the finite quadratic \[ L(w)=\mathbb E_d[(Q^\pi-b)^2]-2w^\top\mathbb E_d[z(Q^\pi-b)]+w^\top Fw. \] Its normal equation is \(Fw=\mathbb E_d[z(Q^\pi-b)]\). The baseline term is zero. If \(u\in\ker F\), then \(u^\top z=0\) \(d\)-almost surely, so \(u^\top\mathbb E_d[z(Q^\pi-b)]=0\). The right-hand side lies in \(\operatorname{range}F\), ensuring that solutions exist even if \(F\) is singular. Therefore \(\mathbb E_d[z(z^\top w)]=Fw=\mathbb E_d[zQ^\pi]\), as claimed.

Two time scales: what must be established

A typical recursion has a critic step \(\beta_n\) and a slower actor step \(\alpha_n\): \[ w_{n+1}=w_n+\beta_n\{h(w_n,\theta_n)+M_{n+1}\},\qquad \theta_{n+1}=\theta_n+\alpha_n\{g(w_n,\theta_n)+N_{n+1}\}. \] The usual step requirements are \(\sum\alpha_n=\sum\beta_n=\infty\), \(\sum(\alpha_n^2+\beta_n^2)<\infty\), and \(\alpha_n/\beta_n\to0\). For example, \(\beta_n=n^{-0.6}\), \(\alpha_n=n^{-0.9}\) satisfy them. These conditions alone do not prove convergence.

External theorem input. Two-time-scale stochastic approximation additionally needs appropriate stability of the iterates, regularity of the mean fields, controlled noise, and an attracting critic equilibrium \(w_*(\theta)\); Markov sampling needs its own mixing/Poisson-equation conditions. Under a theorem that establishes \(\|w_n-w_*(\theta_n)\|\to0\), a locally Lipschitz actor field satisfies, on a compact set, \[ \|g(w_n,\theta_n)-g(w_*(\theta_n),\theta_n)\| \le C\|w_n-w_*(\theta_n)\|\longrightarrow0. \] This is the precise reduction to the limiting actor field. It does not identify that field with \(\nabla J_\rho\) unless the critic and sampling identities above also hold. Nor does convergence to stationary points imply global optimality for arbitrary neural policies. We use the general tracking theorem as a sourced input, rather than omit its stability hypotheses in a purported short proof.

A quantitative policy-surrogate bound

For two stationary policies define \(d=d_{\rho,S}^\pi\), \(d'=d_{\rho,S}^{\pi'}\), \(f(s)=\sum_a\pi'(a\mid s)A^\pi(s,a)\), and \[ \mathcal L_\pi(\pi')=J_\rho(\pi)+\frac{d^\top f}{1-\gamma}, \qquad \epsilon=\max_s\sum_a|\pi'(a\mid s)-\pi(a\mid s)|. \]

Proposition (surrogate error). \[ |J_\rho(\pi')-\mathcal L_\pi(\pi')| \le\frac{\gamma\epsilon\|f\|_\infty}{(1-\gamma)^2}. \]

Proof. Subtract the occupancy flow equations to obtain, as row vectors, \[ (d'-d)^\top=\gamma(d'-d)^\top P_{\pi'} +\gamma d^\top(P_{\pi'}-P_\pi). \] A stochastic matrix does not increase the \(\ell^1\) norm of a signed row vector, and \[ \sum_{s'}|P_{\pi'}(s,s')-P_\pi(s,s')| \le\sum_a|\pi'(a\mid s)-\pi(a\mid s)| \sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\le\epsilon. \] Therefore \((1-\gamma)\|d'-d\|_1\le\gamma\epsilon\). The performance-difference identity gives \[ J_\rho(\pi')-\mathcal L_\pi(\pi')=\frac{(d'-d)^\top f}{1-\gamma}, \] and Hölder's inequality proves the bound. In particular, a surrogate gain exceeding the displayed error certifies improvement. Moreover \(\|f\|_\infty=\|\sum_a(\pi'-\pi)A^\pi\|_\infty \le\epsilon\|A^\pi\|_\infty\), giving a quadratic bound in \(\epsilon\).

TRPO motivates a policy-distance constraint using this type of argument. PPO instead optimizes the empirical clipped expression \[ \mathbb E\!\left[\min\left\{r_\theta\widehat A,\, \operatorname{clip}(r_\theta,1-\epsilon_c,1+\epsilon_c)\widehat A\right\}\right], \qquad r_\theta=\frac{\pi_\theta(A\mid S)}{\pi_{\rm old}(A\mid S)}. \] Clipping this sampled objective does not enforce a uniform statewise policy-distance constraint; the preceding theorem is not automatically a monotonic-improvement theorem for PPO.

Worked example (a critic can be wrong without biasing this actor). In the one-state two-action example, let \(\widetilde V=V^\pi+c\). Since \(Pe=c\) for both actions, \[ \mathbb E[z\,\delta^{\widetilde V}] =\mathbb E[zA^\pi]+(\gamma-1)c\,\mathbb E[z] =\mathbb E[zA^\pi]. \] The actor mean is correct for any constant \(c\), although its variance can change. In multiple states an action-dependent next-state error \(Pe(s,a)\) need not cancel.

Exercises

  1. Why does a replay buffer drawn from a different state law invalidate the exact compatible-gradient conclusion for \(J_\rho\)?

    Hint and solution

    The normal equation then uses the replay-weighted matrix and right-hand side, whereas the gradient theorem uses \(d_\rho^\pi\). Equality requires matching that law or a valid density-ratio correction, in addition to action corrections.

  2. Let \(\gamma=1/2\), \(\epsilon=0.1\), and \(\|A^\pi\|_\infty\le2\). What surrogate gain suffices to certify strict improvement?

    Hint and solution

    The quadratic error is at most \(\gamma\epsilon^2\|A^\pi\|_\infty/(1-\gamma)^2=0.04\). A gain strictly larger than \(0.04\) suffices.
