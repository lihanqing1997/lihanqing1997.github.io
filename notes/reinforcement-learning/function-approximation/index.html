Distinguish three approximation targets, prove on-policy stability, and derive the correct off-policy gradient-TD moments.

Projection geometry

Let \(\Phi\in\mathbb R^{|\mathsf S|\times d}\) have full column rank, with row \(\phi(s)^\top\). Let \(D=\operatorname{diag}(d(s))\), where \(d(s)>0\), and write \[ \langle u,v\rangle_D=u^\top Dv,\quad C=\Phi^\top D\Phi\succ0,\quad \Pi_D=\Phi C^{-1}\Phi^\top D. \] Minimizing \(\|y-\Phi w\|_D^2\) gives the normal equations \(Cw=\Phi^\top Dy\); hence \(\Pi_Dy\) is the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal V=\operatorname{range}(\Phi)\). Three different targets are \[ \Pi_DV^\pi,\qquad \widehat V=\Pi_DT^\pi\widehat V,\qquad \operatorname*{argmin}_{V\in\mathcal V}\|T^\pi V-V\|_D^2. \] The second is the TD target; it is generally neither of the other two.

On-policy contraction and approximation error

Theorem. If \(d^\top P_\pi=d^\top\), then \(\Pi_DT^\pi\) is a \(\gamma\)-contraction on \(\mathcal V\). Its unique fixed point \(\widehat V=\Phi w^*\) solves \[ Aw^*=b,\quad A=\Phi^\top D(I-\gamma P_\pi)\Phi,\quad b=\Phi^\top Dr_\pi, \] and satisfies \[ \|\widehat V-V^\pi\|_D \le\frac{\|\Pi_DV^\pi-V^\pi\|_D}{\sqrt{1-\gamma^2}}. \]

Proof. For every vector \(v\), Jensen's inequality at each transition row and stationarity give \[ \begin{aligned} \|P_\pi v\|_D^2 &=\sum_s d(s)\left(\sum_{s'}P_\pi(s,s')v(s')\right)^2\\ &\le\sum_{s,s'}d(s)P_\pi(s,s')v(s')^2 =\sum_{s'}d(s')v(s')^2=\|v\|_D^2. \end{aligned} \] Projection is nonexpansive, so \[ \|\Pi_DT^\pi V-\Pi_DT^\pi W\|_D \le\gamma\|P_\pi(V-W)\|_D\le\gamma\|V-W\|_D. \] The finite-dimensional subspace is complete, so Banach's theorem gives a unique fixed point. Its projection residual is orthogonal to \(\mathcal V\): \[ \Phi^\top D(r_\pi+\gamma P_\pi\Phi w^*-\Phi w^*)=0, \] equivalently \(Aw^*=b\). To see nonsingularity directly, put \(v=\Phi w\): \[ w^\top Aw=\|v\|_D^2-\gamma\langle v,P_\pi v\rangle_D \ge(1-\gamma)\|v\|_D^2 \ge(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\min}(C)\|w\|_2^2. \] Thus \(Aw=0\) forces \(w=0\); \(A\) need not be symmetric.

For the sharper approximation bound, let \(e=\widehat V-V^\pi\), \(a=\widehat V-\Pi_DV^\pi\), and \(b_0=\Pi_DV^\pi-V^\pi\). Orthogonality gives \(\|e\|_D^2=\|a\|_D^2+\|b_0\|_D^2\). Since \(T^\pi V^\pi=V^\pi\), \[ a=\Pi_D(T^\pi\widehat V-T^\pi V^\pi)=\gamma\Pi_DP_\pi e, \quad \|a\|_D\le\gamma\|e\|_D. \] Therefore \((1-\gamma^2)\|e\|_D^2\le\|b_0\|_D^2\). Taking square roots proves the claim.

Least-squares TD and consistency

For transitions generated under \(\pi\) with stationary state marginal \(d\), set \[ \widehat A_n=\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n\phi_t(\phi_t-\gamma\phi'_t)^\top,\qquad \widehat b_n=\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n\phi_tR_t, \quad \widehat w_n=\widehat A_n^{-1}\widehat b_n \] whenever the inverse exists. Here \(\phi_t=\phi(S_t)\), \(\phi'_t=\phi(S'_t)\), and \(R_t\) denotes the reward in the sampled transition tuple.

Proposition (LSTD consistency). For independent transition tuples with this law, or a stationary ergodic transition sequence, \(\widehat w_n\to w^*=A^{-1}b\) almost surely; the inverse exists for all sufficiently large \(n\), almost surely.

Proof. Every matrix entry is integrable because the state set, features, and rewards are bounded. The strong law, or the ergodic theorem in the second setting, gives \[ \widehat A_n\to A,\qquad \widehat b_n\to b \] entrywise on one probability-one event; finite dimension implies norm convergence. Set \(E_n=A^{-1}(\widehat A_n-A)\). Eventually \(\|E_n\|<1/2\), and \[ \widehat A_n=A(I+E_n),\qquad (I+E_n)^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}(-E_n)^k,\qquad \|(I+E_n)^{-1}\|\le2. \] Thus the inverse exists and \[ \|\widehat A_n^{-1}-A^{-1}\| \le2\|E_n\|\|A^{-1}\|\to0. \] Finally, \[ \|\widehat w_n-w^*\| \le\|\widehat A_n^{-1}\|\|\widehat b_n-b\| +\|\widehat A_n^{-1}-A^{-1}\|\|b\|\to0. \] This argument establishes consistency, not an independent-sample confidence interval for dependent data.

A fully specified linear-TD convergence result

Theorem (independent on-policy transition sampling). Draw independent tuples \((S_n,R_n,S'_n)\) with \(S_n\sim d\), \(S'_n\sim P_\pi(S_n,\cdot)\), and the correct conditional reward law. Under the rank and stationarity assumptions above, linear TD \[ w_{n+1}=w_n+\alpha_n\phi_n[R_n+\gamma{\phi'_n}^\top w_n-\phi_n^\top w_n] \] converges almost surely to \(w^*\) from any fixed finite \(w_0\), for deterministic step sizes with \(\alpha_n>0\), \(\sum_n\alpha_n=\infty\), and \(\sum_n\alpha_n^2<\infty\).

Proof. Let \(e_n=w_n-w^*\) and \(X_n=\|e_n\|_2^2\). A new tuple is independent of the preceding iterates, so the conditional mean increment is \(b-Aw_n=-Ae_n\). With \(m=(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\min}(C)>0\), the quadratic-form bound gives \(e_n^\top Ae_n\ge mX_n\). Bounded features and rewards imply for some finite constant \(K\), \[ \mathbb E[\|\phi_n(R_n+\gamma{\phi'_n}^\top w_n-\phi_n^\top w_n)\|^2\mid w_n] \le K(1+X_n). \] Expanding the square therefore gives \[ \mathbb E[X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n] \le(1+K\alpha_n^2)X_n-2m\alpha_nX_n+K\alpha_n^2. \] To justify convergence rather than merely quote a stochastic-approximation slogan, define \[ p_0=1,\quad p_n=\prod_{j<n}(1+K\alpha_j^2),\quad U_n=\frac{X_n}{p_n}+\sum_{j=n}^\infty\frac{K\alpha_j^2}{p_{j+1}}. \] Since \(\log p_n\le K\sum_j\alpha_j^2<\infty\), \(p_n\to p_\infty\in[1,\infty)\). Direct substitution yields \[ \mathbb E[U_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]\le U_n-\frac{2m\alpha_nX_n}{p_{n+1}}. \] The nonnegative supermartingale theorem and telescoping expectations imply that \(U_n\) converges and \(\sum_n\alpha_nX_n/p_{n+1}<\infty\) almost surely. The deterministic tail tends to zero, so \(X_n\) converges. As \(p_{n+1}\le p_\infty\) and \(\sum_n\alpha_n=\infty\), its limit must be zero.

For one on-policy Markov trajectory, the tuple is not independent of \(w_n\), so the conditional mean used here is not \(b-Aw_n\). The corresponding finite-chain linear-TD theorem needs a Markov-noise argument; see the cited Tsitsiklis–Van Roy paper for that extension. It is not proved by silently treating a trajectory as independent tuples.

Off-policy correction and gradient TD

Let behavior \(\mu\) generate states with positive distribution \(d_\mu\), and assume \(\mu(a\mid s)>0\) whenever \(\pi(a\mid s)>0\). Define \(\rho_t=\pi(A_t\mid S_t)/\mu(A_t\mid S_t)\), \(D_\mu=\operatorname{diag}(d_\mu)\), and the ordinary TD error \(\delta_t=R_{t+1}+\gamma{\phi'_t}^\top w-\phi_t^\top w\). The target moments are \[ A=\mathbb E_\mu[\rho_t\phi_t(\phi_t-\gamma\phi'_t)^\top] =\Phi^\top D_\mu(I-\gamma P_\pi)\Phi,\quad b=\mathbb E_\mu[\rho_tR_{t+1}\phi_t],\quad C=\mathbb E_\mu[\phi_t\phi_t^\top]. \] For example, conditional on \(S_t=s\), \[ \sum_a\mu(a\mid s)\rho(s,a)\mathbb E[\delta_t\mid s,a] =\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)\mathbb E[\delta_t\mid s,a]. \] Without \(\rho_t\), the mean uses the behavior policy's rewards and transitions.

Assume \(C\succ0\). For \(\operatorname{MSPBE}(w)=(b-Aw)^\top C^{-1}(b-Aw)\), direct differentiation gives \[ \nabla_w\operatorname{MSPBE}(w)=-2A^\top C^{-1}(b-Aw). \] One correctly weighted GTD2 formulation is \[ h_{t+1}=h_t+\beta_t[\rho_t\delta_t-\phi_t^\top h_t]\phi_t,\qquad w_{t+1}=w_t+\alpha_t\rho_t(\phi_t-\gamma\phi'_t)(\phi_t^\top h_t). \] For fixed \(w,h\) and a fresh tuple, their mean increments are \(b-Aw-Ch\) and \(A^\top h\). At \(h=C^{-1}(b-Aw)\), the latter equals minus half the MSPBE gradient. These identities explain the algorithm; convergence of coupled recursions further requires step-size, sampling, rank, and stability assumptions.

Worked example (three different approximations). Take \(P_\pi=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\), \(d=(1/2,1/2)\), \(\gamma=1/2\), \(r=(0,1)^\top\), and \(\Phi=(1,0)^\top\). Solving Bellman gives \(V^\pi=(2/3,4/3)^\top\). The best projection is \((2/3,0)^\top\). But \[ A=\tfrac12,\quad b=0,\quad w_{\mathrm{TD}}=0. \] For the Bellman-residual objective, \[ \|T^\pi(\Phi w)-\Phi w\|_D^2 =\tfrac12[w^2+(1+w/2)^2],\quad \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}w}= \tfrac54w+\tfrac12. \] Its minimizer is \(w=-2/5\). Thus best projection, TD, and residual minimization really are distinct.

What neural approximation changes

For neural action values \(Q_w\), DQN uses replay tuples and a frozen target parameter \(w^-\): \[ L(w)=\mathbb E_{\mathrm{replay}} \left[Q_w(S,A)-\{R+\gamma\max_bQ_{w^-}(S',b)\}\right]^2. \] For fixed \(w^-\), this is a regression objective. Updating \(w^-\) changes the target; replay changes the sampling law. Neither supplies a contraction of the parameter update. Off-policy bootstrapping can already be unstable with linear features because \(P_\pi\) need not be nonexpansive in \(L_2(d_\mu)\). Claims about optimization, statistical generalization, and policy performance therefore need separate assumptions.

Exercises

  1. One state, rewards \(0,1\), \(\gamma=1/2\), uniform behavior, and target always selecting reward \(1\): what goes wrong if the auxiliary GTD update omits \(\rho\)?

    Hint and solution

    With feature \(1\), the correct moments are \(A=1/2,b=1,C=1\), giving target value \(w=2\). The unweighted auxiliary mean is \(1/2-w/2-h\); coupled to mean \(w\)-update proportional to \(h\), its equilibrium is \(w=1,h=0\), the wrong value.

  2. Why is \(A\) invertible on-policy even though it need not be symmetric?

    Hint and solution

    If \(Aw=0\), then \(0=w^\top Aw\ge(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\min}(C)\|w\|^2\). Therefore its kernel is zero. A square matrix with zero kernel is invertible; symmetry was never used.
