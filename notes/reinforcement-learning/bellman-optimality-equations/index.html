Identify the optimal Bellman fixed point with the supremum over all policies, and derive its occupancy linear-program dual.

Verification against history-dependent policies

Define \(V^*(s)=\sup_\pi\mathbb E_s^\pi G\), where the supremum includes randomized history-dependent policies. The preceding chapter gives a unique fixed point \(U=TU\) and a finite bound \(\|U\|_\infty\le R_{\max}/(1-\gamma)\), since \[ \|U\|_\infty=\|TU\|_\infty\le R_{\max}+\gamma\|U\|_\infty. \] The remaining issue is to show that this algebraic object is the optimal value.

Theorem (Bellman verification). In a finite discounted MDP, \(V^*=TV^*\). A stationary deterministic policy selecting a maximizing action at every state is optimal simultaneously from every initial state. More generally, any vector \(W\) satisfying \[ W(s)\ge r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)W(s') \quad\text{for every }s,a \] is an upper bound on the value of every policy.

Proof. For any policy, average the assumed inequalities over its current conditional action law: \[ W(S_t)\ge\mathbb E[R_{t+1}+\gamma W(S_{t+1})\mid\mathcal H_t]. \] Multiply by \(\gamma^t\), take expectations from \(S_0=s\), and sum through \(n-1\). The continuation terms cancel: \[ W(s)\ge\mathbb E_s^\pi\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}\gamma^tR_{t+1} +\gamma^n\mathbb E_s^\pi W(S_n). \] The terminal term has absolute value at most \(\gamma^n\|W\|_\infty\to0\); the reward sum converges in \(L^1\). Hence \(W(s)\ge V^\pi(s)\) for every \(\pi\), and \(W\ge V^*\).

Apply this to the fixed point \(U\). Since each action set is finite, choose \[ \pi^*(s)\in\arg\max_a\{r(s,a)+\gamma P_aU(s)\}. \] Then \(T^{\pi^*}U=TU=U\). The policy Bellman equation has a unique solution, so \(U=V^{\pi^*}\). Together, \[ U\ge V^*\ge V^{\pi^*}=U. \] Thus equality holds throughout and identifies the contraction fixed point without assuming optimal continuation exists in advance.

Action values and optimal action support

Set \[ Q^*(s,a)=r(s,a)+\gamma P_aV^*(s). \] The upper-bound part of verification applies after a forced first action, while following \(\pi^*\) afterwards attains the bound. Therefore this \(Q^*\) is the supremum of forced-action returns, and \[ V^*(s)=\max_a Q^*(s,a),\qquad Q^*(s,a)=r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\max_bQ^*(s',b). \] The action-value operator is a \(\gamma\)-contraction by the same maximum inequality and stochastic-row bound already proved.

Proposition (optimality on visited states). Let \(g(s,a)=V^*(s)-r(s,a)-\gamma P_aV^*(s)\ge0\). For every policy, \[ J_\rho^*-J_\rho(\pi)=\frac{\langle d_\rho^\pi,g\rangle}{1-\gamma}. \] Consequently \(\pi\) is optimal for \(\rho\) exactly when \(d_\rho^\pi(s,a)>0\) implies \(g(s,a)=0\).

Proof. Multiply the occupancy flow equation by \(V^*(s')\) and sum: \[ \sum_{s,a}d_\rho^\pi(s,a)[V^*(s)-\gamma P_aV^*(s)] =(1-\gamma)\sum_s\rho(s)V^*(s). \] Subtract \(\langle d_\rho^\pi,r\rangle=(1-\gamma)J_\rho(\pi)\) to obtain the gap identity. All summands \(d_\rho^\pi(s,a)g(s,a)\) are nonnegative, and there are finitely many. Hence \[ \langle d_\rho^\pi,g\rangle=0 \iff d_\rho^\pi(s,a)g(s,a)=0\quad\text{for every }s,a. \] Optimality for a single initial distribution places no restriction on actions at states that policy never visits.

Linear programming and complementary slackness

Theorem (occupancy–Bellman duality). Let \(\mathcal D_\rho\) be the nonnegative arrays satisfying discounted flow. Then \[ \max_{d\in\mathcal D_\rho}\frac{\langle d,r\rangle}{1-\gamma} =\min_{W:\ W(s)\ge r(s,a)+\gamma P_aW(s)}\langle\rho,W\rangle =J_\rho^*. \]

Proof. For dual-feasible \(W\) and primal-feasible \(d\), use flow to calculate the exact gap: \[ \begin{aligned} \langle\rho,W\rangle-\frac{\langle d,r\rangle}{1-\gamma} &=\frac1{1-\gamma}\sum_{s,a}d(s,a) [W(s)-r(s,a)-\gamma P_aW(s)]\ge0. \end{aligned} \] Thus every primal value is at most every dual value. Now choose \(W=V^*\) and \(d=d_\rho^{\pi^*}\). Every action selected by \(\pi^*\) is a Bellman maximizer, so \[ d_\rho^{\pi^*}(s,a)[V^*(s)-r(s,a)-\gamma P_aV^*(s)]=0. \] Their gap is zero. This constructs optimizers and proves strong duality directly. For any optimal pair \((d,W)\), the same nonnegative gap sum gives complementary slackness at every positive-mass action.

Finite horizons

Proposition (backward induction). For stages \(h=1,\ldots,H\), terminal reward zero, and stage-dependent kernels and rewards, \[ V_{H+1}^*=0,\qquad V_h^*(s)=\max_a\left[r_h(s,a)+\sum_{s'}P_h(s'\mid s,a)V_{h+1}^*(s')\right]. \] Stagewise maximizing actions form an optimal, generally nonstationary policy.

Proof. At \(h=H+1\), all continuation returns equal zero. Suppose every policy tail from stage \(h+1\) has value at most \(V_{h+1}^*\), and a Markov tail attains it at all states. For any history ending in state \(s\), \[ \mathbb E[\text{return from }h\mid H_h] \le\sum_a\pi_h(a\mid H_h) [r_h(s,a)+P_{h,a}V_{h+1}^*(s)]\le V_h^*(s). \] Choose an action attaining the finite maximum and then the optimal tail. Both inequalities become equalities. Induction from \(H\) down to \(1\) proves the claim; no infinite limit or contraction is needed.

Worked example (waiting for a future payoff). At state \(s\), action stay yields reward \(1\) and returns to \(s\); action go yields \(0\) and moves to absorbing \(g\), whose reward is always \(2\). With \(\gamma=1/2\), \[ V^*(g)=4,\quad V^*(s)=\max\{1+V^*(s)/2,2\}=2. \] Both actions at \(s\) are optimal. With \(\gamma=0.9\), \(V^*(g)=20\), go gives \(18\), and stay's Bellman score at \(V^*(s)=18\) is \(17.2\); go is strictly optimal. The discount changes the preferred tradeoff, not just the numerical scale.

Exercises

  1. In the worked example with \(\gamma=0.9\) and \(\rho=\delta_s\), compute the occupancy of the policy that immediately goes.

    Hint and solution

    \(d(s,\mathrm{go})=1-\gamma=0.1\), \(d(g)=\gamma=0.9\). Its discounted value is \(\langle d,r\rangle/(1-\gamma)=1.8/0.1=18\).

  2. Why can a dual-optimal \(W\) differ from \(V^*\) at an unreachable state?

    Hint and solution

    The objective weights \(W\) only through \(\rho\). If a state is unreachable from \(\rho\) under every action and has a self-loop of reward zero, any nonnegative value at that state satisfies its Bellman inequality without changing the objective. The minimal feasible vector is \(V^*\), but the LP minimizer need not be unique.
