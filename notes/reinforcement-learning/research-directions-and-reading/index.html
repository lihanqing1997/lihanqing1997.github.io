Learning goal. Use the preceding results to read research claims critically: identify the objective, data law, comparator, approximation class, and kind of guarantee before comparing algorithms.

Research map

The preceding chapters establish a finite discounted core, then make each change of regime explicit. The table below is a selective research map, with references checked on 31 October 2026. It is not an exhaustive survey or a ranking of the newest methods. A proof for a tabular MDP, a simulation experiment, and a deployment guarantee answer different questions.

Research directions

Offline assumptions and guarantees

Jiang–Xie (2025), Offline Reinforcement Learning in Large State Spaces: Algorithms and Guarantees, organizes the interaction of coverage and function approximation. Read it after the approximation and offline chapters. A useful first task is to write each theorem's assumption set explicitly and compare which assumptions are strengthened when moving from evaluation to optimization.

Adaptive datasets

Madhow–Qiao–Yin–Wang (L4DC 2025), Rates for Offline Reinforcement Learning with Adaptively Collected Data, studies evaluation and policy optimization when the dataset was collected adaptively. This is a concrete reason to revisit the filtration and confidence-event arguments rather than replace every dataset by an iid transition sample.

Average reward and transient coverage

Zurek–Zamir–Chen, Optimal Single-Policy Sample Complexity and Transient Coverage for Average-Reward Offline RL (NeurIPS 2025; revised April 2026), distinguishes recurrent and transient coverage and studies target-policy-dependent complexity. The conceptual question is why a stationary distribution may overlook transitions needed to reach a rewarding recurrent region. Compare its assumptions with the stronger common minorization used for the complete proof in this note.

RL for language-model reasoning

DeepSeek-AI, DeepSeek-R1 (2025; revised January 2026), is a prominent empirical entry point to reinforcement learning for reasoning. Connect sequence generation to trajectory gradients, group/state baselines, sparse terminal rewards, and KL regularization. Questions about verifier reliability, reward exploitation, generalization beyond the training distribution, and computation at evaluation time remain distinct from improving measured training reward. The paper's empirical results are not a theorem that policy optimization produces reliable reasoning in general.

Verifying learned policies

Marzari–Bartocci–Marchesini (IJCAI 2026), A Survey on the Verification of Reinforcement Learning Policies, provides an entry to verification questions beyond expected return. Compare the modeled environment and specification with the guarantee: a one-step bound, a multi-step property, and a probabilistic statement over trajectories require different evidence.

World models as a continuing research program

Hafner and coauthors, Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models (DreamerV3), is a foundational modern example rather than a newly released 2026 paper. Read it alongside the model-error identity and belief-state chapter. A productive question is how model errors and representation errors interact with the policy's changing visitation distribution.

How to read a theorem or an experimental claim

Before comparing rates, record the horizon convention, reward normalization, state/action assumptions, sampling access, comparator, and confidence level. For example, a generative model supplying independent samples from any requested state-action pair is stronger access than a single online trajectory. A bound for a fixed target policy is weaker than a simultaneous guarantee supporting data-dependent policy selection. “Converges” must specify the object and mode: parameters, values, average regret, empirical policy distributions, almost sure limits, or finite-sample probabilities.

For experiments, look for the data and environment splits, evaluation-policy selection, random seeds, uncertainty bars, compute budget, and relevant baselines. Separate approximation error from statistical error and optimization error. An ablation can support a mechanism empirically without proving it universally. These questions give a precise place to connect a new method to the derivations in this note.

Self-study projects

  1. One MDP, four estimators. On a two-state discounted MDP, compute the exact value by a linear solve, then compare Monte Carlo, TD, and LSTD under a fixed policy. Include a deliberately misspecified one-dimensional feature map.

    Suggested checks

    Report separately the sampling fluctuation and the limiting approximation bias. Verify the tabular Bellman residual, estimate the LSTD moment matrix, and compare its population solution with the orthogonal projection. Under dependent trajectories, avoid presenting iid error bars as established confidence intervals.

  2. A coverage stress test. Use the one-state offline counterexample with a small, nonzero behavior probability for the uncertain action. Compare importance sampling, a fitted direct estimate, and pessimistic selection as the sample count changes.

    Suggested checks

    The probability of never seeing that action in \(n\) independent observations is \((1-p)^n\). Plot performance conditional on this event and overall. Explain why two datasets of equal size can support very different guarantees when their coverage differs.

  3. From contraction to a research assumption. Reproduce the periodic average-reward counterexample, then replace \(P\) by \(P_\epsilon=(1-\epsilon)P+\epsilon\mathbf1\nu^\top\). Compare relative-value iteration for several \(\epsilon>0\).

    Suggested checks

    Measure span error, not absolute error under an arbitrary additive normalization. The proved contraction factor is at most \(1-\epsilon\). As \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), the guarantee deteriorates; this makes the role of mixing visible before reading weaker-assumption results.

Sources and further reading

  • Sutton–Barto, Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction, second edition: algorithmic intuition and the classical learning methods.
  • Agarwal and coauthors, Reinforcement Learning: Theory and Algorithms: the main bridge to modern statistical and optimization theory. The online text is updated; check the version when citing a specific theorem.
  • Lattimore–Szepesvári, Bandit Algorithms: concentration, sequential information bounds, and regret.
  • The chapter-specific source links: use the original papers for assumptions hidden by a short algorithm description, especially two-time-scale convergence, deep actor-critic approximations, and non-discounted control.

Scope boundary. This note supplies a proof-based route through the main classical foundations and selected extensions. Sharp sample-complexity bounds for broad function classes, general two-time-scale stochastic approximation, continuous-state control, and large-scale neural training are further subjects. Their references are starting points for study, not claims that the finite proofs here already establish their guarantees.
