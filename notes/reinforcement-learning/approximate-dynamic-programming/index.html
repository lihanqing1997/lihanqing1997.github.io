Quantify how backup error, imperfect greediness, and distribution mismatch affect a learned policy.

Errors in repeated Bellman backups

Proposition. For \(V_{k+1}=TV_k+\epsilon_k\), \[ \|V_k-V^*\|_\infty \le\gamma^k\|V_0-V^*\|_\infty+ \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\gamma^{k-1-j}\|\epsilon_j\|_\infty. \] If \(\|\epsilon_j\|_\infty\le\epsilon\), the limiting upper error is \(\epsilon/(1-\gamma)\).

Proof. Subtract \(TV^*=V^*\): \[ \|V_{k+1}-V^*\|_\infty \le\|TV_k-TV^*\|_\infty+\|\epsilon_k\|_\infty \le\gamma\|V_k-V^*\|_\infty+\|\epsilon_k\|_\infty. \] Iterate this scalar recursion to get the finite convolution. Its second term is bounded by \[ \epsilon\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\gamma^{k-1-j} =\epsilon\frac{1-\gamma^k}{1-\gamma}. \] Taking the upper limit gives the claim. Vanishing backup errors imply convergence by the finite-prefix and small-tail argument proved in the contraction chapter.

Imperfect action selection

Theorem (approximate greediness). Suppose \(0\le TV-T^\pi V\le\eta\mathbf1\). Then \[ \|V^*-V^\pi\|_\infty \le\frac{2\gamma\|V-V^*\|_\infty+\eta}{1-\gamma}. \]

Proof. Insert the two approximate backups into the value difference: \[ V^*-V^\pi=(TV^*-TV)+(TV-T^\pi V)+(T^\pi V-T^\pi V^\pi). \] Put \(L=\|V^*-V^\pi\|_\infty\), \(e=\|V-V^*\|_\infty\). The first term is bounded by \(\gamma e\), the second by \(\eta\), and the third by \[ \gamma\|V-V^\pi\|_\infty \le\gamma(e+L). \] Thus \(L\le2\gamma e+\eta+\gamma L\), and division by \(1-\gamma\) proves the result.

For fitted value iteration with uniform backup error \(\epsilon\) and action-selection error \(\eta\), the two results imply an eventual policy-loss bound \(2\gamma\epsilon/(1-\gamma)^2+\eta/(1-\gamma)\). An exact greedy step cannot repair persistent error in the values it uses.

A quantified approximate policy-improvement step

Proposition. If \(\|\widehat V-V^\pi\|_\infty\le\epsilon\) and \(\pi'\) is \(\eta\)-greedy for \(\widehat V\), then \[ V^{\pi'}-V^\pi\ge-\frac{2\gamma\epsilon+\eta}{1-\gamma}\mathbf1. \]

Proof. By monotonicity and the contraction bounds, \[ \begin{aligned} T^{\pi'}V^\pi &\ge T^{\pi'}\widehat V-\gamma\epsilon\mathbf1\\ &\ge T\widehat V-(\eta+\gamma\epsilon)\mathbf1\\ &\ge TV^\pi-(\eta+2\gamma\epsilon)\mathbf1 \ge V^\pi-(\eta+2\gamma\epsilon)\mathbf1. \end{aligned} \] Apply the exact improvement identity to this possibly negative advantage: \[ V^{\pi'}-V^\pi =(I-\gamma P_{\pi'})^{-1}(T^{\pi'}V^\pi-V^\pi) \ge-(\eta+2\gamma\epsilon)\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^tP_{\pi'}^t\mathbf1. \] Since \(P_{\pi'}^t\mathbf1=\mathbf1\), the sum is \(\mathbf1/(1-\gamma)\). This is a one-step guarantee, not a convergence theorem for arbitrary approximate policy iteration.

Fitted Q iteration and statistical error

Given transition tuples, fitted Q iteration forms \[ Y_i=R_i+\gamma\max_aQ_k(S_i',a) \] and regresses \(Y_i\) on \((S_i,A_i)\) in a class \(\mathcal F\). If \(\Pi_{\mathcal F}\mathcal TQ_k\) denotes a chosen population squared-loss minimizer under the data law \(\nu\), then the exact decomposition is \[ \widehat Q_{k+1}-\mathcal TQ_k =(\Pi_{\mathcal F}\mathcal TQ_k-\mathcal TQ_k) +(\widehat Q_{k+1}-\Pi_{\mathcal F}\mathcal TQ_k). \] The first is representation error; the second includes estimation and optimization. If the same data determine \(Q_k\), ordinary fixed-target concentration does not automatically apply. Sample splitting, uniform bounds over the function class, or an explicit stability argument must handle that dependence.

Why the sampling norm matters

Proposition (coverage transfer). For state-action laws \(\nu,\xi\) with \(\xi\ll\nu\) and \(\|\mathrm{d}\xi/\mathrm{d}\nu\|_\infty\le C\), \[ \|e\|_{L_2(\xi)}\le\sqrt C\,\|e\|_{L_2(\nu)},\qquad \mathbb E_\xi|e|\le\sqrt C\,\|e\|_{L_2(\nu)}. \]

Proof. Let \(w=\mathrm{d}\xi/\mathrm{d}\nu\). Since \(0\le w\le C\), \[ \int e^2\,\mathrm{d}\xi=\int we^2\,\mathrm{d}\nu \le C\int e^2\,\mathrm{d}\nu. \] Take square roots. Cauchy–Schwarz under the probability law \(\xi\) gives \[ \int|e|\,\mathrm{d}\xi \le\left(\int e^2\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{1/2} \left(\int1\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{1/2}. \] For control, the needed \(\xi\)'s are occupancies induced by the relevant policies or sequences of policies; specifying only a convenient training distribution is insufficient.

Worked example (small training error, large policy error). Consider a one-step problem with two actions, rewards \(r(a)=0,r(b)=1\), and data law \(\nu(a)=1\). Let \(Q(a)=0,Q(b)=-M\) for \(M>0\). Then \[ \|Q-Q^*\|_{L_2(\nu)}=0, \] but the greedy policy chooses \(a\) and loses reward \(1\). The missing action has zero data mass, so no finite coverage coefficient transfers the training norm to the optimal action distribution.

Exercises

  1. At \(\gamma=0.8\), uniform backup error \(0.01\), and exact greediness, compute the eventual loss bound.

    Hint and solution

    The value bound is \(0.01/0.2=0.05\); the policy bound is \(2(0.8)(0.01)/(0.2)^2=0.4\). This is a worst-case guarantee, not a claim that every fitted algorithm incurs that loss.

  2. Modify the example to \(\nu(b)=p>0\). Compute the error norm and the coverage coefficient for always choosing \(b\).

    Hint and solution

    The error at \(b\) is \(-M-1\), so \(\|Q-Q^*\|_{L_2(\nu)}=\sqrt p(M+1)\). For \(\xi=\delta_b\), \(C=1/p\); the transferred bound is exactly \(M+1\).
