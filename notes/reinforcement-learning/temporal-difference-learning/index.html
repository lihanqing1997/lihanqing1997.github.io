Learning goal. Write TD as an adapted stochastic recursion and prove its tabular convergence, including the argument that accumulated noise disappears.

Initialize the value table at a fixed finite vector.

Condition on the information actually available

Fix a stationary policy \(\pi\) in the finite bounded-reward model. Let \(\mathcal F_t\) contain the trajectory through \(S_t\), before drawing \(A_t\), and the current vector \(V_t\). All estimates and step sizes used at time \(t\) are \(\mathcal F_t\)-measurable. Define \[ \delta_t=R_{t+1}+\gamma V_t(S_{t+1})-V_t(S_t),\qquad V_{t+1}(s)=V_t(s)+a_t(s)\delta_t, \] where \(a_t(s)=0\) unless \(S_t=s\). The action is drawn from \(\pi(\cdot\mid S_t)\), so \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[\delta_t\mid\mathcal F_t] &=\sum_a\pi(a\mid S_t) \left[r(S_t,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid S_t,a)V_t(s')\right]-V_t(S_t)\\ &=(T^\pi V_t)(S_t)-V_t(S_t). \end{aligned} \] Conditioning only on \(S_t\) would still leave the data-dependent \(V_t\) random. The full-history conditioning is what makes the following noise a martingale difference: \[ M_{t+1}=R_{t+1}+\gamma V_t(S_{t+1})-(T^\pi V_t)(S_t), \qquad \mathbb E[M_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]=0. \]

A stochastic contraction theorem

The proof uses the prerequisite theorem that a nonnegative supermartingale converges almost surely to a finite limit. Its proof is in the martingale-convergence chapter. The rest of the argument is developed here.

Theorem 9.1 (bounded asynchronous stochastic contraction). Let \(F:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^d\) be a sup-norm \(\gamma\)-contraction with fixed point \(x^*\). At time \(n\), an \(\mathcal F_n\)-measurable coordinate \(i_n\) is updated: \[ X_{n+1}(i)=X_n(i)+a_n(i)[F(X_n)(i)-X_n(i)+M_{n+1}(i)]. \] Here \(a_n(i)=\mathbf1_{\{i_n=i\}}\alpha_{i,N_n(i)+1}\), where \(N_n(i)\) counts updates of \(i\) before time \(n\). Assume every coordinate is updated infinitely often almost surely; the deterministic local step sequences satisfy \[ 0<\alpha_{i,k}\le1,\qquad \sum_k\alpha_{i,k}=\infty,\qquad \sum_k\alpha_{i,k}^2<\infty; \] the noise satisfies \[ \mathbb E[M_{n+1}(i)\mid\mathcal F_n]=0,\qquad \mathbb E[M_{n+1}(i)^2\mid\mathcal F_n]\le C; \] and \(\sup_n\|X_n\|_\infty<\infty\) almost surely. Then \(X_n\to x^*\) almost surely.

Proof. 1. Filter out the noise. For each coordinate, define \(Z_0(i)=0\) and \[ Z_{n+1}(i)=(1-a_n(i))Z_n(i)+a_n(i)M_{n+1}(i). \] Suppress \(i\) for the next calculation. Since \(a_n,Z_n\) are measurable at time \(n\), the cross term has conditional mean zero. Thus \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E[Z_{n+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_n] &=(1-a_n)^2Z_n^2+a_n^2\mathbb E[M_{n+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_n]\\ &\le Z_n^2-a_nZ_n^2+Ca_n^2, \end{aligned} \] using \((1-a)^2\le1-a\) for \(0\le a\le1\). Add the remaining deterministic local squared-step budget: \[ U_n=Z_n^2+C\sum_{k>N_n(i)}\alpha_{i,k}^2. \] Updating coordinate \(i\) removes exactly \(Ca_n^2\) from this tail; otherwise both tail and coordinate are unchanged. Therefore \[ \mathbb E[U_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]\le U_n-a_nZ_n^2. \] The nonnegative supermartingale \(U_n\) has finite initial expectation and converges almost surely. Taking expectations, summing to \(m-1\), and then using monotone convergence gives \[ \mathbb E\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nZ_n^2\le\mathbb E U_0<\infty. \] Hence this nonnegative series is finite almost surely. Infinite visitation implies \(N_n(i)\to\infty\), so the squared-step tail tends to zero and \(Z_n^2\) has an almost-sure limit. A positive limit would force \(\sum_n a_nZ_n^2=\infty\), since \(\sum_n a_n=\sum_k\alpha_{i,k}=\infty\). Thus \(Z_n(i)\to0\). There are finitely many coordinates, so \(\|Z_n\|_\infty\to0\) on one common probability-one event.

2. Control the remaining deterministic error. Set \(E_n=X_n-x^*-Z_n\). Subtract the two recursions: \[ E_{n+1}(i)=(1-a_n(i))E_n(i)+a_n(i)[F(X_n)(i)-F(x^*)(i)]. \] On the probability-one event above, \(L=\limsup_n\|E_n\|_\infty<\infty\), by boundedness of \(X_n\) and convergence of \(Z_n\). Fix \(\epsilon>0\). For some finite \(N\), all \(n\ge N\) have \(\|E_n\|_\infty\le L+\epsilon\) and \(\|Z_n\|_\infty\le\epsilon\). Consequently \[ |E_{n+1}(i)| \le(1-a_n(i))|E_n(i)|+a_n(i)\gamma(L+2\epsilon). \] Iterating this scalar inequality yields \[ |E_n(i)|\le q_{N,n}(i)|E_N(i)|+ [1-q_{N,n}(i)]\gamma(L+2\epsilon), \quad q_{N,n}(i)=\prod_{t=N}^{n-1}(1-a_t(i)). \] If one factor is zero, the product is zero thereafter; otherwise \[ 0\le q_{N,n}(i)\le\exp\left(-\sum_{t=N}^{n-1}a_t(i)\right)\longrightarrow0. \] Take \(n\to\infty\), then the maximum over finitely many coordinates: \[ L\le\gamma(L+2\epsilon). \] Finally let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). Since \(\gamma<1\), \(L=0\), so \(E_n\to0\). Together with \(Z_n\to0\), this proves \(X_n\to x^*\).

Apply the theorem to tabular TD

Theorem 9.2 (on-policy TD convergence). Assume bounded rewards, finite states and actions, \(0\le\gamma<1\), every state visited infinitely often almost surely, and the local step-size conditions just stated. Then tabular TD converges to \(V^\pi\) almost surely.

Proof. Choose a deterministic \[ B=\max\{\|V_0\|_\infty,R_{\max}/(1-\gamma)\}. \] If \(\|V_t\|_\infty\le B\), the sampled target obeys \[ |R_{t+1}+\gamma V_t(S_{t+1})|\le R_{\max}+\gamma B\le B. \] The update is a convex combination of the old coordinate and this target, so induction gives \(\|V_t\|_\infty\le B\) for all \(t\). Also \(|T^\pi V_t(s)|\le B\), so \(|M_{t+1}|\le2B\) and the conditional second moment is at most \(4B^2\). The history-conditioned calculation above proves zero conditional mean. Finally, \[ \|T^\pi V-T^\pi W\|_\infty\le\gamma\|V-W\|_\infty,\qquad T^\pi V^\pi=V^\pi. \] Use \(F=T^\pi\), selected coordinate \(S_t\), and set noise at unselected coordinates to zero. Every hypothesis of the stochastic contraction theorem is now verified.

Irreducibility of a finite policy chain is one sufficient way to obtain infinite visitation; aperiodicity is not needed for this proof. The step-size conditions concern the visits to each coordinate, not just a single global sum. Constant steps do not satisfy the theorem and generally leave persistent noise.

Worked example 9.3 (deterministic TD still has gradual convergence). In one state with reward \(1\), \(\gamma=1/2\), \(V_0=0\), and local step \(\alpha_k=1/k\), the first estimates are \[ V_1=1,\qquad V_2=1+\tfrac12(1-\tfrac12)=\tfrac54,\qquad V_3=\tfrac54+\tfrac13(1-\tfrac58)=\tfrac{11}8. \] The true value is \(2\). If \(e_k=2-V_k\), then \[ e_k=(1-\alpha_k/2)e_{k-1},\qquad 0\le e_k\le2\exp\left(-\tfrac12\sum_{j=1}^k\alpha_j\right)\to0. \] Noise is absent here; diminishing steps alone make the recursion slower than exact value iteration.

Exercises

  1. Exercise 9.1. For \(\alpha_k=k^{-p}\), which exponents satisfy both step-size requirements?

    Hint

    Apply the power-series convergence criterion separately to the steps and their squares.

    Solution

    The series \(\sum k^{-p}\) diverges for \(p\le1\), while \(\sum k^{-2p}\) converges for \(2p>1\). Thus \(1/2<p\le1\).

  2. Exercise 9.2. At \(\gamma=0\), one state, and independent reward noise of variance \(\sigma^2\), find the limiting variance with constant step \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).

    Hint

    Center the scalar recursion and derive a recurrence for its variance.

    Solution

    The centered recursion is \(e_{n+1}=(1-\alpha)e_n+\alpha\xi_{n+1}\). Hence \(v_{n+1}=(1-\alpha)^2v_n+\alpha^2\sigma^2\), and \(v_\infty=\alpha\sigma^2/(2-\alpha)>0\). A vanishing mean error does not imply almost-sure convergence of the noisy iterates.
