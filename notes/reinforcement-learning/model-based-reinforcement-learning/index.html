Quantify how model error changes value and policy quality, then identify what a learned latent state must preserve.

Learning a model and planning in it

Let \(M=(r,P)\) and \(\widehat M=(\widehat r,\widehat P)\) share finite state/action spaces and \(0\le\gamma<1\). Assume \(|r|,|\widehat r|\le R_{\max}\). A learned model supports simulated Bellman backups, policy search, or short model rollouts. Its usefulness depends on prediction errors at state-action pairs the planner will use.

Theorem (discounted simulation bound). If \(\|r-\widehat r\|_\infty\le\varepsilon_r\) and \(\sup_{s,a}\|P(\cdot\mid s,a)-\widehat P(\cdot\mid s,a)\|_1\le\varepsilon_P\), then every stationary policy satisfies \[ \|V_M^\pi-V_{\widehat M}^\pi\|_\infty \le\varepsilon_{\rm val}:= \frac{\varepsilon_r}{1-\gamma} +\frac{\gamma R_{\max}\varepsilon_P}{(1-\gamma)^2}. \]

Proof. Subtract the two Bellman equations, adding and subtracting \(\gamma P_\pi V_{\widehat M}^\pi\): \[ (I-\gamma P_\pi)(V_M^\pi-V_{\widehat M}^\pi) =(r_\pi-\widehat r_\pi)+\gamma(P_\pi-\widehat P_\pi)V_{\widehat M}^\pi. \] The right-hand side has norm at most \(\varepsilon_r+\gamma\varepsilon_P R_{\max}/(1-\gamma)\). The earlier Neumann-series proof gives \[ \|(I-\gamma P_\pi)^{-1}\|_\infty \le\sum_{t=0}^\infty\gamma^t\|P_\pi^t\|_\infty =1/(1-\gamma). \] Multiplying proves the bound. These are uniform inequalities, so the conclusion remains valid for a policy selected from the same model-fitting data on an event where the model bounds hold simultaneously.

If \(\widehat\pi\) is optimal in \(\widehat M\), or is uniformly \(\eta\)-suboptimal there, then \[ \begin{aligned} V_M^*-V_M^{\widehat\pi} &=(V_M^{\pi^*}-V_{\widehat M}^{\pi^*}) +(V_{\widehat M}^{\pi^*}-V_{\widehat M}^{\widehat\pi}) +(V_{\widehat M}^{\widehat\pi}-V_M^{\widehat\pi})\\ &\le(2\varepsilon_{\rm val}+\eta)\mathbf1. \end{aligned} \] This separates estimation and planning error. It requires model accuracy beyond the fitted policy alone because the comparator policy also appears.

An occupancy-weighted error identity

Proposition (where model error matters). For a fixed policy, define \(e^\pi(s,a)=r(s,a)-\widehat r(s,a)+ \gamma(P-\widehat P)V_{\widehat M}^\pi(s,a)\). Then \[ J_M(\pi)-J_{\widehat M}(\pi) =\frac1{1-\gamma}\mathbb E_{d_{\rho,M}^\pi}[e^\pi(S,A)]. \]

Proof. The preceding Bellman subtraction is \(V_M^\pi-V_{\widehat M}^\pi=(I-\gamma P_\pi)^{-1}e_\pi^\pi\). Multiply by \(\rho^\top\), expand the absolutely convergent resolvent, and use occupancy: \[ \rho^\top\sum_{t=0}^\infty\gamma^tP_\pi^t e_\pi^\pi =\frac1{1-\gamma}\sum_s d_{\rho,S,M}^\pi(s) \sum_a\pi(a\mid s)e^\pi(s,a). \] All terms are bounded by a constant times \(\gamma^t\); no uncontrolled limit exchange is required.

In a finite horizon with \(0\le r,\widehat r\le R_{\max}\), uniform row errors at every stage imply \[ \|V_{M,h}^\pi-V_{\widehat M,h}^\pi\|_\infty \le(H-h+1)\varepsilon_r+ \frac{(H-h)(H-h+1)}2 R_{\max}\varepsilon_P. \] To verify this, write \(D_h\le\varepsilon_r+(H-h)R_{\max}\varepsilon_P+D_{h+1}\), with \(D_{H+1}=0\), and sum the arithmetic series. The quadratic horizon factor is the explicit accumulation of transition error.

Latent states and planning consistency

A representation \(z=\phi(s)\) is an exact controlled abstraction if, whenever \(\phi(s)=\phi(\widetilde s)\), for every action \(a\), \[ r(s,a)=r(\widetilde s,a),\qquad \sum_{s':\phi(s')=z'}P(s'\mid s,a) =\sum_{s':\phi(s')=z'}P(s'\mid\widetilde s,a) \quad\text{for every }z'. \] These equalities define a well-defined latent reward and transition kernel. For any \(v\) on latent states, the ground Bellman backup of \(v\circ\phi\) equals \((T_{\rm latent}v)\circ\phi\), by substitution into the displayed sums. Iterating from zero and taking the uniform contraction limit proves \(V^*=V_{\rm latent}^*\circ\phi\), and a latent greedy policy is optimal in the original MDP. Predicting observations well does not imply these reward-and-control identities.

Worked example (small transition error, long-horizon effect). State \(g\) gives reward one and stays in \(g\) with probability \(1-p\), otherwise entering a zero-reward absorbing state. A model incorrectly sets \(p=0\). Then \[ V_M(g)=\frac1{1-\gamma+\gamma p},\qquad V_{\widehat M}(g)=\frac1{1-\gamma}, \] \[ V_{\widehat M}(g)-V_M(g) =\frac{\gamma p}{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma+\gamma p)}. \] The row \(\ell^1\) error is \(2p\). For \(p\ll1-\gamma\), the actual value error is approximately \(\gamma p/(1-\gamma)^2\), exhibiting the squared effective-horizon dependence.

Exercises

  1. Two states share a latent label but have action rewards \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\). Can a policy using only that label be optimal in both states?

    Hint and solution

    No. The first state requires the first action, the second requires the second. A shared randomized action probability \(q\) gives rewards \(q\) and \(1-q\); at least one is at most \(1/2\). The abstraction violates reward consistency.

  2. If \(\gamma=0.9\), \(R_{\max}=1\), \(\varepsilon_r=0.01\), and \(\varepsilon_P=0.001\), evaluate the uniform planning-loss guarantee.

    Hint and solution

    \(\varepsilon_{\rm val}=0.01/0.1+0.9(0.001)/0.1^2=0.19\). Exact planning gives a policy-loss bound \(2\varepsilon_{\rm val}=0.38\).

Sources and prerequisites. Agarwal and coauthors, Reinforcement Learning: Theory and Algorithms; Hafner and coauthors, DreamerV3 as a modern learned-world-model example. Neural latent-model experiments do not inherit the exact abstraction theorem without its consistency conditions.
