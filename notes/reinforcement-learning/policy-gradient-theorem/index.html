Derive the gradient from a finite Bellman system, then justify its trajectory representation without differentiating an undefined infinite product.

A differentiable policy family

Keep the finite discounted MDP and a fixed initial distribution \(\rho\). Let \(\theta\in U\subset\mathbb R^p\), with \(U\) open, and let each \(\pi_\theta(a\mid s)>0\) be continuously differentiable. The environment and \(\rho\) do not depend on \(\theta\). Write \[ z_\theta(s,a)=\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s),\qquad J(\theta)=\rho^\top V_\theta,\qquad d_\theta=d_\rho^{\pi_\theta}. \] Here \(d_\theta\) is the normalized state-action occupancy. On a sufficiently small compact neighborhood of any parameter, finiteness and positivity give a uniform bound \(\|z_\theta(s,a)\|\le L\). This is a local assumption; a softmax score need not be bounded for arbitrary feature families on infinite spaces.

Theorem (policy gradient). \[ \nabla J(\theta)=\frac1{1-\gamma} \sum_{s,a}d_\theta(s,a)z_\theta(s,a)Q_\theta(s,a). \] The formula accounts for the dependence of the state distribution on the policy; it does not assume that distribution is fixed.

Proof. Put \(P_\theta=P_{\pi_\theta}\), \(r_\theta=r_{\pi_\theta}\). Since \(\|\gamma P_\theta\|_\infty<1\), \[ V_\theta=(I-\gamma P_\theta)^{-1}r_\theta . \] The inverse is continuously differentiable wherever its determinant is nonzero (for example, by the adjugate formula). For each coordinate \(j\), differentiating the finite identity \(V_\theta=r_\theta+\gamma P_\theta V_\theta\) gives \[ (I-\gamma P_\theta)\partial_j V_\theta =\partial_j r_\theta+\gamma(\partial_jP_\theta)V_\theta =g_j,\qquad g_j(s)=\sum_a\partial_j\pi_\theta(a\mid s)Q_\theta(s,a). \] Consequently, \[ \begin{aligned} \partial_jJ &=\rho^\top\sum_{t=0}^\infty\gamma^tP_\theta^t g_j\\ &=\frac1{1-\gamma}\sum_s d_{\rho,S}^{\pi_\theta}(s) \sum_a\pi_\theta(a\mid s)z_{\theta,j}(s,a)Q_\theta(s,a). \end{aligned} \] The matrix series converges in operator norm, and \(\sum_t\gamma^t\|\rho^\top P_\theta^t g_j\|\le\|g_j\|_\infty/(1-\gamma)\). Thus the multiplication and summation are justified. Finally \(d_\theta(s,a)=d_{\rho,S}^{\pi_\theta}(s)\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\).

Why the likelihood-ratio derivation is legitimate

Define \(J_H=\mathbb E_\theta\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^tR_{t+1}\). A finite state-action path has policy factor \(\prod_{k=0}^{H-1}\pi_\theta(a_k\mid s_k)\); the environment factors have zero derivative. If rewards have a non-discrete law, integrate them first conditional on the finite path. The resulting finite sum can be differentiated term by term.

Proof. With \(z_k=z_\theta(S_k,A_k)\), finite differentiation yields \[ \nabla J_H=\mathbb E_\theta\left[ \sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^tR_{t+1}\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}z_k\right] =\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\sum_{k=0}^{t}\gamma^t\mathbb E_\theta[R_{t+1}z_k]. \] For \(k>t\), \(R_{t+1}\) is measurable before action \(A_k\), and \[ \mathbb E_\theta[z_k\mid H_k] =\sum_a\pi_\theta(a\mid S_k)\nabla\log\pi_\theta(a\mid S_k) =\nabla\sum_a\pi_\theta(a\mid S_k)=0. \] Uniformly on the compact parameter neighborhood, \[ \sum_{t=H}^\infty\sum_{k=0}^t \|\gamma^t\mathbb E_\theta[R_{t+1}z_k]\| \le LR_{\max}\sum_{t=H}^\infty(t+1)\gamma^t\longrightarrow0. \] Hence \(\nabla J_H\) converges uniformly to a continuous function \(g\), while \(J_H\to J\) uniformly by the return-tail bound. On any line segment contained in the neighborhood, \[ J_H(\theta+h)-J_H(\theta)=\int_0^1\nabla J_H(\theta+uh)^\top h\,du \ \longrightarrow\ \int_0^1g(\theta+uh)^\top h\,du . \] Continuity of \(g\) now gives \(J(\theta+h)-J(\theta)=g(\theta)^\top h+o(\|h\|)\). Thus \(g=\nabla J\). Absolute summability of the double series permits reordering: \[ \begin{aligned} \nabla J &=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\gamma^k \mathbb E_\theta\left[z_k\sum_{t=k}^\infty\gamma^{t-k}R_{t+1}\right]\\ &=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\gamma^k\mathbb E_\theta[z_kQ_\theta(S_k,A_k)] =\frac{\mathbb E_{d_\theta}[z_\theta Q_\theta]}{1-\gamma}. \end{aligned} \] The conditional-return limit is valid in \(L^1\), since its tail is bounded by \(R_{\max}\gamma^{H-k}/(1-\gamma)\).

Natural and deterministic policy gradients

The occupancy-weighted Fisher matrix is \(F=\mathbb E_{d_\theta}[zz^\top]\succeq0\). If \(F\succ0\), maximizing \(g^\top v-\tfrac12v^\top Fv\) gives \(v=F^{-1}g\). When \(F\) is singular, \(g=\nabla J\) still lies in its range: for \(u\in\ker F\), \(\mathbb E(u^\top z)^2=0\), so \(u^\top g=\mathbb E[(u^\top z)Q]/(1-\gamma)=0\). The solutions are \(F^\dagger g+\ker F\); \(F^\dagger g\) is the minimum Euclidean norm solution. This is a local metric statement, not a global convergence theorem.

For a deterministic policy \(\mu_\theta(s)\) with continuous actions, retain finite states, and assume \(r(s,a)\), \(P(s'\mid s,a)\), and \(\mu_\theta\) are \(C^1\) on the relevant neighborhoods. Define \(Q_\theta(s,a)=r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)V_\theta(s')\). Differentiating the finite Bellman system by the chain rule gives \[ g(s)=D_\theta\mu_\theta(s)^\top \nabla_a Q_\theta(s,a)\big|_{a=\mu_\theta(s)},\qquad \partial_jV_\theta(s)=g_j(s)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P_{\mu_\theta}(s,s')\partial_jV_\theta(s'). \] In vector form, \((I-\gamma P_{\mu_\theta})\partial_jV_\theta=g_j\). Multiplication by the same resolvent therefore proves \[ \nabla J(\theta)=\frac1{1-\gamma}\mathbb E_{s\sim d_{\rho,S}^{\mu_\theta}}[g(s)]. \] Infinite state spaces require additional differentiability and domination hypotheses; this finite-state derivation does not supply them.

Worked example (one-state softmax). Actions have rewards \(0,1\), both return to the same state, and \(p=\pi_\theta(1)=1/(1+e^{-\theta})\). Then \[ J=\frac p{1-\gamma},\quad z(1)=1-p,\quad z(0)=-p,\quad F=p(1-p). \] Since \(Q(1)-Q(0)=1\), \[ \frac{\mathbb E[zQ]}{1-\gamma} =\frac{p(1-p)(Q(1)-Q(0))}{1-\gamma} =\frac{p(1-p)}{1-\gamma}=\frac{dJ}{d\theta}. \] The natural-gradient direction is \(1/(1-\gamma)\), although the ordinary gradient vanishes as \(p\to0\) or \(1\).

Exercises

  1. Show that any state baseline \(b(s)\) can replace \(Q\) by \(Q-b\) in the theorem.

    Hint and solution

    \(\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)z(s,a)b(s)=b(s)\nabla\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)=0\) at every state. On the finite state space any finite baseline is integrable.

  2. Bound the gradient error caused by truncating at horizon \(H\).

    Hint and solution

    The proof gives \[\|\nabla J-\nabla J_H\|\le LR_{\max}\gamma^H\left[\frac{H+1}{1-\gamma}+\frac{\gamma}{(1-\gamma)^2}\right].\] Obtain the sum by writing \(t=H+j\) and using \(\sum_jj\gamma^j=\gamma/(1-\gamma)^2\).
