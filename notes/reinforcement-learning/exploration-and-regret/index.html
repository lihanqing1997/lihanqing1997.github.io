Extend optimism from arms to finite-horizon MDPs and keep statistical, planning, and trajectory errors visible in the regret calculation.

Episodic regret

This chapter uses an undiscounted horizon \(H\), stages \(h=1,\ldots,H\), \(S\) states, \(A\) actions, rewards in \([0,1]\), and stage-dependent kernels \(P_h\). Episodes start at a fixed state \(s_1\); \(K\) episodes contain \(T=KH\) transitions. Before episode \(k\), the learner chooses a policy \(\pi_k\) using previous data. Its pseudo-regret is \[ \operatorname{Reg}_K=\sum_{k=1}^K [V_1^*(s_1)-V_1^{\pi_k}(s_1)]. \] Each summand compares expected total episode returns, not an optimal value with a single immediate reward. Exploration is difficult because actions determine which future states become observable.

A concrete confidence event

Index a model row by \(x=(h,s,a)\), so there are \(D=HSA\) rows. The \(n\) outcomes of any fixed row can be represented by the first \(n\) draws of its independent environment stream. Let \(\widehat r_{x,n}\), \(\widehat P_{x,n}\) be their means. For confidence \(\delta\in(0,1)\), put \[ L=\log\frac{2D(S+1)K}{\delta},\quad u_n=\sqrt{\frac{L}{2n}}\ (n\ge1),\quad \beta_r(n)=\min(1,u_n),\quad \beta_P(n)=\min(2,Su_n). \] For \(n=0\), choose any empirical probability vector and mean in \([0,1]\), and set \(\beta_r(0)=1,\beta_P(0)=2\). Applying Hoeffding to the reward and each next-state indicator and union-bounding over rows and \(1\le n\le K\) proves an event \(\mathcal E\) of probability at least \(1-\delta\) on which \[ |\widehat r_{x,n}-r_x|\le\beta_r(n),\qquad \|\widehat P_{x,n}-P_x\|_1\le\beta_P(n) \] for all counts. The coordinate union gives \(Su_n\); the deterministic distance between two probability vectors is at most two. This construction is conservative but valid under adaptive visitation.

Optimistic backward induction

At the start of episode \(k\), freeze the row counts \(N_{k,h}(s,a)\) and empirical model. Write \[ b_{k,h}(s,a)=\beta_r(N_{k,h}(s,a)) +(H-h)\beta_P(N_{k,h}(s,a)). \] Starting with \(\widehat V_{k,H+1}=0\), define \[ \widehat Q_{k,h}(s,a)=\min\{H-h+1,\, \widehat r_{k,h}(s,a)+\widehat P_{k,h}\widehat V_{k,h+1}(s,a) +b_{k,h}(s,a)\},\quad \widehat V_{k,h}=\max_a\widehat Q_{k,h}, \] and let \(\pi_{k,h}\) choose a maximizing action. All these values lie in \([0,H-h+1]\).

Proof. On \(\mathcal E\), for every vector \(v\in[0,H-h]^S\), including a vector chosen from the same data, \[ |(\widehat r-r)+(\widehat P-P)v| \le\beta_r+(H-h)\beta_P=b_{k,h}. \] Assume \(\widehat V_{k,h+1}\ge V_{h+1}^*\). Positivity of \(\widehat P\) gives \[ \widehat r+\widehat P\widehat V_{k,h+1}+b \ge\widehat r+\widehat PV_{h+1}^*+b \ge r+PV_{h+1}^*=Q_h^*. \] Since \(Q_h^*\le H-h+1\), clipping preserves this inequality. Maximizing and inducting from \(H+1\) proves \(\widehat V_{k,h}\ge V_h^*\). The bound is uniform over \(v\), so no unjustified independence between the fitted value and fitted transition appears.

The pathwise regret decomposition

Proposition (bonus plus martingale bound). With probability at least \(1-2\delta\), \[ \operatorname{Reg}_K \le 2\sum_{k,h}b_{k,h}(S_{k,h},A_{k,h}) +H\sqrt{2KH\log(1/\delta)}. \]

Proof. Fix the pre-episode data and put \(\Delta_{k,h}(s)=\widehat V_{k,h}(s)-V_h^{\pi_k}(s)\). On \(\mathcal E\), \(0\le\Delta_{k,h}\le H-h+1\). At the chosen action define \[ e_{k,h}(s)=\widehat V_{k,h}(s) -r_h(s,\pi_{k,h}(s))-P_h\widehat V_{k,h+1}(s,\pi_{k,h}(s)). \] The clipped update is no larger than its unclipped expression, so \(e_{k,h}\le2b_{k,h}\) on \(\mathcal E\). Subtracting the policy Bellman equation yields \[ \Delta_{k,h}(S_{k,h}) =e_{k,h}(S_{k,h})+\Delta_{k,h+1}(S_{k,h+1})+M_{k,h}, \] where \[ M_{k,h}=P_h\Delta_{k,h+1}(S_{k,h},A_{k,h}) -\Delta_{k,h+1}(S_{k,h+1}). \] Conditional on all data up to the chosen action, \(\mathbb E[M_{k,h}\mid\mathcal F_{k,h}]=0\). Even outside \(\mathcal E\), \(\Delta_{k,h+1}\) lies in \([-(H-h),H-h]\), since both values are in \([0,H-h]\). For a bound valid without conditioning on \(\mathcal E\), use the conditional range length \(2(H-h)\): the centered variable has that same range length. Conditional Hoeffding's lemma gives \[ \mathbb E[e^{\lambda M_{k,h}}\mid\mathcal F_{k,h}] \le e^{\lambda^2 H^2/2}. \] Iterating this bound and applying Markov's inequality with \(\lambda=\sqrt{2\log(1/\delta)/(KH^3)}\) gives \(\sum_{k,h}M_{k,h}\le H\sqrt{2KH\log(1/\delta)}\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\). This martingale statement is unconditional; intersect it with \(\mathcal E\). Finally sum the recurrence over \(h\), use \(\Delta_{k,H+1}=0\), and note \(V_1^*-V_1^{\pi_k}\le\Delta_{k,1}\). Summing episodes proves the result.

For a visible rate, let \(C=1+2HS\). Since \(L>\log2\), the above bonuses satisfy \(b_{k,h}\le C\sqrt{2L/\max(1,N_{k,h})}\), including at count zero. A stage-specific row is visited at most once in an episode. If its final count is \(n_x\), then \[ \sum_{j=0}^{n_x-1}\frac1{\sqrt{\max(1,j)}}\le1+2\sqrt{n_x}. \] Consequently, Cauchy–Schwarz and \(\sum_xn_x=KH\) give \[ \sum_{k,h}b_{k,h}(S_{k,h},A_{k,h}) \le C\sqrt{2L}\,[HSA+2\sqrt{HSA\cdot KH}]. \] This proves sublinear regret in \(K\) for fixed \(H,S,A\). It is deliberately not a sharp minimax bound: coordinatewise transition confidence loses state factors, and a range bound loses variance information. Variance-aware UCBVI and its lower-bound comparisons refine exactly these steps.

Worked example (why immediate optimism is insufficient). At stage one, action “safe” gives reward \(1/2\) and terminates. Action “test” gives zero and reaches a second-stage state with an unknown reward in \([0,1]\). A bonus applied only to the immediate reward of “test” can miss the uncertainty of its successor value. Backward optimism propagates the second-stage upper confidence value into \(\widehat Q_1(\text{test})=0+\widehat V_2\), allowing exploration when that value exceeds \(1/2\).

Exercises

  1. Where would the count argument fail if one used a single stage-independent row count frozen for the whole episode?

    Hint and solution

    The same row could be visited up to \(H\) times before its count is updated. The sequence of denominators is then not simply \(0,1,\ldots,n_x-1\). One needs an additional within-episode count argument or a delayed-update bound. Stage-specific rows avoid this issue here.

  2. Why not condition all martingale calculations on the confidence event \(\mathcal E\)?

    Hint and solution

    The event depends on future observations. Conditioning on it can destroy the zero conditional mean of \(M_{k,h}\). Prove the concentration statement under the original law, then intersect the two high-probability events by a union bound.
