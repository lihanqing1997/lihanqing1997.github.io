Average-reward control describes continuing systems without geometric discounting. The loss of a discount contraction makes recurrence, invariant measures, and Poisson equations central. It is a genuinely different regime, not a notational limit obtained by putting \(\gamma=1\).

Gain and bias

For a stationary policy \(\pi\), define its gain \[ g^\pi(s) =\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac1T E_s^\pi\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}r(S_t,A_t). \] If the policy chain is irreducible positive recurrent with invariant law \(d_\pi\), then the ergodic theorem gives a state-independent gain \[ g^\pi=\sum_sd_\pi(s)r_\pi(s). \] The bias or differential value is \[ h^\pi(s) =E_s^\pi\sum_{t=0}^{\tau-1}\{r_\pi(S_t)-g^\pi\}, \] for a return time \(\tau\) to a reference state, when integrable.

Theorem (Poisson equation). In a finite irreducible Markov chain, \[ g^\pi\mathbf1+h^\pi=r_\pi+P_\pi h^\pi. \] The gain is unique and \(h^\pi\) is unique up to an additive constant.

Proof. First-step analysis of the centered reward until the reference return gives the equation. If \((g,h)\) and \((\tilde g,\tilde h)\) solve it, multiply by the invariant row vector \(d_\pi^\top\) to get \(g=\tilde g=d_\pi^\top r_\pi\). Their difference satisfies \(u=P_\pi u\); irreducibility makes every harmonic function constant.

Average-cost optimality equation

Under finite unichain assumptions, an optimal gain \(g^*\) and bias \(h^*\) satisfy \[ g^*+h^*(s) =\max_a\left\{r(s,a)+\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)h^*(s')\right\}. \] A stationary policy selecting maximizing actions is gain optimal. Unlike discounted Bellman equations, adding a constant to \(h^*\) changes nothing, and the optimality operator is nonexpansive only in span seminorm.

Relative value iteration

Fix a reference state \(s_0\) and iterate \[ \widetilde h_{k+1}=Th_k,\qquad h_{k+1}=\widetilde h_{k+1} -\widetilde h_{k+1}(s_0)\mathbf1. \] Under suitable communicating or aperiodicity conditions, \(h_k\) converges modulo constants and the subtracted scalar converges to \(g^*\). Periodicity can cause oscillation, requiring damping or modified iterations.

Vanishing-discount connection

Theorem (vanishing discount, finite unichain form). Along \(\gamma\uparrow1\), \[ (1-\gamma)V_\gamma^*(s)\to g^*, \] and, after normalization at \(s_0\), \[ V_\gamma^*(s)-V_\gamma^*(s_0)\to h^*(s) \] along convergent subsequences; under stronger assumptions the limit is unique.

The divergent component \(g^*/(1-\gamma)\) is common across states, while relative values retain transient differences. This decomposition explains both the connection and the numerical ill-conditioning of highly discounted problems.

Learning the average reward

Differential TD estimates both gain and bias: \[ \delta_t=R_{t+1}-\widehat g_t+h_w(S_{t+1})-h_w(S_t), \] \[ \widehat g_{t+1}=\widehat g_t+\beta_t\delta_t,\qquad w_{t+1}=w_t+\alpha_t\delta_t\nabla_wh_w(S_t). \] Valid convergence requires ergodicity and an identifiability normalization for the bias. Mixing time or diameter replaces \((1-\gamma)^{-1}\) as the effective horizon in concentration and regret bounds.

Multichain complications

If a policy has multiple recurrent classes, gain may depend on the initial state and a single scalar average-cost equation may fail. Transient choices can select which recurrent class is reached. Unichain assumptions are therefore structural, not technical shorthand.
