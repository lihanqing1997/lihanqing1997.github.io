Prove contraction convergence with its error bounds before using it to establish optimal control.

A complete proof of the contraction theorem

Theorem (Banach). Let \((\mathcal X,d)\) be a nonempty complete metric space and \(F:\mathcal X\to\mathcal X\) satisfy \(d(Fx,Fy)\le\gamma d(x,y)\), \(0\le\gamma<1\). Then \(F\) has a unique fixed point \(x^*\), and for \(x_n=F^nx_0\), \[ d(x_n,x^*)\le\gamma^nd(x_0,x^*),\qquad d(x_n,x^*)\le\frac{\gamma^n}{1-\gamma}d(x_1,x_0). \]

Proof. Induction gives \(d(x_{k+1},x_k)\le\gamma^kd(x_1,x_0)\). For any \(m>n\), \[ d(x_m,x_n)\le\sum_{k=n}^{m-1}d(x_{k+1},x_k) \le\frac{\gamma^n}{1-\gamma}d(x_1,x_0). \] The upper bound tends to zero uniformly in \(m>n\), so \((x_n)\) is Cauchy. Completeness supplies \(x_n\to x^*\). Rather than assuming the limit is fixed, calculate \[ d(Fx^*,x^*)\le d(Fx^*,Fx_n)+d(x_{n+1},x^*) \le\gamma d(x^*,x_n)+d(x_{n+1},x^*)\longrightarrow0. \] Thus \(Fx^*=x^*\). If \(Fy=y\), \[ d(y,x^*)=d(Fy,Fx^*)\le\gamma d(y,x^*), \quad(1-\gamma)d(y,x^*)\le0, \] which forces \(y=x^*\). Repeated contraction against \(x^*\) gives the first rate. Letting \(m\to\infty\) in the Cauchy bound gives the second. When \(\gamma=0\), the map is constant and all iterates after the first equal \(x^*\).

Why Bellman operators contract

In \(\mathbb R^{|\mathsf S|}\) with sup norm, define \[ (TV)(s)=\max_{a\in\mathsf A(s)}\left[r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)V(s')\right]. \] This is a map on a complete finite-dimensional space. At this stage, its fixed point is simply a vector; the next chapter identifies it with the optimal return.

Proposition. Both \(T\) and \(T^\pi\) are monotone \(\gamma\)-contractions. They satisfy \(T(V+c\mathbf1)=TV+\gamma c\mathbf1\), and similarly for \(T^\pi\).

Proof. For finite vectors \((f_a),(g_a)\), choose \(a_f\in\arg\max_a f_a\). Then \[ \max_a f_a-\max_a g_a=f_{a_f}-\max_a g_a \le f_{a_f}-g_{a_f}\le\max_a|f_a-g_a|. \] Exchange \(f\) and \(g\) to obtain the absolute-value inequality. Apply it to Bellman scores: \[ \begin{aligned} |TV(s)-TW(s)| &\le\gamma\max_a\left|\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)(V-W)(s')\right|\\ &\le\gamma\max_a\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\|V-W\|_\infty =\gamma\|V-W\|_\infty. \end{aligned} \] For \(T^\pi\), replace the maximum by averaging over \(\pi\), which yields the same bound. If \(V\le W\), every conditional average and every action score for \(V\) is at most its counterpart for \(W\); this proves monotonicity. Finally, \[ \sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)(V(s')+c)=P_aV(s)+c \] because each transition row sums to one, proving the translation identity.

Residuals and perturbed iterations

Proposition (residual certificate). If \(F\) is a \(\gamma\)-contraction with fixed point \(x^*\), then \[ d(x,x^*)\le\frac{d(x,Fx)}{1-\gamma}. \] If \(\widetilde F\tilde x=\tilde x\), then \[ d(x^*,\tilde x)\le\frac{d(F\tilde x,\widetilde F\tilde x)}{1-\gamma}. \]

Proof. The triangle inequality and contraction yield \[ d(x,x^*)\le d(x,Fx)+d(Fx,Fx^*) \le d(x,Fx)+\gamma d(x,x^*). \] Therefore \((1-\gamma)d(x,x^*)\le d(x,Fx)\). For the second statement substitute \(x=\tilde x\) and use \[ d(\tilde x,F\tilde x)=d(\widetilde F\tilde x,F\tilde x). \] No contraction assumption on \(\widetilde F\) is needed once its fixed point is supplied.

Proposition (accumulated error). If \(d(x_{k+1},Fx_k)\le\epsilon_k\), then \[ d(x_n,x^*)\le\gamma^nd(x_0,x^*)+ \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\gamma^{n-1-j}\epsilon_j. \] In particular, \(\epsilon_j\le\epsilon\) gives \(\limsup_n d(x_n,x^*)\le\epsilon/(1-\gamma)\); if \(\epsilon_j\to0\), then \(x_n\to x^*\).

Proof. Set \(e_k=d(x_k,x^*)\). Then \[ e_{k+1}\le d(x_{k+1},Fx_k)+d(Fx_k,Fx^*)\le\epsilon_k+\gamma e_k. \] Substitution, or induction on \(n\), gives the displayed finite convolution. A uniform \(\epsilon\) bounds it by \(\epsilon(1-\gamma^n)/(1-\gamma)\). For vanishing errors, fix \(\eta>0\) and choose \(N\) with \(\epsilon_j\le\eta\) for \(j\ge N\). Split \[ \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\gamma^{n-1-j}\epsilon_j =\underbrace{\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\gamma^{n-1-j}\epsilon_j}_{\longrightarrow0} +\sum_{j=N}^{n-1}\gamma^{n-1-j}\epsilon_j \le o(1)+\frac\eta{1-\gamma}. \] First let \(n\to\infty\) with \(N\) fixed, and then \(\eta\downarrow0\). This order of limits proves \(e_n\to0\). For \(\gamma=0\), the direct inequality \(e_{k+1}\le\epsilon_k\) suffices.

Worked example (a computable error certificate). For \(F(x)=1+0.8x\), the fixed point is \(5\). At \(x=4.9\), \[ |F(x)-x|=|4.92-4.9|=0.02,\qquad \frac{0.02}{1-0.8}=0.1=|x-5|. \] The horizon factor is sharp. A Bellman residual of \(0.02\) cannot be read as a value error of \(0.02\).

Exercises

  1. Give a nonexpansive map with no fixed point, and another with many fixed points.

    Hint and solution

    On \(\mathbb R\), \(F(x)=x+1\) has none and \(F(x)=x\) has every point fixed. Both have Lipschitz constant one. The strict inequality \(\gamma<1\) is essential.

  2. If every backup has error at most \(10^{-3}\), compare the guaranteed asymptotic value error at discounts \(0.9\) and \(0.99\).

    Hint and solution

    The bounds are \(0.01\) and \(0.1\). A longer effective horizon amplifies persistent local error; it does not merely require more iterations.
