Prove policy improvement and finite termination, and define partial policy evaluation without conflicting conventions.

The exact improvement identity

Given \(\pi\), let \(g=T^{\pi'}V^\pi-V^\pi\). It is the advantage of changing the first action distribution while using \(V^\pi\) as continuation value.

Theorem (policy improvement). \[ V^{\pi'}-V^\pi=(I-\gamma P_{\pi'})^{-1}g =\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^tP_{\pi'}^tg. \] Thus \(g\ge0\) implies \(V^{\pi'}\ge V^\pi\). Under \(g\ge0\), improvement at \(s\) is strict if \(g(x)>0\) and \(\gamma^tP_{\pi'}^t(s,x)>0\) for some \(t,x\).

Proof. Subtract \(V^\pi\) from the Bellman equation for \(\pi'\): \[ \begin{aligned} V^{\pi'}-V^\pi &=r_{\pi'}+\gamma P_{\pi'}V^{\pi'}-V^\pi\\ &=g+\gamma P_{\pi'}(V^{\pi'}-V^\pi). \end{aligned} \] Apply the convergent resolvent. If \(g\ge0\), all terms are nonnegative, and \[ (V^{\pi'}-V^\pi)(s)\ge\gamma^tP_{\pi'}^t(s,x)g(x)>0 \] under the stated condition. At \(\gamma=0\), only immediate improvement matters.

An algorithm that terminates

Start with a deterministic stationary policy. Evaluate \(V^{\pi_k}\) exactly and choose \(\pi_{k+1}\) greedily. Keep \(\pi_k(s)\) whenever it already maximizes the score; otherwise select a maximizer by a fixed ordering. Stop when the policy is unchanged.

Theorem (finite termination). This algorithm terminates after finitely many policy changes, at an optimal policy.

Proof. Greediness gives \[ g_k=TV^{\pi_k}-V^{\pi_k} =T^{\pi_{k+1}}V^{\pi_k}-T^{\pi_k}V^{\pi_k}\ge0. \] A changed action was not previously maximizing, by the tie rule, so \(g_k(s)>0\) at that state. The improvement identity yields \[ V^{\pi_{k+1}}-V^{\pi_k} =\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^tP_{\pi_{k+1}}^tg_k\ge g_k. \] Every change therefore improves some component strictly and decreases none. An earlier policy cannot recur, because its value vector would recur, contradicting monotone strict improvement. Only \[ N_{\mathrm{pol}}=\prod_s|\mathsf A(s)| \] deterministic policies exist. When no change occurs, \[ TV^{\pi_k}=T^{\pi_k}V^{\pi_k}=V^{\pi_k}. \] Uniqueness of the optimal fixed point proves \(V^{\pi_k}=V^*\). Without the tie rule, policies can keep alternating between equal optimal actions.

Modified policy iteration

Our convention is \[ \pi_{k+1}\in\operatorname{Greedy}(V_k),\qquad V_{k+1}=(T^{\pi_{k+1}})^{m_k}V_k,\quad m_k\ge1. \] For \(m_k=1\), \(V_{k+1}=T^{\pi_{k+1}}V_k=TV_k\), exactly value iteration. Holding the chosen policy fixed while increasing the number of sweeps gives \[ \|(T^{\pi_{k+1}})^{m}V_k-V^{\pi_{k+1}}\|_\infty \le\gamma^{m}\|V_k-V^{\pi_{k+1}}\|_\infty\longrightarrow0. \] Thus exact evaluation is the infinite-sweep limit. Setting \(m_k=0\) would leave the value unchanged. Approximate-greedy error bounds will be developed later.

The Newton calculation on one greedy region

Where a single policy \(\pi\) is greedy, \(F(V)=V-TV=(I-\gamma P_\pi)V-r_\pi\). Its Jacobian is invertible and the Newton update is \[ V-(I-\gamma P_\pi)^{-1}F(V) =(I-\gamma P_\pi)^{-1}r_\pi=V^\pi. \] This explains the relation to Newton methods on affine pieces. Across action ties, ordinary smooth differentiability need not hold.

Worked example (one strict improvement). At state \(s\), stay gives reward \(1\) and remains at \(s\); go gives reward \(0\) and moves to absorbing \(g\), with reward \(2\). For \(\gamma=0.9\), the stay policy has value \((10,20)\). Its scores are \[ Q^\pi(s,\mathrm{stay})=1+0.9(10)=10,\quad Q^\pi(s,\mathrm{go})=0.9(20)=18. \] Switching to go gives \((18,20)\). Its stay score is \(17.2<18\), so the next policy stays unchanged and is optimal.

Exercises

  1. Show that equal consecutive values imply optimality even without the tie rule.

    Hint and solution

    If \(V^{\pi'}=V^\pi\) and \(\pi'\) is greedy, then \(TV^\pi=T^{\pi'}V^\pi=T^{\pi'}V^{\pi'}=V^{\pi'}=V^\pi\). Thus \(V^\pi=V^*\), though the recorded policies might still alternate.

  2. Compute \(g\) and the resolvent improvement in the example.

    Hint and solution

    \(g=(8,0)^\top\). Under the new policy \(P_{\pi'}g=0\), since \(s\) immediately moves to \(g\). Thus the resolvent sum equals \((8,0)^\top\), precisely the value improvement.
