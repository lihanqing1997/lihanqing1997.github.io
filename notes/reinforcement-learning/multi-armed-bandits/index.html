Learning goal. Prove an adaptive-sampling UCB bound and understand why a change of measure forces logarithmic exploration.

Reward streams and the regret convention

Definition 18.1 (Stochastic bandit and pseudo-regret).

There are \(K\) arms. Arm \(a\) has an iid stream \(X_{a,1},X_{a,2},\ldots\in[0,1]\), with mean \(\mu_a\); all streams are independent. At round \(t\), the algorithm chooses \(A_t\) from past observations and fresh internal randomness, then observes the next unused sample from that arm. Write \[ N_a(T)=\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf1_{\{A_t=a\}},\quad \mu_*=\max_a\mu_a,\quad \Delta_a=\mu_*-\mu_a. \] The expected pseudo-regret is \[ \mathcal R_T=\mathbb E\sum_{t=1}^T(\mu_*-\mu_{A_t}) =\sum_a\Delta_a\,\mathbb E N_a(T). \] It also equals \(T\mu_*-\mathbb E\sum_t X_t\), by conditional expectation. The realized reward difference fluctuates and is not the same random variable as the pseudo-regret sum.

UCB with a valid adaptive-count argument

Pull every arm once. For \(t>K\), let \(n_a=N_a(t-1)\) and choose a maximizing arm for \[ U_a(t)=\widehat\mu_{a,n_a}+\sqrt{\frac{2\log t}{n_a}}, \qquad \widehat\mu_{a,n}=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^nX_{a,j}. \] The external concentration input is Hoeffding's inequality for a fixed sample count: \(\Pr(\widehat\mu_{a,n}-\mu_a\ge x)\le e^{-2nx^2}\), and the same for the lower tail. It cannot be applied by pretending the adaptively selected \(n_a\) is deterministic.

Theorem 18.2 (logarithmic UCB bound). For \(T\ge K\) and each arm with \(\Delta_a>0\), \[ \mathbb E N_a(T)\le \left\lceil\frac{8\log T}{\Delta_a^2}\right\rceil+4, \quad \mathcal R_T\le \sum_{a:\Delta_a>0}\Delta_a \left(\left\lceil\frac{8\log T}{\Delta_a^2}\right\rceil+4\right). \]

Proof. Fix an optimal arm \(a_*\). At round \(t\), define the event \(E_t\) that for every integer \(1\le n\le t\), \[ \widehat\mu_{a,n}\le\mu_a+\sqrt{2\log t/n}, \qquad \widehat\mu_{a_*,n}\ge\mu_*-\sqrt{2\log t/n}. \] A union bound over the fixed-count inequalities gives \[ \Pr(E_t^c)\le 2t\exp(-4\log t)=2t^{-3}. \] This event controls the eventual random counts without conditioning on them. Put \(L=\lceil8\log T/\Delta_a^2\rceil+1\). If \(n_a\ge L\), \(t\le T\), and \(E_t\) holds, then \[ U_a(t)\le\mu_a+2\sqrt{2\log t/n_a} <\mu_a+\Delta_a=\mu_*\le U_{a_*}(t). \] Thus arm \(a\) cannot be selected. Pathwise, \[ N_a(T)\le L+\sum_{t=K+1}^T\mathbf1_{E_t^c}. \] Taking expectations and using \(\sum_{t=1}^\infty t^{-3}\le1+\int_1^\infty x^{-3}\,\mathrm dx=3/2\) proves \(\mathbb EN_a(T)\le L+3\). Sum over arms to obtain regret.

The gap-dependent bound becomes large when gaps are small. Splitting arms at a threshold \(\delta>0\) gives \[ \mathcal R_T\le T\delta+\sum_{\Delta_a>\delta} (8\log T/\Delta_a+5\Delta_a) \le T\delta+\frac{8K\log T}{\delta}+5K. \] Choosing \(\delta=\sqrt{8K\log T/T}\) when it is at most one yields \(\mathcal R_T\le2\sqrt{8KT\log T}+5K\). This simple proof has a logarithmic overhead relative to sharp minimax results.

Why suboptimal arms must be sampled

For Bernoulli means in \((0,1)\), define \(\operatorname{kl}(p,q)=p\log(p/q)+(1-p)\log[(1-p)/(1-q)]\). Call an algorithm uniformly efficient if its expected regret is \(o(T^\alpha)\) for every \(\alpha>0\) on every such fixed instance.

Theorem 18.3 (Bernoulli information lower bound). On an instance with a unique best arm and \(0<\mu_a<\mu_*<1\), \[ \liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb E N_a(T)}{\log T} \ge\frac1{\operatorname{kl}(\mu_a,\mu_*)}. \]

Proof. Change only arm \(a\)'s mean to \(q>\mu_*\); denote the original and changed history laws by \(P_T,Q_T\). The algorithm's conditional action probabilities are the same functions of history under both laws, so they cancel in the likelihood ratio. The chain rule, conditioning on the chosen action at every round, gives \[ \mathrm{KL}(P_T\|Q_T) =\mathbb E_{P_T}\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf1_{\{A_t=a\}} \log\frac{p_{\mu_a}(X_t)}{p_q(X_t)} =\mathbb E_{P_T}N_a(T)\operatorname{kl}(\mu_a,q). \] Let \(E_T=\{N_a(T)<T/2\}\), \(p_T=P_T(E_T)\), \(q_T=Q_T(E_T)\). For any \(0<\alpha<1\), efficiency and the positive gaps imply \[ P_T(E_T^c)\le2\mathbb E_PN_a(T)/T=o(T^{\alpha-1}), \quad q_T\le2\mathbb E_Q[T-N_a(T)]/T=o(T^{\alpha-1}). \] Thus \(p_T\to1\). The log-sum inequality applied separately on \(E_T\) and its complement gives \[ \mathrm{KL}(P_T\|Q_T)\ge\operatorname{kl}(p_T,q_T) \ge p_T\log(1/q_T)-\log2. \] The last inequality uses binary entropy at most \(\log2\) and the nonnegative term \(-(1-p_T)\log(1-q_T)\). Since eventually \(q_T\le T^{\alpha-1}\), \[ \liminf_T\frac{\mathbb E_PN_a(T)\operatorname{kl}(\mu_a,q)}{\log T} \ge1-\alpha. \] First let \(\alpha\downarrow0\) for fixed \(q\); then let \(q\downarrow\mu_*\), using continuity of the Bernoulli divergence. This order of limits proves the result.

Thompson sampling chooses an arm by drawing a model from a posterior and acting optimally for that draw. Its analysis is different from the confidence-event proof above. Contextual bandits add observed covariates before the action; adversarial bandits allow a non-stochastic reward sequence and compare against a fixed comparator. Their assumptions and regret guarantees should not be interchanged.

Worked example 18.4 (posterior and confidence calculations). For a Bernoulli arm with a \(\mathrm{Beta}(1,1)\) prior, one success and two failures give \[ p(\mu\mid X)\propto \mu(1-\mu)^2,\qquad \mu\mid X\sim\mathrm{Beta}(2,3),\qquad \mathbb E[\mu\mid X]=2/5. \] This posterior mean differs from the empirical mean \(1/3\). A Thompson step draws from this posterior; a UCB step uses \(1/3+\sqrt{2\log t/3}\), which can exceed one without invalidating optimism.

Exercises

  1. Exercise 18.1. Explain why conditioning on \(N_a(t-1)=n\) does not justify a fixed-\(n\) Hoeffding bound.

    Hint

    The event specifying a pull count depends on earlier rewards; compare it with a union over fixed counts.

    Solution

    That event depends on earlier rewards. Conditional on it, the first \(n\) samples need not retain their original iid distribution. Instead, bound each fixed-count deviation under the original stream law and take a union over all possible counts, as in the proof.

  2. Exercise 18.2. For means \(0.4,0.6\), express the leading information lower bound on regret.

    Hint

    Evaluate the Bernoulli divergence, then multiply the sample lower bound by the suboptimality gap.

    Solution

    Only the first arm is suboptimal, with gap \(0.2\). Therefore \(\liminf_T\mathcal R_T/\log T\ge0.2/\operatorname{kl}(0.4,0.6)\), where \(\operatorname{kl}(0.4,0.6)=0.2\log(3/2)\); the ratio equals \(1/\log(3/2)\approx2.466\).
