Relate state values, action values, advantages, and discounted occupancy measures using one consistent normalization.

Starting a process at a specified state

In the finite discounted model of the previous chapter, \(\mathbb E_s^\pi\) denotes the process initialized at \(S_0=s\), even if \(\rho(s)=0\). For a stationary policy \(\pi\), define \[ V^\pi(s)=\mathbb E_s^\pi\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^tR_{t+1},\qquad Q^\pi(s,a)=\mathbb E_{s,a}^\pi\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^tR_{t+1}. \] In \(\mathbb E_{s,a}^\pi\), the first action is forced to be \(a\); subsequent actions follow \(\pi\). This definition covers actions with \(\pi(a\mid s)=0\), where an ordinary conditional expectation would not specify a unique value. Write \[ A^\pi(s,a)=Q^\pi(s,a)-V^\pi(s),\qquad J_\rho(\pi)=\sum_s\rho(s)V^\pi(s). \]

Proposition (one-step identities). \[ Q^\pi(s,a)=r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)V^\pi(s'),\qquad V^\pi(s)=\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)Q^\pi(s,a). \] In particular, \(\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)A^\pi(s,a)=0\).

Proof. For an \(n\)-step return after the forced action, conditioning on the first transition gives \[ \mathbb E_{s,a}^\pi G^{(n+1)} =r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\mathbb E_{s'}^\pi G^{(n)}. \] The error in each truncated expectation is bounded uniformly by \(R_{\max}\gamma^n/(1-\gamma)\). Thus the finite sum over states converges term by term and gives the first identity. Conditioning on the first action similarly yields \[ \mathbb E_s^\pi G^{(n)} =\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)\mathbb E_{s,a}^\pi G^{(n)} \longrightarrow\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)Q^\pi(s,a). \] Finally, \[ \sum_a\pi(a\mid s)A^\pi(s,a) =V^\pi(s)-V^\pi(s)\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)=0. \]

Discounted occupancy and the flow equation

For any policy, including a history-dependent one, define the normalized state-action occupancy and its state marginal by \[ d_\rho^\pi(s,a)=(1-\gamma)\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^t\mathbb P_\rho^\pi(S_t=s,A_t=a), \qquad d_{\rho,S}^\pi(s)=\sum_a d_\rho^\pi(s,a). \] All terms are nonnegative, so summing them in either order gives \(\sum_{s,a}d_\rho^\pi(s,a)=1\). This notation always reserves \(d_\rho^\pi\) for state-action mass and \(d_{\rho,S}^\pi\) for state mass.

Proposition (occupancy identities). For every bounded \(f\), \[ \mathbb E_\rho^\pi\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^tf(S_t,A_t) =\frac{\langle d_\rho^\pi,f\rangle}{1-\gamma}, \] and \[ d_{\rho,S}^\pi(s')=(1-\gamma)\rho(s') +\gamma\sum_{s,a}d_\rho^\pi(s,a)P(s'\mid s,a). \] For stationary \(\pi\), \(d_\rho^\pi(s,a)=d_{\rho,S}^\pi(s)\pi(a\mid s)\).

Proof. The series of absolute expected values is bounded by \(\|f\|_\infty/(1-\gamma)\). Therefore \[ \begin{aligned} \langle d_\rho^\pi,f\rangle &=(1-\gamma)\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^t \sum_{s,a}f(s,a)\mathbb P(S_t=s,A_t=a)\\ &=(1-\gamma)\mathbb E\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^tf(S_t,A_t). \end{aligned} \] For the flow equation, isolate time zero and reindex a nonnegative series: \[ \begin{aligned} d_{\rho,S}^\pi(s')-(1-\gamma)\rho(s') &=(1-\gamma)\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\gamma^t\mathbb P(S_t=s')\\ &=\gamma(1-\gamma)\sum_{u\ge0}\gamma^u \sum_{s,a}\mathbb P(S_u=s,A_u=a)P(s'\mid s,a)\\ &=\gamma\sum_{s,a}d_\rho^\pi(s,a)P(s'\mid s,a). \end{aligned} \] Under stationarity, substitute \(\mathbb P(S_t=s,A_t=a)=\mathbb P(S_t=s)\pi(a\mid s)\) into the defining sum to obtain the factorization.

Which arrays are realizable occupancies?

Theorem (finite occupancy polytope). An array \(d\ge0\) is the occupancy of a stationary randomized policy if and only if its state marginal \(d_S\) satisfies the flow equations above. A realizing policy is \(\pi(a\mid s)=d(s,a)/d_S(s)\) whenever \(d_S(s)>0\).

Proof. Necessity was proved above. Conversely, summing the assumed flow equations gives \[ \sum_{s,a}d(s,a)=(1-\gamma)+\gamma\sum_{s,a}d(s,a), \quad\text{hence }\sum_{s,a}d(s,a)=1. \] Define \(\pi\) by the ratio and choose it arbitrarily on zero-mass states. Then \(d(s,a)=d_S(s)\pi(a\mid s)\) and, using row vectors, \[ d_S=(1-\gamma)\rho+\gamma d_SP_\pi. \] The actual occupancy marginal \(\tilde d_S\) for this policy satisfies the same identity. Set \(z=d_S-\tilde d_S\). Since \(P_\pi\) is stochastic, \[ \begin{aligned} \|z\|_1&=\gamma\|zP_\pi\|_1\\ &\le\gamma\sum_{s'}\sum_s|z(s)|P_\pi(s,s') =\gamma\|z\|_1. \end{aligned} \] Because \(\gamma<1\), \(z=0\); hence \(d=d_\rho^\pi\). In particular, any history-dependent policy's occupancy can be realized by a stationary randomized one for the fixed initial law \(\rho\).

A precise comparison between policies

Theorem (performance difference). For stationary policies \(\pi,\pi'\), \[ J_\rho(\pi')-J_\rho(\pi) =\frac1{1-\gamma}\sum_{s,a}d_\rho^{\pi'}(s,a)A^\pi(s,a). \]

Proof. Under the trajectory law of \(\pi'\), the one-step identity gives \[ \mathbb E A^\pi(S_t,A_t) =\mathbb E[R_{t+1}+\gamma V^\pi(S_{t+1})-V^\pi(S_t)]. \] Multiply by \(\gamma^t\) and sum only to \(n-1\): \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{t=0}^{n-1}\gamma^t\mathbb E A^\pi(S_t,A_t) &=\mathbb E G^{(n)}-\mathbb E V^\pi(S_0) +\gamma^n\mathbb E V^\pi(S_n). \end{aligned} \] Here \(|\gamma^n\mathbb E V^\pi(S_n)|\le\gamma^nR_{\max}/(1-\gamma)\to0\), and \(\mathbb E G^{(n)}\to J_\rho(\pi')\). Also \(|A^\pi|\le2R_{\max}/(1-\gamma)\), so the advantage series is absolutely integrable. Pass to the limit and use the occupancy identity to obtain the formula.

Worked example (advantage and repeated improvement). In one state, let actions \(a,b\) have rewards \(0,1\), both returning to that state, and let \(\pi(b)=p\). Then \[ V^\pi=\frac p{1-\gamma},\quad Q^\pi(a)=\frac{\gamma p}{1-\gamma},\quad Q^\pi(b)=1+\frac{\gamma p}{1-\gamma}. \] Thus \(A^\pi(a)=-p\), \(A^\pi(b)=1-p\). For \(\pi'(b)=q\), \[ \mathbb E_{d^{\pi'}}A^\pi=(1-q)(-p)+q(1-p)=q-p, \quad J(\pi')-J(\pi)=\frac{q-p}{1-\gamma}. \] The advantage measures one-step improvement; occupancy and the horizon factor accumulate its repeated effect.

Exercises

  1. In the example, take \(p=1/4,q=3/4,\gamma=1/2\). Compute both values and the performance difference.

    Hint and solution

    \(V^\pi=1/2\), \(V^{\pi'}=3/2\), and the difference is \(1\). The occupancy advantage is \(q-p=1/2\), divided by \(1-\gamma=1/2\).

  2. Show directly from the flow equations that \(\langle d,r\rangle/(1-\gamma)\) is a linear objective over a compact polytope.

    Hint and solution

    The constraints are finitely many linear equalities and inequalities. Summing flow shows \(\sum d=1\), so the feasible set is closed and bounded, hence compact. Nonemptiness follows from any stationary policy. The objective is linear, so its maximum is attained. Bellman duality is established in the optimality chapter.
