Learning goal. Derive the discounted Riccati equation, prove the infinite-horizon limit under a stated stability assumption, and differentiate a stabilizing policy's cost.

Quadratic cost and discounted stability

Here we minimize cost. Let \(x_t\in\mathbb R^d\), \(u_t\in\mathbb R^m\), \[ x_{t+1}=Ax_t+Bu_t,\qquad J(x_0;u)=\sum_{t=0}^\infty\gamma^t(x_t^\top Qx_t+u_t^\top Ru_t), \] where \(0<\gamma<1\), \(Q\succ0\), \(R\succ0\), and all matrices are real with compatible dimensions. A feedback \(u=-Kx\) is discount-stable if the spectral radius \(\rho(\sqrt\gamma(A-BK))<1\). Assume at least one such \(K_0\) exists. This is the stability hypothesis used below; undiscounted stability \(\rho(A-BK)<1\) is stronger.

Complete the square before taking a limit

Proposition 24.1 (one Riccati step). If the continuation cost is \(x^\top Px\), \(P\succeq0\), then \[ \begin{aligned} \min_u\{x^\top Qx+u^\top Ru+\gamma(Ax+Bu)^\top P(Ax+Bu)\} &=x^\top\mathcal R(P)x,\\ K(P)&=(R+\gamma B^\top PB)^{-1}\gamma B^\top PA,\\ \mathcal R(P)&=Q+\gamma A^\top PA- \gamma^2A^\top PB(R+\gamma B^\top PB)^{-1}B^\top PA. \end{aligned} \] The unique minimizing control is \(u=-K(P)x\).

Proof. Set \(M=R+\gamma B^\top PB\succ0\), \(N=\gamma B^\top PA\). Expand the objective: \[ x^\top(Q+\gamma A^\top PA)x+2u^\top Nx+u^\top Mu. \] Since \[ u^\top Mu+2u^\top Nx =(u+M^{-1}Nx)^\top M(u+M^{-1}Nx)-x^\top N^\top M^{-1}Nx, \] the nonnegative square is uniquely minimized at \(u=-M^{-1}Nx\). Substitution yields the formula, including both discount factors in the final \(\gamma^2\) term.

Existence, convergence, and verification

Theorem 24.2 (infinite-horizon discounted LQR). Under the assumptions above, \(P_0=0,\ P_{n+1}=\mathcal R(P_n)\) converges to a positive definite matrix \(P_*\) solving \(P_*=\mathcal R(P_*)\). The feedback \(K_*=K(P_*)\) is discount-stable and has optimal cost \(x^\top P_*x\) among all control sequences.

Proof. The square-completion result and backward induction show that \(x^\top P_nx\) is the optimal \(n\)-step cost with zero terminal cost. Nonnegative stage costs give \(P_n\preceq P_{n+1}\). Write \(F_0=A-BK_0\), \(C_0=Q+K_0^\top RK_0\). Discount stability implies \[ P_{K_0}=\sum_{t=0}^\infty (\sqrt\gamma F_0)^{t\top}C_0(\sqrt\gamma F_0)^t \] converges in matrix norm: for any number \(r\) strictly between the spectral radius and one, finite-dimensional Jordan form gives \(\|(\sqrt\gamma F_0)^t\|\le Cr^t\) after increasing \(C\). The feasible feedback \(K_0\) bounds every finite-horizon optimum: \[ 0\preceq P_n\preceq P_{K_0}. \] For each vector \(x\), \(x^\top P_nx\) is monotone and bounded. Taking \(x=e_i\) and \(x=e_i+e_j\) shows every matrix entry converges, hence \(P_n\to P_*\) in norm. Since \(R+\gamma B^\top P_nB\succeq R\succ0\), matrix inversion is continuous along this sequence. Passing to the limit in the finite Riccati formula yields \(P_*=\mathcal R(P_*)\), and \(P_*\succeq P_1=Q\succ0\).

Let \(F_*=A-BK_*\). The minimizing-square identity gives \[ P_*=Q+K_*^\top RK_*+\gamma F_*^\top P_*F_*. \] For any complex eigenvector \(v\ne0\) of \(F_*\) with eigenvalue \(\lambda\), \[ (1-\gamma|\lambda|^2)v^*P_*v =v^*(Q+K_*^\top RK_*)v>0. \] Thus \(\gamma|\lambda|^2<1\) for every eigenvalue, proving discount stability. Iterating the identity along \(x_t=F_*^tx_0\) gives \[ x_0^\top P_*x_0 =\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}\gamma^t x_t^\top(Q+K_*^\top RK_*)x_t +\gamma^n x_n^\top P_*x_n. \] The terminal term is \(((\sqrt\gamma F_*)^nx_0)^\top P_*((\sqrt\gamma F_*)^nx_0)\to0\). Therefore the feedback cost equals \(x_0^\top P_*x_0\). Any other control sequence has \(n\)-step cost at least \(x_0^\top P_nx_0\); taking \(n\to\infty\), using monotonicity of its nonnegative partial costs and \(P_n\to P_*\), proves its total cost is no smaller. No unproved terminal-limit assumption is imposed on competing controls.

Policy evaluation and the policy gradient

For any discount-stable \(K\), put \(F=A-BK\), \(C=Q+K^\top RK\). Then \[ P_K=C+\gamma F^\top P_KF =\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^tF^{t\top}CF^t. \] Let \(S_0=\mathbb E[x_0x_0^\top]\) be finite, and define the unnormalized discounted state second moment \[ \Sigma_K=\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^tF^tS_0F^{t\top} =S_0+\gamma F\Sigma_KF^\top,\qquad J(K)=\operatorname{tr}(P_KS_0). \]

Proof. Discount-stable matrices form an open set. On it, the linear map \(\mathcal A_K(X)=X-\gamma F^\top XF\) is invertible by its norm-convergent series inverse, so \(P_K\) is differentiable in \(K\). Differentiate its finite Lyapunov equation, using \(\mathrm dF=-B\,\mathrm dK\): \[ \mathrm dP_K-\gamma F^\top(\mathrm dP_K)F =(\mathrm dK)^\top E_K+E_K^\top\mathrm dK,\quad E_K=(R+\gamma B^\top P_KB)K-\gamma B^\top P_KA. \] Multiply by \(\Sigma_K\), take traces, and use cyclicity and \(\Sigma_K-\gamma F\Sigma_KF^\top=S_0\): \[ \mathrm dJ =\operatorname{tr}[(\mathrm dP_K)S_0] =2\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_KE_K^\top\mathrm dK), \qquad \nabla_KJ=2E_K\Sigma_K. \] If \(S_0\succ0\), then \(\Sigma_K\succeq S_0\succ0\); a stationary point satisfies \(E_K=0\), so \(K=K(P_K)\). Its Lyapunov equation is then the Riccati equation. Completing the square gives, for every control, \[ x^\top Qx+u^\top Ru+\gamma(Ax+Bu)^\top P_K(Ax+Bu)-x^\top P_Kx =(u+Kx)^\top(R+\gamma B^\top P_KB)(u+Kx)\ge0. \] For any competing control of finite cost, \(Q\succ0\) implies \(\sum_t\gamma^t\|x_t\|^2<\infty\), hence \(\gamma^n x_n^\top P_Kx_n\to0\). Summing the inequality and taking this limit proves that control's cost is at least \(x_0^\top P_Kx_0\). Infinite-cost controls cannot improve it. Thus a discount-stable stationary point is globally optimal under \(S_0\succ0\). This does not by itself prove that an arbitrary gradient step preserves stability.

With additive independent zero-mean noise \(\xi_t\) of covariance \(W\), the optimal feedback is unchanged for this fully observed quadratic problem. Substituting \(V(x)=x^\top P_*x+c\) gives \[ \begin{aligned} &\mathbb E[(Ax+Bu+\xi)^\top P_*(Ax+Bu+\xi)]\\ &\qquad=(Ax+Bu)^\top P_*(Ax+Bu)+\operatorname{tr}(P_*W). \end{aligned} \] The constant solves \(c=\gamma c+\gamma\operatorname{tr}(P_*W)\), hence \(c=\gamma\operatorname{tr}(P_*W)/(1-\gamma)\). Partially observed linear-Gaussian control additionally uses a Kalman filter; its separation principle is an external control-theory result, not a consequence of square completion alone.

Worked example 24.3 (a scalar discounted Riccati equation). Take \(A=B=Q=R=1\), \(\gamma=1/2\). The equation becomes \[ P=1+\frac{P}{2}-\frac{P^2/4}{1+P/2} =1+\frac{P}{2+P}, \quad P^2=2. \] The positive solution is \(P_*=\sqrt2\), with \[ K_*=\frac{P_*}{2+P_*}=\sqrt2-1,\qquad F_*=2-\sqrt2,\qquad \sqrt\gamma|F_*|<1. \] The first finite-horizon matrices are \(P_0=0,P_1=1,P_2=4/3,P_3=7/5\), increasing to \(\sqrt2\).

Exercises

  1. Exercise 24.1. Give a scalar system that is discount-stable but not stable without discounting.

    Hint

    Choose a scalar closed-loop factor larger than one whose discounted square is smaller than one.

    Solution

    Let \(F=1.1\), \(\gamma=1/2\). Then \(|F|>1\), but \(\sqrt\gamma|F|\approx0.778<1\); the discounted quadratic series converges although the unscaled state grows.

  2. Exercise 24.2. In the worked example, with scalar noise variance \(W=1\), compute the additional optimal cost.

    Hint

    Insert the noise covariance and Riccati solution into the constant term of the value.

    Solution

    The constant is \(\gamma P_*W/(1-\gamma)=\sqrt2\). Thus \(V(x)=\sqrt2 x^2+\sqrt2\).
