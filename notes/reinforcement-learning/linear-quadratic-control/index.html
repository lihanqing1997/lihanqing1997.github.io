Linear-quadratic control is the central exactly solvable continuous-state control problem. It exposes Riccati equations, stability, certainty equivalence, and the relation between dynamic programming and policy optimization without hiding them inside a nonlinear function approximator.

Finite-horizon linear-quadratic regulation

Consider deterministic dynamics and cost \[ x_{t+1}=Ax_t+Bu_t, \] \[ J=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \left(x_t^\top Qx_t+u_t^\top Ru_t\right) +x_T^\top Q_Tx_T, \] where \(Q,Q_T\succeq0\) and \(R\succ0\).

Theorem (Riccati recursion). The optimal value is \(V_t(x)=x^\top P_tx\), where \[ P_T=Q_T, \] \[ P_t =Q+A^\top P_{t+1}A -A^\top P_{t+1}B (R+B^\top P_{t+1}B)^{-1} B^\top P_{t+1}A. \] The optimal action is linear, \[ u_t^*=-K_tx_t,\qquad K_t=(R+B^\top P_{t+1}B)^{-1}B^\top P_{t+1}A. \]

Proof. Assume \(V_{t+1}(x)=x^\top P_{t+1}x\). The Bellman objective is \[ x^\top Qx+u^\top Ru+(Ax+Bu)^\top P_{t+1}(Ax+Bu). \] Its Hessian in \(u\) is \(2(R+B^\top P_{t+1}B)\succ0\). Completing the square gives the minimizing \(u=-K_tx\); substituting it leaves the displayed quadratic form \(P_t\). Backward induction starts from \(P_T=Q_T\).

Infinite-horizon algebraic Riccati equation

For discounted or undiscounted stabilizing control, the stationary matrix \(P\) solves the discrete algebraic Riccati equation \[ P=Q+A^\top PA -A^\top PB(R+B^\top PB)^{-1}B^\top PA. \] Under stabilizability of \((A,B)\) and detectability of \((Q^{1/2},A)\), there is a unique stabilizing solution \(P\succeq0\); the closed-loop matrix \(A-BK\) has spectral radius below one. These hypotheses cannot be omitted: finite one-step cost does not imply that an unstable system can be controlled.

Gaussian noise and certainty equivalence

Proposition. If \[ X_{t+1}=AX_t+BU_t+W_{t+1}, \qquad E(W_{t+1})=0,\quad \operatorname{Cov}(W_{t+1})=\Sigma_W, \] then the finite-horizon optimal feedback gains \(K_t\) are the same as in the deterministic problem. Noise adds only constants to the value: \[ V_t(x)=x^\top P_tx+c_t,\qquad c_t=c_{t+1}+\operatorname{tr}(P_{t+1}\Sigma_W). \]

Proof. Conditional expectation of the next quadratic value is \[ E[(Ax+Bu+W)^\top P_{t+1}(Ax+Bu+W)] =(Ax+Bu)^\top P_{t+1}(Ax+Bu) +\operatorname{tr}(P_{t+1}\Sigma_W). \] The added term is independent of \(u\), so completion of the square gives the same gain.

Partial observation and the separation principle

For linear observations \[ Y_t=CX_t+V_t \] with Gaussian noises, the Kalman filter computes \(\widehat X_t=E(X_t\mid Y_{0:t},U_{0:t-1})\). The optimal LQG controller is \[ U_t=-K_t\widehat X_t. \] Estimation and control Riccati equations separate because conditional covariance evolves independently of the realized controls. This separation is special; generic POMDPs do not permit an independently optimal filter and fully observed controller.

Policy optimization landscape

For a stabilizing linear policy \(u=-Kx\), its infinite-horizon cost is \[ J(K)=\operatorname{tr}(P_K\Sigma_0), \] where \[ P_K=Q+K^\top RK+(A-BK)^\top P_K(A-BK). \] On the open set of stabilizing gains, every stationary point is globally optimal under standard controllability conditions, and policy-gradient methods can converge linearly with suitable step sizes. The stability domain is nonconvex, however, and a step leaving it makes the cost infinite. Safe initialization and stability-preserving updates are substantive assumptions.

Beyond quadratic structure

Continuous-action actor--critic methods approximate the same two objects: a value or action-value function and a policy-improvement step. LQR shows what is lost in the nonlinear case: exact quadratic closure, analytic minimization, global stability certificates, and a finite-dimensional Riccati recursion. Locally linear models and iterative LQR recover these structures only along a nominal trajectory.
