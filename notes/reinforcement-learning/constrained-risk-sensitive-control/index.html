Learning goal. Distinguish expectation constraints, tail risk, and robustness; derive their finite-dimensional identities without mixing cost and reward signs.

Expected constraints and occupancy measures

Return to the finite discounted reward-maximization problem. Let \(d(s,a)\) be normalized occupancy from \(\rho\), and let \(\mathcal D\) be the flow polytope proved in the policies chapter: \[ d\ge0,\qquad \sum_a d(s,a)=(1-\gamma)\rho(s)+ \gamma\sum_{x,a}d(x,a)P(s\mid x,a). \] For cost functions \(c_i\) and normalized budgets \(b_i\), the constrained problem is \[ \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\langle r,d\rangle \quad\text{subject to }\langle c_i,d\rangle\le b_i. \] Dividing objective and budgets by \(1-\gamma\) converts to unnormalized discounted returns. Every feasible occupancy is realized by a stationary randomized policy; deterministic policies need not suffice under constraints.

For \(\lambda_i\ge0\), set \[ L(d,\lambda)=\langle r,d\rangle-\sum_i\lambda_i(\langle c_i,d\rangle-b_i), \quad g(\lambda)=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}L(d,\lambda). \] For feasible \(d\), \(L(d,\lambda)\ge\langle r,d\rangle\), so \(g(\lambda)\) is an upper bound on the constrained optimum. External theorem input: finite-dimensional linear-programming strong duality gives equality with \(\min_{\lambda\ge0}g(\lambda)\) when the displayed finite LP is feasible (its objective is bounded by compactness). This is an LP theorem, not a claim of strong duality for arbitrary neural-policy parameterizations.

Proof. At a primal-dual optimal pair \(d_*,\lambda_*\), equality of the primal and dual values gives \[ 0=g(\lambda_*)-\langle r,d_*\rangle \ge L(d_*,\lambda_*)-\langle r,d_*\rangle =\sum_i\lambda_{*,i}(b_i-\langle c_i,d_*\rangle)\ge0. \] All summands are nonnegative; hence \(\lambda_{*,i}(b_i-\langle c_i,d_*\rangle)=0\) for every \(i\). This proves complementary slackness. For any maximizing \(d_\lambda\), \[ g(\lambda')\ge L(d_\lambda,\lambda') =g(\lambda)+\sum_i(\lambda_i'-\lambda_i) (b_i-\langle c_i,d_\lambda\rangle), \] so \(b_i-\langle c_i,d_\lambda\rangle\) is a subgradient of \(g\). Projected descent therefore increases the multiplier when a cost exceeds its budget: \(\lambda_i^+=[\lambda_i+\eta(\langle c_i,d_\lambda\rangle-b_i)]_+\).

Entropic risk: the sign of the parameter matters

Now let \(C\) denote a cost on a finite outcome space with probabilities \(p_i>0\). For \(\eta\ne0\), define \(\mathcal R_\eta(C)=\eta^{-1}\log\sum_i p_i e^{\eta C_i}\). Positive \(\eta\) penalizes upper-tail cost; negative \(\eta\) has the opposite tilt.

Proposition 25.1 (entropic variational identity). \[ \mathcal R_\eta(C)= \begin{cases} \displaystyle\sup_{q\in\Delta}\left\{\mathbb E_qC- \eta^{-1}\mathrm{KL}(q\|p)\right\},&\eta>0,\\[3pt] \displaystyle\inf_{q\in\Delta}\left\{\mathbb E_qC- \eta^{-1}\mathrm{KL}(q\|p)\right\},&\eta<0. \end{cases} \]

Proof. Let \(Z=\sum_i p_i e^{\eta C_i}\) and \(q_i^*=p_i e^{\eta C_i}/Z\). For every \(q\), \[ \mathrm{KL}(q\|q^*)=\mathrm{KL}(q\|p)-\eta\mathbb E_qC+\log Z, \] hence \[ \mathbb E_qC-\eta^{-1}\mathrm{KL}(q\|p) =\mathcal R_\eta(C)-\eta^{-1}\mathrm{KL}(q\|q^*). \] Nonnegativity of KL proves the supremum for \(\eta>0\) and the infimum for \(\eta<0\), both attained at \(q^*\). Using a supremum for both signs would reverse the second conclusion.

For bounded cost, the log moment-generating function is three times continuously differentiable near zero, with derivatives obtained from a finite sum (or dominated differentiation for a bounded random variable). Taylor's theorem gives \[ \mathcal R_\eta(C)=\mathbb EC+\frac\eta2\operatorname{Var}(C)+O(\eta^2), \qquad \eta\to0. \] Thus \(\mathcal R_0(C)\) is defined continuously as \(\mathbb EC\). For a discounted total cost, the effective future exponential parameter becomes \(\eta\gamma^t\); a stationary recursion with an unchanged parameter generally represents a different, nested risk criterion. Time consistency must be specified rather than assumed.

CVaR and a finite dual representation

For upper-tail cost and \(0<\alpha<1\), define \[ \operatorname{CVaR}_\alpha(C) =\min_{v\in\mathbb R}\left\{v+\frac1{1-\alpha}\mathbb E(C-v)_+\right\}. \] The convex objective has subgradients between \(1-\Pr(C\ge v)/(1-\alpha)\) and \(1-\Pr(C>v)/(1-\alpha)\). A minimizing \(v\) therefore satisfies \(\Pr(C>v)\le1-\alpha\le\Pr(C\ge v)\), the quantile condition.

Proof. For any random weight \(Z\) with \(0\le Z\le1/(1-\alpha)\) and \(\mathbb EZ=1\), \[ \mathbb E[ZC]=v+\mathbb E[Z(C-v)] \le v+\frac1{1-\alpha}\mathbb E(C-v)_+. \] Choose a minimizing quantile \(v_*\). Set \(Z=1/(1-\alpha)\) on \(C>v_*\), \(Z=0\) on \(C<v_*\), and choose a constant on \(C=v_*\) to make \(\mathbb EZ=1\). The quantile inequalities ensure this constant lies in \([0,1/(1-\alpha)]\); if the atom has zero mass, no adjustment is needed. Every displayed inequality is then an equality. Hence \[ \operatorname{CVaR}_\alpha(C) =\max_{\substack{0\le Z\le1/(1-\alpha)\\\mathbb EZ=1}}\mathbb E[ZC]. \] This proof includes atoms at the quantile, where “average above the quantile” without a partial-atom convention is ambiguous.

Rectangular robust control

For cost minimization with an adversarial transition row in a nonempty compact set \(\mathcal P_{s,a}\), define \[ (T_{\rm rob}V)(s)=\min_a\{c(s,a)+ \gamma\max_{p\in\mathcal P_{s,a}}p^\top V\}. \] For all probability vectors \(p\), \(|p^\top(V-W)|\le\|V-W\|_\infty\); taking maxima and then minima preserves this bound. Thus \(T_{\rm rob}\) is a \(\gamma\)-contraction. Its sequential game interpretation assumes rectangularity: nature can choose each admissible row independently at each visited state-action pair. A single unknown model with coupled row restrictions is a different uncertainty model and need not obey this Bellman recursion.

Worked example 25.2 (randomization and tail cost). One state has actions \(a,b\) with rewards \(0,1\) and costs \(0,2\). Under normalized budget \(b_1=1\), choosing \(b\) with probability \(q\) requires \(2q\le1\), so the optimal constrained policy uses \(q=1/2\). Neither deterministic policy attains this feasible reward. Separately, a cost that is \(10\) with probability \(0.1\) and \(0\) otherwise has mean \(1\), but \(\operatorname{CVaR}_{0.9}=10\) and \(\mathcal R_\eta=\eta^{-1}\log(0.9+0.1e^{10\eta})\). An expectation budget and a tail-risk budget constrain different behavior.

Exercises

  1. Exercise 25.1. Compute the entropic-risk derivative at \(\eta=0\) for the worked cost distribution.

    Hint

    Compute the cost variance and use the local expansion of entropic risk.

    Solution

    Its second moment is \(10\), mean is \(1\), and variance is \(9\). The Taylor coefficient is \(\operatorname{Var}(C)/2=4.5\).

  2. Exercise 25.2. Find a multiplier certifying the constrained one-state optimum.

    Hint

    Write the two actionwise Lagrangian values and find where their upper envelope is minimized.

    Solution

    The dual is \(g(\lambda)=\lambda+\max\{0,1-2\lambda\}\), \(\lambda\ge0\). It decreases to \(1/2\) on \([0,1/2]\) and increases afterward, so \(\lambda_*=1/2\), matching primal reward \(q=1/2\).
