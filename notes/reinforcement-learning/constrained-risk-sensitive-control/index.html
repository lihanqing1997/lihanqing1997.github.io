Expected return is only one preference over trajectories. Safety budgets, tail losses, robustness, and nonlinear utility produce distinct control problems. Their Bellman recursions remain valid only when the objective has an appropriate time-consistent state representation.

Constrained Markov decision processes

Let \(r\) be reward and \(c_j\) be costs. A discounted constrained problem is \[ \max_\pi J_r(\pi) \quad\text{subject to}\quad J_{c_j}(\pi)\le b_j,\quad j=1,\ldots,m. \] In normalized occupancy variables this is the linear program \[ \max_{d\in\mathcal D_\rho} \frac{\langle d,r\rangle}{1-\gamma} \quad\text{subject to}\quad \frac{\langle d,c_j\rangle}{1-\gamma}\le b_j. \] Randomized stationary policies may be necessary because an optimum can lie on a face between deterministic occupancy vertices.

Theorem (Lagrangian duality). In a finite discounted constrained MDP with a strictly feasible occupancy, strong duality holds: \[ \max_{\pi:\,J_c(\pi)\le b}J_r(\pi) =\min_{\lambda\ge0} \max_\pi\{J_r(\pi)-\lambda^\top(J_c(\pi)-b)\}. \]

Proof. The occupancy formulation is a finite-dimensional linear program. Slater feasibility prevents a duality gap. Introducing multipliers for cost constraints changes one-step reward to \(r_\lambda=r-\lambda^\top c\); maximization over feasible occupancies is the ordinary MDP for \(r_\lambda\). Linear-program duality yields the identity.

Primal--dual algorithms update a policy for \(r_\lambda\) and ascend in \(\lambda\) when costs exceed budgets. Finite-time safety is not guaranteed by asymptotic constraint satisfaction; safe exploration needs confidence sets, barrier policies, or robust invariant sets.

Entropic risk-sensitive control

Definition. For cumulative cost \(C\) and risk parameter \(\eta\ne0\), the entropic risk is \[ \rho_\eta(C)=\frac1\eta\log E e^{\eta C}. \] Positive \(\eta\) penalizes upper tails; as \(\eta\to0\), \[ \rho_\eta(C) =EC+\frac{\eta}{2}\operatorname{Var}(C)+O(\eta^2) \] when exponential moments exist.

For finite-horizon additive costs, exponentiating the value produces a multiplicative Bellman recursion: \[ Z_h(s) =\min_a E\left[ \exp\{\eta c_h(s,a,S')\}Z_{h+1}(S') \mid s,a \right], \qquad V_h=\eta^{-1}\log Z_h. \] This criterion is dynamically consistent because conditional entropic risk composes recursively.

Conditional value at risk

For a loss \(L\) and \(0<\alpha<1\), \[ \operatorname{CVaR}_\alpha(L) =\inf_{\tau\in\mathbb R} \left\{\tau+\frac1{1-\alpha}E(L-\tau)_+\right\}. \] The auxiliary threshold \(\tau\) turns tail risk into an expectation, but a static CVaR of total return is generally time inconsistent. Dynamic programming requires augmenting the state with a confidence or budget variable, or using a nested conditional risk measure.

Distributional robustness

If a transition law lies in an ambiguity set \(\mathcal U(s,a)\), robust control uses \[ (T_{\mathrm{rob}}V)(s) =\max_a\left\{ r(s,a)+\gamma \inf_{Q\in\mathcal U(s,a)}E_QV(S') \right\}. \] Rectangular ambiguity, where uncertainty factors independently across state-action pairs, yields a time-consistent Bellman equation and stationary robust policies. Coupled ambiguity sets can destroy this recursion because nature's choices across time are no longer separable.

Risk is not uncertainty

Risk-sensitive objectives alter preferences under a known law; robust control protects against model uncertainty; constrained control enforces expected budgets. Combining them is possible, but their guarantees answer different questions. A low-variance return does not certify safety, and a worst-case model need not represent a tail event under the nominal model.
