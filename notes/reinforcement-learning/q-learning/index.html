Derive off-policy Bellman targets, prove tabular Q-learning convergence, and distinguish maximization bias from sampling-policy bias.

Initialize the action-value table at a fixed finite vector.

The action-value optimality operator

For a vector on admissible state-action pairs, define \[ (\mathcal TQ)(s,a)=r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a)\max_bQ(s',b). \] Its unique fixed point is \(Q^*\). The maximum inequality proves \[ \begin{aligned} |(\mathcal TQ-\mathcal TU)(s,a)| &\le\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a) \left|\max_bQ(s',b)-\max_bU(s',b)\right|\\ &\le\gamma\|Q-U\|_\infty. \end{aligned} \] Thus the same stochastic-contraction theorem can act on state-action coordinates.

Behavior, information, and the update

Let \(\mathcal F_t\) contain the observed history, \(Q_t\), and the already chosen action \(A_t\). The behavior policy can depend on the past. The environment still samples from \(K(\cdot\mid S_t,A_t)\). Update only \((S_t,A_t)\): \[ Q_{t+1}(S_t,A_t) =(1-\alpha_t)Q_t(S_t,A_t) +\alpha_t\left[R_{t+1}+\gamma\max_bQ_t(S_{t+1},b)\right]. \] Since \(Q_t,S_t,A_t\) are measurable at time \(t\), \[ \mathbb E[R_{t+1}+\gamma\max_bQ_t(S_{t+1},b)\mid\mathcal F_t] =(\mathcal TQ_t)(S_t,A_t). \] No next-action likelihood ratio is needed: the next action is maximized inside the target, not sampled from the behavior policy.

Theorem (bounded tabular convergence). Assume a finite discounted MDP with \(|R_{t+1}|\le R_{\max}\); every state-action pair is sampled infinitely often almost surely; and the step on its \(k\)-th visit is a deterministic \(\alpha_{s,a,k}\in(0,1]\) with divergent sum and square-summable squares. Then \(Q_t\to Q^*\) almost surely.

Proof. Take \(B=\max\{\|Q_0\|_\infty,R_{\max}/(1-\gamma)\}\). If \(\|Q_t\|_\infty\le B\), then \[ \left|R_{t+1}+\gamma\max_bQ_t(S_{t+1},b)\right| \le R_{\max}+\gamma B\le B. \] Every updated coordinate is a convex combination of two numbers in \([-B,B]\). Hence \(\|Q_t\|_\infty\le B\) by induction. Define \[ M_{t+1}=R_{t+1}+\gamma\max_bQ_t(S_{t+1},b)-(\mathcal TQ_t)(S_t,A_t). \] The conditional-mean identity implies \(\mathbb E[M_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]=0\). Both target and its conditional mean lie in \([-B,B]\), so \[ |M_{t+1}|\le2B,\qquad \mathbb E[M_{t+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_t]\le4B^2. \] Apply the stochastic-contraction theorem from TD with \(F=\mathcal T\), selected coordinate \((S_t,A_t)\), and zero noise at unselected coordinates. Boundedness, noise moments, contraction, local steps, and infinite coordinate coverage have all been checked.

The theorem says what follows from infinite coverage; it does not supply an exploration algorithm. A behavior rule that stops visiting a relevant action invalidates the result, even if its global step-size sum diverges. Bounded-variance unbounded rewards can also be treated, but then boundedness of the iterates requires an additional argument not covered by this proof.

SARSA and expected SARSA

For a fixed policy \(\pi\), SARSA replaces the maximum by \(Q_t(S_{t+1},A_{t+1})\), where the next action is drawn from \(\pi\). Expected SARSA uses its conditional mean: \[ Y_t^{\mathrm{ES}}=R_{t+1}+\gamma\sum_b\pi(b\mid S_{t+1})Q_t(S_{t+1},b). \] With fixed \(\pi\), the mean operator is \(T_Q^\pi\), whose fixed point is \(Q^\pi\). Control with an evolving policy additionally requires control of that evolving mean operator. Greedy-in-the-limit with infinite exploration is a condition on both approximation to greediness and visitation; neither condition follows from the other.

Proposition (conditional variance for expected SARSA). For a fixed \(Q_t\) and policy, replacing the sampled next action by its conditional average preserves the mean target and cannot increase its conditional variance.

Proof. Let \(\mathcal G_t=\sigma(\mathcal F_t,R_{t+1},S_{t+1})\), and let \(Y_t=R_{t+1}+\gamma Q_t(S_{t+1},A_{t+1})\). Then \[ \mathbb E[Y_t\mid\mathcal G_t]=Y_t^{\mathrm{ES}}. \] The tower property preserves the mean conditional on \(\mathcal F_t\). The conditional variance decomposition gives \[ \operatorname{Var}(Y_t\mid\mathcal F_t) =\operatorname{Var}(Y_t^{\mathrm{ES}}\mid\mathcal F_t) +\mathbb E[\operatorname{Var}(Y_t\mid\mathcal G_t)\mid\mathcal F_t] \ge\operatorname{Var}(Y_t^{\mathrm{ES}}\mid\mathcal F_t). \] This is a genuine conditional-expectation variance reduction with its conditioning fields specified.

Maximization bias and double estimation

Proposition. If \(\widehat q_a=q_a+\epsilon_a\) are integrable and \(\mathbb E\epsilon_a=0\), then \(\mathbb E\max_a\widehat q_a\ge\max_aq_a\).

Proof. Choose \(a^*\in\arg\max_aq_a\). Pointwise, \(\max_a\widehat q_a\ge\widehat q_{a^*}\), so \[ \mathbb E\max_a\widehat q_a\ge\mathbb E\widehat q_{a^*}=q_{a^*}=\max_aq_a. \] For an independent evaluation vector \(\widetilde q\) with \(\mathbb E\widetilde q_a=q_a\), and selector \(\hat a\) determined only by \(\widehat q\), \[ \mathbb E[\widetilde q_{\hat a}\mid\widehat q]=q_{\hat a}, \qquad \mathbb E\widetilde q_{\hat a}=\mathbb E q_{\hat a}\le\max_aq_a. \] Independent selection and evaluation remove this upward same-noise bias, but can introduce loss by selecting the wrong action. Online double-Q estimates are not generally independent, so this identity is an explanatory idealization, not a blanket finite-sample guarantee.

Worked example (two noisy tied actions). Let \(q_1=q_2=0\), and let the estimation errors be independent fair signs. Their maximum is \(-1\) with probability \(1/4\), and \(1\) with probability \(3/4\). Therefore \[ \mathbb E\max(\widehat q_1,\widehat q_2)=-\tfrac14+\tfrac34=\tfrac12. \] Evaluating the selected action with an independent fair-sign estimate gives mean zero. In a discounted target, the upward contribution from this example is \(\gamma/2\).

Exercises

  1. In a one-state two-action problem, can Q-learning identify both values if one action is never sampled?

    Hint and solution

    No. Its untouched Q entry retains its initialization and may dominate the max target. Two reward models agreeing on the sampled action but differing on the other generate identical data, yet can have different optimal policies.

  2. For fixed \(\pi\), bound the mean-target difference between the max backup and the expected-SARSA backup if \(\pi\) puts mass at least \(1-\epsilon\) on greedy actions and \(\|Q\|_\infty\le B\).

    Hint and solution

    At each state, \(0\le\max_bQ(s,b)-\sum_b\pi(b\mid s)Q(s,b)\le2B\epsilon\). Multiplying by \(\gamma\) and averaging over the transition gives a bound \(2\gamma B\epsilon\).
