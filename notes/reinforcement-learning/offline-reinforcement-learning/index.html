Offline reinforcement learning chooses a new policy from a fixed dataset, without further interaction. The statistical obstacle is distribution shift: the learned policy changes which state-action pairs matter, while Bellman targets can assign high values precisely where the dataset gives little information.

Data-generating regimes

Offline data may consist of iid transitions from a distribution \(\nu\), independent episodes from behavior policies, or one dependent trajectory. These regimes support different concentration tools. In each case, a target policy \(\pi\) induces a discounted occupancy \(d^\pi\), and a basic coverage measure is \[ C(\pi;\nu) =\left\|\frac{\mathrm{d} d^\pi}{\mathrm{d}\nu}\right\|_\infty. \] Uniform guarantees over a class \(\Pi\) require \(\sup_{\pi\in\Pi}C(\pi;\nu)<\infty\) or a weaker function-class analogue.

An impossibility without coverage

Theorem. If the dataset law assigns zero probability to a state-action pair that a candidate policy can reach with positive probability, no offline algorithm can uniformly identify that policy's value over unrestricted bounded-reward MDPs.

Proof. Construct two MDPs identical on the support of the data and different only in the reward or transition at the unobserved pair. Their offline-data distributions are exactly equal, so every algorithm has the same output law under both. Choose the unseen outcome so the candidate policy is optimal in one MDP and poor in the other. No estimator or policy selector can be uniformly correct in both experiments.

Pessimistic value selection

Let \(\widehat Q^\pi\) estimate \(Q^\pi\) with a policy-dependent confidence radius \(b^\pi(s,a)\). A lower confidence value is \[ \underline J(\pi) =\widehat J(\pi)-\operatorname{pen}(\pi), \] where the penalty grows with uncertainty under \(d^\pi\). Selecting \(\widehat\pi=\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\underline J(\pi)\) follows the principle “optimize only what the data can certify.”

Theorem (generic pessimism oracle inequality). Suppose simultaneously for every \(\pi\in\Pi\), \[ |\,\widehat J(\pi)-J(\pi)\,|\le\operatorname{pen}(\pi). \] Then \[ J(\pi^*)-J(\widehat\pi) \le2\operatorname{pen}(\pi^*) \] for every comparator \(\pi^*\in\Pi\).

Proof. The confidence event gives \[ J(\widehat\pi)\ge \widehat J(\widehat\pi)-\operatorname{pen}(\widehat\pi) \ge\widehat J(\pi^*)-\operatorname{pen}(\pi^*) \ge J(\pi^*)-2\operatorname{pen}(\pi^*). \]

The bound depends on uncertainty of the comparator, not the entire policy class. A strong policy is learnable if its own occupancy is covered, even when unsupported policies are heavily penalized.

Conservative value learning

Methods such as conservative Q-learning add a term that lowers values of actions favored by the current model but rare in data, schematically \[ \alpha\left[ E_{S\sim\nu_S}\log\sum_a e^{Q(S,a)} -E_{(S,A)\sim\nu}Q(S,A) \right]. \] The log-sum-exp searches for high unseen actions; the data term anchors observed actions. This is a surrogate for pessimism, not an automatic confidence bound. Its strength must be related to model class, sampling error, and coverage.

Behavior regularization and support constraints

An alternative restricts the learned policy through \[ E_{S\sim\nu_S} D_{\mathrm{KL}}\{\pi(\cdot\mid S)\|\widehat\mu(\cdot\mid S)\} \le\varepsilon. \] This controls action-level shift but not necessarily state-distribution shift: small local deviations can compound over a long horizon. Occupancy-ratio or concentrability analysis remains necessary.

Statistical inference after policy selection

Evaluating the same policy chosen to maximize noisy estimated value incurs selection bias. Sample splitting, cross-fitting, uniform confidence bands, or online validation can restore valid inference. Doubly robust scores reduce nuisance sensitivity, but their overlap conditions do not disappear in the offline setting.
