Learning goal. Prove a concrete impossibility without coverage and a policy-selection guarantee from simultaneous pessimistic bounds.

A fixed dataset changes the statistical question

Offline RL chooses a policy without further interaction. Specify whether the data are iid transitions, independent behavior episodes, or one dependent/adaptively collected trajectory: these experiments support different concentration arguments. For a transition sampling law \(\nu\) and normalized discounted occupancy \(d_\rho^\pi\), define \[ C(\pi;\nu)=\max_{(s,a):\,d_\rho^\pi(s,a)>0} \frac{d_\rho^\pi(s,a)}{\nu(s,a)}, \] with \(C=\infty\) if a denominator is zero. Coverage is a relation between the data and a policy, not a property of dataset size alone.

An indistinguishable pair of MDPs

Theorem 21.1 (a coverage obstruction). For any offline algorithm, there are two one-state discounted MDPs with identical dataset laws such that its expected suboptimality is at least \(1/[4(1-\gamma)]\) in one of them, regardless of the number of observations.

Proof. Both actions return to the only state. The behavior always chooses \(a\), whose reward is \(1/2\). In model \(M_0\), action \(b\) has reward \(0\); in \(M_1\), it has reward \(1\). All rewards are deterministic. Every dataset, of any size, consists only of observed action \(a\) and reward \(1/2\), so its law is identical in the two models. For a returned stationary policy, let \(q\in[0,1]\) be its probability of \(b\), including algorithmic randomness. The law of \(q\) is the same in both models. Direct evaluation gives \[ J_0^*-J_0(\pi)=\frac{q}{2(1-\gamma)},\qquad J_1^*-J_1(\pi)=\frac{1-q}{2(1-\gamma)}. \] Taking expectations and adding yields \(1/[2(1-\gamma)]\); their maximum is at least half that sum. For a returned time-dependent policy, replace \(q\) by its normalized discounted frequency of action \(b\); the same calculation applies if the policy cannot learn from new deployment rewards. Allowing such new information is an online/adaptive-deployment problem, outside the fixed-policy offline claim.

Pessimism and a comparator-specific guarantee

Suppose a policy class \(\Pi\) has simultaneous confidence bounds, on one event \(\mathcal E\) of probability at least \(1-\delta\): \[ |\widehat J(\pi)-J(\pi)|\le b(\pi)\qquad\text{for every }\pi\in\Pi. \] The functions and the selected policy may be data dependent; simultaneity is what makes selection valid. Put \(\underline J(\pi)=\widehat J(\pi)-b(\pi)\), and choose \(\widehat\pi\) satisfying \(\underline J(\widehat\pi)\ge\sup_{\pi\in\Pi}\underline J(\pi)-\eta\).

Proposition 21.2 (pessimistic policy selection). On \(\mathcal E\), for every comparator \(\pi\in\Pi\), \[ J(\pi)-J(\widehat\pi)\le2b(\pi)+\eta. \]

Proof. On the common confidence event, \[ \begin{aligned} J(\widehat\pi) &\ge\underline J(\widehat\pi) \ge\underline J(\pi)-\eta\\ &=\widehat J(\pi)-b(\pi)-\eta \ge J(\pi)-2b(\pi)-\eta. \end{aligned} \] Rearrange. The bound depends on the uncertainty of the chosen comparator, not directly on \(b(\widehat\pi)\). Simultaneous validity is still needed even if some policies have very large or vacuous bounds.

A model-based version uses a confidence set \(\mathcal M\) containing the true model and \(\underline J(\pi)=\inf_{M\in\mathcal M}J_M(\pi)\). The same proof replaces \(2b(\pi)\) by \(J(\pi)-\inf_{M\in\mathcal M}J_M(\pi)\). To make this algorithm concrete, one must specify a statistically valid model set and solve or approximate the resulting optimization; writing an infimum alone is not an efficient algorithm.

What function approximation must control

For finite state-action spaces and \(d=d_\rho^\pi\le C\nu\), every function \(f\) obeys \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E_d|f| &=\sum_{s,a}\nu(s,a)\sqrt{\frac{d(s,a)}{\nu(s,a)}}\, \sqrt{\frac{d(s,a)}{\nu(s,a)}}|f(s,a)|\\ &\le\sqrt{\mathbb E_\nu[d/\nu]}\, \sqrt{\mathbb E_\nu[(d/\nu)f^2]} \le\sqrt C\,\|f\|_{L^2(\nu)}. \end{aligned} \] Terms with \(\nu=0\) have \(d=0\) and contribute zero. This explains how an estimation error under the data law can become a policy-return error. Without coverage, even zero training error leaves unobserved values unrestricted.

Realizability asks whether the desired value belongs to the function class. Bellman completeness asks whether applying the relevant Bellman operator keeps functions in the class. They are different: a class containing just \(Q^*\) is realizable for control, but need not support policy-evaluation backups for every other policy. Fitted iteration proofs need the closure property they actually use. Conservative value penalties, behavior-constrained policy search, and implicit policy improvement are different responses to distribution shift; none abolishes the indistinguishability example without an additional assumption.

Worked example 21.3 (a pessimistic choice). Two candidate policies have estimates \(\widehat J(\pi_1)=8\), \(\widehat J(\pi_2)=10\), and radii \(b(\pi_1)=1\), \(b(\pi_2)=4\). Their lower bounds are \(7,6\), so pessimistic selection chooses \(\pi_1\). On the common confidence event, \[ J(\pi_2)-J(\pi_1)\le2b(\pi_2)=8. \] The guarantee is weak for the poorly covered comparator; a high estimated value is not a certificate of a useful policy.

Exercises

  1. Exercise 21.1. Under the same confidence event, derive a lower bound on improvement over a known baseline policy \(\pi_b\).

    Hint

    Compare a lower confidence value for the selected policy with an upper confidence value for the baseline.

    Solution

    \(J(\widehat\pi)-J(\pi_b)\ge\underline J(\widehat\pi)-[\widehat J(\pi_b)+b(\pi_b)]\). A positive right-hand side certifies improvement; merely maximizing a lower bound does not automatically certify improvement over the true baseline value.

  2. Exercise 21.2. If \(b(\pi)\le B\sqrt{C(\pi;\nu)/n}\), how large must \(n\) be for the theorem to give comparator loss at most \(\varepsilon\), with exact optimization?

    Hint

    Insert the proposed radius into the comparator guarantee and solve for the sample count.

    Solution

    It suffices that \(2B\sqrt{C(\pi;\nu)/n}\le\varepsilon\), or \(n\ge4B^2C(\pi;\nu)/\varepsilon^2\). This deduction is conditional on a valid simultaneous radius; it is not a derivation of that radius.
