Learning goal. Derive importance weighting and doubly robust evaluation, and identify precisely which independence and overlap conditions they need.

Finite-horizon change of measure

Definition 20.1 (Importance ratios and overlap).

Use stages \(t=0,\ldots,H-1\), discount \(0\le\gamma\le1\), target policy \(\pi_t\), and known behavior policy \(\mu_t\). Initial laws and environment kernels are identical. Assume overlap: \(\pi_t(a\mid s)>0\Rightarrow\mu_t(a\mid s)>0\) on target-reachable histories. Under behavior data define \[ \rho_t=\frac{\pi_t(A_t\mid S_t)}{\mu_t(A_t\mid S_t)},\quad W_t=\prod_{j=0}^t\rho_j,\quad W_{-1}=1. \] Policies may be history dependent if the ratios use those same histories. We display Markov notation for readability.

Proposition 20.2 (per-decision importance sampling). For integrable \(f_t\) measurable through \((S_t,A_t,R_{t+1},S_{t+1})\), \[ \mathbb E_\mu[W_tf_t]=\mathbb E_\pi[f_t],\qquad J_\pi=\mathbb E_\mu\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^tW_tR_{t+1}. \]

Proof. For a finite prefix, its probability/kernel product under \(\mu\) has factors \[ \rho_{\rm init}(s_0) \prod_{j=0}^{t}\mu_j(a_j\mid s_j) K_j(\mathrm dr_{j+1},s_{j+1}\mid s_j,a_j). \] Multiplying by \(W_t\) cancels each behavior action factor and replaces it by \(\pi_j(a_j\mid s_j)\). Overlap ensures that every target-positive path is represented. Summing states/actions and integrating rewards therefore yields the target expectation, first for nonnegative \(f_t\), then for integrable signed \(f_t\) by positive and negative parts. Taking \(f_t=R_{t+1}\) and summing the finite horizon proves the value identity. In particular \(\mathbb E_\mu W_t=1\).

For bounded rewards and \(\gamma<1\), the infinite-horizon extension is justified in \(L^1\), even without uniformly bounded ratios: \[ \sum_{t=0}^\infty\mathbb E_\mu|\gamma^tW_tR_{t+1}| \le\sum_{t=0}^\infty\gamma^tR_{\max}\mathbb E_\mu W_t =\frac{R_{\max}}{1-\gamma}<\infty. \] Finite variance is a separate requirement: \(\mathbb E W_t^2\) can grow exponentially. Self-normalizing weights may reduce instability but generally sacrifices finite-sample unbiasedness.

A telescoping doubly robust estimator

Choose fixed approximations \(\widehat Q_t\), set \(\widehat V_t(s)=\sum_a\pi_t(a\mid s)\widehat Q_t(s,a)\), and \(\widehat V_H=0\). Define \[ \widehat J_{\rm DR}=\widehat V_0(S_0)+ \sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t W_t [R_{t+1}+\gamma\widehat V_{t+1}(S_{t+1})-\widehat Q_t(S_t,A_t)]. \]

Proof. With true weights, the change-of-measure identity and action averaging give \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E_\mu\widehat J_{\rm DR} &=\mathbb E_\pi\widehat V_0(S_0)+ \sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t \mathbb E_\pi[R_{t+1}+\gamma\widehat V_{t+1}(S_{t+1}) -\widehat V_t(S_t)]\\ &=J_\pi+\gamma^H\mathbb E_\pi\widehat V_H(S_H)=J_\pi. \end{aligned} \] Every intermediate value term cancels with the adjacent time step. Alternatively, if \(\widehat Q_t=Q_t^\pi\), the bracket has conditional mean zero given the history through \(A_t\): \[ \mathbb E_\mu[R_{t+1}+\gamma V_{t+1}^\pi(S_{t+1}) -Q_t^\pi(S_t,A_t)\mid\mathcal F_t]=0. \] It remains zero after multiplication by any integrable \(\mathcal F_t\)-measurable weight (with the product integrable). Therefore exact \(Q^\pi\) yields unbiasedness even for misspecified predictable weights, provided the initial value is also exact. These are the two separate robustness statements. They do not imply unbiasedness when both components are wrong.

The product error in the one-step case

For a contextual bandit let \(q(s,a)=\mathbb E[R\mid s,a]\), and use a positive fitted propensity \(\widehat\mu\). With fixed fitted functions, the conditional DR bias at state \(s\) is exactly \[ \begin{aligned} &\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)\widehat q(s,a) +\sum_a\mu(a\mid s)\frac{\pi(a\mid s)}{\widehat\mu(a\mid s)} [q(s,a)-\widehat q(s,a)] -\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)q(s,a)\\ &=\sum_a\pi(a\mid s) \frac{\widehat\mu(a\mid s)-\mu(a\mid s)}{\widehat\mu(a\mid s)} [\widehat q(s,a)-q(s,a)]. \end{aligned} \] This algebra explains the product of nuisance errors. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its averaged magnitude by the product of the corresponding \(L^2\) norms, with the inverse propensity included in the first factor.

Cross-fitting trains nuisance functions on other independent episodes and evaluates each held-out episode with those fixed functions. Conditional on training data, the preceding identities apply. Splitting transitions from one dependent trajectory does not create independent folds. Likewise, selecting the target policy on evaluation data requires additional uniform or held-out analysis. A classical iid central limit theorem needs a finite second moment; weights having mean one alone do not ensure it.

Worked example 20.3 (rare behavior actions). In a one-step problem, the target always takes action \(b\), the behavior takes it with probability \(0.1\), and its deterministic reward is one. The importance-sampling estimate is \(10\) with probability \(0.1\) and \(0\) otherwise. Thus \[ \mathbb E\widehat J_{\rm IS}=1,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\widehat J_{\rm IS})=0.1(10)^2-1=9. \] For \(n\) independent episodes the mean has variance \(9/n\). With exact \(\widehat q(b)=1\), the DR correction is zero and its estimate is identically one.

Exercises

  1. Exercise 20.1. Derive the bias from clipping a nonnegative one-step weight \(W\) at \(c\), when \(|R|\le R_{\max}\).

    Hint

    Subtract the unclipped estimator and isolate the positive excess of the weight over the clipping threshold.

    Solution

    \(\mathbb E[\min(W,c)R]-J=-\mathbb E[(W-c)_+R]\). Hence the absolute bias is at most \(R_{\max}\mathbb E[(W-c)_+]\). It is downward for nonnegative rewards, but has no fixed sign for signed rewards.

  2. Exercise 20.2. If behavior takes the target action with probability \(p\) independently at every stage, what is \(\mathbb E W_{H-1}^2\) for a deterministic target action sequence?

    Hint

    List the probability and the weight of the sole action sequence that agrees with the target.

    Solution

    The sequence occurs with probability \(p^H\), and its weight is \(p^{-H}\); other paths have weight zero. Thus \(\mathbb EW_{H-1}^2=p^{-H}\), exposing exponential horizon dependence despite \(\mathbb EW_{H-1}=1\).

Sources and further reading
