Regularization restricts the effective size of the predictor chosen by empirical risk minimization. It can be written as a penalty, a constraint, or an algorithmic bias.

Penalized empirical risk

A penalty expresses a preference among predictors with similar empirical fit.

Definition. Given a penalty \(J(f)\ge0\), a penalized ERM solves \[ \hat f_\lambda\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{f\in\mathcal F} \left\{P_n\ell_f+\lambda J(f)\right\}. \]

Proposition. For every \(f\in\mathcal F\), \[ P_n\ell_{\hat f_\lambda}+\lambda J(\hat f_\lambda) \le P_n\ell_f+\lambda J(f). \] Consequently, after adding and subtracting population risks, the excess risk is controlled by empirical-process terms plus \(\lambda\{J(f)-J(\hat f_\lambda)\}\).

Proof. The first inequality is the definition of the minimizer. Subtract \(P\ell_{\hat f_\lambda}\) and \(P\ell_f\) on the two sides to express the population risk difference through \((P_n-P)(\ell_f-\ell_{\hat f_\lambda})\) and the penalty difference.

Strong convexity and stability

Strongly convex regularization can make the algorithm stable, and stability implies generalization.

Theorem. If the objective is \(\lambda\)-strongly convex and each loss is \(L\)-Lipschitz in the predictor norm, then changing one observation changes the regularized solution by \(O(L/(\lambda n))\) under standard boundedness assumptions.

Proof. Let \(\hat f\) and \(\hat f^{(i)}\) be solutions trained on samples differing in one point. Strong convexity gives quadratic growth of each objective away from its minimizer. Adding the two optimality inequalities cancels all common sample terms, leaving only the two changed losses. Lipschitzness bounds these by \(L\|\hat f-\hat f^{(i)}\|/n\), which yields \(\|\hat f-\hat f^{(i)}\|=O(L/(\lambda n))\).

Implicit regularization

Some algorithms regularize without an explicit penalty.

Remark. Gradient descent, early stopping, minimum-norm interpolation, and boosting can all select low-complexity predictors from a large class. The statistical question is then to identify the complexity measure actually controlled by the algorithm.

Lagrange multipliers and the regularization path

Theorem. Let \(L\) and \(J\) be proper closed convex functions on a finite-dimensional space. Suppose the constrained problem \[ \min_f L(f)\quad\text{subject to }J(f)\le t \] has a solution \(f_t\) and satisfies Slater's condition. Then there exists \(\lambda\ge0\) such that \(f_t\) minimizes \(L(f)+\lambda J(f)\). Conversely, every penalized minimizer solves the constrained problem with \(t=J(f_\lambda)\).

Proof. Slater's condition gives strong Lagrange duality and a multiplier \(\lambda\ge0\) satisfying the KKT conditions \[ 0\in\partial L(f_t)+\lambda\partial J(f_t),\qquad \lambda\{J(f_t)-t\}=0. \] The first condition is necessary and sufficient for \(f_t\) to minimize \(L+\lambda J\). The converse was proved above directly from penalized optimality.

Remark. The correspondence between \(t\) and \(\lambda\) need not be one-to-one. Flat portions, nonunique minimizers, and inactive constraints can map many tuning values to the same solution.

Proximal geometry

Definition. For a proper closed convex \(J\), the proximal operator is \[ \operatorname{prox}_{\tau J}(v) =\operatorname*{argmin}_u\left\{ \frac12\|u-v\|_2^2+\tau J(u)\right\}. \]

Proposition. For \(J(u)=\|u\|_1\), \[ \{\operatorname{prox}_{\tau\|\cdot\|_1}(v)\}_j =\operatorname{sign}(v_j)(|v_j|-\tau)_+. \] For \(J(u)=\tfrac12\|u\|_2^2\), \(\operatorname{prox}_{\tau J}(v)=v/(1+\tau)\).

Proof. The \(\ell^1\) objective separates by coordinate. Its optimality condition is \(0\in u_j-v_j+\tau\partial|u_j|\), whose three cases give soft thresholding. The quadratic case has first-order equation \((1+\tau)u-v=0\).

These formulas reveal two distinct regularization geometries: ridge continuously shrinks every coordinate, whereas the \(\ell^1\) proximal map creates exact zeros by a nonsmooth threshold.

Ridge as spectral filtering

Proposition. For least squares with \(X=U\operatorname{diag}(s_j)V^\top\), \[ X\widehat\beta_\lambda =U\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{s_j^2}{s_j^2+n\lambda}\right)U^\top Y. \] The effective degrees of freedom are \[ \operatorname{df}(\lambda) =\operatorname{tr}\{X(X^\top X+n\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top\} =\sum_j\frac{s_j^2}{s_j^2+n\lambda}. \]

Proof. Substitute the singular-value decomposition into the ridge estimator and multiply by \(X\). The trace of the resulting linear smoother is the sum of its eigenvalues.

Strongly convex regularized ERM is uniformly stable

Theorem. Let \[ F_S(w)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\ell(w,z_i)+\frac\lambda2\|w\|_2^2, \] where every \(\ell(\cdot,z)\) is convex and \(L\)-Lipschitz. If \(w_S=\arg\min F_S\), then for samples \(S,S^{(i)}\) differing in one observation, \[ \|w_S-w_{S^{(i)}}\|_2\le\frac{2L}{\lambda n}, \qquad \sup_z|\ell(w_S,z)-\ell(w_{S^{(i)}},z)| \le\frac{2L^2}{\lambda n}. \]

Proof. Strong convexity and minimality give \[ F_S(w_{S^{(i)}})-F_S(w_S)\ge\frac\lambda2\|\Delta\|^2,\qquad F_{S^{(i)}}(w_S)-F_{S^{(i)}}(w_{S^{(i)}})\ge\frac\lambda2\|\Delta\|^2. \] Add them. All shared losses cancel; the two differing losses are bounded by \(2L\|\Delta\|/n\). Thus \(\lambda\|\Delta\|^2\le2L\|\Delta\|/n\), proving the first inequality. Apply Lipschitzness once more for the second.

Early stopping in linear inverse problems

Proposition. Gradient descent on \(\tfrac1{2n}\|Y-X\beta\|_2^2\), initialized at zero with step size \(0<\eta<2n/\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\), has singular-coordinate filter \[ v_j^\top\beta_t = \frac{1-(1-\eta s_j^2/n)^t}{s_j}\,u_j^\top Y. \] Early iterations fit large singular directions first and suppress small singular directions.

Proof. The recursion \(\beta_{t+1}=(I-\eta X^\top X/n)\beta_t+\eta X^\top Y/n\) decouples in the \(V\)-basis. Summing the resulting geometric series yields the displayed filter.

Ridge uses filter \(s_j/(s_j^2+n\lambda)\); early stopping uses \(\{1-(1-\eta s_j^2/n)^t\}/s_j\). They are not identical, but both damp directions that are poorly identified by the design.

Parameter choice and honest validation

Proposition. Let \(\Lambda\) be a finite set of tuning parameters and suppose \(0\le\ell\le1\). Fit \(\widehat f_\lambda\) on a training sample, independently evaluate all candidates on a validation sample of size \(m\), and select \(\widehat\lambda\) by validation ERM. Then with probability at least \(1-\delta\), \[ R(\widehat f_{\widehat\lambda}) \le\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda}R(\widehat f_\lambda) 2\sqrt{\frac{\log(2|\Lambda|/\delta)}{2m}}. \]

Proof. Conditional on the training sample, the candidate functions are fixed. Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound control every validation-risk deviation. The basic finite-class ERM inequality then proves the result.

The independence clause is substantive: adapting the candidate grid after inspecting validation outcomes incurs an additional selection cost. Nested cross-validation separates performance assessment from tuning when the same finite dataset must serve both purposes.
