High-dimensional learning is possible when the target has low effective complexity, such as sparsity, even if the ambient dimension exceeds the sample size.

Sparse linear model

The canonical example is linear regression with many covariates but few active coefficients.

Definition. In the linear model \(y=X\beta^*+\xi\), the vector \(\beta^*\in\mathbb R^p\) is \(s\)-sparse if \(|S|=s\) for \(S=\{j:\beta_j^*\ne0\}\). The Lasso estimator solves \[ \hat\beta\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{\beta\in\mathbb R^p} \left\{\frac{1}{2n}\|y-X\beta\|_2^2+\lambda\|\beta\|_1\right\}. \]

Remark. The \(\ell^1\) norm is convex but has corners aligned with coordinate sparsity. That geometry is why it can select sparse solutions.

Basic inequality

Most Lasso theory begins with one deterministic inequality.

Lemma. Let \(\Delta=\hat\beta-\beta^*\). If \(\lambda\ge 2\|X^\top\xi/n\|_\infty\), then \[ \frac1{2n}\|X\Delta\|_2^2+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\Delta_{S^c}\|_1 \le \frac{3\lambda}{2}\|\Delta_S\|_1. \]

Proof. Optimality of \(\hat\beta\) compared with \(\beta^*\) gives \[ \frac1{2n}\|X\Delta\|_2^2 \le \frac{\xi^\top X\Delta}{n}+\lambda(\|\beta^*\|_1-\|\hat\beta\|_1). \] The stochastic term is bounded by \((\lambda/2)\|\Delta\|_1\). Since \(\beta^*_{S^c}=0\), \[ \|\beta^*\|_1-\|\hat\beta\|_1\le \|\Delta_S\|_1-\|\Delta_{S^c}\|_1. \] Combining terms gives the displayed inequality.

Restricted eigenvalues

Prediction and estimation rates require curvature on sparse cones, not on all of \(\mathbb R^p\).

Definition. A restricted eigenvalue condition holds with constant \(\kappa>0\) if \[ \frac{\|X\Delta\|_2}{\sqrt n}\ge \kappa\|\Delta_S\|_2 \] for all \(\Delta\) satisfying \(\|\Delta_{S^c}\|_1\le3\|\Delta_S\|_1\).

Theorem. Under the basic-inequality event and the restricted eigenvalue condition, \[ \frac1n\|X(\hat\beta-\beta^*)\|_2^2\lesssim \frac{\lambda^2s}{\kappa^2}, \qquad \|\hat\beta-\beta^*\|_1\lesssim \frac{\lambda s}{\kappa^2}. \]

Proof. The basic inequality implies the cone condition \(\|\Delta_{S^c}\|_1\le3\|\Delta_S\|_1\). Then \(\|\Delta_S\|_1\le\sqrt s\|\Delta_S\|_2\le \sqrt s\,\|X\Delta\|_2/(\sqrt n\kappa)\). Substitute this into the basic inequality and solve for \(\|X\Delta\|_2^2/n\). The \(\ell^1\) bound follows by combining the cone condition with the resulting \(\ell^2\) bound on \(\Delta_S\).

Choice of penalty

The penalty level must dominate noise correlations with all covariates.

Proposition. If columns of \(X\) have empirical norm at most \(1\) and \(\xi_i\) are sub-Gaussian with scale \(\sigma\), then \[ \left\|X^\top\xi/n\right\|_\infty =O_P\left(\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log p}{n}}\right). \]

Proof. For each coordinate, \(X_j^\top\xi/n\) is sub-Gaussian with scale at most \(\sigma/\sqrt n\). A union bound over \(p\) coordinates gives the \(\sqrt{\log p/n}\) maximum scale.

Compatibility and restricted curvature

Definition. For \(L\ge1\) and \(S\subset\{1,\ldots,p\}\), the compatibility constant is \[ \phi^2(L,S)= \min_{\substack{\Delta\ne0\\ \|\Delta_{S^c}\|_1\le L\|\Delta_S\|_1}} \frac{|S|\|X\Delta\|_2^2} {n\|\Delta_S\|_1^2}. \] It is weaker than a restricted eigenvalue condition because it controls \(\|\Delta_S\|_1\), not the full Euclidean norm.

Theorem. On the event \(\lambda\ge2\|X^\top\xi/n\|_\infty\), if \(\phi(3,S)>0\), then \[ \frac1n\|X\Delta\|_2^2+\lambda\|\Delta\|_1 \le C\frac{\lambda^2s}{\phi^2(3,S)} \] for a universal constant \(C\).

Proof. The basic inequality gives the cone \(\|\Delta_{S^c}\|_1\le3\|\Delta_S\|_1\) and \[ \frac1{2n}\|X\Delta\|^2+\frac\lambda2\|\Delta_{S^c}\|_1 \le\frac{3\lambda}{2}\|\Delta_S\|_1. \] Compatibility yields \(\|\Delta_S\|_1\le \sqrt{s}\|X\Delta\|/(\sqrt n\,\phi)\). Apply \(2ab\le a^2+b^2\) to absorb half of the prediction term. The resulting bound on \(\|X\Delta\|^2/n\), together with the cone inequality, controls \(\lambda\|\Delta\|_1\).

Gaussian penalty calibration

Proposition. Suppose \(\xi\sim N(0,\sigma^2I_n)\) and \(\|X_j\|_2^2/n\le1\). Then for every \(\delta\in(0,1)\), \[ P\!\left( \left\|\frac{X^\top\xi}{n}\right\|_\infty \le\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2p/\delta)}{n}} \right)\ge1-\delta. \]

Proof. Each coordinate is centered normal with variance at most \(\sigma^2/n\). The Gaussian tail bound and a union bound over both signs and \(p\) coordinates give the display.

Slow and fast oracle inequalities

Theorem. Without a restricted-eigenvalue assumption, on the penalty event the Lasso satisfies for every comparator \(\beta\), \[ \frac1n\|X\widehat\beta-X\beta^*\|_2^2 \le \frac1n\|X\beta-X\beta^*\|_2^2 4\lambda\|\beta\|_1. \] This is a slow-rate oracle inequality. Restricted curvature converts the linear penalty term into the fast term \(\lambda^2|S|/\kappa^2\).

Proof. Compare the penalized objective at \(\widehat\beta\) and \(\beta\), expand \(Y=X\beta^*+\xi\), and bound \(2\xi^\top X(\widehat\beta-\beta)/n\) by \(\lambda\|\widehat\beta-\beta\|_1\). Triangle inequalities then leave at most a constant times \(\lambda\|\beta\|_1\). No conversion of \(\ell^1\)-error to prediction error is used, which is why no design condition is needed and why the rate is slower.

Prediction is easier than support recovery

Counterexample. If two columns are identical, \(X_1=X_2\), then the distributions induced by \((\beta_1,\beta_2)=(a,0)\) and \((0,a)\) are identical. Prediction of \(X\beta\) may be exact, but no procedure can identify which variable is active. Thus prediction rates require only curvature on identifiable directions; exact support recovery requires stronger nonredundancy and signal conditions.

Group sparsity

Definition. Given a partition \(\mathcal G\) of coordinates, the group Lasso solves \[ \min_\beta\frac1{2n}\|Y-X\beta\|_2^2+ \lambda\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}w_g\|\beta_g\|_2. \] Its dual norm is \(\max_g\|u_g\|_2/w_g\), so the penalty event controls the maximum groupwise noise correlation.

Proposition. If the true support consists of \(s_G\) groups and a group restricted-eigenvalue condition holds, then the prediction rate has order \[ \frac{\sigma^2}{n} \left\{\sum_{g\in S}|g|+s_G\log|\mathcal G|\right\}, \] up to design constants and weighting conventions.

The first term estimates coefficients within active groups; the second searches over groups. This decomposition generalizes the scalar sparse complexity \(s\log(p/s)\).

Debiasing a Lasso coordinate

Definition. Let \(\widehat\Sigma=X^\top X/n\), and let \(\widehat\Theta_j\) approximate the \(j\)th column of \(\widehat\Sigma^{-1}\), often by nodewise Lasso. The debiased estimator is \[ \widetilde\beta_j =\widehat\beta_j+ \widehat\Theta_j^\top X^\top(Y-X\widehat\beta)/n. \]

Proposition. Its exact decomposition is \[ \widetilde\beta_j-\beta_j^* = \widehat\Theta_j^\top X^\top\xi/n+ (e_j^\top-\widehat\Theta_j^\top\widehat\Sigma) (\widehat\beta-\beta^*). \] If the second term is \(o_P(n^{-1/2})\), then conditional Gaussian or central-limit arguments make the first term asymptotically normal.

Proof. Substitute \(Y-X\widehat\beta=\xi-X(\widehat\beta-\beta^*)\) into the definition and collect terms. The identity separates the leading score from the inverse-covariance approximation remainder.
