Empirical risk minimization is the basic bridge from data to predictors. Its validity comes from uniform laws of large numbers, and its sharp forms come from localization around low-risk functions.

Exact and approximate minimizers

Optimization error should be visible in the notation. An algorithm rarely returns a mathematical exact minimizer.

Definition. Given a loss class \(\mathcal G=\{\ell_f:f\in\mathcal F\}\), an \(\eta_n\)-ERM satisfies \[ P_n\ell_{\hat f_n}\le \inf_{f\in\mathcal F}P_n\ell_f+\eta_n. \]

Proposition. If \(\mathcal G\) is \(P\)-Glivenko-Cantelli and \(\eta_n\to0\) in probability, then \[ R(\hat f_n)-\inf_{f\in\mathcal F}R(f)\to0 \] in probability.

Proof. Use the basic ERM inequality \[ R(\hat f_n)-\inf_{\mathcal F}R \le 2\sup_{g\in\mathcal G}|P_ng-Pg|+\eta_n. \] The right side converges to zero by the Glivenko-Cantelli assumption and the optimization condition.

Finite classes

For finite classes, uniform convergence is just concentration plus a union bound.

Theorem. If \(0\le \ell_f\le1\) and \(|\mathcal F|=M<\infty\), then with probability at least \(1-\delta\), \[ \sup_{f\in\mathcal F}|R_n(f)-R(f)| \le \sqrt{\frac{\log(2M/\delta)}{2n}}. \]

Proof. Hoeffding's inequality gives \[ P(|R_n(f)-R(f)|>t)\le2e^{-2nt^2} \] for each fixed \(f\). Taking a union bound over \(M\) functions and solving \(2Me^{-2nt^2}=\delta\) gives the bound.

Oracle inequalities

A learning bound is strongest when it compares the estimator with the best member of a reference class.

Proposition. On the same event as the finite-class theorem, every ERM satisfies \[ R(\hat f_n)\le \inf_{f\in\mathcal F}R(f) +2\sqrt{\frac{\log(2M/\delta)}{2n}}. \]

Proof. Apply the basic ERM inequality with \(\eta_n=0\) and the uniform deviation bound from the finite-class theorem.

Remark. The phrase oracle inequality means the right side is the performance of the best inaccessible oracle choice plus an explicit price for learning it from data.

Localization

Global uniform convergence often wastes information because high-risk functions do not compete with the empirical minimizer.

Definition. For a radius \(r>0\), a localized class may be written \[ \mathcal G(r)=\{g_f-g_{f^*}:P(g_f-g_{f^*})\le r\}. \] Localized analyses bound the empirical process on \(\mathcal G(r)\) and solve a fixed-point inequality for \(r\).

Remark. Fast rates in least squares, classification with margin, and strongly convex regularized ERM all use this idea in different languages.

Argmin consistency without global uniformity

Uniform convergence over the whole parameter space is sufficient but stronger than necessary. Classical \(M\)-estimation instead combines separation of the population minimizer with uniform convergence only on relevant compact sets.

Theorem. Let \((\Theta,d)\) be a metric space, let \(M_n:\Theta\to\mathbb R\) be random, and let \(M:\Theta\to\mathbb R\) have a unique minimizer \(\theta_0\). Assume that for every \(\varepsilon>0\), \[ \inf_{d(\theta,\theta_0)\ge\varepsilon}M(\theta)>M(\theta_0), \qquad \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|M_n(\theta)-M(\theta)|\xrightarrow{P}0. \] If \(M_n(\widehat\theta_n)\le\inf_\theta M_n(\theta)+o_P(1)\), then \(d(\widehat\theta_n,\theta_0)\xrightarrow{P}0\).

Proof. Fix \(\varepsilon>0\) and set \[ \gamma_\varepsilon= \inf_{d(\theta,\theta_0)\ge\varepsilon}\{M(\theta)-M(\theta_0)\}>0. \] On the event \(\sup_\theta|M_n-M|<\gamma_\varepsilon/3\) and with optimization error below \(\gamma_\varepsilon/3\), every \(\theta\) outside the \(\varepsilon\)-ball satisfies \[ M_n(\theta)>M(\theta_0)+2\gamma_\varepsilon/3 >M_n(\theta_0)+\gamma_\varepsilon/3. \] Hence \(\widehat\theta_n\) must lie inside the ball. The event has probability tending to one.

Penalized empirical risk

Definition. A regularized ERM solves \[ \widehat f_\lambda\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{f\in\mathcal F} \{P_n\ell_f+\lambda\Omega(f)\}, \] where \(\Omega\) encodes structure such as smoothness, sparsity, or small RKHS norm.

Proposition. For every comparator \(f\in\mathcal F\), \[ R(\widehat f_\lambda)-R(f) \le (P-P_n)(\ell_{\widehat f_\lambda}-\ell_f) +\lambda\{\Omega(f)-\Omega(\widehat f_\lambda)\}. \] This is the basic inequality for regularized ERM.

Proof. Empirical optimality gives \(P_n\ell_{\widehat f_\lambda}+\lambda\Omega(\widehat f_\lambda) \le P_n\ell_f+\lambda\Omega(f)\). Add \((P-P_n)(\ell_{\widehat f_\lambda}-\ell_f)\) to both sides and rearrange.

Unlike the crude global ERM bound, the basic inequality retains the sign of the penalty and the increment \(\ell_{\widehat f_\lambda}-\ell_f\). Sparse and fast-rate proofs depend on this sharper form.

Curvature and stochastic gradients

Theorem. Let \(\Theta\subset\mathbb R^d\), suppose \(R\) is \(\mu\)-strongly convex with minimizer \(\theta^*\), and let \(\widehat\theta\) minimize a differentiable \(R_n\). If \[ \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|\nabla R_n(\theta)-\nabla R(\theta)\|_2\le\varepsilon, \] then \[ \|\widehat\theta-\theta^*\|_2\le\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu}, \qquad R(\widehat\theta)-R(\theta^*)\le \frac{L\varepsilon^2}{2\mu^2} \] whenever \(R\) is also \(L\)-smooth.

Proof. Strong monotonicity of \(\nabla R\) gives \[ \mu\|\widehat\theta-\theta^*\|_2^2 \le\langle\nabla R(\widehat\theta)-\nabla R(\theta^*), \widehat\theta-\theta^*\rangle. \] Since \(\nabla R_n(\widehat\theta)=0\) and \(\nabla R(\theta^*)=0\), Cauchy-Schwarz bounds the right side by \(\varepsilon\|\widehat\theta-\theta^*\|_2\). Cancel the distance. Smoothness then yields \(R(\widehat\theta)-R(\theta^*)\le(L/2)\|\widehat\theta-\theta^*\|_2^2\).

A localized fixed-point theorem

Definition. Let \(g_f=\ell_f-\ell_{f^*}\) be an excess-loss class with \(Pg_f\ge0\). A Bernstein condition with exponent \(\beta\in[0,1]\) is \[ Pg_f^2\le B(Pg_f)^\beta \quad\text{for all }f\in\mathcal F. \]

Theorem. Suppose a nondecreasing function \(\psi\) satisfies \[ \mathbb E\sup_{Pg_f\le r}|(P_n-P)g_f|\le\psi(r), \] and \(r\mapsto\psi(r)/\sqrt r\) is nonincreasing. If \(r_*\) solves \(\psi(r_*)\le r_*/8\), then, under boundedness and the Bernstein condition, an ERM satisfies with high probability \[ P g_{\widehat f}\lesssim r_*+\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{n} \] with constants depending on the envelope and \(B\).

Proof outline. Partition the class into shells \(2^jr_*-\tfrac12Pg_f\) for all \(f\) outside the innermost shell. Such an \(f\) cannot minimize empirical risk because \(P_ng_{f^*}=0\). Hence the ERM lies in the inner shell.

When ERM is inconsistent

Counterexample. Let \(\mathcal X=[0,1]\), let \(Y=0\) surely, and let \(\mathcal F\) contain indicators of all finite subsets of \([0,1]\), together with the zero function. Under squared loss, for any sample \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\), the function \[ f_n=\mathbf1_{\{X_1,\ldots,X_n\}} \] has empirical risk zero, but so does \(f\equiv0\). If a tie-breaking rule instead selects \(1-f_n\), its empirical risk is still zero while its population risk is one because a finite set has Lebesgue measure zero. Thus pointwise laws of large numbers do not justify arbitrary ERM tie breaking; uniform control or structural selection is necessary.

Quantitative control of the deviations appearing above comes from concentration inequalities, while their dependence on the size of \(\mathcal F\) is measured by VC dimension, Rademacher complexity, and metric entropy.
