VC dimension is the fundamental combinatorial measure for binary classification classes. It controls how many labelings the class can realize and therefore how severe overfitting can be.

Shattering and growth

A class is complex if it can realize all dichotomies on large finite sets.

Definition. For a class \(\mathcal C\) of subsets of \(\mathcal X\), the growth function is \[ \Pi_{\mathcal C}(n)=\max_{x_1,\ldots,x_n}\left|\{(1_C(x_1),\ldots,1_C(x_n)):C\in\mathcal C\}\right|. \] The VC dimension is the largest \(d\) such that some \(d\)-point set is shattered, meaning \(\Pi_{\mathcal C}(d)=2^d\).

Example. Intervals on \(\mathbb R\) have VC dimension \(2\). Two points can be shattered; three ordered points cannot realize the labeling \(1,0,1\).

Sauer-Shelah lemma

Finite VC dimension forces polynomial growth of the number of labelings.

Theorem. If \(\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal C)=d<\infty\), then for \(n\ge d\), \[ \Pi_{\mathcal C}(n)\le \sum_{j=0}^d {n\choose j}\le \left(\frac{en}{d}\right)^d. \]

Proof. The Sauer-Shelah induction counts traces on \(n\) points by separating sets according to whether their trace changes when the last point is removed. The changing part corresponds to subsets shattered by the class on the first \(n-1\) points, so the recursion is bounded by the binomial recursion \(\sum_{j\le d}{n-1\choose j}+\sum_{j\le d-1}{n-1\choose j}=\sum_{j\le d}{n\choose j}\). The final inequality is the standard binomial bound.

VC inequality

VC classes satisfy uniform convergence for binary losses.

Theorem. For a VC class \(\mathcal C\) of dimension \(d\), there is a universal constant \(K\) such that with probability at least \(1-\delta\), \[ \sup_{C\in\mathcal C}|P_nC-PC| \le K\sqrt{\frac{d\log(en/d)+\log(1/\delta)}{n}} \] for \(n\ge d\).

Proof. Symmetrization reduces the expected supremum to random signs on the sample. Conditional on the sample, only \(\Pi_{\mathcal C}(n)\) distinct label vectors occur. A finite-class maximal inequality and Sauer-Shelah bound the expected supremum by the displayed complexity scale. A bounded-differences concentration step converts expectation to a high-probability statement.

Examples and invariances

VC dimension is stable under many geometric transformations but sensitive to algebraic richness.

Proposition. Halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^p\) have VC dimension \(p+1\).

Proof. Any \(p+1\) affinely independent points can be shattered by separating selected vertices of a simplex from the remaining vertices. Conversely, Radon's theorem says any \(p+2\) points can be partitioned into two subsets with intersecting convex hulls, so no hyperplane can separate those two labels. Hence the VC dimension is \(p+1\).

The combinatorial dimension of common classes

Proposition. The following VC dimensions hold: \[ \begin{array}{c|c} \text{class}&\text{VC dimension}\\ \hline \{(-\infty,t]:t\in\mathbb R\}&1\\ \{[a,b]:a\le b\}&2\\ \text{axis-aligned rectangles in }\mathbb R^p&2p\\ \text{Euclidean balls in }\mathbb R^p&p+1. \end{array} \]

Proof outline. Thresholds can realize both labels on one point but cannot realize \(1,0\) on two ordered points. Intervals shatter two points but not the alternating labeling on three. For rectangles, take two extreme points on each coordinate axis for the lower bound; every rectangle is determined by \(2p\) coordinate thresholds, and an extremal-coordinate argument gives the upper bound. Balls lift to halfspaces through \(\|x-c\|^2\le r^2\iff \|x\|^2-2c^\top x+\|c\|^2-r^2\le0\); affine independence gives the matching lower bound.

A complete Sauer-Shelah induction

Proof of the Sauer-Shelah lemma. Let \(m(n,d)\) be the largest number of traces on an \(n\)-point set among classes of VC dimension at most \(d\). Fix the last point \(x_n\). Let \(\mathcal A\) be the traces on the first \(n-1\) points that occur with at least one label at \(x_n\), and let \(\mathcal B\subset\mathcal A\) be those occurring with both labels. Then the total number of traces is \[ |\mathcal A|+|\mathcal B|. \] The class \(\mathcal A\) has VC dimension at most \(d\). The class \(\mathcal B\) has VC dimension at most \(d-1\): if it shattered \(d\) points, adjoining \(x_n\) would produce a shattered set of size \(d+1\). Therefore \[ m(n,d)\le m(n-1,d)+m(n-1,d-1). \] With boundary values \(m(n,0)=1\) and \(m(n,d)=2^n\) for \(d\ge n\), induction and Pascal's identity yield \(m(n,d)\le\sum_{j=0}^d{n\choose j}\).

Symmetrized VC deviation bound

Theorem. For every class \(\mathcal C\) and \(\varepsilon>0\) with \(n\varepsilon^2\ge2\), \[ P\!\left(\sup_{C\in\mathcal C}|P_nC-PC|>\varepsilon\right) \le 8\,\Pi_{\mathcal C}(2n)\exp\!\left(-\frac{n\varepsilon^2}{32}\right). \]

Proof outline. Introduce an independent ghost sample \(P_n'\). A binomial concentration argument shows that if \(|P_nC-PC|>\varepsilon\), then with conditional probability at least \(1/2\), \(|P_nC-P_n'C|>\varepsilon/2\). Thus the target probability is at most twice the symmetrized probability. Conditional on the pooled \(2n\) observations, \(\mathcal C\) induces at most \(\Pi_{\mathcal C}(2n)\) binary vectors. Randomly swapping each observation pair converts the difference into a Rademacher sum. Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound over the induced vectors produce the display.

Corollary. If \(\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal C)=d<\infty\), then \[ \sup_{C\in\mathcal C}|P_nC-PC|\longrightarrow0 \quad\text{almost surely}. \]

Proof. Sauer-Shelah makes the preceding tail bound at a fixed \(\varepsilon\) at most a polynomial in \(n\) times \(e^{-cn}\), which is summable. Borel-Cantelli gives eventual control for each rational \(\varepsilon>0\), hence convergence to zero.

Fundamental theorem for binary classification

Theorem. For a binary concept class \(\mathcal C\), the following are equivalent:

  1. \(\mathcal C\) is PAC learnable in the realizable setting;
  2. ERM over \(\mathcal C\) is a PAC learner;
  3. \(\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal C)<\infty\).
If the VC dimension is \(d\), a sufficient realizable sample size is \[ n\gtrsim\frac{d\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log(1/\delta)}{\varepsilon}, \] while the agnostic rate is of order \[ n\gtrsim\frac{d+\log(1/\delta)}{\varepsilon^2}. \]

Proof outline. Finite VC dimension and the growth bound give uniform convergence, which implies agnostic ERM learning; in the realizable case a one-sided argument controls the probability that a zero-training-error classifier has true error above \(\varepsilon\), improving the dependence on \(\varepsilon\). Conversely, if \(\mathcal C\) shatters arbitrarily large finite sets, place a distribution on a shattered set and reveal only part of its random labeling in the sample. Labels of unseen points remain independent fair bits, forcing every learner to incur nonvanishing error with positive probability.

Pseudo-dimension and real-valued classes

Definition. A real-valued class \(\mathcal F\) pseudo-shatters \(x_1,\ldots,x_d\) if there are thresholds \(r_1,\ldots,r_d\) such that every binary vector \(b\in\{0,1\}^d\) is realized as \[ \mathbf1\{f(x_i)>r_i\}=b_i,\qquad i=1,\ldots,d, \] for some \(f\in\mathcal F\). The largest such \(d\) is the pseudo-dimension.

Pseudo-dimension controls bounded regression losses in much the same way that VC dimension controls classification. Fat-shattering dimension refines it by requiring a positive margin around each threshold and is therefore scale-sensitive.

Closure operations

Proposition. If classes \(\mathcal C_1,\ldots,\mathcal C_k\) have VC dimensions at most \(d\), then the class of intersections \[ \mathcal C_\cap=\{C_1\cap\cdots\cap C_k:C_j\in\mathcal C_j\} \] has growth function \(\Pi_{\mathcal C_\cap}(n)\le\prod_{j=1}^k\Pi_{\mathcal C_j}(n)\). Consequently its VC dimension is \(O(kd\log k)\).

Proof. On a fixed sample, an intersection labeling is determined by one labeling from each constituent class, so the number of possible intersections is no larger than the product. If \(n\) points were shattered, then \(2^n\le(en/d)^{kd}\); solving this inequality gives \(n=O(kd\log k)\).

VC dimension is distribution-free and combinatorial. It can be pessimistic when the realized design or norm geometry is favorable; Rademacher complexity provides that finer sample-dependent scale.
