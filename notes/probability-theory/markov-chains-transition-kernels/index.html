A Markov chain retains the present state as a sufficient summary of its past for prediction of the future. A transition kernel expresses that statement without assuming a countable state space or a transition density. Kernel composition, path-law construction, strong stopping-time restart, and harmonic functions are the basic structural tools.

Probability kernels and their actions

Definition. A transition kernel \(K\) on \((S,\mathcal S)\) satisfies:

  1. \(A\mapsto K(x,A)\) is a probability measure for every \(x\in S\);
  2. \(x\mapsto K(x,A)\) is measurable for every \(A\in\mathcal S\).
It acts on bounded measurable functions and probability measures by \[ Kf(x)=\int_Sf(y)K(x,\mathrm{d} y), \qquad \mu K(A)=\int_SK(x,A)\mu(\mathrm{d} x). \]

Proposition. If \(f\) is bounded measurable, then \(Kf\) is bounded measurable and \(\|Kf\|_\infty\le\|f\|_\infty\). Moreover, \(K1=1\) and \(f\ge0\) implies \(Kf\ge0\).

Proof. Measurability holds first for indicators by the kernel definition, then for simple functions, nonnegative functions by monotone convergence, and bounded signed functions by decomposition. The remaining claims follow from integration against a probability measure.

The Markov property

Definition. An adapted process \(X_n\) is a time-homogeneous Markov chain with kernel \(K\) if \[ P(X_{n+1}\in A\mid\mathcal F_n)=K(X_n,A) \] almost surely for every \(A\in\mathcal S\). Equivalently, for every bounded measurable \(f\), \[ \mathbb E[f(X_{n+1})\mid\mathcal F_n]=Kf(X_n). \]

Proof of equivalence. Indicators give the set form. Linearity gives simple functions, bounded monotone approximation gives nonnegative measurable functions, and positive-negative decomposition gives bounded measurable \(f\). The converse takes \(f=1_A\).

Theorem (path-law construction). Given an initial law \(\mu\) and a transition kernel \(K\) on a standard Borel space, there is a unique law on \(S^{\mathbb N_0}\) such that \[ P(X_0\in\mathrm{d} x_0,\ldots,X_n\in\mathrm{d} x_n) = \mu(\mathrm{d} x_0) K(x_0,\mathrm{d} x_1)\cdots K(x_{n-1},\mathrm{d} x_n). \]

Proof. Iterated kernel integration defines a probability measure on each finite product. Integrating out the last coordinate uses \(K(x,S)=1\) and gives the preceding marginal, so the family is consistent. Kolmogorov extension constructs the path law; cylinder uniqueness makes it unique. Conditioning the displayed factorization verifies the Markov property.

Chapman-Kolmogorov equations

Definition. Define \(K^0(x,\cdot)=\delta_x\) and recursively \[ K^{n+1}(x,A)=\int_SK^n(y,A)K(x,\mathrm{d} y). \]

Theorem. For all \(m,n\ge0\), \[ K^{m+n}=K^mK^n, \qquad P(X_{m+n}\in A\mid\mathcal F_m)=K^n(X_m,A). \]

Proof. Induct on \(n\). The one-step case is the Markov property. If the conditional identity holds for \(n\), then the tower property gives \[ \begin{aligned} P(X_{m+n+1}\in A\mid\mathcal F_m) &= \mathbb E[K(X_{m+n},A)\mid\mathcal F_m]\\ &=K^n(K1_A)(X_m)=K^{n+1}(X_m,A). \end{aligned} \] Evaluating from \(X_0=x\) yields the kernel composition identity.

The strong Markov property

Theorem. Let \(\tau\) be a stopping time for the natural filtration of a Markov chain. On \(\{\tau<\infty\}\), for every bounded measurable \(f\) and \(n\ge0\), \[ \mathbb E[f(X_{\tau+n})\mid\mathcal F_\tau] =K^nf(X_\tau). \]

Proof. For \(A\in\mathcal F_\tau\), \[ \mathbb E[1_A1_{\{\tau<\infty\}}f(X_{\tau+n})] = \sum_{m=0}^\infty \mathbb E[1_{A\cap\{\tau=m\}}f(X_{m+n})]. \] Since \(A\cap\{\tau=m\}\in\mathcal F_m\), the deterministic-time Markov property turns the \(m\)th summand into \[ \mathbb E[1_{A\cap\{\tau=m\}}K^nf(X_m)]. \] Summing identifies the conditional expectation as \(K^nf(X_\tau)\).

Harmonic functions and martingales

Proposition. If \(h\) is bounded and \(Kh=h\), then \(h(X_n)\) is a martingale. If \(Kh\ge h\), it is a submartingale.

Proof. \[ \mathbb E[h(X_{n+1})\mid\mathcal F_n] =Kh(X_n). \] Insert the harmonic or subharmonic relation.

Example (random walk). If \(X_{n+1}=X_n+\xi_{n+1}\) with i.i.d. increments of law \(\nu\), then \[ K(x,A)=\nu(A-x),\qquad Kf(x)=\int f(x+z)\nu(\mathrm{d} z). \] When \(\mathbb E\xi_1=0\), the function \(h(x)=x\) is harmonic whenever integrable, so \(X_n\) is a martingale. For a simple symmetric walk, \(h(x)=x^2\) satisfies \(Kh(x)=h(x)+1\), yielding the martingale \(X_n^2-n\).

On a countable state space, \(K\) is a stochastic matrix and \(K^n\) is its matrix power. The kernel formulation is the same theory without coordinates and is needed for general-state-space Monte Carlo and diffusion sampling.
