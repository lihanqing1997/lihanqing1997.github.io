Brownian motion is the continuous process whose increments are independent Gaussian fluctuations with variance equal to elapsed time. Its construction illustrates the two-stage method for processes: first prescribe consistent finite-dimensional laws, then prove that a regular modification exists.

Equivalent defining data

Definition. A standard Brownian motion is a real process \(B=(B_t)_{t\ge0}\) satisfying:

  1. \(B_0=0\) almost surely;
  2. increments over pairwise disjoint time intervals are independent;
  3. \(B_t-B_s\sim N(0,t-s)\) for \(0\le s
  4. sample paths are continuous almost surely.

Proposition. A centered continuous Gaussian process with covariance \[ \mathbb E[B_sB_t]=s\wedge t \] is Brownian motion.

Proof. The increment variance is \[ \mathbb E(B_t-B_s)^2=t+s-2s=t-s. \] For disjoint intervals \([s,t]\) and \([u,v]\) with \(t\le u\), \[ \operatorname{Cov}(B_t-B_s,B_v-B_u) =(t\wedge v-t\wedge u)-(s\wedge v-s\wedge u)=0. \] Any finite collection of increments is jointly Gaussian. Zero cross-covariances therefore imply independence.

Existence of Wiener measure

Theorem. There is a unique probability measure \(W\) on \[ C_0([0,\infty)) =\{\omega\in C([0,\infty)):\omega(0)=0\} \] such that the coordinate process \(B_t(\omega)=\omega(t)\) is Brownian motion. This probability is Wiener measure.

Proof. On the raw product space \(\mathbb R^{[0,\infty)}\), prescribe centered Gaussian finite-dimensional laws with covariance \(K(s,t)=s\wedge t\). The kernel is positive semidefinite because \[ \sum_{i,j}a_ia_j(t_i\wedge t_j) = \int_0^\infty \left(\sum_ia_i1_{[0,t_i]}(u)\right)^2\mathrm{d} u\ge0. \] Kolmogorov extension gives a coordinate process. The preceding proposition gives independent stationary Gaussian increments. For \(p\ge2\), \[ \mathbb E|B_t-B_s|^p =\mathbb E|Z|^p\,|t-s|^{p/2}, \qquad Z\sim N(0,1). \] Taking \(p>2\), Kolmogorov-Chentsov produces a continuous modification. Its law is supported on \(C_0([0,\infty))\), with that space equipped by uniform convergence on compact intervals. Uniqueness follows because coordinate evaluations generate the Borel sigma-field and the finite-dimensional laws agree.

Symmetries

Theorem (Brownian symmetries). If \(B\) is Brownian motion, then:

  1. for \(c>0\), \[ \widetilde B_t=c^{-1/2}B_{ct} \] is Brownian motion;
  2. for fixed \(s\ge0\), \[ \widetilde B_t=B_{s+t}-B_s \] is Brownian motion independent of \(\mathcal F_s\);
  3. for fixed \(T>0\), \[ \widetilde B_t=B_T-B_{T-t},\qquad0\le t\le T, \] has the law of Brownian motion on \([0,T]\);

A series construction

Theorem (Levy-Ciesielski representation). On \([0,1]\), Brownian motion admits an almost surely uniformly convergent expansion \[ B_t=Z_0t+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \sum_{k=0}^{2^j-1} Z_{j,k}\,2^{-(j+2)/2}\, \psi_{j,k}(t), \] where the \(Z_0,Z_{j,k}\) are i.i.d. \(N(0,1)\) and \(\psi_{j,k}\) are appropriately normalized tent functions obtained by integrating the Haar basis.

Proof outline. Expand white noise in the orthonormal Haar basis and integrate term by term. Orthogonality gives the covariance \[ \mathbb E[B_sB_t] =\langle1_{[0,s]},1_{[0,t]}\rangle_{L^2} =s\wedge t. \] Gaussian maxima at level \(j\) grow only like \(\sqrt j\), while the integrated Haar coefficients decay like \(2^{-j/2}\). Borel-Cantelli therefore gives uniform convergence. The representation makes Brownian motion an explicit random continuous function rather than only an abstract extension.
