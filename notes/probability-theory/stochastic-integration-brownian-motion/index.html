The Ito integral is built as an isometry from predictable square-integrable processes into square-integrable martingales. Predictability enforces nonanticipation, the isometry supplies completion, and localization extends the construction beyond finite second moments.

Definition. Let \[ \mathcal H_T^2 = \left\{ H:\ H\text{ predictable and } \mathbb E\int_0^TH_t^2\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty \right\}, \] modulo equality under \(P\otimes\mathrm{d}t\), with norm \[ \|H\|_{\mathcal H_T^2}^2 =\mathbb E\int_0^TH_t^2\,\mathrm{d}t. \]

Theorem (construction). There is a unique linear isometry \[ I_T:\mathcal H_T^2\to L^2(\Omega),\qquad H\mapsto\int_0^TH_t\,\mathrm{d}B_t, \] extending the simple-process definition.

Proof. Choose simple predictable \(H^n\to H\) in \(\mathcal H_T^2\). The isometry makes \(I_T(H^n)\) Cauchy in \(L^2(\Omega)\); define \(I_T(H)\) as its limit. If another approximation is used, the isometry applied to the difference shows the same limit. Linearity and preservation of the norm pass to the completion.

The integral process

Theorem. For \(H\in\mathcal H_T^2\), the process \[ M_t=\int_0^tH_s\,\mathrm{d}B_s \] has a continuous modification and is a square-integrable martingale with \[ [M]_t=\langle M\rangle_t =\int_0^tH_s^2\,\mathrm{d} s. \] Moreover, \[ \mathbb E[(M_t-M_s)^2\mid\mathcal F_s] = \mathbb E\!\left[ \int_s^tH_u^2\,\mathrm{d} u \middle|\mathcal F_s\right]. \]

Proof. For simple \(H\), conditional centering of future Brownian increments gives the martingale identity. Expanding the square shows \[ M_t^2-\int_0^tH_s^2\,\mathrm{d} s \] is a martingale. Approximation in \(\mathcal H_T^2\), Doob's \(L^2\) inequality, and the isometry give uniform-in-time convergence in probability of the simple integral processes, so their continuous modifications converge to a continuous modification of \(M\). The martingale and bracket identities pass to the limit. Subtracting at \(s\) and conditioning gives the final formula.

Local square integrability

Definition. If \(H\) is predictable and \[ \int_0^tH_s^2\,\mathrm{d} s<\infty \quad\text{almost surely for every }t, \] define \[ \tau_n=\inf\left\{t:\int_0^tH_s^2\,\mathrm{d} s\ge n\right\}. \] The localized integrals agree on overlaps and define the continuous local martingale \[ \int_0^tH_s\,\mathrm{d}B_s. \]

Example. A deterministic \(h\in L^2[0,T]\) gives a centered Gaussian variable \[ \int_0^Th_s\,\mathrm{d}B_s \sim N\!\left(0,\int_0^Th_s^2\,\mathrm{d} s\right). \] A random adapted integrand need not produce a Gaussian variable: \[ \int_0^tB_s\,\mathrm{d}B_s =\frac12(B_t^2-t), \] which is not Gaussian. The isometry determines the second moment, not the entire law.
