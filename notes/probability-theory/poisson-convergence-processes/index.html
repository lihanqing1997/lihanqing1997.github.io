Poisson laws are the universal limits of many independent rare events. Passing from a count to a random measure preserves where those events occur and leads to Poisson processes, point processes, thinning, superposition, and compensated jump martingales.

The Poisson law

Definition. A random variable \(N\) has the Poisson law with parameter \(\lambda\ge0\) if \[ P(N=k)=e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^k}{k!}, \qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots. \] Its probability generating function, characteristic function, mean, and variance are \[ \mathbb Ez^N=e^{\lambda(z-1)},\qquad \mathbb Ee^{itN}=e^{\lambda(e^{it}-1)},\qquad \mathbb EN=\operatorname{Var}(N)=\lambda. \]

Proposition (additivity). If \(N_1\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_1)\) and \(N_2\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_2)\) are independent, then \(N_1+N_2\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)\).

Proof. Multiply their generating functions: \[ \mathbb Ez^{N_1+N_2} =e^{\lambda_1(z-1)}e^{\lambda_2(z-1)} =e^{(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)(z-1)}. \]

The law of small numbers

Theorem (Poisson convergence). Let \(X_{n,k}\) be row-wise independent Bernoulli variables with \(P(X_{n,k}=1)=p_{n,k}\). If \[ \max_{k\le k_n}p_{n,k}\to0, \qquad \sum_{k=1}^{k_n}p_{n,k}\to\lambda, \] then \[ \sum_{k=1}^{k_n}X_{n,k} \Rightarrow\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda). \]

Proof. For \(z\in[0,1]\), \[ \mathbb Ez^{\sum_kX_{n,k}} =\prod_k\{1+p_{n,k}(z-1)\}. \] Since \[ \sum_kp_{n,k}^2 \le\left(\max_kp_{n,k}\right)\sum_kp_{n,k}\to0, \] the expansion \(\log(1+u)=u+O(u^2)\), uniformly for the relevant \(u\), gives \[ \log\mathbb Ez^{\sum_kX_{n,k}} =(z-1)\sum_kp_{n,k}+o(1) \to\lambda(z-1). \] The generating functions converge to that of \(\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\), which determines the law on \(\mathbb N_0\).

Theorem (Le Cam inequality). If \(W=\sum_{k=1}^nX_k\) is a sum of independent Bernoulli\((p_k)\) variables and \(Z\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\), \(\lambda=\sum_kp_k\), then \[ d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mathcal L(W),\mathcal L(Z)) \le 2\sum_{k=1}^np_k^2. \]

Proof outline. Couple each Bernoulli \(X_k\) to an independent Poisson variable \(Z_k\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(p_k)\). Direct comparison of their masses gives a coupling with \(P(X_k\ne Z_k)\le 2p_k^2\). Under the product coupling, \(Z=\sum_kZ_k\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\sum_kp_k)\), and \[ d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mathcal L(W),\mathcal L(Z)) \le P(W\ne Z) \le\sum_kP(X_k\ne Z_k) \le2\sum_kp_k^2. \]

Poisson processes and waiting times

Definition. A homogeneous Poisson process with rate \(\lambda>0\) is a right-continuous counting process \((N_t)_{t\ge0}\) such that \(N_0=0\), its increments over disjoint intervals are independent, and \[ N_t-N_s\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda(t-s)), \qquad0\le s

Theorem (arrival-time construction). Let \(E_1,E_2,\ldots\) be i.i.d. exponential\((\lambda)\), put \(T_n=\sum_{k=1}^nE_k\), and define \[ N_t=\max\{n:T_n\le t\}. \] Then \(N\) is a Poisson process of rate \(\lambda\). Conversely, the interarrival times of a Poisson process are i.i.d. exponential\((\lambda)\).

Proof. Since \(T_n\) has the gamma density \[ f_{T_n}(u)=\frac{\lambda^nu^{n-1}e^{-\lambda u}}{(n-1)!}, \quad u>0, \] integration over \(T_n\le tt)=P(N_t=0)=e^{-\lambda t}, \] and the independent-increment property restarts the process after each arrival, producing independent exponential gaps.

Proposition (compensation). For a rate-\(\lambda\) Poisson process with natural filtration, \[ M_t=N_t-\lambda t \] is a square-integrable martingale, and \(\mathbb E(M_t-M_s)^2=\lambda(t-s)\).

Proof. Independent increments give \[ \mathbb E[N_t-N_s\mid\mathcal F_s]=\lambda(t-s), \] hence \(\mathbb E[M_t\mid\mathcal F_s]=M_s\). The variance identity follows from \(\operatorname{Var}(N_t-N_s)=\lambda(t-s)\).

Poisson random measures

Definition. Let \((E,\mathcal E)\) carry a sigma-finite measure \(\nu\). A Poisson random measure \(N\) with intensity \(\nu\) is an integer-valued random measure such that: \[ N(A)\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\nu(A)) \quad\text{when }\nu(A)<\infty, \] and \(N(A_1),\ldots,N(A_m)\) are independent for pairwise disjoint \(A_1,\ldots,A_m\).

Theorem (Laplace functional). For every measurable \(f:E\to[0,\infty)\), \[ \mathbb E\exp\!\left\{-\int_E f(x)N(\mathrm{d} x)\right\} = \exp\!\left\{-\int_E(1-e^{-f(x)})\nu(\mathrm{d} x)\right\}. \] This identity determines the law of \(N\).

Proof. First take \(f=\sum_{j=1}^ma_j1_{A_j}\) on disjoint sets of finite intensity. Independence and the Poisson generating function give \[ \prod_{j=1}^m \mathbb Ee^{-a_jN(A_j)} = \prod_{j=1}^m \exp\{\nu(A_j)(e^{-a_j}-1)\}, \] which is the displayed formula. Approximate a general nonnegative \(f\) increasingly by simple functions and use monotone convergence on both sides.

Proposition (thinning and superposition). Independently retain each point of a Poisson random measure of intensity \(\nu\) with location-dependent probability \(q(x)\). The retained and deleted processes are independent Poisson random measures with intensities \(q\nu\) and \((1-q)\nu\). The sum of independent Poisson random measures with intensities \(\nu_1,\nu_2\) is Poisson with intensity \(\nu_1+\nu_2\).

Proof. Conditional on \(N(A)=m\), independent marking splits the count binomially. Evaluating the joint generating function of retained and deleted counts yields the product of Poisson generating functions. Apply this on disjoint simple-function cells and then use the Laplace-functional characterization. Superposition follows by multiplying the two Laplace functionals.

Rare exceedances as a point process

Example. Let \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\) be i.i.d. and choose levels \(u_n\) such that \(nP(X_1>u_n)\to\tau\). Then \[ \sum_{i=1}^n1_{\{X_i>u_n\}} \Rightarrow\operatorname{Poisson}(\tau), \] and therefore \[ P\!\left(\max_{1\le i\le n}X_i\le u_n\right) =P(\text{no exceedance})\longrightarrow e^{-\tau}. \] Recording rescaled exceedance locations as \(\sum_i1_{\{X_i>u_n\}}\delta_{i/n}\) retains more information and leads to Poisson point-process limits in extreme-value theory.
