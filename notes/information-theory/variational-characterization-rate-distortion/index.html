The minimizer in the rate-distortion problem satisfies a Gibbs-type fixed point. This is the analytic core behind the Blahut-Arimoto algorithm.

Lagrange form

Proposition. For slope parameter \(s\le0\), minimizers of \[ I(X;\widehat X)-s\,\mathbb E d(X,\widehat X) \] trace supporting hyperplanes of the convex curve \(R(D)\).

Gibbs form of the test channel

Theorem. At an interior optimum, the reproduction kernel has the form \[ P_{\widehat X\mid X}(\hat x\mid x)=\frac{P_{\widehat X}(\hat x)\exp(s d(x,\hat x))}{\sum_{z}P_{\widehat X}(z)\exp(s d(x,z))},\qquad s\le0. \]

Proof by first variation

Proof. Differentiate the Lagrangian with respect to the conditional probabilities \(P_{\widehat X\mid X}(\cdot\mid x)\), adding a normalization multiplier for each \(x\). Solving the stationarity equations gives the displayed exponential tilt, coupled with the consistency equation for \(P_{\widehat X}\).

Blahut-Arimoto fixed point

Remark. Alternating between the tilted conditional law and its induced reproduction marginal yields the Blahut-Arimoto iteration. The algorithm is secondary here; the important fact is the convex variational structure.

Lagrangian form

Theorem. For \(\lambda\ge0\), define \[ F(\lambda)= \inf_{P_{\widehat X\mid X}} \{I(X;\widehat X)+\lambda\mathbb Ed(X,\widehat X)\}. \] Then \[ R(D)=\sup_{\lambda\ge0}\{F(\lambda)-\lambda D\} \] at continuity points under standard convex duality conditions.

Proof outline. \(R(D)\) is a closed convex decreasing function. The constrained optimization Lagrangian is \(I+\lambda(\mathbb Ed-D)\). Slater's condition gives strong duality for interior distortions, yielding the Legendre-Fenchel representation.

Optimal test-channel equations

Theorem. For finite alphabets and multiplier \(\lambda\ge0\), an optimal test channel satisfies \[ P_{\widehat X\mid X}^*(\widehat x\mid x) = \frac{P_{\widehat X}^*(\widehat x) e^{-\lambda d(x,\widehat x)}} {\sum_{\widetilde x}P_{\widehat X}^*(\widetilde x) e^{-\lambda d(x,\widetilde x)}}. \]

Proof. For fixed reproduction marginal \(Q\), minimize \[ \sum_xP(x)\sum_{\widehat x} W(\widehat x\mid x) \left[ \log\frac{W(\widehat x\mid x)}{Q(\widehat x)} +\lambda d(x,\widehat x)\right] \] subject to each row summing to one. Rowwise Lagrange multipliers give the Gibbs form. At a joint optimum, \(Q\) must equal the marginal induced by \(P W\).

Blahut-Arimoto algorithm

Definition. Initialize \(Q^{(0)}(\widehat x)>0\). Iterate \[ W^{(t+1)}(\widehat x\mid x) \propto Q^{(t)}(\widehat x) e^{-\lambda d(x,\widehat x)}, \] \[ Q^{(t+1)}(\widehat x) =\sum_xP(x)W^{(t+1)}(\widehat x\mid x). \]

Theorem. For finite alphabets and positive initialization, these alternating minimizations converge in objective value to \[ \inf_W\{I(P,W)+\lambda P Wd\}. \]

Proof outline. Introduce auxiliary marginal \(Q\) and objective \[ J(W,Q)= \sum_xP(x)D\{W(\cdot\mid x)\|Q\} +\lambda PWd. \] For fixed \(Q\), the Gibbs update exactly minimizes \(J\) over \(W\). For fixed \(W\), its induced marginal minimizes the average KL over \(Q\). Thus the objective decreases. Compactness gives limit points, and strict convexity on the relevant support makes every stationary limit globally optimal.

Dual lower bound

Theorem. A dual representation is \[ R(D)= \sup_{\lambda\ge0,\;g} \left\{-\lambda D+\mathbb Eg(X)\right\} \] subject to \[ \mathbb E_X \exp\{g(X)-\lambda d(X,\widehat x)\}\le1 \quad\text{for every }\widehat x. \]

Feasible dual pairs certify lower bounds on \(R(D)\); primal test channels certify upper bounds. Matching them gives a numerical optimality certificate.

Slope and distortion

Proposition. At differentiability points, \[ R'(D)=-\lambda^*, \] where \(\lambda^*\) is the optimal distortion multiplier.

Proof. The supporting affine function from the dual is \(F(\lambda^*)-\lambda^*D\). For a differentiable convex function, the unique supporting slope equals its derivative.

Information bottleneck relation

The information bottleneck problem \[ \min_{P_{T\mid X}} I(X;T)-\beta I(T;Y) \] is a rate-distortion problem with a distribution-dependent distortion related to \(D(P_{Y\mid X}\|P_{Y\mid T})\). Its fixed-point equations have the same Gibbs form, explaining the algorithmic kinship with Blahut-Arimoto.

Support transitions

As \(D\) changes, the optimal reproduction marginal can gain or lose support. At these points \(R(D)\) may fail to be twice differentiable, and naive gradient-based numerical continuation can jump. Monitoring KKT residuals and dual gaps is more reliable than inspecting objective decrease alone.
