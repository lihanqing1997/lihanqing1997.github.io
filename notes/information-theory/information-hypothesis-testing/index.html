Hypothesis testing gives relative entropy another operational identity: it is the sharp exponent for one error probability when the other is constrained.

Binary testing errors

Definition. For testing \(P\) against \(Q\), a test \(\phi\in[0,1]\) has type-I error \(\alpha=P\phi\) and type-II error \(\beta=Q(1-\phi)\), depending on the convention for rejection. Only the pair of achievable errors matters.

Chernoff-Stein lemma

Theorem. For iid testing between \(P^n\) and \(Q^n\), if the type-I error is bounded above by a fixed \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), then the optimal type-II error exponent is \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac1n\log\beta_n^\star=D(P\|Q), \] with the convention determined by which hypothesis is protected.

Chernoff information

Theorem. When the two error probabilities are balanced, the optimal Bayesian error exponent is the Chernoff information \[ C(P,Q)=-\inf_{0\le s\le1}\log\sum_x P(x)^sQ(x)^{1-s}. \]

Proof idea by likelihood ratios

Proof. Neyman-Pearson tests threshold the likelihood ratio. Under iid sampling, normalized log-likelihood ratios obey laws of large numbers and large-deviation bounds. Their means under \(P\) and \(Q\) are relative entropies, which determine the exponents.

Binary testing and likelihood ratios

Definition. A randomized test \(\varphi:\mathcal X\to[0,1]\) rejects \(P\) in favor of \(Q\). Type-I and type-II errors are \[ \alpha(\varphi)=P\varphi,\qquad \beta(\varphi)=Q(1-\varphi), \] under this rejection convention.

Neyman-Pearson lemma. Among tests with \(\alpha\le\alpha_0\), a threshold test of the likelihood ratio \(\mathrm{d}Q/\mathrm{d}P\), randomized at the threshold if needed, minimizes \(\beta\).

Proof. Let \(\varphi^*\) reject where \(q>cp\). For any feasible \(\varphi\), \[ \int(\varphi-\varphi^*)(q-cp)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\le0 \] pointwise by the threshold rule. Therefore \(Q\varphi-Q\varphi^*\le c(P\varphi-P\varphi^*)\le0\), so \(\varphi^*\) maximizes power and minimizes type-II error.

Hypothesis-testing divergence

Definition. \[ \beta_{1-\epsilon}(P,Q)= \inf_{\varphi:P\varphi\ge1-\epsilon}Q\varphi, \qquad D_h^\epsilon(P\|Q)= -\log\frac{\beta_{1-\epsilon}(P,Q)}{1-\epsilon}, \] under the acceptance-test convention.

This one-shot divergence directly controls finite-blocklength coding, privacy, and testing. Its iid first-order limit is KL divergence.

Chernoff-Stein lemma

Theorem. For iid testing \(P^n\) against \(Q^n\), for every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1\), \[ \lim_{n\to\infty} -\frac1n\log\beta_{1-\epsilon}(P^n,Q^n) =D(P\|Q). \]

Proof. Accept \(P\) when \[ \frac1n\log\frac{\mathrm{d}P^n}{\mathrm{d}Q^n} \ge D(P\|Q)-\delta. \] Under \(P\), the log likelihood ratio average converges to \(D(P\|Q)\), so type-I error vanishes. On the acceptance set, \(Q^n\le e^{-n(D-\delta)}P^n\), giving the achievable type-II exponent. Conversely, intersect any acceptance set of \(P\)-probability at least \(1-\epsilon\) with the event that log likelihood ratio is at most \(n(D+\delta)\), whose \(P\)-probability tends to one. On this intersection, \(Q^n\ge e^{-n(D+\delta)}P^n\), preventing a larger exponent.

Chernoff information

Theorem. Under equal priors, the optimal Bayesian probability of error in testing \(P^n\) versus \(Q^n\) has exponent \[ C(P,Q)= -\min_{0\le s\le1} \log\int p^s q^{1-s}\,\mathrm{d}\mu. \]

Proof outline. Neyman-Pearson makes Bayes error \(\tfrac12\int\min(p^n,q^n)\). The inequality \(\min(a,b)\le a^sb^{1-s}\) gives the upper exponent. A tilted distribution proportional to \(p^sq^{1-s}\), with \(s\) chosen to balance likelihood-ratio drift, gives the matching lower exponent by change of measure.

Hoeffding tradeoff

If type-I error must decay exponentially at rate at least \(r\), the optimal type-II exponent is a Legendre-type transform of Rényi divergences. This refines Stein's fixed-type-I regime and Chernoff's symmetric Bayes regime into a full error-exponent frontier.

Total variation and minimum error

Theorem. With equal priors, \[ P_e^*= \frac12\{1-\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}. \]

Proof. The likelihood-ratio test assigns each point to the larger density. Its correct probability is \[ \frac12\int\max(p,q)\,\mathrm{d}\mu =\frac12\{1+\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\}. \] Subtract from one.

Bretagnolle-Huber bound

Proposition. \[ 1-\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}} \ge\frac12e^{-D(P\|Q)}. \] Hence every test satisfies \[ \alpha+\beta\ge\frac12e^{-D(P\|Q)}. \]

This converts KL control into a testing lower bound and is often sharper than Pinsker when KL is large and an exponential form is needed.

Composite testing and minimax priors

For composite hypotheses, least-favorable mixtures turn estimation or testing into a difficult simple-versus-simple problem. A prior is a lower-bound device: Bayes risk under any prior is no larger than minimax risk, and mutual information or mixture KL can then quantify indistinguishability.
