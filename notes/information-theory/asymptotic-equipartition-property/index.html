The asymptotic equipartition property is the bridge from single-letter entropy to high-dimensional geometry. It says that most long samples have nearly the same probability.

Typical set

Definition. For iid \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\sim P\), the \(\varepsilon\)-typical set is \[ A_\varepsilon^{(n)}=\left\{x^n:\left|-\frac1n\log P^n(x^n)-H(P)\right|\le\varepsilon\right\}. \]

AEP theorem

Theorem. If \(X_i\) are iid with finite entropy, then \[ -\frac1n\log P^n(X^n)\to H(P) \] in probability and almost surely. Consequently, for large \(n\), \(P^n(A_\varepsilon^{(n)})\to1\) and \[ \exp(n(H-\varepsilon))\lesssim |A_\varepsilon^{(n)}|\lesssim \exp(n(H+\varepsilon)). \]

Proof by the law of large numbers

Proof. Since \(-\log P^n(X^n)=\sum_{i=1}^n -\log P(X_i)\), the normalized information density is an empirical average with mean \(H(P)\). The law of large numbers gives convergence. The cardinality bounds follow because every typical sequence has probability between \(\exp(-n(H+\varepsilon))\) and \(\exp(-n(H-\varepsilon))\).

Operational meaning

Remark. The AEP explains why entropy is the compression rate: a high-probability set has exponential size \(\exp(nH)\), so about \(nH\) nats are necessary and sufficient to name its elements.

Information density of an iid sample

Theorem. If \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\) are iid on a finite alphabet with law \(P\), then \[ -\frac1n\log P(X_1,\ldots,X_n) \longrightarrow H(P) \quad\text{almost surely}. \]

Proof. Product structure gives \[ -\frac1n\log P(X^n) =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n-\log P(X_i). \] The summands are iid and integrable on a finite alphabet, with mean \(H(P)\). Apply the strong law of large numbers.

Weakly typical sets

Definition. \[ \mathcal T_\varepsilon^{(n)} =\left\{x^n: \left|-\frac1n\log P(x^n)-H(P)\right| \le\varepsilon\right\}. \]

Theorem. For every \(\varepsilon>0\), \[ P\{X^n\in\mathcal T_\varepsilon^{(n)}\}\to1, \] and, for large \(n\), \[ (1-o(1))e^{n(H-\varepsilon)} \le|\mathcal T_\varepsilon^{(n)}| \le e^{n(H+\varepsilon)}. \]

Proof. The probability statement is the AEP. Each typical string has probability between \(e^{-n(H+\varepsilon)}\) and \(e^{-n(H-\varepsilon)}\). Since total probability is at most one, the lower probability bound per string gives the cardinality upper bound. Since typical-set probability tends to one, the upper probability bound per string gives the cardinality lower bound.

Strong typicality

Definition. A sequence \(x^n\) is strongly \(\varepsilon\)-typical if its empirical frequencies satisfy \[ |\widehat P_{x^n}(a)-P(a)|\le\varepsilon \] for every symbol \(a\), with no occurrence of symbols having \(P(a)=0\).

Strong typicality controls frequencies of every symbol and extends naturally to joint and conditional typicality. Weak typicality controls only the average log probability. They coincide at the exponential scale for finite iid alphabets but support different technical lemmas.

Joint typicality lemma

Proposition. If \((X^n,Y^n)\) are iid from \(P_{XY}\), they are jointly typical with probability tending to one. If instead \(X^n\sim P_X^n\) and \(Y^n\sim P_Y^n\) independently, then \[ P\{(X^n,Y^n)\text{ jointly typical}\} \doteq e^{-nI(X;Y)}, \] where \(\doteq\) denotes equality at exponential rate.

Proof outline. The first claim is a law of large numbers for joint empirical frequencies. A jointly typical pair has \(P_{XY}^n(x^n,y^n)\approx e^{-nH(X,Y)}\), while under independent sampling its probability is \(P_X^n(x^n)P_Y^n(y^n)\approx e^{-n\{H(X)+H(Y)\}}\). Summing over about \(e^{nH(X,Y)}\) jointly typical pairs gives exponent \(-I(X;Y)\).

Conditional typicality

Theorem. If \(x^n\) is typical and \(Y_i\) are generated independently through a memoryless channel \(P_{Y\mid X}(\cdot\mid x_i)\), then \((x^n,Y^n)\) is jointly typical with probability tending to one.

Proof outline. Within positions carrying each input symbol \(x\), channel outputs are iid with law \(P_{Y\mid X=x}\). Conditional laws of large numbers make each joint frequency close to \(\widehat P_{x^n}(x)P(y\mid x)\), which is close to \(P(x,y)\).

Second-order fluctuations

Proposition. If \[ V(P)=\operatorname{Var}\{-\log P(X)\}<\infty, \] then \[ \sqrt n\left\{-\frac1n\log P(X^n)-H(P)\right\} \rightsquigarrow N\{0,V(P)\}. \]

Proof. This is the classical central limit theorem for the iid self-information variables.

The varentropy \(V(P)\) governs finite-blocklength source coding: at error probability \(\epsilon\), the required log codebook size has correction approximately \(\sqrt{nV(P)}\,\Phi^{-1}(1-\epsilon)\).

Beyond iid sources

The Shannon-McMillan-Breiman theorem extends the AEP to stationary ergodic finite-alphabet processes: \[ -\frac1n\log P(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\to\overline H \quad\text{almost surely}, \] where \(\overline H\) is the entropy rate. Without ergodicity, the limit can be a random component-specific entropy rate rather than a constant.
