Difference-in-differences identifies treatment effects from changes over time, using untreated units to estimate the counterfactual trend of treated units. Its key assumption concerns trends in untreated potential outcomes, not equality of outcome levels.

Two groups and two periods

Definition. Let \(G\in\{0,1\}\) indicate the treated group and \(t\in\{0,1\}\) the period. Treatment occurs only when \(G=t=1\). Write \(Y_t(1)\) and \(Y_t(0)\) for period-\(t\) potential outcomes. The target is \[ \operatorname{ATT} =\mathbb E\{Y_1(1)-Y_1(0)\mid G=1\}. \]

Parallel trends

Definition. Unconditional parallel trends is \[ \mathbb E\{Y_1(0)-Y_0(0)\mid G=1\} = \mathbb E\{Y_1(0)-Y_0(0)\mid G=0\}. \]

Theorem. Under consistency, no anticipation, and parallel trends, \[ \operatorname{ATT} = [\mathbb E(Y_1\mid G=1)-\mathbb E(Y_0\mid G=1)] - [\mathbb E(Y_1\mid G=0)-\mathbb E(Y_0\mid G=0)]. \]

Proof. No anticipation and consistency identify treated-group baseline outcomes with \(Y_0(0)\), control outcomes with \(Y_t(0)\), and treated post outcomes with \(Y_1(1)\). Subtract the control trend from the treated change and replace the treated group's missing untreated trend by the parallel control trend. The remaining contrast is \(\mathbb E\{Y_1(1)-Y_1(0)\mid G=1\}\).

Regression representation

Proposition. In the saturated two-by-two regression \[ Y_{it}=\alpha+\gamma G_i+\lambda\,\mathbf1(t=1) +\tau G_i\mathbf1(t=1)+\varepsilon_{it}, \] the OLS coefficient \(\widehat\tau\) equals the sample difference-in-differences contrast.

Proof. A saturated model reproduces the four cell means. Solving for the interaction coefficient gives \(\overline Y_{11}-\overline Y_{10}- \overline Y_{01}+\overline Y_{00}\).

Conditional parallel trends

Definition. Conditional parallel trends requires \[ \mathbb E\{Y_1(0)-Y_0(0)\mid G=1,X\} = \mathbb E\{Y_1(0)-Y_0(0)\mid G=0,X\}. \]

Theorem. Under conditional parallel trends and group overlap, \[ \operatorname{ATT} = \mathbb E\left[ \Delta Y- \mathbb E(\Delta Y\mid G=0,X) \,\middle|\,G=1\right], \quad \Delta Y=Y_1-Y_0. \]

Proof. Conditional on \(X\), subtract the control group's observed untreated trend from the treated group's observed change. Conditional parallel trends replaces the missing treated untreated trend. Average over the treated covariate distribution.

Event studies

With multiple periods and a common treatment date, an event-study regression estimates coefficients at leads and lags relative to treatment. Pretreatment lead estimates diagnose certain deviations from parallel trends, but failure to reject zero leads does not prove the assumption: tests may have low power, and violations can occur only after treatment.

Staggered adoption and heterogeneous effects

Definition. Let \(G_i=g\) denote first treatment time. The cohort-time effect is \[ \operatorname{ATT}(g,t) =\mathbb E\{Y_t(1)-Y_t(0)\mid G=g\}, \qquad t\ge g. \]

Two-way fixed-effects regressions with staggered adoption can compare already-treated units with newly treated units. With heterogeneous or dynamic effects, the coefficient is a nontransparent weighted average that may include negative weights.

Proposition. Under homogeneous treatment effects and a correctly specified additive untreated outcome model, the two-way fixed-effects coefficient recovers the common effect. Under heterogeneous \(\operatorname{ATT}(g,t)\), it generally does not equal a convex average of them.

Proof outline. Frisch-Waugh-Lovell residualizes treatment with respect to unit and time indicators. The coefficient is the covariance of residualized treatment and outcome divided by residualized treatment variance. Substituting cohort-time treatment effects yields weights proportional to residualized treatment values, which can be negative for already-treated comparison cells.

Group-time estimators

Modern staggered-adoption methods identify each \(\operatorname{ATT}(g,t)\) using not-yet-treated or never-treated controls under a cohort-specific parallel-trends assumption, then aggregate with explicit nonnegative population weights. This preserves the estimand under effect heterogeneity.

Anticipation and treatment reversals

If units change behavior before recorded treatment, leads are themselves affected and cannot serve as untreated baselines. One must define an anticipation window or redefine treatment timing. Reversible or intermittent treatment requires methods for treatment histories rather than irreversible-adoption DiD.

Serial correlation and clustering

Outcomes and treatment are correlated within units and often within assignment clusters. Standard errors should reflect the level at which treatment shocks vary. With few clusters, ordinary cluster-robust asymptotics can be poor; randomization inference, wild cluster bootstrap, or small-sample corrections may be needed.

Sensitivity to differential trends

Proposition. If the treated group's untreated trend differs from the control trend by \(\delta\), then the DiD estimand equals \[ \operatorname{ATT}+\delta. \]

Proof. Repeat the identification algebra without imposing equality of trends. The unmatched trend difference remains additively in the contrast.

This direct bias formula motivates sensitivity analyses that bound post-treatment trend deviations using pretreatment curvature or domain-specific limits rather than treating parallel trends as binary.
