Transportability asks whether a causal effect learned in a study population can identify an effect in a different target population. Internal validity concerns causal comparison within the study; external validity concerns how that comparison changes across populations.

Study participation as a selection process

Definition. Let \(S=1\) denote study membership and \(S=0\) the target population. The target ATE is \[ \tau_0=\mathbb E\{Y(1)-Y(0)\mid S=0\}. \]

Definition. Conditional sample exchangeability in treatment effects is \[ \mathbb E\{Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X,S=1\} = \mathbb E\{Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X,S=0\}. \] A stronger condition asserts \(Y(a)\perp\!\!\!\perp S\mid X\) for each \(a\).

Outcome-standardization transport formula

Theorem. Suppose treatment is randomized or exchangeable within the study, consistency holds, sample exchangeability of potential-outcome means holds given \(X\), and study participation is positive on the target covariate support: \[ P(S=1\mid X=x)>0\quad\text{for }P(X\mid S=0)\text{-almost every }x. \] Then \[ \tau_0= \mathbb E\left[ m_1(X,S=1)-m_0(X,S=1) \,\middle|\,S=0\right]. \]

Proof. Condition the target effect on \(X\). Sample exchangeability replaces the target conditional potential-outcome means by study conditional means. Treatment exchangeability and consistency identify those means as \(m_a(X,S=1)=\mathbb E(Y\mid A=a,X,S=1)\). Average over the target covariate distribution.

Inverse odds of sampling weights

Theorem. Let \(\pi(X)=P(S=1\mid X)\) and suppose treatment probability in the study is \(e_a(X)=P(A=a\mid X,S=1)\). Then \[ \mathbb EY(a)\mid S=0 = \frac{1}{P(S=0)} \mathbb E\left[ \frac{S\mathbf1(A=a)Y}{e_a(X)} \frac{1-\pi(X)}{\pi(X)} \right]. \]

Proof. Condition on \(X\). The treatment weight recovers the study conditional mean \(m_a(X,S=1)\). Multiplication by \(S\{1-\pi(X)\}/\pi(X)\) changes the study covariate measure into the unnormalized target measure: \[ \mathbb E\left[ S\frac{1-\pi(X)}{\pi(X)}g(X)\right] =\mathbb E[(1-S)g(X)]. \] Normalize by \(P(S=0)\) and use the transport formula.

Doubly robust transport

An efficient transport estimator combines study outcome regressions with sampling and treatment weights. Its influence function contains:

  • weighted study residuals for each treatment arm;
  • the target-population conditional effect minus its target mean;
  • normalization terms for target membership.

The resulting estimator can be consistent if either the outcome-effect model or the sampling model is correct, under the precise nuisance structure of the chosen transport functional. Cross-fitting permits flexible estimation when product-rate conditions hold.

Effect modifiers versus prognostic variables

Proposition. If a covariate changes outcome levels but not the conditional treatment effect, differences in its distribution across populations do not change the ATE. If it modifies treatment effect, those distribution differences do matter.

Proof. Write \(m_a(x)=g(x)+a\tau(x)\). The conditional effect is \(\tau(x)\); the common prognostic term \(g(x)\) cancels. Population ATE is the population average of \(\tau(X)\), so only modifiers of \(\tau\) affect transport.

In practice, variables predicting selection and outcome can improve precision even if they are not effect modifiers. Identifying a sufficient transport set is a causal question, not merely a variable-selection exercise.

Selection diagrams

Definition. A selection diagram augments a causal DAG with selection nodes pointing into mechanisms that may differ across populations. Transportability asks whether the target interventional distribution can be expressed using study experimental distributions and target observational distributions.

If a selection node points only into baseline \(X\), standardization over target \(X\) may suffice. If outcome mechanisms differ even after conditioning on measured variables, ordinary transport fails unless target outcome information or additional invariance assumptions are available.

Generalizability versus transportability

Generalizability often refers to a study sample nested within a known target population, so participation probabilities can be estimated from combined data. Transportability may involve separate study and target samples with only partial variable overlap. The formulas are related, but data fusion and normalization differ.

Population overlap

Counterexample. If the study enrolls only \(X=0\) and the target contains \(X=1\), the conditional effect at \(X=1\) is not learned from the study. A smooth outcome model can extrapolate, but nonparametric transport is unidentified. Sampling weights diverge because \(\pi(1)=0\).

Trial engagement effects

Participation can itself alter treatment delivery, adherence, monitoring, or outcomes. Then \(S\) is not merely a sampling indicator: potential outcomes should be indexed by trial engagement, such as \(Y(a,s)\). Transporting \(Y(a,1)\) to nonparticipants differs from estimating the real-world intervention \(Y(a,0)\), and an additional engagement-effect assumption is required.

Data fusion

Some variables are observed only in the trial and others only in the target sample. Identification may combine outcome models from one source, covariate distributions from another, and validation data linking measurements. Each source receives a sampling indicator and contributes specific conditional distributions. Semiparametric efficiency theory can derive optimal combinations once the fused-data model is explicit.

Transport sensitivity

Definition. A conditional transport-bias function is \[ \delta_a(x)= \mathbb E\{Y(a)\mid X=x,S=0\}- \mathbb E\{Y(a)\mid X=x,S=1\}. \] The target mean becomes \[ \mathbb E[m_a(X,S=1)+\delta_a(X)\mid S=0]. \]

Varying \(\delta_1-\delta_0\) over scientifically calibrated ranges directly assesses violations of conditional effect exchangeability.

Reporting an externally valid claim

State the study and target populations, the covariates assumed sufficient for transport, their overlap, which mechanisms are assumed invariant, the target intervention, and whether participation itself changes outcomes. External validity is an identified functional only after these coordinates are fixed.
