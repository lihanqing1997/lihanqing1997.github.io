Learning goal. Identify the roles of dimension, tails, and support structure in empirical transport error.

When a distribution is observed through samples, its empirical measure is the canonical plug-in estimator in Wasserstein space. Consistency is simple, but rates depend sharply on dimension, moment assumptions, and the local structure of the underlying law.

Empirical measures

Definition. For i.i.d. \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\sim\mu\), \[ \widehat\mu_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{X_i}. \] Its integral against \(f\) is the empirical average \(\widehat\mu_nf=n^{-1}\sum_i f(X_i)\).

Strong Wasserstein consistency. If \(\mu\in\mathcal P_p(\mathbb R^d)\), then \[ W_p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)\longrightarrow0 \qquad\text{almost surely}. \]

Proof. Varadarajan's theorem, or the strong law on a countable convergence-determining class, gives \(\widehat\mu_n\Rightarrow\mu\) almost surely. The scalar strong law gives \[ \int|x|^p\,\mathrm{d}\widehat\mu_n(x) =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|X_i|^p \longrightarrow\mathbb E|X_1|^p. \] The topology theorem on Wasserstein distances and the topology of probability measures converts weak convergence plus moment convergence into \(W_p\)-convergence.

Dimension-dependent expected rates

Generic compact-support rates. Suppose \(\mu\) is supported in a bounded subset of \(\mathbb R^d\). Up to constants depending on \(d,p\) and the support diameter, \[ \mathbb E W_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu) \lesssim \begin{cases} n^{-1/2},&d<2p,\\ n^{-1/2}\log(1+n),&d=2p,\\ n^{-p/d},&d>2p. \end{cases} \] Comparable bounds hold under sufficiently high finite moments, with additional tail terms.

Proof for bounded support. Translation and scaling reduce to \([0,1]^d\). Let \(\mathcal D_j\) be its \(2^{jd}\) dyadic cells of side \(2^{-j}\), with a fixed disjoint boundary convention. For any two probability laws \(\alpha,\beta\), a hierarchical matching gives \[ W_p^p(\alpha,\beta)\le C_{d,p}\left[2^{-Lp}+\sum_{j=1}^L2^{-p(j-1)}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal D_j}|\alpha(Q)-\beta(Q)|\right]. \tag{1} \] Here is the construction: at level \(L\), match the common mass inside each cell; every such displacement has length at most \(\sqrt d\,2^{-L}\). Each cell's remaining mass has a single sign and size \(|\alpha(Q)-\beta(Q)|\). In each parent cell, match opposite residual signs between its children, then pass the unbalanced remainder to the next parent. Inductively its size is exactly the parent's mass discrepancy. At level \(j\), at most \(\frac12\sum_{Q\in\mathcal D_j}|\alpha(Q)-\beta(Q)|\) is newly matched, within parents of diameter \(\sqrt d\,2^{-(j-1)}\). The root has zero discrepancy, so all mass is matched. Product couplings of the restricted residual measures implement each matching, proving (1).

For \(\alpha=\widehat\mu_n\), \(\beta=\mu\), binomial variance and Cauchy–Schwarz imply \[ \mathbb E|\widehat\mu_n(Q)-\mu(Q)|\le\sqrt{\mu(Q)/n},\qquad \mathbb E\sum_{Q\in\mathcal D_j}|\widehat\mu_n(Q)-\mu(Q)| \le\min\{2,n^{-1/2}2^{jd/2}\}. \] Let \(L\to\infty\) in the upper bound (the series converges since each fluctuation sum is at most two). Put \(J=\lfloor(\log_2 n)/d\rfloor\). Splitting the series gives \[ \mathbb EW_p^p\le C_{d,p}\left[n^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^J2^{j(d/2-p)}+\sum_{j>J}2^{-jp}\right]. \] The tail is \(O(2^{-Jp})=O(n^{-p/d})\). The first sum is bounded when \(d<2p\), equals \(J\) when \(d=2p\), and is \(O(2^{J(d/2-p)})\) when \(d>2p\). These are exactly the three claimed bounds. Adjusting the constant handles \(n=1\). The unbounded-support moment extensions are external results of Fournier–Guillin; they are not consequences of this bounded-support proof alone.

These are distribution-free rates and need not be sharp for smooth low-dimensional laws. They expose the central obstruction: at fine scales there are exponentially many cells relative to the dimension.

Why the curse of dimensionality appears

Quantization lower bound. If \(\mu\) has a density bounded below on a set of positive \(d\)-dimensional volume, then every probability measure \(\nu_n\) supported on at most \(n\) points satisfies \[ W_p(\mu,\nu_n)\ge c\,n^{-1/d}. \]

Proof. Let the support points be \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\). For any transport to these points, each \(x\) must move at least \(\min_i|x-z_i|\), so \[ W_p^p(\mu,\nu_n)\ge\int\min_i|x-z_i|^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x). \] The union of \(n\) balls of radius \(r\) has volume at most \(n\omega_dr^d\). Choose a measurable region \(A\) with \(0<|A|<\infty\) on which the density is at least \(a>0\), and set \(r=(|A|/(2n\omega_d))^{1/d}\). The union covers at most \(|A|/2\), leaving \(\mu\)-mass at least \(a|A|/2\). On the uncovered part the distance is at least \(r\), giving the lower bound.

Since \(\widehat\mu_n\) has at most \(n\) support points, no estimator constrained to the raw empirical support can beat this geometric approximation barrier uniformly over full-dimensional distributions.

Discrete and low-dimensional exceptions

Finite support. If \(\mu\) is supported on a fixed finite set with pairwise distances bounded above and below, then \(W_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)\) is comparable to the \(\ell^1\) error of the empirical cell probabilities, which is \(O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1/2})\). The effective dimension is the number of atoms, not the ambient Euclidean dimension.

One dimension. The identity \[ W_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu) =\int_0^1|F_n^{-1}(u)-F^{-1}(u)|^p\,\mathrm{d}u \] reduces the problem to quantile-process theory. If \(F\) has a positive smooth density on a compact interval, \(W_p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)=O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1/2})\), much faster than the worst-case bound for \(W_p\) obtained by taking a \(p\)-th root above.

Concentration around the mean

Bounded-support concentration. If \(\mu\) has support diameter \(D\), changing one observation changes \(W_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)\) by at most \(D^p/n\). Therefore McDiarmid's inequality gives \[ \mathbb P\left( W_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu) -\mathbb EW_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)\ge t \right) \le\exp\left(-\frac{2nt^2}{D^{2p}}\right). \]

Proof. If \(X_i\) is replaced by \(X_i'\), couple the two empirical measures by matching all unchanged observations and moving only mass \(1/n\) from \(X_i\) to \(X_i'\). Their \(W_p^p\) distance is at most \(D^p/n\). Disintegrate an optimal coupling with respect to the labeled sample atoms, and replace just the destination source label of mass \(1/n\). Both old and new costs on that part lie in \([0,D^p]\); all other mass is unchanged. Reversing the roles gives the claimed absolute difference. This does not use a triangle inequality for \(W_p^p\), which is not a metric for \(p>1\). Apply McDiarmid's inequality.

Two-sample empirical transport

For independent samples with laws \(\mu,\nu\), \[ |W_p(\widehat\mu_n,\widehat\nu_m)-W_p(\mu,\nu)| \le W_p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)+W_p(\widehat\nu_m,\nu). \] Thus one-sample rates immediately imply consistency and deviation bounds away from the null. Under \(\mu=\nu\), however, the first-order derivative of the distance may vanish and the limiting scale can change. Inference for transport costs, maps, and barycenters treats this nonregular behavior.

Tail assumptions are structural

A small empirical error in weak topology can hide a large transport error in the tails. Finite \(p\)-th moment is sufficient for consistency but generally not for a clean finite-sample rate. Polynomial moments yield polynomial tail terms, exponential moments yield exponential concentration, and compact support removes tail error entirely. Any statistical claim about empirical Wasserstein distance must therefore state both dimension and tail assumptions.

Sources and proof details. Fournier–Guillin, On the rate of convergence in Wasserstein distance of the empirical measure, for finite-sample bounds with explicit moment regimes.

Practice: rates with their assumptions

  1. For \(p=2,d=5\), what generic bounded-support rate does the theorem give for \(\mathbb EW_2^2\)?

    Hint and answer

    It gives \(n^{-2/5}\). Taking a square root bounds \(\mathbb EW_2\) by a constant times \(n^{-1/5}\), using Jensen; the squared-cost rate must not be mislabeled as a distance rate.

  2. For a Bernoulli law with success probability \(a\in(0,1)\), express \(W_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)\).

    Hint and answer

    It is \(|\widehat a-a|\), of stochastic order \(n^{-1/2}\). Hence \(W_p\) has order \(n^{-1/(2p)}\), unlike the smooth one-dimensional quantile regime.
