Learning goal. Identify the roles of dimension, tails, and support structure in empirical transport error.

When a distribution is observed through samples, its empirical measure is the canonical plug-in estimator in Wasserstein space. Consistency is simple, but rates depend sharply on dimension, moment assumptions, and the local structure of the underlying law.

Empirical measures

Definition. For i.i.d. \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\sim\mu\), \[ \widehat\mu_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{X_i}. \] Its integral against \(f\) is the empirical average \(\widehat\mu_nf=n^{-1}\sum_i f(X_i)\).

Strong Wasserstein consistency. If \(\mu\in\mathcal P_p(\mathbb R^d)\), then \[ W_p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)\longrightarrow0 \qquad\text{almost surely}. \]

Proof. Let \(\{f_j:j\ge1\}\subset C_b(\mathbb R^d)\) be a countable convergence-determining class, whose existence on Polish spaces is the probability input. For each \(j\), the strong law gives an event \(A_j\) of probability one on which \[ \int f_j\,\mathrm{d}\widehat\mu_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nf_j(X_i)\longrightarrow\mathbb Ef_j(X_1)=\int f_j\,\mathrm{d}\mu. \] Since \(\mathbb E|X_1|^p<\infty\), another probability-one event \(B\) satisfies \[ \int|x|^p\,\mathrm{d}\widehat\mu_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|X_i|^p\longrightarrow\mathbb E|X_1|^p=\int|x|^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu. \] On the single event \(A=B\cap\bigcap_{j\ge1}A_j\), whose probability is one by countability, the first set of limits implies \(\widehat\mu_n\Rightarrow\mu\). Combined with the moment limit, the Wasserstein topology theorem gives \[ \omega\in A\quad\Longrightarrow\quad W_p(\widehat\mu_n(\omega),\mu)\to0. \] This is the claimed almost-sure convergence.

Dimension-dependent expected rates

Generic compact-support rates. Suppose \(\mu\) is supported in a bounded subset of \(\mathbb R^d\). Up to constants depending on \(d,p\) and the support diameter, \[ \mathbb E W_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu) \lesssim \begin{cases} n^{-1/2},&d<2p,\\ n^{-1/2}\log(1+n),&d=2p,\\ n^{-p/d},&d>2p. \end{cases} \] Comparable bounds hold under sufficiently high finite moments, with additional tail terms.

Proof for bounded support. Translation and scaling reduce to \([0,1]^d\). Let \(\mathcal D_j\) be its \(2^{jd}\) dyadic cells of side \(2^{-j}\), with a fixed disjoint boundary convention. For any two probability laws \(\alpha,\beta\), a hierarchical matching gives \[ W_p^p(\alpha,\beta)\le C_{d,p}\left[2^{-Lp}+\sum_{j=1}^L2^{-p(j-1)}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal D_j}|\alpha(Q)-\beta(Q)|\right]. \tag{1} \] Here is the construction: at level \(L\), match the common mass inside each cell; every such displacement has length at most \(\sqrt d\,2^{-L}\). Each cell's remaining mass has a single sign and size \(|\alpha(Q)-\beta(Q)|\). In each parent cell, match opposite residual signs between its children, then pass the unbalanced remainder to the next parent. Inductively its size is exactly the parent's mass discrepancy. At level \(j\), at most \(\frac12\sum_{Q\in\mathcal D_j}|\alpha(Q)-\beta(Q)|\) is newly matched, within parents of diameter \(\sqrt d\,2^{-(j-1)}\). The root has zero discrepancy, so all mass is matched. More explicitly, if residual positive and negative measures \(a,b\) in a parent have masses \(A,B\), set \(r=\min(A,B)\). When \(r>0\), use the coupling \(r(a/A)\otimes(b/B)\), leaving residuals \((1-r/A)a\), \((1-r/B)b\). One residual is zero, and the remaining mass is \(|A-B|\). The cost is bounded by \(r\,\operatorname{diam}(\text{parent})^p\). Since \(r\le(A+B)/2\), summing over parents gives \[ \text{cost at level }j\le\frac{d^{p/2}}2\,2^{-p(j-1)}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal D_j}|\alpha(Q)-\beta(Q)|. \] The within-leaf cost is at most \(d^{p/2}2^{-Lp}\). Adding the finite collection of constructed couplings proves (1).

For \(\alpha=\widehat\mu_n\), \(\beta=\mu\), binomial variance and Cauchy–Schwarz imply \[ \mathbb E|\widehat\mu_n(Q)-\mu(Q)|\le\sqrt{\mu(Q)/n},\qquad \mathbb E\sum_{Q\in\mathcal D_j}|\widehat\mu_n(Q)-\mu(Q)| \le\min\{2,n^{-1/2}2^{jd/2}\}. \] Indeed, for \(a_Q=\mu(Q)\), \[ \mathbb E|\widehat\mu_n(Q)-a_Q| \le\sqrt{\mathbb E|\widehat\mu_n(Q)-a_Q|^2} =\sqrt{a_Q(1-a_Q)/n}\le\sqrt{a_Q/n}, \] and \(\sum_Q\sqrt{a_Q}\le\sqrt{\#\mathcal D_j}\sqrt{\sum_Qa_Q}=2^{jd/2}\); the deterministic sum is also at most \(\sum_Q(\widehat\mu_n(Q)+\mu(Q))=2\). Taking expectations in (1) and then letting \(L\to\infty\) in the upper bound (the series converges since each fluctuation sum is at most two). Put \(J=\lfloor(\log_2 n)/d\rfloor\). Splitting the series gives \[ \mathbb EW_p^p\le C_{d,p}\left[n^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^J2^{j(d/2-p)}+\sum_{j>J}2^{-jp}\right]. \] The tail is \(O(2^{-Jp})=O(n^{-p/d})\). The first sum is bounded when \(d<2p\), equals \(J\) when \(d=2p\), and is \(O(2^{J(d/2-p)})\) when \(d>2p\). For \(a=d/2-p\ne0\), the exact geometric sum is \[ \sum_{j=1}^J2^{ja}=\frac{2^a(1-2^{Ja})}{1-2^a}, \qquad\sum_{j>J}2^{-jp}=\frac{2^{-(J+1)p}}{1-2^{-p}}. \] For \(a=0\), the first sum is \(J\le d^{-1}\log_2 n\). If \(d<2p\), \(n^{-p/d}\le n^{-1/2}\); if \(d>2p\), \[ n^{-1/2}2^{J(d/2-p)}\le n^{-1/2}n^{(d/2-p)/d}=n^{-p/d}. \] These calculations give precisely the three asserted rates. Adjusting the constant handles \(n=1\). The unbounded-support moment extensions are external results of Fournier–Guillin; they are not consequences of this bounded-support proof alone.

These are distribution-free rates and need not be sharp for smooth low-dimensional laws. They expose the central obstruction: at fine scales there are exponentially many cells relative to the dimension.

Why the curse of dimensionality appears

Quantization lower bound. If \(\mu\) has a density bounded below on a set of positive \(d\)-dimensional volume, then every probability measure \(\nu_n\) supported on at most \(n\) points satisfies \[ W_p(\mu,\nu_n)\ge c\,n^{-1/d}. \]

Proof. Let \(S=\{z_1,\ldots,z_n\}\) contain the support of \(\nu_n\). For every coupling \(\pi\), its second coordinate lies in \(S\) almost surely, so \[ \int|x-y|^p\,\mathrm{d}\pi\ge\int\operatorname{dist}(x,S)^p\,\mathrm{d}\pi =\int\operatorname{dist}(x,S)^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x). \] This lower bound survives the infimum over \(\pi\). Choose measurable \(A\) of finite positive volume with density \(f\ge a>0\) on \(A\), and put \(r=(|A|/(2n\omega_d))^{1/d}\), where \(\omega_d=|B_1|\). Then \[ \left|\bigcup_{i=1}^n B(z_i,r)\right|\le\sum_{i=1}^n\omega_dr^d=|A|/2. \] Thus \(E=A\setminus\bigcup_iB(z_i,r)\) satisfies \(|E|\ge|A|/2\), \(\mu(E)\ge a|A|/2\), and \(\operatorname{dist}(x,S)\ge r\) on \(E\). Consequently \[ W_p^p(\mu,\nu_n)\ge\int_Er^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu \ge\frac{a|A|}{2}\left(\frac{|A|}{2\omega_d}\right)^{p/d}n^{-p/d}. \] Taking the positive \(p\)-th root gives the stated constant \(c>0\), independent of the support locations and weights.

Since \(\widehat\mu_n\) has at most \(n\) support points, no estimator constrained to the raw empirical support can beat this geometric approximation barrier uniformly over full-dimensional distributions.

Discrete and low-dimensional exceptions

Finite support. If \(\mu\) is supported on a fixed finite set with pairwise distances bounded above and below, then \(W_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)\) is comparable to the \(\ell^1\) error of the empirical cell probabilities, which is \(O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1/2})\). The effective dimension is the number of atoms, not the ambient Euclidean dimension.

One dimension. The identity \[ W_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu) =\int_0^1|F_n^{-1}(u)-F^{-1}(u)|^p\,\mathrm{d}u \] reduces the problem to quantile-process theory. If \(F\) has a positive smooth density on a compact interval, \(W_p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)=O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1/2})\), much faster than the worst-case bound for \(W_p\) obtained by taking a \(p\)-th root above.

Concentration around the mean

Bounded-support concentration. If \(\mu\) has support diameter \(D\), changing one observation changes \(W_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)\) by at most \(D^p/n\). Therefore McDiarmid's inequality gives \[ \mathbb P\left( W_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu) -\mathbb EW_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)\ge t \right) \le\exp\left(-\frac{2nt^2}{D^{2p}}\right). \]

Proof. For fixed sample \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\), disintegrate an optimal coupling as \[ \pi=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\delta_{x_j}\otimes K_j,\qquad \frac1n\sum_jK_j=\mu, \] where each \(K_j\) is a probability on the support of \(\mu\). Duplicate sample locations can use the same conditional kernel, so this labeled representation remains valid. Replacing only \(x_i\) by \(x_i'\), set \[ \pi'=\pi-\frac1n\delta_{x_i}\otimes K_i+\frac1n\delta_{x_i'}\otimes K_i. \] This is a coupling of the new empirical law with \(\mu\). If \(F(x)=W_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)\), then \[ \begin{aligned} F(x')-F(x) &\le\int|a-y|^p\,\mathrm{d}\pi'(a,y)-\int|a-y|^p\,\mathrm{d}\pi(a,y)\\ &=\frac1n\int\bigl(|x_i'-y|^p-|x_i-y|^p\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}K_i(y) \le D^p/n. \end{aligned} \] The last bound uses \(0\le|x_i-y|^p,|x_i'-y|^p\le D^p\). Swapping the old and new samples gives \(|F(x')-F(x)|\le D^p/n\). McDiarmid's bounded-differences theorem therefore gives \[ \mathbb P(F-\mathbb EF\ge t) \le\exp\left(-\frac{2t^2}{\sum_{i=1}^n(D^p/n)^2}\right) =\exp(-2nt^2/D^{2p}). \] If \(D=0\), \(F=0\) identically and concentration is trivial.

Two-sample empirical transport

For independent samples with laws \(\mu,\nu\), \[ |W_p(\widehat\mu_n,\widehat\nu_m)-W_p(\mu,\nu)| \le W_p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)+W_p(\widehat\nu_m,\nu). \] Thus one-sample rates immediately imply consistency and deviation bounds away from the null. Under \(\mu=\nu\), however, the first-order derivative of the distance may vanish and the limiting scale can change. Inference for transport costs, maps, and barycenters treats this nonregular behavior.

Tail assumptions are structural

A small empirical error in weak topology can hide a large transport error in the tails. Finite \(p\)-th moment is sufficient for consistency but generally not for a clean finite-sample rate. Polynomial moments yield polynomial tail terms, exponential moments yield exponential concentration, and compact support removes tail error entirely. Any statistical claim about empirical Wasserstein distance must therefore state both dimension and tail assumptions.

Sources and proof details. Fournier–Guillin, On the rate of convergence in Wasserstein distance of the empirical measure, for finite-sample bounds with explicit moment regimes.

Practice: rates with their assumptions

  1. For \(p=2,d=5\), what generic bounded-support rate does the theorem give for \(\mathbb EW_2^2\)?

    Hint and answer

    It gives \(n^{-2/5}\). Taking a square root bounds \(\mathbb EW_2\) by a constant times \(n^{-1/5}\), using Jensen; the squared-cost rate must not be mislabeled as a distance rate.

  2. For a Bernoulli law with success probability \(a\in(0,1)\), express \(W_p^p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)\).

    Hint and answer

    It is \(|\widehat a-a|\), of stochastic order \(n^{-1/2}\). Hence \(W_p\) has order \(n^{-1/(2p)}\), unlike the smooth one-dimensional quantile regime.
