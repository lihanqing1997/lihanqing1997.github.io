Learning goal. Derive a JKO step, its energy estimate, and the distinction between an EVI theorem and a formal PDE.

A gradient flow moves in the direction of steepest energy decrease. In Wasserstein space, the motion is a continuity equation whose velocity is the negative gradient of the first variation. The Jordan--Kinderlehrer--Otto scheme constructs this flow through repeated variational minimization.

Implicit Euler in a metric space

For a smooth function \(F\) on \(\mathbb R^d\), implicit Euler for \(\dot x=-\nabla F(x)\) is \[ x_{k+1}\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_x \left\{F(x)+\frac{|x-x_k|^2}{2\tau}\right\}. \] The optimality condition \(\nabla F(x_{k+1})+(x_{k+1}-x_k)/\tau=0\) is the backward Euler equation. Replacing Euclidean distance by \(W_2\) gives a definition that requires no linear structure on measures.

The JKO minimizing movement

Definition. Starting from \(\mu_\tau^0\in\mathcal P_2\) with finite energy, set \[ \mu_\tau^{k+1}\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mu\in\mathcal P_2} \left\{\mathcal F(\mu)+\frac{W_2^2(\mu,\mu_\tau^k)}{2\tau}\right\}. \] Write \(\bar\mu_\tau(0)=\mu_\tau^0\) and \(\bar\mu_\tau(t)=\mu_\tau^k\) on \(((k-1)\tau,k\tau]\). The step trades energy reduction against transport effort.

One-step existence: a sufficient weak-topology criterion. Suppose \(\mathcal F\) is proper, bounded below, and sequentially lower semicontinuous under weak convergence of probability measures in \(\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\). If the old iterate has finite energy, every step has a minimizer.

Proof. Write \(\eta\) for the old iterate and \(b=\inf\mathcal F> -\infty\). For a minimizing sequence \(\mu_j\), discard finitely many terms so \[ \mathcal F(\mu_j)+\frac{W_2^2(\mu_j,\eta)}{2\tau}\le\mathcal F(\eta)+1. \] Thus \(W_2^2(\mu_j,\eta)\le2\tau(\mathcal F(\eta)+1-b)\). An optimal coupling and \(|x|^2\le2|x-y|^2+2|y|^2\) give a uniform second-moment bound on \(\mu_j\). Markov and Prokhorov yield a weakly convergent subsequence \(\mu_j\Rightarrow\mu\); Portmanteau gives \(\int|x|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu<\infty\). Optimal couplings of \(\mu_j,\eta\) are tight, so the cost liminf inequality gives \(W_2^2(\mu,\eta)\le\liminf_jW_2^2(\mu_j,\eta)\). Adding the assumed energy liminf inequality proves that \(\mu\) attains the infimum. Weak convergence, rather than unproved \(W_2\)-compactness, is what this argument uses.

This is a sufficient criterion, not the most general JKO theorem. Entropy on all of \(\mathbb R^d\) is not bounded below; it needs a separate lower-bound argument or a confining potential. Another valid route assumes compact penalized sublevels in \(W_2\) and \(W_2\)-lower semicontinuity. A mere second-moment bound cannot replace that compactness assumption.

Discrete energy estimates

Energy dissipation. Each JKO step satisfies \[ \mathcal F(\mu_\tau^{k+1}) +\frac{1}{2\tau}W_2^2(\mu_\tau^{k+1},\mu_\tau^k) \le\mathcal F(\mu_\tau^k). \] Hence, for \(N\tau\le T\), \[ \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{W_2^2(\mu_\tau^{k+1},\mu_\tau^k)}{\tau} \le2\{\mathcal F(\mu_\tau^0)-\inf\mathcal F\}. \]

Proof. Use \(\mu=\mu_\tau^k\) as a competitor in the minimization. Sum the resulting inequalities; the energy terms telescope.

Temporal modulus. For grid times \(s=m\tau<n\tau=t\), \[ W_2(\mu_\tau^m,\mu_\tau^n) \le\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}W_2(\mu_\tau^k,\mu_\tau^{k+1}) \le C\sqrt{t-s+\tau}. \] More precisely, writing \(C^2=2(\mathcal F(\mu_\tau^0)-\inf\mathcal F)\), \[ \sum_{k=m}^{n-1}W_2(\mu_\tau^k,\mu_\tau^{k+1}) \le\sqrt{(n-m)\tau}\left(\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}\frac{W_2^2(\mu_\tau^k,\mu_\tau^{k+1})}{\tau}\right)^{1/2} \le C\sqrt{t-s}. \] For arbitrary times of the piecewise constant interpolation, rounding to grid times changes \(t-s\) by at most \(\tau\), yielding the stated relaxed bound.

Euler equation of one step

First-order condition (smooth setting). Assume the energy has the directional expansion \[ \mathcal F((\operatorname{id}+h\xi)_\#\mu)=\mathcal F(\mu)+h\int\nabla(\delta\mathcal F/\delta\rho)\cdot\xi\,\mathrm{d}\mu+o(|h|) \] for every \(\xi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^d;\mathbb R^d)\), with the displayed gradient locally integrable against \(\mu\). Suppose \(\mu_\tau^{k+1}=\rho\,\mathrm{d}x\), \(\mathcal F\) has first variation \(\delta\mathcal F/\delta\rho\), and \(T\) is the optimal map from \(\mu_\tau^{k+1}\) to \(\mu_\tau^k\). Then \[ \frac{T(x)-x}{\tau} = \nabla\!\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal F}{\delta\rho}(x)\right) \quad\text{for }\mu_\tau^{k+1}\text{-almost every }x. \]

Proof. Write \(\mu=\mu_\tau^{k+1}\), \(\eta=\mu_\tau^k\), and \(\mu_h=(\operatorname{id}+h\xi)_\#\mu\). The coupling \((x+h\xi(x),T(x))_\#\mu\) gives the exact upper bound \[ \frac12W_2^2(\mu_h,\eta)-\frac12W_2^2(\mu,\eta) \le h\int(x-T(x))\cdot\xi(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\frac{h^2}{2}\int|\xi|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu. \] Minimality and the assumed energy expansion imply \[ 0\le\mathcal F(\mu_h)+\frac{W_2^2(\mu_h,\eta)}{2\tau}-\mathcal F(\mu)-\frac{W_2^2(\mu,\eta)}{2\tau} \le h A+o(|h|), \] where \(A=\int[\nabla(\delta\mathcal F/\delta\rho)+(x-T(x))/\tau]\cdot\xi\,\mathrm{d}\mu\). Divide by \(h>0\), then by \(h<0\), and let \(h\to0\); the two inequalities force \(A=0\). Testing all \(\xi\) gives the asserted almost-everywhere identity. This proof uses an upper bound for transport cost and both signs of the perturbation; an upper bound alone does not establish differentiability of that cost.

Metric slope and energy dissipation

Definition. The descending metric slope is \[ |\partial\mathcal F|(\mu) =\limsup_{\nu\to\mu} \frac{(\mathcal F(\mu)-\mathcal F(\nu))_+}{W_2(\mu,\nu)}. \] A curve of maximal slope satisfies the energy-dissipation inequality \[ \mathcal F(\mu_T) +\frac12\int_0^T|\dot\mu_t|^2\,\mathrm{d}t +\frac12\int_0^T|\partial\mathcal F|(\mu_t)^2\,\mathrm{d}t \le\mathcal F(\mu_0). \]

For sufficiently regular displacement-convex functionals, the inequality is an equality, and the minimal velocity satisfies \[ v_t=-\nabla\!\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal F}{\delta\rho_t}\right), \qquad \partial_t\rho_t =\nabla\cdot\left( \rho_t\nabla\frac{\delta\mathcal F}{\delta\rho_t} \right). \]

Passage to continuous time

The discrete action estimate gives temporal control but not spatial \(W_2\)-compactness. To obtain a curve of maximal slope one also needs a topology with compact sublevels, lower semicontinuity of the slope, and a chain-rule property (a strong upper gradient). General minimizing-movement theory makes these hypotheses precise; see Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré, Chapters 2–4 and 11. Ordinary geodesic convexity in a general metric space should not be silently substituted for all of them.

A concrete evolution theorem (external result). Let \(V\in C^2(\mathbb R^d)\) satisfy \(\lambda I\le D^2V\le L I\), with \(0<\lambda\le L<\infty\), and define \[ \mathcal F(\rho\,\mathrm{d}x)=\int\rho\log\rho\,\mathrm{d}x+\int V\rho\,\mathrm{d}x, \] extended by \(+\infty\) off densities. For \(\mu_0\in\mathcal P_2\) of finite energy, JKO interpolants converge locally uniformly in \(W_2\) as \(\tau\downarrow0\) to the unique \(EVI_\lambda\) flow. Its densities solve \(\partial_t\rho=\Delta\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla V)\) weakly.

Theorem used and hypotheses. This is the JKO/EVI convergence theorem for entropy plus a uniformly convex potential, from Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré, Chapter 11. The Hessian bounds give quadratic confinement, a globally Lipschitz drift, and \(\lambda\)-convexity along generalized geodesics. Writing \(\mathcal F=\operatorname{KL}(\cdot\|Z^{-1}e^{-V}\mathrm{d}x)-\log Z\) gives properness and the needed lower bound. Finite initial energy and second moment are included in the statement. The theorem supplies convergence and identification of the weak PDE; the discrete action estimate by itself supplies neither. The following solvable case proves a continuous-time limit directly.

A complete JKO limit for Gaussian heat flow. For entropy alone, starting from \(N(0,s_0^2I_d)\), \(s_0>0\), each step is \(N(0,s_k^2I_d)\), where \[ s_{k+1}=\frac{s_k+\sqrt{s_k^2+4\tau}}2. \] The interpolants converge uniformly in \(W_2\) on every bounded time interval to \(N(0,(s_0^2+2t)I_d)\), the heat flow.

Proof. For any competitor of mean \(m\) and \(d\sigma^2=\int|x-m|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu\), nonnegativity of entropy relative to \(N(m,\sigma^2I_d)\) gives \[ \operatorname{Ent}(\mu)\ge-\frac d2\log(2\pi e\sigma^2). \] For every coupling with \(Y\sim N(0,s_k^2I_d)\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\mathbb E[(X-m)\cdot Y]\le d\sigma s_k\), so \[ W_2^2(\mu,N(0,s_k^2I_d))\ge |m|^2+d(\sigma-s_k)^2. \] Both bounds are attained by \(N(0,\sigma^2I_d)\). Measures without densities have infinite entropy; the bounds also exclude a minimizing sequence with \(\sigma\to0\) or \(\sigma\to\infty\). Thus minimization over all \(\mathcal P_2\) reduces to \(m=0\) and the strictly convex scalar function \(-d\log\sigma+d(\sigma-s_k)^2/(2\tau)\). Its derivative vanishes exactly when \(\sigma-s_k=\tau/\sigma\), giving the recurrence.

It follows that \(s_{k+1}\ge s_0\) and \[ s_{k+1}^2-s_k^2=2\tau-\frac{\tau^2}{s_{k+1}^2},\qquad 0\le s_0^2+2k\tau-s_k^2\le\frac{k\tau^2}{s_0^2}. \] If \(t\in((k-1)\tau,k\tau]\subset[0,T+\tau]\), then \[ |s_k^2-(s_0^2+2t)|\le\frac{(T+\tau)\tau}{s_0^2}+2\tau. \] The Gaussian distance is \(\sqrt d\,|s_k-\sqrt{s_0^2+2t}|\), at most \(\sqrt d/(2s_0)\) times this bound. It tends uniformly to zero. Finally, the density with variance \(a(t)=s_0^2+2t\) satisfies \(\partial_t\rho=(-d/a+|x|^2/a^2)\rho=\Delta\rho\), by direct differentiation.

Evolution variational inequalities

Definition. An \(EVI_\lambda\) gradient flow satisfies, for every comparison measure \(\nu\), \[ \frac12\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}W_2^2(\mu_t,\nu) +\frac{\lambda}{2}W_2^2(\mu_t,\nu) \le\mathcal F(\nu)-\mathcal F(\mu_t) \] for almost every \(t>0\).

Contraction and uniqueness. If two curves \(\mu_t,\nu_t\) solve the same \(EVI_\lambda\) flow, then \[ W_2(\mu_t,\nu_t)\le e^{-\lambda t}W_2(\mu_0,\nu_0). \] In particular, the flow is unique and depends continuously on its initial law.

Proof. Work on \([a,b]\subset(0,\infty)\), where the EVI curves are locally absolutely continuous and their energies locally integrable. Set \(D(t,s)=\tfrac12W_2^2(\mu_t,\nu_s)\). For a fixed comparison time, each EVI holds almost everywhere in the other time. Fubini and the two EVI inequalities give, for almost every \((t,s)\), \[ \partial_tD(t,s)+\lambda D(t,s)\le\mathcal F(\nu_s)-\mathcal F(\mu_t),\qquad \partial_sD(t,s)+\lambda D(t,s)\le\mathcal F(\mu_t)-\mathcal F(\nu_s). \] Distances between locally absolutely continuous curves have integrable separate derivatives on compact rectangles. Adding the inequalities is therefore valid also in distributions: \[ (\partial_t+\partial_s)D+2\lambda D\le0. \] Use coordinates \(t=r+q\), \(s=r-q\). For almost every \(q\), the function \(r\mapsto D(r+q,r-q)\) is absolutely continuous and obeys \(\partial_rD\le-2\lambda D\). Integrating after multiplication by \(e^{2\lambda r}\) yields \[ D(b+q,b-q)\le e^{-2\lambda(b-a)}D(a+q,a-q) \] whenever the points remain in the time domain. Choose admissible \(q_j\to0\); continuity of the curves gives the same inequality on the diagonal. Let \(a\downarrow0\), use continuity at the initial laws, and take square roots. This justifies using moving comparisons without assuming their exceptional time sets agree on the diagonal.

Sources and proof details. Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré, Chapters 2–4 and 11, for minimizing-movement convergence and EVI theory; Santambrogio, Optimal transport for applied mathematicians, Chapter 8, for the PDE route.

Practice: implicit steps and contraction

  1. For \(\mathcal F(\mu)=\tfrac12\int|x|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu\), compute one JKO step from an arbitrary \(\mu_k\in\mathcal P_2\).

    Hint and answer

    Minimize \(|x|^2/2+|x-y|^2/(2\tau)\) for each old point \(y\). The minimizer is \(x=y/(1+\tau)\), so \(\mu_{k+1}=(\operatorname{id}/(1+\tau))_\#\mu_k\).

  2. For this energy, compare the discrete contraction factor after \(k\) steps with the continuous flow.

    Hint and answer

    The factor is \((1+\tau)^{-k}\), tending to \(e^{-t}\) when \(k\tau\to t\). This agrees with the \(EVI_1\) contraction rate.
