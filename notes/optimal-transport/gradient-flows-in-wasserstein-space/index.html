Learning goal. Derive a JKO step, its energy estimate, and the distinction between an EVI theorem and a formal PDE.

A gradient flow moves in the direction of steepest energy decrease. In Wasserstein space, the motion is a continuity equation whose velocity is the negative gradient of the first variation. The Jordan--Kinderlehrer--Otto scheme constructs this flow through repeated variational minimization.

Implicit Euler in a metric space

For a smooth function \(F\) on \(\mathbb R^d\), implicit Euler for \(\dot x=-\nabla F(x)\) is \[ x_{k+1}\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_x \left\{F(x)+\frac{|x-x_k|^2}{2\tau}\right\}. \] The optimality condition \(\nabla F(x_{k+1})+(x_{k+1}-x_k)/\tau=0\) is the backward Euler equation. Replacing Euclidean distance by \(W_2\) gives a definition that requires no linear structure on measures.

The JKO minimizing movement

Definition. Starting from \(\mu_\tau^0\in\mathcal P_2\) with finite energy, set \[ \mu_\tau^{k+1}\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mu\in\mathcal P_2} \left\{\mathcal F(\mu)+\frac{W_2^2(\mu,\mu_\tau^k)}{2\tau}\right\}. \] Write \(\bar\mu_\tau(0)=\mu_\tau^0\) and \(\bar\mu_\tau(t)=\mu_\tau^k\) on \(((k-1)\tau,k\tau]\). The step trades energy reduction against transport effort.

One-step existence: a sufficient weak-topology criterion. Suppose \(\mathcal F\) is proper, bounded below, and sequentially lower semicontinuous under weak convergence of probability measures in \(\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\). If the old iterate has finite energy, every step has a minimizer.

Proof. The old iterate is a finite competitor. A minimizing sequence therefore has bounded \(W_2\)-distance to it, and hence bounded second moments. Extract a weakly convergent subsequence. Portmanteau places its limit in \(\mathcal P_2\). The energy is weakly lower semicontinuous by assumption, as is squared transport cost by compactness of plans and Portmanteau. Their sum attains the infimum.

This is a sufficient criterion, not the most general JKO theorem. Entropy on all of \(\mathbb R^d\) is not bounded below; it needs a separate lower-bound argument or a confining potential. Another valid route assumes compact penalized sublevels in \(W_2\) and \(W_2\)-lower semicontinuity. A mere second-moment bound cannot replace that compactness assumption.

Discrete energy estimates

Energy dissipation. Each JKO step satisfies \[ \mathcal F(\mu_\tau^{k+1}) +\frac{1}{2\tau}W_2^2(\mu_\tau^{k+1},\mu_\tau^k) \le\mathcal F(\mu_\tau^k). \] Hence, for \(N\tau\le T\), \[ \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{W_2^2(\mu_\tau^{k+1},\mu_\tau^k)}{\tau} \le2\{\mathcal F(\mu_\tau^0)-\inf\mathcal F\}. \]

Proof. Use \(\mu=\mu_\tau^k\) as a competitor in the minimization. Sum the resulting inequalities; the energy terms telescope.

Temporal modulus. For grid times \(s=m\tau<n\tau=t\), \[ W_2(\mu_\tau^m,\mu_\tau^n) \le\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}W_2(\mu_\tau^k,\mu_\tau^{k+1}) \le C\sqrt{t-s+\tau}. \] The second inequality follows from Cauchy--Schwarz and the discrete action bound.

Euler equation of one step

First-order condition (smooth setting). Assume sufficient regularity and decay to justify the following variations. Suppose \(\mu_\tau^{k+1}=\rho\,\mathrm{d}x\), \(\mathcal F\) has first variation \(\delta\mathcal F/\delta\rho\), and \(T\) is the optimal map from \(\mu_\tau^{k+1}\) to \(\mu_\tau^k\). Then \[ \frac{T(x)-x}{\tau} = \nabla\!\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal F}{\delta\rho}(x)\right) \quad\text{for }\mu_\tau^{k+1}\text{-almost every }x. \]

Proof. Perturb the new measure by \(S_\varepsilon=\operatorname{id}+\varepsilon\xi\). The derivative of \(\mathcal F(S_{\varepsilon\#}\mu)\) is \[ \int\nabla\!\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal F}{\delta\rho}\right)\cdot\xi\,\mathrm{d}\mu. \] Coupling \(S_\varepsilon(x)\) with \(T(x)\) shows that the derivative of \(\tfrac12W_2^2(S_{\varepsilon\#}\mu,\mu_\tau^k)\) is \(\int(x-T(x))\cdot\xi(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\). Stationarity for every \(\xi\) gives the formula. Thus the forward discrete velocity \((x-T(x))/\tau\) is the negative Wasserstein gradient.

Metric slope and energy dissipation

Definition. The descending metric slope is \[ |\partial\mathcal F|(\mu) =\limsup_{\nu\to\mu} \frac{(\mathcal F(\mu)-\mathcal F(\nu))_+}{W_2(\mu,\nu)}. \] A curve of maximal slope satisfies the energy-dissipation inequality \[ \mathcal F(\mu_T) +\frac12\int_0^T|\dot\mu_t|^2\,\mathrm{d}t +\frac12\int_0^T|\partial\mathcal F|(\mu_t)^2\,\mathrm{d}t \le\mathcal F(\mu_0). \]

For sufficiently regular displacement-convex functionals, the inequality is an equality, and the minimal velocity satisfies \[ v_t=-\nabla\!\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal F}{\delta\rho_t}\right), \qquad \partial_t\rho_t =\nabla\cdot\left( \rho_t\nabla\frac{\delta\mathcal F}{\delta\rho_t} \right). \]

Passage to continuous time

The discrete action estimate gives temporal control but not spatial \(W_2\)-compactness. To obtain a curve of maximal slope one also needs a topology with compact sublevels, lower semicontinuity of the slope, and a chain-rule property (a strong upper gradient). General minimizing-movement theory makes these hypotheses precise; see Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré, Chapters 2–4 and 11. Ordinary geodesic convexity in a general metric space should not be silently substituted for all of them.

A concrete evolution theorem. Let \(V\in C^2(\mathbb R^d)\) satisfy \(\lambda I\le D^2V\le L I\), with \(0<\lambda\le L<\infty\), and define \[ \mathcal F(\rho\,\mathrm{d}x)=\int\rho\log\rho\,\mathrm{d}x+\int V\rho\,\mathrm{d}x, \] extended by \(+\infty\) off densities. For \(\mu_0\in\mathcal P_2\) of finite energy, JKO interpolants converge locally uniformly in \(W_2\) as \(\tau\downarrow0\) to the unique \(EVI_\lambda\) flow. Its densities solve \(\partial_t\rho=\Delta\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla V)\) weakly.

Proof outline. Write the energy as relative entropy with respect to the confining Gibbs law, up to a constant. Entropy estimates and moment estimates give compactness in a weak topology. The discrete Euler equation identifies the limiting diffusion; generalized-geodesic convexity yields the EVI and uniqueness. Additional moment estimates justify convergence in \(W_2\). These are substantive steps of the minimizing-movement theorem, rather than consequences of the action bound alone; the cited gradient-flow reference proves them.

Evolution variational inequalities

Definition. An \(EVI_\lambda\) gradient flow satisfies, for every comparison measure \(\nu\), \[ \frac12\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}W_2^2(\mu_t,\nu) +\frac{\lambda}{2}W_2^2(\mu_t,\nu) \le\mathcal F(\nu)-\mathcal F(\mu_t) \] for almost every \(t>0\).

Contraction and uniqueness. If two curves \(\mu_t,\nu_t\) solve the same \(EVI_\lambda\) flow, then \[ W_2(\mu_t,\nu_t)\le e^{-\lambda t}W_2(\mu_0,\nu_0). \] In particular, the flow is unique and depends continuously on its initial law.

Proof. Insert \(\nu_t\) as comparison in the EVI for \(\mu_t\), and \(\mu_t\) in the EVI for \(\nu_t\). The energy differences cancel. A standard doubling-of-time argument yields \[ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}W_2^2(\mu_t,\nu_t)+2\lambda W_2^2(\mu_t,\nu_t)\le0. \] Grönwall's inequality gives the result.

Sources and proof details. Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré, Chapters 2–4 and 11, for minimizing-movement convergence and EVI theory; Santambrogio, Optimal transport for applied mathematicians, Chapter 8, for the PDE route.

Practice: implicit steps and contraction

  1. For \(\mathcal F(\mu)=\tfrac12\int|x|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu\), compute one JKO step from an arbitrary \(\mu_k\in\mathcal P_2\).

    Hint and answer

    Minimize \(|x|^2/2+|x-y|^2/(2\tau)\) for each old point \(y\). The minimizer is \(x=y/(1+\tau)\), so \(\mu_{k+1}=(\operatorname{id}/(1+\tau))_\#\mu_k\).

  2. For this energy, compare the discrete contraction factor after \(k\) steps with the continuous flow.

    Hint and answer

    The factor is \((1+\tau)^{-k}\), tending to \(e^{-t}\) when \(k\tau\to t\). This agrees with the \(EVI_1\) contraction rate.
