Learning goal. Compare intrinsic geometries and distinguish zero distance up to isomorphism from identifiable pointwise correspondence.

Classical transport compares measures whose points already live in a common space and can be charged by a cross-space cost \(c(x,y)\). Gromov--Wasserstein geometry removes that common coordinate system. It compares two metric-measure spaces by asking whether pairs of points have similar internal distances under a probabilistic correspondence.

Metric-measure spaces and their equivalence

Definition 28.1 (Metric-measure spaces and their equivalence). A metric-measure space is a triple \[ \mathsf X=(X,d_X,\mu_X), \] where \((X,d_X)\) is a Polish metric space and \(\mu_X\in\mathcal P(X)\). Two such spaces are isomorphic if there are full-measure subsets \(X_0,Y_0\) and a bijection \(T:X_0\to Y_0\) such that \[ T_\#\mu_X=\mu_Y,\qquad d_Y(Tx,Tx')=d_X(x,x'). \]

Only the intrinsic distance table and its probability weights matter. Rotating, translating, relabeling, or isometrically embedding the entire space does not change its metric-measure isomorphism class.

Distortion of a coupling

Definition 28.2 (Distortion of a coupling). Let \(1\le p<\infty\) and \(\pi\in\Pi(\mu_X,\mu_Y)\). Its \(p\)-distortion is \[ \operatorname{dis}_p(\pi) = \left[ \iint |d_X(x,x')-d_Y(y,y')|^p \,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x',y') \right]^{1/p}. \] In this note the Gromov--Wasserstein distance is \[ \operatorname{GW}_p(\mathsf X,\mathsf Y) = \inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu_X,\mu_Y)} \operatorname{dis}_p(\pi). \] Some references insert a factor \(1/2\); all formulas below use the convention displayed here.

A draw \((x,y)\sim\pi\) declares \(x\) and \(y\) probabilistic counterparts. Drawing two such pairs independently lets the objective compare the within-\(X\) distance \(d_X(x,x')\) with the within-\(Y\) distance \(d_Y(y,y')\). No distance between \(x\) and \(y\) is required.

Theorem 28.3 (Existence of an optimal correspondence). If \(X,Y\) are compact, then the infimum defining \(\operatorname{GW}_p\) is attained.

Proof. Put \(Z=X\times Y\). The coupling set is weakly compact. We first verify continuity of \(\pi\mapsto\pi\otimes\pi\). If \(\pi_n\Rightarrow\pi\), then for \(f,g\in C(Z)\), \[ \int f(z)g(z')\,\mathrm{d}(\pi_n\otimes\pi_n) =\left(\int f\,\mathrm{d}\pi_n\right)\left(\int g\,\mathrm{d}\pi_n\right) \longrightarrow\left(\int f\,\mathrm{d}\pi\right)\left(\int g\,\mathrm{d}\pi\right). \] For arbitrary \(H\in C(Z^2)\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), Stone–Weierstrass supplies \(H_\varepsilon(z,z')=\sum_{j=1}^m f_j(z)g_j(z')\) with \(\|H-H_\varepsilon\|_\infty<\varepsilon\). Hence \[ \limsup_n\left|\int H\,\mathrm{d}(\pi_n\otimes\pi_n)-\int H\,\mathrm{d}(\pi\otimes\pi)\right|\le2\varepsilon. \] Let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\); this proves product weak convergence. Now \[ H((x,y),(x',y'))=|d_X(x,x')-d_Y(y,y')|^p \] is continuous and bounded on the compact product. Its integral, \(\operatorname{dis}_p(\pi)^p\), is therefore continuous. A minimizing sequence has a feasible convergent subsequence, and its limiting objective is the infimum. Taking a positive \(p\)-th root proves attainment for \(\operatorname{GW}_p\).

The metric theorem

Theorem 28.4 (The metric theorem). On isomorphism classes of compact metric-measure spaces, \(\operatorname{GW}_p\) is a metric.

Proof. Nonnegativity and symmetry are immediate. If the spaces are isomorphic through \(T\), then \((\operatorname{id},T)_\#\mu_X\) has zero distortion.

For the triangle inequality, let \(\pi_{XY}\in\Pi(\mu_X,\mu_Y)\) and \(\pi_{YZ}\in\Pi(\mu_Y,\mu_Z)\). The gluing lemma produces a law \(\eta\) on \(X\times Y\times Z\) with these two marginals. Let \(\pi_{XZ}\) be its \((X,Z)\)-marginal. For two independent triples under \(\eta\otimes\eta\), put \(A=d_X(x,x')-d_Y(y,y')\), \(B=d_Y(y,y')-d_Z(z,z')\). Then \[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{dis}_p(\pi_{XZ}) &=\|A+B\|_{L^p(\eta\otimes\eta)}\\ &\le\|A\|_{L^p(\eta\otimes\eta)}+\|B\|_{L^p(\eta\otimes\eta)} =\operatorname{dis}_p(\pi_{XY})+\operatorname{dis}_p(\pi_{YZ}). \end{aligned} \] This is Minkowski's inequality with the correct product marginals and gives \[ \operatorname{dis}_p(\pi_{XZ}) \le \operatorname{dis}_p(\pi_{XY}) + \operatorname{dis}_p(\pi_{YZ}), \] because \(d_X-d_Z=(d_X-d_Y)+(d_Y-d_Z)\). Taking infima proves the triangle inequality.

Finally suppose \(\operatorname{GW}_p=0\). By compactness an optimal \(\pi\) exists with zero distortion. Hence \[ d_X(x,x')=d_Y(y,y') \] for \((\pi\otimes\pi)\)-almost every pair \(((x,y),(x',y'))\). Let \(S=\operatorname{supp}\pi\). If the nonnegative continuous distortion \(H\) were positive at some \((z,z')\in S^2\), neighborhoods \(U,V\) would satisfy \(H\ge\delta>0\) on \(U\times V\). But \[ \int H\,\mathrm{d}(\pi\otimes\pi)\ge\delta\pi(U)\pi(V)>0, \] a contradiction. Thus the distance identity holds for every pair in \(S^2\). If one \(x\) corresponded to both \(y\) and \(\tilde y\), setting the two \(X\)-coordinates equal would give \(d_Y(y,\tilde y)=0\), hence \(y=\tilde y\); similarly in the other direction. Let \(S=\operatorname{supp}\pi\). Its coordinate projections are compact, hence closed, and have full marginal mass. Closedness and full marginal mass imply \(\operatorname{supp}\mu_X\subset p_X(S)\). Conversely, if \((x,y)\in S\), every open neighborhood \(U\ni x\) has \[ \mu_X(U)=\pi(U\times Y)>0, \] so \(x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu_X\). Hence \(p_X(S)=\operatorname{supp}\mu_X\); the identical argument gives \(p_Y(S)=\operatorname{supp}\mu_Y\). The uniqueness of each fiber just proved makes \(S\) the graph of a bijection \(T\) between these supports. The distance identity makes \(T\) an isometry, hence continuous with continuous inverse. Finally, for every Borel \(B\), \[ \mu_Y(B)=\pi(X\times B)=\pi(\{(x,T(x)):T(x)\in B\})=\mu_X(T^{-1}(B)). \] Thus it is measure preserving, proving the zero-distance implication with the required measurability.

Finite spaces and a quadratic assignment problem

Let \(X=\{x_i\}_{i=1}^n\), \(Y=\{y_j\}_{j=1}^m\), with weights \(a_i,b_j\), and distance matrices \(D^X,D^Y\). A coupling is a nonnegative matrix \(P\) satisfying \(P\mathbf1_m=a\) and \(P^\top\mathbf1_n=b\). Then \[ \operatorname{GW}_p^p = \min_{P} \sum_{i,k=1}^n\sum_{j,\ell=1}^m |D^X_{ik}-D^Y_{j\ell}|^pP_{ij}P_{k\ell}. \] Unlike discrete Kantorovich transport, this objective is quadratic and generally nonconvex.

Proposition 28.5 (Quadratic expansion). For \(p=2\), \[ \operatorname{dis}_2^2(P) = \sum_{i,k}(D^X_{ik})^2a_ia_k + \sum_{j,\ell}(D^Y_{j\ell})^2b_jb_\ell - 2\sum_{i,k,j,\ell} D^X_{ik}D^Y_{j\ell}P_{ij}P_{k\ell}. \] Indeed, expand \((D^X_{ik}-D^Y_{j\ell})^2\) and use the marginal identities: \[ \sum_{j,\ell}P_{ij}P_{k\ell}=\left(\sum_jP_{ij}\right)\left(\sum_\ell P_{k\ell}\right)=a_ia_k, \quad \sum_{i,k}P_{ij}P_{k\ell}=b_jb_\ell. \] The two square terms are therefore fixed by the marginals; the cross term is exactly the displayed four-index sum. Thus minimizing distortion is equivalent to maximizing that correlation.

If both spaces have \(n\) points with uniform weights and \(P\) is restricted to scaled permutation matrices \(P_\sigma/n\), this becomes a quadratic assignment problem. The relaxed coupling polytope allows soft correspondences and spaces with different numbers of points, but it does not make the objective convex.

Intrinsic lower bounds

Let \[ \rho_X=(d_X)_\#(\mu_X\otimes\mu_X) \] be the distribution of the distance between two independent points of \(\mathsf X\), and define \(\rho_Y\) similarly.

Proposition 28.6 (Distance-distribution bound). \[ W_p(\rho_X,\rho_Y) \le \operatorname{GW}_p(\mathsf X,\mathsf Y). \]

Proof. For \(\pi\in\Pi(\mu_X,\mu_Y)\), define \[ \zeta=(d_X(x,x'),d_Y(y,y'))_\#(\pi\otimes\pi). \] Its first marginal is \(\rho_X\), because the \((x,x')\)-marginal of \(\pi\otimes\pi\) is \(\mu_X\otimes\mu_X\); its second is similarly \(\rho_Y\). Therefore \[ \begin{aligned} W_p(\rho_X,\rho_Y)^p &\le\int|r-s|^p\,\mathrm{d}\zeta(r,s)\\ &=\iint|d_X(x,x')-d_Y(y,y')|^p\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x',y') =\operatorname{dis}_p(\pi)^p. \end{aligned} \] Taking the infimum over \(\pi\) and then the positive \(p\)-th root proves the bound.

A sharper hierarchy can compare pointwise eccentricities \[ e_X(x)=\left(\int d_X(x,x')^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu_X(x')\right)^{1/p}. \] Reverse Minkowski implies that the Wasserstein distance between \((e_X)_\#\mu_X\) and \((e_Y)_\#\mu_Y\) is also bounded by \(\operatorname{GW}_p\). Such invariants are inexpensive certificates, but equality need not hold because they discard higher-order geometry.

Gromov--Wasserstein geodesics

Theorem 28.7 (Displacement interpolation of metrics). Let \(\pi^\star\) be optimal between compact spaces \(\mathsf X,\mathsf Y\). For \(0<t<1\), put \[ \mathsf Z_t = (X\times Y,d_t,\pi^\star),\qquad d_t((x,y),(x',y')) = (1-t)d_X(x,x')+t\,d_Y(y,y'). \] With the natural endpoint identifications, \[ \operatorname{GW}_p(\mathsf Z_s,\mathsf Z_t) = |t-s|\,\operatorname{GW}_p(\mathsf X,\mathsf Y). \]

Proof. Put \(D=\operatorname{GW}_p(\mathsf X,\mathsf Y)\). For \(0<t<1\), \(d_t\) is a metric: it is symmetric, has the triangle inequality termwise, and \(d_t(z,z')=0\) forces both coordinates of \(z,z'\) to agree. At \(t=0,1\) its metric quotient is the corresponding endpoint space with the correct projected measure.

Couple two intermediate spaces by the diagonal law \((z,z)_\#\pi_*\), where \(z=(x,y)\). For independent \(z,z'\sim\pi_*\), \[ d_t(z,z')-d_s(z,z')=(t-s)(d_Y(y,y')-d_X(x,x')), \] so this coupling has distortion \[ \left(\iint|d_t-d_s|^p\,\mathrm{d}\pi_*\,\mathrm{d}\pi_*\right)^{1/p} =|t-s|\operatorname{dis}_p(\pi_*)=|t-s|D. \] Thus \(\operatorname{GW}_p(\mathsf Z_s,\mathsf Z_t)\le|t-s|D\), also at the quotient endpoints. For \(0\le s\le t\le1\), triangle inequality gives \[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{GW}_p(\mathsf Z_s,\mathsf Z_t) &\ge D-\operatorname{GW}_p(\mathsf X,\mathsf Z_s)-\operatorname{GW}_p(\mathsf Z_t,\mathsf Y)\\ &\ge D-sD-(1-t)D=(t-s)D. \end{aligned} \] The bounds agree; symmetry handles \(s>t\).

The interpolation changes the metric itself rather than moving points through a fixed ambient space. This is the intrinsic analogue of displacement interpolation.

Gromov--Monge maps and relaxation

If a measure-preserving map \(T:X\to Y\) exists, its distortion is \[ \left[ \iint |d_X(x,x')-d_Y(Tx,Tx')|^p \,\mathrm{d}\mu_X(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_X(x') \right]^{1/p}. \] Minimizing over such \(T\) is the Gromov--Monge problem. Replacing the graph coupling \((\operatorname{id},T)_\#\mu_X\) by an arbitrary \(\pi\) gives the Gromov--Wasserstein relaxation. As in classical transport, relaxation ensures compactness, but here even the relaxed objective remains nonlinear in \(\pi\).

Entropic computation and its limitation

A common numerical objective is \[ \operatorname{GW}_{p,\varepsilon}^p = \inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu_X,\mu_Y)} \left\{ \operatorname{dis}_p^p(\pi) + \varepsilon\operatorname{KL} (\pi\,\|\,\mu_X\otimes\mu_Y) \right\}. \] Linearizing the quadratic distortion at the current coupling produces an ordinary entropic transport subproblem, which can be solved by Sinkhorn scaling. Alternating or conditional-gradient schemes repeat this step.

Proposition 28.8 (What entropy does not fix). Entropic regularization makes each linearized subproblem smooth and numerically stable, but it does not in general make the full Gromov--Wasserstein objective convex. Some special regimes admit global guarantees, including sufficiently strong regularization under quantitative hypotheses; these require separate analysis. Iterations may converge to different stationary points from different initializations; a small primal update is not a certificate of global optimality.

Features, barycenters, and structured data

If points also carry comparable features \(f_X(x),f_Y(y)\) in a common feature space with distance \(\delta\), fused Gromov--Wasserstein transport minimizes \[ (1-\alpha)\int\delta(f_X(x),f_Y(y))^p\,\mathrm{d}\pi + \alpha\iint|d_X(x,x')-d_Y(y,y')|^p \,\mathrm{d}\pi\,\mathrm{d}\pi. \] The endpoints \(\alpha=0\) and \(\alpha=1\) retain only feature matching and only intrinsic geometry, respectively.

A Gromov--Wasserstein barycenter minimizes \[ \mathsf Z\longmapsto \sum_{r=1}^R\lambda_r \operatorname{GW}_p^p(\mathsf X_r,\mathsf Z) \] over metric-measure spaces in a chosen model class. Unlike a Wasserstein barycenter, both the representative points and their internal distance matrix may be unknown.

Empirical metric-measure spaces

Proposition 28.9 (Comparison on a common ambient space). If \(\mu,\nu\in\mathcal P_p(X)\) on the same metric space, then \[ \operatorname{GW}_p((X,d,\mu),(X,d,\nu)) \le 2W_p(\mu,\nu). \]

Proof. For any \(\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\), draw \((x,y),(x',y')\) independently with law \(\gamma\). The two triangle inequalities \[ d(x,x')\le d(x,y)+d(y,y')+d(y',x'), \quad d(y,y')\le d(y,x)+d(x,x')+d(x',y') \] imply \(|d(x,x')-d(y,y')|\le d(x,y)+d(x',y')\). Minkowski on \(\gamma\otimes\gamma\) gives \[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{dis}_p(\gamma) &\le\|d(x,y)+d(x',y')\|_{L^p(\gamma\otimes\gamma)}\\ &\le\|d(x,y)\|_{L^p(\gamma\otimes\gamma)}+\|d(x',y')\|_{L^p(\gamma\otimes\gamma)}\\ &=2\left(\int d(x,y)^p\,\mathrm{d}\gamma\right)^{1/p}. \end{aligned} \] Taking the infimum over \(\gamma\) proves \(\operatorname{GW}_p\le2W_p\).

Corollary 28.10 (Empirical consistency). If \(X\) is compact and \(\widehat\mu_n=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{X_i}\) for i.i.d. \(X_i\sim\mu\), then \[ \operatorname{GW}_p((X,d,\widehat\mu_n),(X,d,\mu)) \longrightarrow0 \quad\text{almost surely}. \] Indeed, compactness gives \(W_p(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)\to0\).

Consistency does not by itself give a dimension-free rate, an identifiable pointwise correspondence, or reliable global optimization. Statistical error, symmetry-induced nonidentifiability, and numerical nonconvexity are three separate sources of uncertainty.

Relation to Gromov--Hausdorff geometry

Gromov--Hausdorff distance controls the largest distortion of a correspondence; Gromov--Wasserstein distance controls an \(L^p\) average distortion weighted by probability. The former is sensitive to every point, while the latter may ignore sets of very small mass. This distinction explains both the statistical robustness and the possible geometric blindness of the measure-weighted formulation.

Exercises

intrinsic structure and symmetry

  1. Exercise 28.1. Compare two equally weighted two-point spaces with respective distances \(a,b>0\). Prove \(\operatorname{GW}_p=2^{-1/p}|a-b|\).

    Hint

    Parameterize the two-by-two coupling and compare the distances of two independently drawn matched pairs.

    Solution

    Each distance law is \((\delta_0+\delta_a)/2\) or \((\delta_0+\delta_b)/2\). The distance-distribution lower bound gives the claimed value, attained by either bijection.

  2. Exercise 28.2. If a finite metric-measure space has a nontrivial measure-preserving symmetry, is its zero-distortion correspondence to itself unique?

    Hint

    Compose the identity correspondence with a measure-preserving isometry.

    Solution

    No. Both identity and that symmetry induce zero-distortion plans. Zero distance identifies an isomorphism class, not a unique alignment.
