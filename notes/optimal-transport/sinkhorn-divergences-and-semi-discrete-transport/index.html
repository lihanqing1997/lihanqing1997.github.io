Learning goal. Explain debiasing and recover a semi-discrete map from cell masses.

Two complementary constructions improve the usability of transport without abandoning its geometry. Sinkhorn divergences remove the self-interaction bias of entropy. Semi-discrete transport keeps the source continuous and the target finite, converting the dual problem into a concave optimization over weighted geometric cells, differentiable when ties have zero source mass.

Debiasing entropic transport

Definition. The Sinkhorn divergence is \[ S_\varepsilon(\mu,\nu) = \operatorname{OT}_\varepsilon(\mu,\nu) -\frac12\operatorname{OT}_\varepsilon(\mu,\mu) -\frac12\operatorname{OT}_\varepsilon(\nu,\nu). \] The subtraction makes every self-comparison zero. It does not remove every finite-regularization bias relative to ordinary transport.

Definiteness (external theorem). Let \(X\subset\mathbb R^d\) be compact, \(c(x,y)=|x-y|^q\) with \(0<q\le2\), and \(\varepsilon>0\). For probability measures on \(X\), \(S_\varepsilon(\mu,\nu)\ge0\) and \[ S_\varepsilon(\mu,\nu)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mu=\nu. \] Symmetry follows when the cost is symmetric.

This is the positive-kernel definiteness theorem of Feydy et al., Theorem 1; its kernel-positivity argument is an external input. The statement is cost dependent: debiasing an arbitrary asymmetric or non-negative-type cost need not produce a metric-like divergence. Even in the favorable case, the triangle inequality is not generally asserted.

Interpolation between transport and kernel discrepancies

Two limiting regimes. For the compact Euclidean setting above, \[ \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}S_\varepsilon(\mu,\nu) =\operatorname{OT}_0(\mu,\nu), \] while \[ \lim_{\varepsilon\to\infty}S_\varepsilon(\mu,\nu) = \int c\,\mathrm{d}\mu\,\mathrm{d}\nu -\frac12\int c\,\mathrm{d}\mu\,\mathrm{d}\mu -\frac12\int c\,\mathrm{d}\nu\,\mathrm{d}\nu. \] The latter is an energy-distance or maximum-mean-discrepancy form when \(-c\) induces a positive kernel modulo constants.

Proof. The preceding chapter proves convergence of the entropic values for each fixed pair of compactly supported laws. At zero regularization the diagonal coupling gives \(\operatorname{OT}_0(\alpha,\alpha)=0\). Applying the small-regularization theorem to all three terms therefore gives the first limit. At large regularization that chapter proves \(\operatorname{OT}_\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta)\to\int c\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}\beta\), including the fact that the entropy penalty tends to zero. Substituting this convergence for \((\mu,\nu),(\mu,\mu),(\nu,\nu)\) gives the second displayed expression. Convergence of the plans alone would not control the entropy penalties, which are multiplied by \(\varepsilon\).

Statistical smoothing and bias

For fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), positive kernels make dual potentials smooth functions of the marginals, so \(S_\varepsilon(\widehat\mu_n,\widehat\nu_m)\) can have root-sample Gaussian limits away from degenerate cases under substantially weaker dimensional conditions than unregularized transport. If \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_n\downarrow0\), inference must balance three terms: \[ \text{total error} = \text{sampling error} +\text{entropic bias} +\text{numerical error}. \] Sending \(\varepsilon_n\) to zero too quickly restores the nonsmooth and dimension-sensitive behavior of ordinary transport.

The semi-discrete setting

Definition. Let the source be \(\mu=\rho(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\) on \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d\), and let \[ \nu=\sum_{j=1}^Nb_j\delta_{y_j},\qquad b_j>0,\quad\sum_jb_j=1. \] For the results below assume \(\Omega\) compact, \(\mu\) absolutely continuous, the sites distinct, and \(c(x,y)=\tfrac12|x-y|^2\). Ties lie in hyperplanes of zero source mass. Semi-discrete transport seeks a measurable partition of \(\Omega\) into cells of masses \(b_j\), sending each cell to \(y_j\).

Laguerre cells and the dual objective

Definition. For weights \(\psi\in\mathbb R^N\), define \[ L_j(\psi) = \{x:c(x,y_j)-\psi_j \le c(x,y_k)-\psi_k\ \text{for every }k\}. \] These are Laguerre cells. For quadratic cost they are power cells, bounded by affine hyperplanes.

Semi-discrete dual. The dual problem is the unconstrained concave maximization \[ \max_{\psi\in\mathbb R^N} \Phi(\psi), \qquad \Phi(\psi) = \sum_{j=1}^Nb_j\psi_j +\int_\Omega\min_j\{c(x,y_j)-\psi_j\}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x). \] At a maximizer, \(\mu(L_j(\psi))=b_j\) for every \(j\), and the optimal map is \[ T(x)=y_j\quad\text{for }x\in L_j(\psi). \]

Proof. In the Kantorovich dual, optimize the source potential pointwise: \[ \varphi(x)=\min_j\{c(x,y_j)-\psi_j\}. \] Substitution gives \(\Phi\). At almost every \(x\), the minimizing index is unique, because the tie sets are hyperplanes and \(\mu\) has a density. For a coordinate perturbation of size \(h\), the difference quotient of the minimum is bounded by one and converges to \(-\mathbf1_{L_j}(x)\). Dominated convergence therefore yields \[ \frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\psi_j} =b_j-\mu(L_j(\psi)). \] Thus stationarity is exactly the cell-mass condition. Complementary slackness then sends each cell to its defining target point.

Power diagrams for quadratic cost

For \(c(x,y)=\tfrac12|x-y|^2\), \[ L_j(\psi) = \left\{ x:x\cdot(y_k-y_j) \le\frac{|y_k|^2-|y_j|^2}{2}+\psi_j-\psi_k \ \forall k \right\}. \] Each cell is the intersection of \(\Omega\) with a convex polyhedron; the cell is convex when \(\Omega\) is convex. Changing \(\psi_j\) moves its facets without moving the sites \(y_j\); the weights are exactly the variables required to match prescribed cell masses.

Hessian and Newton geometry

Hessian structure. Assume \(\Omega\) is a bounded polytope, \(\rho\) is continuous and positive, all cells have positive mass, and the facets have stable combinatorics near the chosen weights. Assume intersections of distinct facet hyperplanes, and their intersections with boundary facets when they are not coincident, have codimension at least two; exclude coincident moving and boundary facets. For \(i\ne j\), \[ \frac{\partial^2\Phi}{\partial\psi_i\partial\psi_j} = \int_{F_{ij}} \frac{\rho(x)} {|\nabla_xc(x,y_i)-\nabla_xc(x,y_j)|} \,\mathrm{d}\mathcal H^{d-1}(x), \] where \(F_{ij}=L_i\cap L_j\) is the common facet, and \[ \frac{\partial^2\Phi}{\partial\psi_i^2} =-\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{\partial^2\Phi}{\partial\psi_i\partial\psi_j}. \] Thus the Hessian is a negative weighted graph Laplacian.

Proof. For \(i\ne j\), put \(g_{ij}(x)=c(x,y_i)-c(x,y_j)\). The inequality defining cell \(i\) against \(j\) is \(g_{ij}(x)\le\psi_i-\psi_j\). Under \(\psi_j\mapsto\psi_j+h\), its threshold decreases by \(h\); cell \(i\) loses a slab of normal thickness \(h/|\nabla g_{ij}|\) along \(F_{ij}\). The coarea formula expresses that slab's mass as \[ \int_{\psi_i-\psi_j-h}^{\psi_i-\psi_j} \left(\int_{\{g_{ij}=s\}\cap L_i\text{ against the other sites}} \frac{\rho}{|\nabla g_{ij}|}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal H^{d-1}\right)\mathrm{d}s. \] The regularity assumptions make the inner integral continuous at the facet level. Changes near intersections of facets have volume \(O(h^2)\), since they have codimension at least two; boundedness of \(\rho\) makes their mass \(o(|h|)\). Dividing by \(h\) gives \[ \partial_{\psi_j}\mu(L_i)=-w_{ij},\qquad w_{ij}=\int_{F_{ij}}\frac{\rho}{|\nabla g_{ij}|}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal H^{d-1}. \] Since \(\partial_{\psi_i}\Phi=b_i-\mu(L_i)\), the off-diagonal Hessian entry is \(w_{ij}\). Invariance of every cell under \(\psi\mapsto\psi+a\mathbf1\) gives the diagonal entry \(-\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}\). Hence for every \(z\in\mathbb R^N\), \[ z^\top D^2\Phi\,z=-\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}(z_i-z_j)^2\le0. \] Its nullspace consists of vectors constant on each connected component of the positive-weight adjacency graph. In particular it consists only of constants when that graph is connected.

After fixing a gauge such as \(\sum_j\psi_j=0\), Newton's method solves \[ D^2\Phi(\psi)\,\Delta\psi=-\nabla\Phi(\psi). \] Damping keeps all cells nonempty and ensures ascent. The Laplacian structure explains both fast local convergence and ill-conditioning when cells nearly disappear.

A one-dimensional cell calculation

Example. Let \(y_1<y_2\) and \(c(x,y)=\tfrac12|x-y|^2\). The boundary \(r\) between the two cells satisfies \[ \frac12|r-y_1|^2-\psi_1 = \frac12|r-y_2|^2-\psi_2, \] hence \[ r=\frac{y_1+y_2}{2}+\frac{\psi_1-\psi_2}{y_2-y_1}. \] The mass condition \(F_\mu(r)=b_1\) determines the weight difference. Thus the semi-discrete dual recovers the quantile boundary exactly.

Consistency under discretization

If discrete targets \(\nu_N\to\nu\) in \(W_2\), stability gives convergence of semi-discrete optimal values. Under uniqueness and regularity, the cell maps converge in measure to the Brenier map. This makes semi-discrete transport both a geometric solver and a principled map estimator, provided the discretization and density estimation errors are controlled.

Sources and proof details. Feydy et al., for finite-regularization definiteness; Peyré–Cuturi, Computational optimal transport, Chapter 5, for semi-discrete cells and optimization.

Practice: self-costs and cell boundaries

  1. For uniform source on \([0,1]\), target sites 0 and 1, and weights \(b=(1/4,3/4)\), find the quadratic semi-discrete map.

    Hint and answer

    The cell boundary is the source quantile \(r=1/4\). Send \([0,1/4]\) to 0 and the rest to 1. With cost \(|x-y|^2/2\), the dual difference is \(\psi_1-\psi_2=-1/4\).

  2. For quadratic ground cost, simplify the large-\(\varepsilon\) limit of \(S_\varepsilon\). Can it distinguish all laws?

    Hint and answer

    Expansion leaves \(|m_\mu-m_\nu|^2\). Distinct laws with the same mean have zero limiting discrepancy, although finite-\(\varepsilon\) definiteness holds under the stated assumptions.
