Learning goal. Reduce a path-space entropy problem to its endpoints and distinguish diffusion drift from current velocity.

A Schrödinger bridge asks for the most likely random evolution connecting two unlikely endpoint distributions under a reference diffusion. It is a path-space entropy projection whose endpoint reduction is entropic transport and whose zero-noise limit is classical optimal transport.

The path-space problem

Definition. Let \(R^\varepsilon\) be a reference Markov law on path space \(\Omega=C([0,1];\mathbb R^d)\), typically Brownian motion with generator \(\tfrac\varepsilon2\Delta\). For prescribed endpoints \(\mu_0,\mu_1\), the Schrödinger problem is \[ \inf_{P\in\mathcal P(\Omega)} \left\{ \operatorname{KL}(P\,\|\,R^\varepsilon): P_0=\mu_0,\ P_1=\mu_1 \right\}, \] where \(P_t=(e_t)_\#P\).

The minimizer changes the reference dynamics as little as possible in relative entropy while enforcing both endpoint laws. It is not conditioned on two fixed points, but on two full marginal distributions.

Reduction to a static entropy projection

Static reduction. Let \(R^\varepsilon_{01}\) be the joint endpoint law of the reference process. Then \[ \inf_{\substack{P:P_0=\mu_0\\P_1=\mu_1}} \operatorname{KL}(P\,\|\,R^\varepsilon) = \inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu_0,\mu_1)} \operatorname{KL}(\pi\,\|\,R^\varepsilon_{01}). \] If \(\pi^\star\) solves the static problem, the dynamic minimizer is \[ P^\star(\mathrm{d}\omega) =\int R^\varepsilon(\mathrm{d}\omega\mid X_0=x,X_1=y) \,\pi^\star(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}y). \]

Proof. Disintegrate \(P\) and \(R^\varepsilon\) with respect to \((X_0,X_1)\). The chain rule for entropy gives \[ \operatorname{KL}(P\,\|\,R^\varepsilon) = \operatorname{KL}(P_{01}\,\|\,R^\varepsilon_{01}) +\int \operatorname{KL}\!\left( P^{xy}\,\|\,R^{\varepsilon,xy} \right) \,\mathrm{d}P_{01}(x,y). \] The second term is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when the conditional path law given endpoints is the reference bridge. Minimizing leaves only the endpoint coupling problem.

Relation to entropic optimal transport

Fix Brownian motion with generator \(\varepsilon\Delta/2\) and initial probability density \(r_0>0\). Its endpoint density is \[ r_0(x)(2\pi\varepsilon)^{-d/2} \exp\{-|x-y|^2/(2\varepsilon)\}. \] If \(\mu_i=\rho_i\,\mathrm{d}x\) have finite entropy and the following integrals are finite, then for \(\pi\in\Pi(\mu_0,\mu_1)\), \[ \begin{aligned} \operatorname{KL}(\pi\|R^\varepsilon_{01}) ={}&\operatorname{KL}(\pi\|\mu_0\otimes\mu_1) +\frac1{2\varepsilon}\int|x-y|^2\,\mathrm{d}\pi\\ &+\operatorname{KL}(\mu_0\|r_0\,\mathrm{d}x) +\operatorname{Ent}(\mu_1) +\frac d2\log(2\pi\varepsilon). \end{aligned} \] Only the first two terms vary with the coupling. Multiplying by \(\varepsilon\) therefore gives exactly the entropic problem with cost \(|x-y|^2/2\), up to fixed endpoint constants. The Brownian initial law must be specified: “Brownian motion with Lebesgue initial law” would be a sigma-finite reference, not a probability law.

Schrödinger potentials

In the strictly positive finite-state model, entropy duality gives the unique optimizer in the form \[ \pi^\star(x,y)=f(x)g(y)R^\varepsilon_{01}(x,y), \] with positive factors unique up to reciprocal scaling. Enforcing marginals gives \[ f(x)\mathbb E_R[g(X_1)\mid X_0=x] =\frac{\mu_0(x)}{R_0(x)},\qquad g(y)\mathbb E_R[f(X_0)\mid X_1=y] =\frac{\mu_1(y)}{R_1(y)}. \] These are the Schrödinger equations; alternating them is Sinkhorn scaling with the reference endpoint matrix as kernel. Analogous factorizations hold for diffusion references under additional absolute-continuity and integrability hypotheses. We use such a factorization in the smooth calculations below; the analytic existence theory is in Léonard's survey, §§2–4.

Entropic interpolation

The marginals \(\mu_t=P_t^\star\) form the entropic interpolation. For a Markov reference and \(P^\star=f(X_0)g(X_1)R\), past and future are conditionally independent given \(X_t\). Consequently \[ \frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_t}{\mathrm{d}R_t} =\mathbb E_R[f(X_0)\mid X_t]\, \mathbb E_R[g(X_1)\mid X_t]. \] For the Brownian reference above, let \(H_t\) be convolution with the heat kernel of variance \(\varepsilon t\). Define the Lebesgue-density factors \[ a_t=H_t(f r_0),\qquad b_t=H_{1-t}g. \] Then \(\rho_t=a_tb_t\), \(\partial_ta_t=\varepsilon\Delta a_t/2\), and \(\partial_tb_t=-\varepsilon\Delta b_t/2\). These factors differ from the conditional-expectation factors when the initial reference density is nonconstant.

Forward, backward, and current velocities

For smooth positive factors, the Brownian bridge's forward drift and backward drift (in the forward-time convention) are \[ b_t^+=\varepsilon\nabla\log b_t,\qquad b_t^-=-\varepsilon\nabla\log a_t. \] Their average and half-difference are \[ v_t=\frac\varepsilon2\nabla\log(b_t/a_t),\qquad u_t=\frac\varepsilon2\nabla\log\rho_t. \] The forward drift solves a Fokker–Planck equation with diffusion; the current velocity solves the continuity equation without a diffusion term. Indeed, \[ \partial_t(a_tb_t)=\frac\varepsilon2(b_t\Delta a_t-a_t\Delta b_t) =-\nabla\cdot(\rho_tv_t). \] Mixing reference-relative factors with Lebesgue densities would give an incorrect backward drift.

Dynamic action with Fisher information

Entropic dynamic formulation (smooth finite-action setting). Up to endpoint-dependent entropy constants, the Brownian Schrödinger problem is equivalent to minimizing \[ \int_0^1\int \left\{ \frac{1}{2\varepsilon}|v_t|^2 +\frac{\varepsilon}{8}|\nabla\log\rho_t|^2 \right\} \rho_t\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t \] subject to the continuity equation and endpoint constraints.

Proof outline. Girsanov's theorem expresses path-space entropy through the squared controlled forward drift \(b^+\). Decompose \[ b^+=v+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\nabla\log\rho. \] Expanding the square gives kinetic energy, Fisher information, and a cross term. The continuity equation turns the cross term into the difference of endpoint entropies, which is fixed by the constraints.

Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman structure

The logarithmic potentials \(\phi_t=\varepsilon\log b_t\) and \(\widehat\phi_t=\varepsilon\log a_t\) satisfy viscous Hamilton--Jacobi equations. For example, \[ \partial_t\phi_t+\frac12|\nabla\phi_t|^2 +\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Delta\phi_t=0 \] Here \(b_t\) solves the backward heat equation, fixing the sign of the Laplacian. As \(\varepsilon\to0\), the viscosity term disappears and the equation approaches the Hamilton--Jacobi equation of Wasserstein geodesics.

Zero-noise limit

A concrete small-noise limit. Suppose \(\mu_0,\mu_1\) have bounded densities supported in compact subsets of \(\mathbb R^d\), and take a strictly positive Gaussian initial reference density \(r_0\). Then \[ \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0} \varepsilon\min_{P:P_i=\mu_i}\operatorname{KL}(P\|R^\varepsilon) =\tfrac12W_2^2(\mu_0,\mu_1). \] The optimal endpoint couplings converge weakly to the unique quadratic optimal plan. Optimal path laws converge weakly to its straight-line dynamical plan.

Proof outline. All endpoint constants in the entropy identity are finite, and vanish after multiplying by \(\varepsilon\) (including \(\varepsilon\log\varepsilon\)). The compact continuous-cost result on entropic transport gives convergence of the values and endpoint plans. The source has a density, so Brenier gives uniqueness. Conditional on endpoints, a Brownian bridge is its straight-line mean plus a centered Brownian bridge of size \(\sqrt\varepsilon\). Its fluctuations vanish uniformly in probability. Mixing over the converging compactly supported endpoint laws proves weak path-space convergence. General small-noise results require more care with entropy and tails; finite second moments alone do not ensure finite bridge entropy at positive noise.

Gaussian bridges

For Gaussian endpoint laws and a linear Gaussian reference process, the Schrödinger bridge remains Gaussian. Its mean solves a deterministic minimum-energy interpolation, while its covariance solves coupled matrix Riccati equations. In the zero-noise limit these equations reduce to the Gaussian Wasserstein geodesic from Geodesics and displacement interpolation.

What the bridge adds to transport

Classical transport chooses deterministic least-action motion whenever a map exists. A Schrödinger bridge retains stochastic fluctuations around the most likely motion, remains smooth for positive noise, and naturally incorporates a reference dynamics. This makes it a genuine dynamical theory rather than merely a computational regularization.

Sources and proof details. Léonard, A survey of the Schrödinger problem, for entropy factorization, reference measures, and stochastic dynamics. Smooth action formulas need finite entropy, Fisher information, and justified integrations by parts.

Practice: bridges and deterministic limits

  1. Why does the optimal path law retain the reference conditional bridge for each endpoint pair?

    Hint and answer

    The conditional relative-entropy term in the chain rule is nonnegative and is zero precisely when the conditional laws coincide.

  2. For \(\rho_t=a_tb_t\) with the two heat equations stated above, verify the continuity equation for \(v_t\).

    Hint and answer

    Expand \(\partial_t(a_tb_t)\) and \(-\nabla\cdot[\varepsilon(a_t\nabla b_t-b_t\nabla a_t)/2]\). The mixed gradient terms cancel.
