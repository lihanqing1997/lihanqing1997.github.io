Learning goal. Use the direct method and recognize the separate lower-bound and recovery steps in stability.

The Kantorovich relaxation is useful because minimizers survive limiting operations. The proof is a clean instance of the direct method: compactness of feasible plans supplies a limit, closed marginal constraints preserve feasibility, and lower semicontinuity keeps the limiting cost below the liminf of the minimizing costs.

Lower semicontinuity of integral costs

Portmanteau lemma for costs. Let \(Z\) be Polish, \(\eta_n\Rightarrow\eta\), and let \(f:Z\to(-\infty,\infty]\) be lower semicontinuous and bounded below. Then \[ \int_Z f\,\mathrm{d}\eta \le\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_Z f\,\mathrm{d}\eta_n. \]

Proof. Subtract a finite lower bound so that \(f\ge0\). Define \[ f_k(z)=\min\left\{k,\inf_w\bigl(f(w)+k\,d(z,w)\bigr)\right\}. \] If \(f\equiv\infty\), then \(f_k\equiv k\); otherwise the infimum is finite and the triangle inequality implies \(|f_k(z)-f_k(z')|\le k d(z,z')\). Thus \(f_k\in C_b(Z)\), and \[ 0\le f_k(z)\le f_{k+1}(z)\le f(z). \] To prove \(f_k(z)\uparrow f(z)\), suppose its limit is \(a<f(z)\). Choose \(b\) with \(a<b<f(z)\). For all large \(k>b\), the cap is inactive and some \(w_k\) satisfies \[ f(w_k)+k\,d(z,w_k)<b,\qquad d(z,w_k)<b/k\to0,\qquad f(w_k)<b. \] Lower semicontinuity would give \(f(z)\le\liminf_k f(w_k)\le b\), a contradiction. For fixed \(k\), weak convergence gives \[ \int f_k\,\mathrm{d}\eta =\lim_n\int f_k\,\mathrm{d}\eta_n \le\liminf_n\int f\,\mathrm{d}\eta_n. \] Taking the supremum in \(k\), monotone convergence yields \[ \int f\,\mathrm{d}\eta =\sup_k\int f_k\,\mathrm{d}\eta \le\liminf_n\int f\,\mathrm{d}\eta_n. \] Adding back the lower bound proves the original claim.

Existence by the direct method

Existence theorem. Let \(X,Y\) be Polish, \(\mu\in\mathcal P(X)\), \(\nu\in\mathcal P(Y)\), and \(c:X\times Y\to(-\infty,\infty]\) be lower semicontinuous and bounded below. If at least one \(\pi_0\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\) has finite cost, then the Kantorovich problem admits an optimal plan.

Proof. Let \(m\) be a finite lower bound for \(c\). The finite-cost feasible plan ensures \[ m\le K:=K_c(\mu,\nu)\le\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi_0<\infty. \] By the definition of infimum choose \(\pi_n\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\) with \[ K\le\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi_n\le K+n^{-1}. \] The compactness theorem in Measures, pushforwards, and couplings gives a subsequence \(\pi_{n_k}\Rightarrow\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\). Feasibility gives the first inequality below; the preceding Portmanteau lemma gives the second: \[ K\le\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi \le\liminf_k\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{n_k} \le\lim_k(K+n_k^{-1})=K. \] Thus \(\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi=K\), so \(\pi\) is an optimal plan.

Nothing in this argument implies uniqueness or determinism. Those are geometric properties of the cost and the marginals, not consequences of compactness.

Stability when the marginals vary

Stability theorem. Suppose \(\mu_n\Rightarrow\mu\), \(\nu_n\Rightarrow\nu\), and \(\pi_n\in\Pi(\mu_n,\nu_n)\). Then \((\pi_n)\) is tight. Every weak limit point \(\pi\) belongs to \(\Pi(\mu,\nu)\). If \(\pi_{n_k}\Rightarrow\pi\) and \(c\) is lower semicontinuous and bounded below, then \[ \int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi\le\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{n_k}. \]

Proof. Weakly convergent probability sequences on Polish spaces are uniformly tight. Given \(\varepsilon>0\), choose compact \(K_X,K_Y\) with \[ \sup_n\mu_n(K_X^c)\le\varepsilon/2,\qquad \sup_n\nu_n(K_Y^c)\le\varepsilon/2. \] For every \(n\), \[ \pi_n((K_X\times K_Y)^c) \le\pi_n(K_X^c\times Y)+\pi_n(X\times K_Y^c) =\mu_n(K_X^c)+\nu_n(K_Y^c)\le\varepsilon. \] This proves tightness. If \(\pi_{n_k}\Rightarrow\pi\), then for \(f\in C_b(X)\), \[ \int f\,\mathrm{d}(\operatorname{pr}_X)_\#\pi =\lim_k\int f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{n_k} =\lim_k\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n_k} =\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu. \] Hence the first marginal is \(\mu\); testing \(g(y)\), \(g\in C_b(Y)\), identifies the second as \(\nu\). The cost bound applies along this same convergent subsequence: \[ \int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi\le\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{n_k}. \] It follows from Portmanteau because \(c\) is lower semicontinuous and bounded below.

Convergence of optimal values

The preceding theorem gives the lower-bound half. To identify the limit as optimal, one also needs recovery plans whose costs approach that of a prescribed limiting plan.

Continuity for bounded continuous costs. If \(c\in C_b(X\times Y)\), \(\mu_n\Rightarrow\mu\), and \(\nu_n\Rightarrow\nu\), then \[ K_c(\mu_n,\nu_n)\longrightarrow K_c(\mu,\nu). \] Moreover, every weak limit of optimal plans for \((\mu_n,\nu_n)\) is optimal for \((\mu,\nu)\).

Proof. Write \(K_n=K_c(\mu_n,\nu_n)\), \(K=K_c(\mu,\nu)\). All these values are finite and attained by the existence theorem.

Lower bound. Choose \(n_k\) with \(K_{n_k}\to\liminf_nK_n\), and optimizers \(\pi_{n_k}\). Stability gives a further subsequence converging to \(\bar\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\). Since \(c\in C_b\), \[ K\le\int c\,\mathrm{d}\bar\pi =\lim_k\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{n_k} =\liminf_nK_n. \]

Recovery coupling. Fix an optimizer \(\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\). Skorokhod representation gives couplings \(\alpha_n\in\Pi(\mu,\mu_n)\), \(\beta_n\in\Pi(\nu,\nu_n)\) with \[ \alpha_n\{d_X(x,x')>r\}\to0,\qquad \beta_n\{d_Y(y,y')>r\}\to0\quad(r>0). \] Disintegrate \(\alpha_n=\mu(\mathrm{d}x)A_n(x,\mathrm{d}x')\), \(\beta_n=\nu(\mathrm{d}y)B_n(y,\mathrm{d}y')\), and set \[ \lambda_n=\pi(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}y)A_n(x,\mathrm{d}x')B_n(y,\mathrm{d}y'), \qquad \widetilde\pi_n=(x',y')_\#\lambda_n. \] For Borel \(E\subset X\), \[ \widetilde\pi_n(E\times Y) =\int A_n(x,E)\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y) =\int A_n(x,E)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\mu_n(E). \] The analogous calculation gives \(\nu_n\), so \(\widetilde\pi_n\) is feasible.

Convergence of recovery costs. Put \(z=(x,y)\), \(z'=(x',y')\) and \(d_Z=d_X+d_Y\). The union bound gives \[ \lambda_n\{d_Z(z,z')>r\} \le\alpha_n\{d_X>r/2\}+\beta_n\{d_Y>r/2\}\to0. \] Choose compact \(H\subset X\times Y\) with \(\pi(H^c)<\delta\). Continuity and compactness imply that some \(r>0\) satisfies \[ z\in H,\ d_Z(z,z')<r\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |c(z')-c(z)|<\delta. \] To see the uniform radius, otherwise take \(z_j\in H\), \(d_Z(z_j,z_j')\to0\), \(|c(z_j')-c(z_j)|\ge\delta\); a convergent subsequence in \(H\) contradicts continuity. With \(M=\|c\|_\infty\), \[ \int|c(z')-c(z)|\,\mathrm{d}\lambda_n \le\delta+2M\bigl[\pi(H^c)+\lambda_n\{d_Z\ge r\}\bigr]. \] First take \(\limsup_n\), then \(\delta\downarrow0\), to obtain convergence to zero. Hence \[ \limsup_nK_n\le\lim_n\int c\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde\pi_n =\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi=K. \] Combined with the lower bound, \(K_n\to K\). Finally, if optimizers \(\pi_{n_k}\Rightarrow\pi_*\), then \[ \pi_*\in\Pi(\mu,\nu),\qquad \int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi_* =\lim_k\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{n_k}=\lim_kK_{n_k}=K, \] which proves optimality of every limit.

Unbounded costs and moment control

Proposition. Let \(X=Y=\mathbb R^d\), \(p\ge1\), and suppose \(\mu_n\to\mu\), \(\nu_n\to\nu\) in \(W_p\). If \(c\) is continuous and \[ |c(x,y)|\le C(1+|x|^p+|y|^p), \] then \(K_c(\mu_n,\nu_n)\to K_c(\mu,\nu)\), and limit points of optimal plans are optimal.

Proof. Include \(\mu,\nu\) in the marginal families and write \(\mathcal G\) for all couplings of the corresponding pairs. Put \(A=|x|^p\), \(B=|y|^p\). The topology theorem gives \[ \tau(M):=\sup_n\left[\int_{\{A>M\}}A\,\mathrm{d}\mu_n+\int_{\{B>M\}}B\,\mathrm{d}\nu_n\right]\longrightarrow0, \quad D:=\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal G}\int(A+B)\,\mathrm{d}\pi<\infty. \] For any event \(E\) and \(\pi\in\mathcal G\), splitting each term at \(M\) gives \[ \int_E(A+B)\,\mathrm{d}\pi \le2M\pi(E)+\tau(M). \] For \(E=\{A+B>R\}\), Markov gives \(\pi(E)\le D/R\); hence \[ \sup_{\pi\in\mathcal G}\int_{\{A+B>R\}}(A+B)\,\mathrm{d}\pi \le2MD/R+\tau(M). \] Taking \(R\to\infty\) first, then \(M\to\infty\), proves uniform integrability over all couplings, without independence.

Set \(c_L=\max(-L,\min(c,L))\in C_b\). The growth assumption gives \[ |c-c_L|\le C(1+A+B)\mathbf1_{\{C(1+A+B)>L\}},\qquad \epsilon_L:=\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal G}\int|c-c_L|\,\mathrm{d}\pi\to0. \] The elementary inequality \(|\inf F-\inf G|\le\sup|F-G|\) yields \[ |K_c(\mu_n,\nu_n)-K_c(\mu,\nu)| \le2\epsilon_L+|K_{c_L}(\mu_n,\nu_n)-K_{c_L}(\mu,\nu)|. \] For fixed \(L\), the last term tends to zero by the bounded-cost theorem; letting \(L\to\infty\) proves the claim about values.

If \(\pi_{n_k}\Rightarrow\pi\), stability identifies its marginals, and \[ \left|\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{n_k}-\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi\right| \le2\epsilon_L+\left|\int c_L\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{n_k}-\int c_L\,\mathrm{d}\pi\right|. \] The same ordered limits prove convergence of costs. For fixed marginals this also shows the cost functional is continuous on the compact coupling set, so a minimizer exists despite the signed unbounded cost. For optimal \(\pi_{n_k}\), the limit cost equals \(\lim_kK_c(\mu_{n_k},\nu_{n_k})=K_c(\mu,\nu)\), proving optimality.

Failure without the hypotheses

Moment escape. Let \(\mu_n=(1-n^{-1})\delta_0+n^{-1}\delta_n\) and \(\mu=\delta_0\). Then \(\mu_n\Rightarrow\mu\), but \[ W_1(\mu_n,\mu)=\int|x|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_n(x)=1. \] Weak convergence alone does not control an unbounded transport cost: a vanishing amount of mass may travel an unbounded distance.

Loss of lower semicontinuity. On \(\mathbb R^2\), define \(c(0,0)=1\) and \(c(x,y)=0\) elsewhere. For \(\pi_n=\delta_{(1/n,0)}\Rightarrow\delta_{(0,0)}=\pi\), the costs satisfy \(\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi_n=0\) but \(\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi=1\). A discontinuous upward jump can destroy existence by allowing minimizing sequences to approach a more expensive limit.

Sources and proof details. Ambrosio–Gigli, A user's guide to optimal transport, §§1–2, for direct-method existence and stability. The weak convergence note supplies Portmanteau and Prokhorov.

Practice: existence and recovery plans

  1. Which part of the existence proof fails if the cost has an upward jump at the limiting point?

    Hint and answer

    Portmanteau no longer gives the required lower bound on the limiting cost. The feasible plans may still be compact.

  2. If \(\mu_n\to\mu\) and \(\nu_n\to\nu\) in \(W_p\), bound \(|W_p(\mu_n,\nu_n)-W_p(\mu,\nu)|\).

    Hint and answer

    Apply the triangle inequality in both directions. The bound is \(W_p(\mu_n,\mu)+W_p(\nu_n,\nu)\).
