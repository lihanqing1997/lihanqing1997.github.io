Learning goal. Use the direct method and recognize the separate lower-bound and recovery steps in stability.

The Kantorovich relaxation is useful because minimizers survive limiting operations. The proof is a clean instance of the direct method: compactness of feasible plans supplies a limit, closed marginal constraints preserve feasibility, and lower semicontinuity keeps the limiting cost below the liminf of the minimizing costs.

Lower semicontinuity of integral costs

Portmanteau lemma for costs. Let \(Z\) be Polish, \(\eta_n\Rightarrow\eta\), and let \(f:Z\to(-\infty,\infty]\) be lower semicontinuous and bounded below. Then \[ \int_Z f\,\mathrm{d}\eta \le\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_Z f\,\mathrm{d}\eta_n. \]

Proof. After adding a constant, assume \(f\ge0\). There exist bounded continuous functions \(f_k\uparrow f\); for example one may use the infimal regularizations \(f_k(z)=\min\{k,\inf_w(f(w)+k\,d(z,w))\}\). For fixed \(k\), \[ \int f_k\,\mathrm{d}\eta=\lim_n\int f_k\,\mathrm{d}\eta_n \le\liminf_n\int f\,\mathrm{d}\eta_n. \] Letting \(k\to\infty\) and applying monotone convergence proves the claim.

Existence by the direct method

Existence theorem. Let \(X,Y\) be Polish, \(\mu\in\mathcal P(X)\), \(\nu\in\mathcal P(Y)\), and \(c:X\times Y\to(-\infty,\infty]\) be lower semicontinuous and bounded below. If at least one \(\pi_0\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\) has finite cost, then the Kantorovich problem admits an optimal plan.

Proof. Choose a minimizing sequence \(\pi_n\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\). Measures, pushforwards, and couplings showed that \(\Pi(\mu,\nu)\) is weakly compact, so a subsequence converges weakly to some \(\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\). The preceding lemma gives \[ \int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi \le\liminf_n\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi_n =K_c(\mu,\nu). \] Since \(\pi\) is feasible, the reverse inequality follows from the definition of the infimum. Thus equality holds and \(\pi\) is optimal.

Nothing in this argument implies uniqueness or determinism. Those are geometric properties of the cost and the marginals, not consequences of compactness.

Stability when the marginals vary

Stability theorem. Suppose \(\mu_n\Rightarrow\mu\), \(\nu_n\Rightarrow\nu\), and \(\pi_n\in\Pi(\mu_n,\nu_n)\). Then \((\pi_n)\) is tight. Every weak limit point \(\pi\) belongs to \(\Pi(\mu,\nu)\). If \(c\) is lower semicontinuous and bounded below, then \[ \int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi\le\liminf_n\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi_n. \]

Proof. Weakly convergent sequences of probability measures are uniformly tight. Choose compact \(K_X,K_Y\) carrying mass at least \(1-\varepsilon/2\) for all sufficiently large \(n\); enlarge them to handle the finitely many remaining indices. Then \[ \pi_n(K_X\times K_Y)\ge1-\mu_n(K_X^c)-\nu_n(K_Y^c)\ge1-\varepsilon. \] Prokhorov gives subsequential compactness. If \(\pi_{n_k}\Rightarrow\pi\), pushing forward by the continuous projections identifies the marginals of \(\pi\) as \(\mu,\nu\). The cost inequality is the Portmanteau lemma.

Convergence of optimal values

The preceding theorem gives the lower-bound half. To identify the limit as optimal, one also needs recovery plans whose costs approach that of a prescribed limiting plan.

Continuity for bounded continuous costs. If \(c\in C_b(X\times Y)\), \(\mu_n\Rightarrow\mu\), and \(\nu_n\Rightarrow\nu\), then \[ K_c(\mu_n,\nu_n)\longrightarrow K_c(\mu,\nu). \] Moreover, every weak limit of optimal plans for \((\mu_n,\nu_n)\) is optimal for \((\mu,\nu)\).

Proof. For the liminf, take a subsequence realizing the liminf of the optimal values and optimal plans along it. Tightness gives a further weakly convergent subsequence with limit \(\bar\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\). Bounded continuity implies convergence of the costs, so \(K_c(\mu,\nu)\le\int c\,\mathrm{d}\bar\pi=\liminf_n K_c(\mu_n,\nu_n)\).

For the limsup, fix an optimal \(\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\). Skorokhod representations of the two marginal convergences supply \(\alpha_n\in\Pi(\mu,\mu_n)\), \(\beta_n\in\Pi(\nu,\nu_n)\) such that their coordinate distances tend to zero in probability. Disintegrate \(\alpha_n=\mu(\mathrm{d}x)A_n(x,\mathrm{d}x')\), \(\beta_n=\nu(\mathrm{d}y)B_n(y,\mathrm{d}y')\), and define \[ \lambda_n(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}y,\mathrm{d}x',\mathrm{d}y') =\pi(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}y)A_n(x,\mathrm{d}x')B_n(y,\mathrm{d}y'). \] The \((x',y')\)-law \(\widetilde\pi_n\) has the required new marginals. Under \(\lambda_n\), the product distance between \((x,y)\) and \((x',y')\) tends to zero in probability. For any \(\delta>0\), choose a compact set carrying \(\pi\)-mass at least \(1-\delta\). Continuity at its points and a finite subcover give a uniform neighborhood size on which the change in \(c\) is at most \(\delta\). Outside this event the difference is at most \(2\|c\|_\infty\). Consequently \[ \limsup_n\int|c(x',y')-c(x,y)|\,\mathrm{d}\lambda_n \le\delta+2\|c\|_\infty\delta. \] Let \(\delta\downarrow0\). Hence \(\int c\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde\pi_n\to\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi\), which proves the limsup inequality. Applying the same cost continuity to any convergent subsequence of optimal plans proves the final assertion.

Unbounded costs and moment control

Proposition. Let \(X=Y=\mathbb R^d\), \(p\ge1\), and suppose \(\mu_n\to\mu\), \(\nu_n\to\nu\) in \(W_p\). If \(c\) is continuous and \[ |c(x,y)|\le C(1+|x|^p+|y|^p), \] then \(K_c(\mu_n,\nu_n)\to K_c(\mu,\nu)\), and limit points of optimal plans are optimal.

Proof. Set \(A=|x|^p\), \(B=|y|^p\), and include the limiting marginals in the families below. Their \(p\)-moments are uniformly integrable by the topology theorem. For any coupling \(\pi\) of a pair of these marginals, any measurable event \(E\), and \(M>0\), \[ \int_E(A+B)\,\mathrm{d}\pi \le 2M\pi(E)+\int_{\{A>M\}}A\,\mathrm{d}\mu_n+\int_{\{B>M\}}B\,\mathrm{d}\nu_n. \tag{1} \] The last two terms tend to zero uniformly in \(n\). Also \(\sup_\pi\int(A+B)\,\mathrm{d}\pi<\infty\), so Markov's inequality makes \(\pi(A+B>R)\to0\) uniformly. In (1), first choose \(M\), then let \(R\to\infty\) with \(E=\{A+B>R\}\). This proves uniform integrability of \(A+B\) over all these couplings, without imposing independence.

Let \(c_L=\max(-L,\min(c,L))\). It is bounded continuous, and \[ |c-c_L|\le C(1+A+B)\mathbf1_{\{C(1+A+B)>L\}}. \] Thus \(\sup_\pi\int|c-c_L|\,\mathrm{d}\pi\to0\). The bounded-cost theorem applied to \(c_L\), followed by \(L\to\infty\), proves convergence of the infima. For any weakly convergent sequence of optimal plans the identical truncation argument proves convergence of their \(c\)-integrals, so its limit is optimal. The same bounds justify existence for these unbounded signed costs by the direct method.

Failure without the hypotheses

Moment escape. Let \(\mu_n=(1-n^{-1})\delta_0+n^{-1}\delta_n\) and \(\mu=\delta_0\). Then \(\mu_n\Rightarrow\mu\), but \[ W_1(\mu_n,\mu)=\int|x|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_n(x)=1. \] Weak convergence alone does not control an unbounded transport cost: a vanishing amount of mass may travel an unbounded distance.

Loss of lower semicontinuity. On \(\mathbb R^2\), define \(c(0,0)=1\) and \(c(x,y)=0\) elsewhere. For \(\pi_n=\delta_{(1/n,0)}\Rightarrow\delta_{(0,0)}=\pi\), the costs satisfy \(\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi_n=0\) but \(\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi=1\). A discontinuous upward jump can destroy existence by allowing minimizing sequences to approach a more expensive limit.

Sources and proof details. Ambrosio–Gigli, A user's guide to optimal transport, §§1–2, for direct-method existence and stability. The weak convergence note supplies Portmanteau and Prokhorov.

Practice: existence and recovery plans

  1. Which part of the existence proof fails if the cost has an upward jump at the limiting point?

    Hint and answer

    Portmanteau no longer gives the required lower bound on the limiting cost. The feasible plans may still be compact.

  2. If \(\mu_n\to\mu\) and \(\nu_n\to\nu\) in \(W_p\), bound \(|W_p(\mu_n,\nu_n)-W_p(\mu,\nu)|\).

    Hint and answer

    Apply the triangle inequality in both directions. The bound is \(W_p(\mu_n,\mu)+W_p(\nu_n,\nu)\).
