Learning goal. Prove the metric triangle inequality by gluing and distinguish weak convergence from convergence of transport costs.

A Wasserstein distance does more than assign a transport cost. It equips a space of probability measures with a metric that simultaneously records weak convergence and control of tails. The geometry developed later rests on this topology.

Definition and finiteness

Definition 4.1 (Definition and finiteness). Let \((X,d)\) be Polish and \(1\le p<\infty\). For \(\mu,\nu\in\mathcal P_p(X)\), define \[ W_p(\mu,\nu) =\left( \inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)} \int_{X\times X}d(x,y)^p\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y) \right)^{1/p}. \] An optimizer exists because \(d^p\) is continuous and nonnegative.

Proposition 4.2 (Finiteness). For any base point \(x_0\), \[ W_p(\mu,\nu)^p \le 2^{p-1}\left( \int d(x,x_0)^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu +\int d(y,x_0)^p\,\mathrm{d}\nu \right). \]

Proof. The product measure is a coupling. The triangle inequality and convexity of \(t\mapsto t^p\) give \[ d(x,y)^p\le(d(x,x_0)+d(y,x_0))^p \le2^{p-1}(d(x,x_0)^p+d(y,x_0)^p). \] Tonelli's theorem applies to these nonnegative functions; because both marginals have total mass one, \[ \begin{aligned} W_p(\mu,\nu)^p &\le\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)^p\,\mathrm{d}(\mu\otimes\nu)\\ &\le2^{p-1}\left[\left(\int_Xd(x,x_0)^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)\nu(X) +\mu(X)\left(\int_Xd(y,x_0)^p\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)\right]<\infty. \end{aligned} \]

The metric axioms

Theorem 4.3 (The metric axioms). \(W_p\) is a metric on \(\mathcal P_p(X)\).

Proof. Nonnegativity follows from \(d^p\ge0\). The map \(S(x,y)=(y,x)\) gives a cost-preserving bijection \(S_\#: \Pi(\mu,\nu)\to\Pi(\nu,\mu)\), so \(W_p(\mu,\nu)=W_p(\nu,\mu)\). The diagonal plan gives \[ W_p(\mu,\mu)^p\le\int_Xd(x,x)^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=0. \] Conversely, for bounded 1-Lipschitz \(f\) and any \(\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)\), Hölder's inequality on the probability space \((X^2,\pi)\) gives \[ \left|\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\int f\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right| =\left|\int(f(x)-f(y))\,\mathrm{d}\pi\right| \le\int d(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\pi \le\left(\int d(x,y)^p\,\mathrm{d}\pi\right)^{1/p}. \] Taking the infimum shows \(W_p=0\) forces equality of all bounded Lipschitz integrals, which determine probabilities on a metric space. Thus \(\mu=\nu\).

For \(\varepsilon>0\), choose couplings \(\pi_{12},\pi_{23}\) with respective \(L^p\)-costs at most \(W_p(\mu_1,\mu_2)+\varepsilon\), \(W_p(\mu_2,\mu_3)+\varepsilon\). Glue them to \(\gamma\). Its \((1,3)\)-marginal is feasible, so \[ \begin{aligned} W_p(\mu_1,\mu_3) &\le\|d(x_1,x_3)\|_{L^p(\gamma)}\\ &\le\|d(x_1,x_2)+d(x_2,x_3)\|_{L^p(\gamma)}\\ &\le\|d(x_1,x_2)\|_{L^p(\gamma)}+\|d(x_2,x_3)\|_{L^p(\gamma)} &&\text{(Minkowski)}\\ &=\|d(x_1,x_2)\|_{L^p(\pi_{12})}+\|d(x_2,x_3)\|_{L^p(\pi_{23})}\\ &\le W_p(\mu_1,\mu_2)+W_p(\mu_2,\mu_3)+2\varepsilon. \end{aligned} \] Let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). This proof uses approximate minimizers and does not depend on the later existence theorem.

Elementary comparisons

Proposition 4.4 (Elementary comparisons). If \(1\le p\le q\) and \(\mu,\nu\in\mathcal P_q(X)\), then \[ W_p(\mu,\nu)\le W_q(\mu,\nu). \] Also, \[ W_p(\mu,\delta_{x_0})^p=\int d(x,x_0)^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x). \]

Proof. If \(p<q\), Hölder with exponents \(q/p\) and \(q/(q-p)\) yields, for every probability coupling \(\pi\), \[ \int d^p\,\mathrm{d}\pi \le\left(\int d^q\,\mathrm{d}\pi\right)^{p/q} \left(\int1\,\mathrm{d}\pi\right)^{1-p/q} =\left(\int d^q\,\mathrm{d}\pi\right)^{p/q}. \] Therefore \(W_p\le\|d\|_{L^p(\pi)}\le\|d\|_{L^q(\pi)}\); taking the infimum over \(\pi\) gives \(W_p\le W_q\). For \(p=q\) equality is immediate.

If \(\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\delta_{x_0})\), then \(\pi(X\times\{x_0\})=1\), and for Borel \(A,B\), \[ \pi(A\times B)=\mathbf1_B(x_0)\pi(A\times X)=\mu(A)\delta_{x_0}(B). \] Thus \(\Pi(\mu,\delta_{x_0})=\{\mu\otimes\delta_{x_0}\}\), and \[ W_p(\mu,\delta_{x_0})^p =\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)^p\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\delta_{x_0}(\mathrm{d}y) =\int_Xd(x,x_0)^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x). \]

On a space of finite diameter \(D\), interpolation gives \(W_q^q\le D^{q-p}W_p^p\). On unbounded spaces no reverse comparison is possible without stronger moment bounds.

Characterization of \(W_p\)-convergence

Theorem 4.5 (Topology theorem). For \(\mu_n,\mu\in\mathcal P_p(X)\), the following are equivalent:

(i) \(W_p(\mu_n,\mu)\to0\).

(ii) \(\mu_n\Rightarrow\mu\) and, for one base point \(x_0\), \[ \int d(x,x_0)^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu_n(x) \to \int d(x,x_0)^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x). \]

(iii) \(\mu_n\Rightarrow\mu\) and the \(p\)-th moments are uniformly integrable: \[ \lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_n \int_{\{d(x,x_0)>R\}}d(x,x_0)^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu_n(x)=0. \]

Proof. Write \(r(x)=d(x,x_0)\). We use the probability theorems of Skorokhod representation and Vitali convergence, in their Polish-space and uniform-integrability forms.

(i) implies (ii). Choose \(\pi_n\in\Pi(\mu_n,\mu)\) with \[ \int d(x,y)^p\,\mathrm{d}\pi_n\le W_p(\mu_n,\mu)^p+n^{-1}\longrightarrow0. \] For bounded 1-Lipschitz \(f\), \[ \left|\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_n-\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right| \le\left(\int d^p\,\mathrm{d}\pi_n\right)^{1/p}\to0. \] The bounded-Lipschitz characterization gives \(\mu_n\Rightarrow\mu\). The reverse triangle inequality for \(L^p(\pi_n)\), followed by \(|r(x)-r(y)|\le d(x,y)\), gives \[ \left|\left(\int r^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu_n\right)^{1/p} -\left(\int r^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{1/p}\right| \le\|r(x)-r(y)\|_{L^p(\pi_n)}\le\|d\|_{L^p(\pi_n)}\to0. \] Continuity of \(t\mapsto t^p\) gives convergence of the moments.

(ii) implies (iii) and (i). Skorokhod representation supplies \(X_n\sim\mu_n,X\sim\mu\) on one space with \(X_n\to X\) almost surely. Let \(Y_n=r(X_n)^p,Y=r(X)^p\). Then \(Y_n\to Y\) almost surely and \(\mathbb EY_n\to\mathbb EY<\infty\). Since \(0\le\min(Y_n,Y)\le Y\), dominated convergence gives \[ \mathbb E|Y_n-Y| =\mathbb EY_n+\mathbb EY-2\mathbb E\min(Y_n,Y)\longrightarrow0. \] Here is the needed uniform-integrability implication explicitly. For any event \(E\), \[ \mathbb E[Y_n\mathbf1_E]\le\mathbb E|Y_n-Y|+\mathbb E[Y\mathbf1_E]. \] For large \(n\), the first term is uniformly small; absolute continuity of the integral of \(Y\) controls the second when \(\mathbb P(E)\) is small. The finitely many remaining \(Y_n\) have the same property. Also \(M=\sup_n\mathbb EY_n<\infty\), so \[ \sup_n\mathbb P(Y_n>R)\le M/R\to0,\qquad \sup_n\mathbb E[Y_n\mathbf1_{\{Y_n>R\}}]\to0. \] This is (iii). Moreover, \[ Z_n=d(X_n,X)^p\to0\text{ a.s.},\qquad 0\le Z_n\le2^{p-1}(Y_n+Y). \] The small-event bound just proved also holds for \(Y_n+Y\); together with its bounded expectations it proves uniform integrability of \(Z_n\). Vitali now gives \(\mathbb EZ_n\to0\), and \[ 0\le W_p(\mu_n,\mu)^p\le\mathbb E Z_n\longrightarrow0. \]

(iii) implies (ii). Let \(f_R=r^p\wedge R\in C_b(X)\). For fixed \(R\), weak convergence gives convergence of its integrals. Thus \[ \begin{aligned} \left|\int r^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu_n-\int r^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right| &\le\left|\int f_R\,\mathrm{d}\mu_n-\int f_R\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right|\\ &\quad+\int_{\{r^p>R\}}r^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu_n +\int_{\{r^p>R\}}r^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu. \end{aligned} \] Taking \(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\), then \(R\to\infty\), gives zero: (iii) controls the first tail uniformly, and dominated convergence controls the tail of \(\mu\).

Completeness and separability

Theorem 4.6 (Completeness and separability). If \((X,d)\) is complete and separable, then \((\mathcal P_p(X),W_p)\) is complete and separable.

Proof. Completeness. From a \(W_p\)-Cauchy sequence choose indices \(n_k\) with \(W_p(\mu_{n_k},\mu_{n_{k+1}})<2^{-k}\). Choose couplings whose \(L^p\)-costs are at most \(2^{-k}\). Disintegrating them into transition kernels and applying the countable kernel-product theorem produces \(X_k\sim\mu_{n_k}\) with \[ \|d(X_k,X_{k+1})\|_p\le2^{-k}. \] By Tonelli and Hölder, \[ \mathbb E\left[\sum_{k=1}^\infty d(X_k,X_{k+1})\right] =\sum_{k=1}^\infty\mathbb Ed(X_k,X_{k+1}) \le\sum_{k=1}^\infty2^{-k}=1. \] Hence the sum is finite almost surely. For \(\ell>k\), \[ d(X_k,X_\ell)\le\sum_{j=k}^{\ell-1}d(X_j,X_{j+1})\longrightarrow0 \quad\text{as }k,\ell\to\infty\text{ a.s.} \] Completeness gives \(X_k\to X_\infty\) almost surely; extend the measurable limit arbitrarily on the null exceptional set. Fatou for the nonnegative \(p\)-th powers and Minkowski give \[ \begin{aligned} \|d(X_k,X_\infty)\|_p &\le\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\|d(X_k,X_\ell)\|_p\\ &\le\sum_{j=k}^\infty\|d(X_j,X_{j+1})\|_p \le2^{1-k}. \end{aligned} \] Also \(\|d(X_\infty,x_0)\|_p\le1+\|d(X_1,x_0)\|_p<\infty\). With \(\mu_\infty=\mathcal L(X_\infty)\), \[ W_p(\mu_{n_k},\mu_\infty)\le2^{1-k},\qquad W_p(\mu_n,\mu_\infty)\le W_p(\mu_n,\mu_{n_k})+2^{1-k}. \] Given \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(k\) large enough that the second term is below \(\varepsilon/2\) and \(n_k\) lies in a Cauchy tail; then the first term is below \(\varepsilon/2\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).

Separability. Enumerate a dense countable set \(D=\{x_1,x_2,\ldots\}\). Let \(q_N(x)\) be a nearest point among the first \(N\) points, with ties broken by index. Its fibers are Borel comparisons of continuous distance functions, and \[ d(x,q_N(x))=\min_{1\le j\le N}d(x,x_j)\downarrow0, \qquad d(x,q_N(x))^p\le d(x,x_1)^p\in L^1(\mu). \] Dominated convergence gives \[ W_p(\mu,q_{N\#}\mu)^p\le\int d(x,q_N(x))^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu\to0. \] For two weight vectors \(a,b\) on this finite support, put \(r_i=(a_i-b_i)_+\), \(s_i=(b_i-a_i)_+\), \(\delta=\sum_i r_i=\sum_i s_i=\|a-b\|_1/2\). If \(\delta>0\), a coupling is \[ p_{ij}=\min(a_i,b_i)\mathbf1_{i=j}+r_is_j/\delta. \] Writing \(D_N=\max_{i,j\le N}d(x_i,x_j)\), its cost is at most \[ \sum_{ij}d(x_i,x_j)^pp_{ij}\le D_N^p\frac{\sum_ir_i\sum_js_j}{\delta} =D_N^p\|a-b\|_1/2. \] For \(a=b\) the cost is zero. Rational probability vectors are dense in the finite simplex, so finitely supported laws on \(D\) with rational weights form a countable \(W_p\)-dense set.

Relative compactness

Proposition 4.7 (Compactness criterion). A family \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal P_p(X)\) is relatively compact in \(W_p\) if it is tight and its \(p\)-th moments are uniformly integrable. On a proper metric space, a uniform bound on a moment of order \(q>p\) implies these conditions.

Proof. Put \(r(x)=d(x,x_0)\). From any sequence \(\mu_n\in\mathcal K\), tightness and Prokhorov give \(\mu_{n_k}\Rightarrow\mu\). Uniform integrability implies \(M=\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}\int r^p\,\mathrm{d}\eta<\infty\). For \(R>0\), \[ \int(r^p\wedge R)\,\mathrm{d}\mu =\lim_k\int(r^p\wedge R)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n_k}\le M. \] Monotone convergence as \(R\uparrow\infty\) yields \(\int r^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu\le M\). Consequently \[ \begin{aligned} \limsup_k\left|\int r^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n_k}-\int r^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right| \le\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}\int_{\{r^p>R\}}r^p\,\mathrm{d}\eta +\int_{\{r^p>R\}}r^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu\longrightarrow0. \end{aligned} \] The topology theorem gives \(W_p(\mu_{n_k},\mu)\to0\). Every sequence has such a subsequence, which is relative compactness in a metric space.

If \(\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}\int r^q\,\mathrm{d}\eta=M_q<\infty\), then \[ \eta(r>R)\le R^{-q}\int r^q\,\mathrm{d}\eta\le M_qR^{-q}, \qquad \int_{\{r>R\}}r^p\,\mathrm{d}\eta \le R^{p-q}\int_{\{r>R\}}r^q\,\mathrm{d}\eta\le M_qR^{p-q}. \] On a proper space \(\{r\le R\}\) is compact, so the first bound proves tightness; since \(q>p\), the second proves uniform integrability.

The extra tail condition is essential. Weak compactness alone permits a small amount of mass to escape to infinity while carrying nonvanishing \(p\)-transport cost, as the example on Existence and stability of optimal plans showed.

Counterexample 4.8 (Bounded moments are not uniform integrability). On \(\mathbb R\), let \[ \mu_n=(1-n^{-2})\delta_0+n^{-2}\delta_n. \] Then \(\mu_n\Rightarrow\delta_0\) and \(\int x^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_n=1\), but \(W_2^2(\mu_n,\delta_0)=1\). Every subsequence has the same weak limit, so none converges in \(W_2\). A second-moment bound gives tightness on Euclidean space, not \(W_2\)-compactness.

The \(W_\infty\) distance

For bounded-support measures one may also define \[ W_\infty(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)} \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{(x,y)\sim\pi}d(x,y). \] It minimizes the largest displacement rather than an average displacement. It is the limit of \(W_p\) as \(p\to\infty\) under suitable boundedness assumptions, but its topology and geodesics can be more rigid.

Exercises

tails and topology

  1. Exercise 4.1. For \(\mu_n=(1-n^{-q})\delta_0+n^{-q}\delta_n\), when does \(W_p(\mu_n,\delta_0)\to0\)?

    Hint

    Only the small atom moves. Multiply its mass by the distance raised to the required power.

    Solution

    The \(p\)-th power is \(n^{p-q}\). It tends to zero exactly when \(q>p\), although weak convergence holds for every \(q>0\).

  2. Exercise 4.2. Why does a uniform \((p+\eta)\)-moment bound imply uniformly integrable \(p\)-th moments?

    Hint

    On the tail region, compare the lower power with the higher power times a negative power of the cutoff.

    Solution

    On \(|x|>R\), \(|x|^p\le R^{-\eta}|x|^{p+\eta}\). Integrate and take the supremum over the family.
