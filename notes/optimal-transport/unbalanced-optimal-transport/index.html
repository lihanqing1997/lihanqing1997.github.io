Learning goal. Separate arbitrary marginal penalties from the specific Hellinger–Kantorovich geometry and track normalization constants.

Classical transport requires equal total mass and preserves every particle. Unbalanced transport permits mass to be created, removed, or attenuated, charging separately for spatial motion and variation of mass. The resulting geometry combines Wasserstein and Fisher--Rao structures.

Relaxing the marginal constraints

Definition. Let \(\mu,\nu\) be finite nonnegative measures, not necessarily of equal mass. For divergences \(D_1,D_2\) on positive measures, define \[ \operatorname{UOT}(\mu,\nu) = \inf_{\pi\in\mathcal M_+(X\times Y)} \left\{ \int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi +\tau_1D_1(\pi_X\,\|\,\mu) +\tau_2D_2(\pi_Y\,\|\,\nu) \right\}. \] The marginals of \(\pi\) need not equal \(\mu,\nu\); the divergences penalize their mismatch.

Transported mass is represented by \(\pi\). Source mass not used by \(\pi_X\) may be destroyed, and missing target mass relative to \(\pi_Y\) may be created. The parameters \(\tau_1,\tau_2\) determine whether it is cheaper to move mass or modify it.

For finite measures, use the generalized relative entropy \[ \operatorname{KL}(\alpha\|\mu)=\int(r\log r-r+1)\,\mathrm{d}\mu, \qquad r=\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha}{\mathrm{d}\mu}, \] and \(+\infty\) if \(\alpha\not\ll\mu\), with \(0\log0=0\). The mass-correction terms matter when masses differ. For the static duality below, assume a compact space, finite continuous nonnegative cost, and \(\tau_i>0\).

KL-penalized unbalanced transport

Dual formulation (general compact-space theorem). For \(D_i=\operatorname{KL}\), \[ \operatorname{UOT}(\mu,\nu) = \sup_{\varphi+\psi\le c} \left[ -\tau_1\int\{e^{-\varphi/\tau_1}-1\}\,\mathrm{d}\mu -\tau_2\int\{e^{-\psi/\tau_2}-1\}\,\mathrm{d}\nu \right]. \]

Proof in the finite positive-weight case; general theorem input. The compact-space version is the entropy-transport duality theorem of Liero–Mielke–Savaré. Here is a full proof for finite marginals \(a_i,b_j>0\), which also derives the potentials without an unjustified interchange of infimum and supremum.

For a nonnegative matrix \(P\), write \(r=P\mathbf1\), \(s=P^\top\mathbf1\), and let \(J(P)\) be the primal objective. A minimizer exists: Jensen gives \(\operatorname{KL}(r\|a)\ge M\log(M/A)-M+A\), where \(M=\sum_{ij}P_{ij}\), \(A=\sum_i a_i\); together with nonnegative cost this makes sublevels bounded, and continuity gives attainment. At a minimizer every \(r_i,s_j\) is positive. Otherwise adding a small entry to an empty row or column decreases the entropy by a leading negative multiple of \(h|\log h|\), dominating all finite linear changes; if \(P=0\), the same perturbation fills both an empty row and column and again decreases the objective.

The partial derivatives now exist, and orthant optimality gives \[ c_{ij}+\tau_1\log(r_i/a_i)+\tau_2\log(s_j/b_j)\ge0, \] with equality whenever \(P_{ij}>0\). Define \(\varphi_i=-\tau_1\log(r_i/a_i)\), \(\psi_j=-\tau_2\log(s_j/b_j)\). They satisfy \(\varphi_i+\psi_j\le c_{ij}\), and equality on transported edges gives \(\sum c_{ij}P_{ij}=\sum_i r_i\varphi_i+\sum_j s_j\psi_j\). Substitution into \(J\) cancels the logarithmic terms: \[ J(P)=\tau_1\sum_i(a_i-r_i)+\tau_2\sum_j(b_j-s_j) =-\tau_1\sum_i a_i(e^{-\varphi_i/\tau_1}-1)-\tau_2\sum_j b_j(e^{-\psi_j/\tau_2}-1). \] Finally the scalar Fenchel inequality \(\tau\operatorname{KL}(r\|a)\ge-\varphi r-\tau a(e^{-\varphi/\tau}-1)\), summed over marginals and combined with \(c\ge\varphi+\psi\), proves weak duality for every competitor. The constructed pair attains equality, proving finite-dimensional strong duality.

Unlike balanced dual potentials, \(\varphi,\psi\) cannot be shifted by opposite constants without changing the objective: the total transported mass is itself an optimized quantity.

Entropic scaling for unequal mass

Using counting-measure entropy for the discrete coupling, with parameter \(\varepsilon\) again gives a Gibbs plan \(P=\operatorname{diag}(u)K\operatorname{diag}(v)\). For discrete KL marginal penalties, generalized Sinkhorn updates take the form \[ u\leftarrow\left(a\oslash Kv\right)^{\tau_1/(\tau_1+\varepsilon)}, \qquad v\leftarrow\left(b\oslash K^\top u\right)^{\tau_2/(\tau_2+\varepsilon)}. \] Exponents below one perform only a partial marginal correction. As \(\tau_i\to\infty\), the exponents approach one and balanced Sinkhorn is recovered.

The continuity equation with reaction

Definition. A dynamic unbalanced path consists of a measure curve \(\mu_t\), a velocity \(v_t\), and a reaction rate \(\alpha_t\) satisfying \[ \partial_t\mu_t+\nabla\cdot(v_t\mu_t)=\alpha_t\mu_t. \] In one common normalization, its action is \[ \mathcal A_\delta(\mu,v,\alpha) = \int_0^1\int \{|v_t|^2+\delta^2|\alpha_t|^2\} \,\mathrm{d}\mu_t\,\mathrm{d}t. \]

The left side moves existing mass; the right side changes its amplitude. The parameter \(\delta\) sets the relative price of reaction. On \(\mathbb R^d\) with Euclidean ground distance, minimizing this action defines the Hellinger--Kantorovich or Wasserstein--Fisher--Rao metric, with constants depending on normalization.

Pure growth and Fisher--Rao geometry

Scalar mass distance. If all mass stays at one point and only its amount changes from \(m_0\) to \(m_1\), the minimal action is \[ 4\delta^2(\sqrt{m_1}-\sqrt{m_0})^2. \]

Proof, including zero endpoints. For an admissible absolutely continuous mass path, \(m'=\alpha m\) and \(\int\alpha^2m<\infty\). To avoid dividing by zero, set \(r_\eta=\sqrt{m+\eta}\), \(\eta>0\). Then \[ |r_\eta'|^2=\frac{\alpha^2m^2}{4(m+\eta)}\le\frac14\alpha^2m. \] The functions \(r_\eta\) converge uniformly to \(r=\sqrt m\), since their difference is at most \(\sqrt\eta\). Weak compactness in \(H^1(0,1)\) and lower semicontinuity give \(r\in H^1\) and \(4\int|r'|^2\le\int\alpha^2m\). Cauchy–Schwarz therefore yields \[ \delta^2\int_0^1\alpha^2m\,\mathrm{d}t\ge4\delta^2\int_0^1|r'|^2\,\mathrm{d}t \ge4\delta^2|r(1)-r(0)|^2. \] For attainment, choose \(r(t)=(1-t)\sqrt{m_0}+t\sqrt{m_1}\), \(m=r^2\), and \(\alpha=2r'/r\) wherever \(r>0\). Put \(\alpha=0\) on the zero set; apart from irrelevant endpoints, that set is either empty or the whole interval. Then \(m'=\alpha m\) almost everywhere and \(\alpha^2m=4|r'|^2\) almost everywhere, giving equality even when one or both endpoint masses vanish.

Cone geometry and Dirac measures

Dirac formula (cone theorem). On \(\mathbb R^d\) in the normalization above, \[ \operatorname{HK}_\delta^2(a\delta_x,b\delta_y) = 4\delta^2\left[ a+b-2\sqrt{ab}\, \cos_+\!\left(\frac{d(x,y)}{2\delta}\right) \right], \] where \(\cos_+(r)=\cos(\min\{r,\pi/2\})\).

The square root of mass is a radial coordinate on a metric cone over \(X\). Nearby Dirac masses are partly transported; beyond the cutoff angle it is cheaper to annihilate at \(x\) and recreate at \(y\). This gives a precise geometric meaning to the transport--reaction tradeoff.

Dynamic Hellinger--Kantorovich formula

Dynamic representation (external cone-lifting theorem). For finite nonnegative measures on \(\mathbb R^d\), the squared Hellinger--Kantorovich distance is the infimum of \(\mathcal A_\delta\) over all reaction--continuity triples connecting the endpoint measures. Minimizers exist under standard compactness assumptions, and the distance metrizes weak convergence together with convergence of total mass on bounded spaces.

External theorem and normalization. The general equality is the cone-lifting and dynamic representation theorem of Liero–Mielke–Savaré. It supplies the lift of every finite-action reaction–continuity curve and the converse projection, a substantial existence result. With reaction coefficient \(\delta^2\), the radial coordinate is \(2\delta\sqrt m\), and the cone angle is \(d(x,y)/(2\delta)\) with transport cut off at \(\pi/2\). These choices give the displayed Dirac formula and reduce at \(x=y\) to the scalar action just proved. A geometric description of a lift alone would not prove the converse or equality of the two infima.

Unbalanced barycenters

An unbalanced barycenter minimizes \[ \nu\longmapsto\sum_i\lambda_i\operatorname{HK}_\delta^2(\nu,\mu_i) \] or an analogous static UOT objective. It can average measures with different total mass and can suppress features present in only a few inputs rather than transporting mass over a long distance to accommodate them.

Gradient flows with mass variation

The formal tangent norm now contains transport and reaction: \[ \|(\nabla\phi,\alpha)\|_\mu^2 =\int(|\nabla\phi|^2+\delta^2\alpha^2)\,\mathrm{d}\mu. \] Gradient flows combine spatial drift with a reaction term. For a first variation \(F=\delta\mathcal E/\delta\rho\), one obtains, up to normalization, \[ \partial_t\rho =\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla F)-\delta^{-2}\rho F. \] The first term transports mass down the energy gradient; the second creates or destroys it according to the sign of \(F\).

Balanced and pure-reaction limits

As reaction becomes infinitely expensive, the dynamic constraint approaches mass conservation and the geometry tends toward Wasserstein distance on equal-mass measures. If spatial motion becomes infinitely expensive, only Fisher--Rao change remains. The static KL-penalized objective with arbitrary ground cost is not automatically this dynamic HK metric: the exact correspondence uses a specific logarithmic-cosine ground cost. Unbalanced transport is therefore not a small variant of classical transport but a geometry interpolating two distinct mechanisms.

Sources and proof details. Liero–Mielke–Savaré, Optimal entropy-transport problems and a new Hellinger–Kantorovich distance, for the static/dynamic correspondence and cone-lifting theorem.

Practice: moving mass or changing it

  1. With only reaction, find the optimal mass path from mass 1 to mass 4 at a fixed point.

    Hint and answer

    The square root of mass interpolates linearly: \(m_t=(1+t)^2\). The action is \(4\delta^2\).

  2. In the HK Dirac formula, what happens once \(d(x,y)\ge\pi\delta\)?

    Hint and answer

    The truncated cosine vanishes. Squared distance is \(4\delta^2(a+b)\); increasing separation further does not change it, because annihilation and recreation are cheaper.
