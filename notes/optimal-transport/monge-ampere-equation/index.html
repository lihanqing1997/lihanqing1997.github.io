Learning goal. Derive the Jacobian equation and explain why the target image condition is part of the problem.

Brenier's theorem turns quadratic transport into a convex potential \(u\). When the measures have densities, the pushforward condition becomes a nonlinear partial differential equation for \(u\). Its determinant measures how the gradient map changes volume.

Derivation from change of variables

Jacobian equation. Let \(\mu=f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\) on a domain \(\Omega\), \(\nu=g(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\) on \(\Lambda\), and suppose \(T:\Omega\to\Lambda\) is a \(C^1\)-diffeomorphism with \(T_\#\mu=\nu\). Then \[ f(x)=g(T(x))|\det DT(x)| \] for almost every \(x\). If \(T=\nabla u\) with \(u\) convex and \(D^2u>0\), then \[ \boxed{\det D^2u(x)=\frac{f(x)}{g(\nabla u(x))}}, \qquad \nabla u(\Omega)=\Lambda. \]

Proof. For every bounded continuous \(h\), \[ \int_\Lambda h(y)g(y)\,\mathrm{d}y =\int_\Omega h(T(x))f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x. \] Changing variables \(y=T(x)\) in the left side gives \[ \int_\Omega h(T(x))g(T(x))|\det DT(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x. \] Equality for all \(h\) yields the Jacobian identity. For a convex potential, \(DT=D^2u\) is positive semidefinite, so the absolute value may be removed. The image condition is the second boundary condition, not a pointwise Dirichlet condition.

Ellipticity and convexity

The operator \(u\mapsto\det D^2u\) is elliptic only on convex functions. Indeed, differentiating the determinant at a positive-definite matrix \(A\) in direction \(H\) gives \[ D(\det)_A[H]=\operatorname{cof}(A):H =\det(A)\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}H), \] whose coefficient matrix \(\operatorname{cof}(A)\) is positive definite. Convexity is therefore both the transport structure and the ellipticity condition.

Linearized Monge--Ampère operator. At a smooth strictly convex solution \(u\), the linearization of \(\det D^2u=F\) is \[ L_uv=\operatorname{cof}(D^2u):D^2v. \] Moreover, each row of \(\operatorname{cof}(D^2u)\) is divergence free, so \[ L_uv=\nabla\cdot(\operatorname{cof}(D^2u)\nabla v). \]

Alexandrov solutions

Optimal potentials need not be twice differentiable. The correct weak formulation uses the geometry of the subgradient rather than distributional determinants.

Definition. For a convex \(u:\Omega\to\mathbb R\), its Monge--Ampère measure is \[ M_u(E)=\mathcal L^d(\partial u(E)), \qquad E\subset\Omega\ \text{Borel}. \] We call \(u\) an Alexandrov solution of \(\det D^2u=\rho\) if \(M_u(E)=\int_E\rho(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\) for every Borel \(E\).

Consistency. If \(u\in C^2(\Omega)\) is strictly convex, then \[ M_u(E)=\int_E\det D^2u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x. \]

Proof. Differentiability makes \(\partial u(x)=\{\nabla u(x)\}\), and the area formula for the injective gradient map gives \[ \mathcal L^d(\nabla u(E)) =\int_E|\det D(\nabla u)(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x =\int_E\det D^2u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x. \]

Transport formulation of weak solutions

Weighted Alexandrov formulation. Let \(\Omega,\Lambda\) be bounded convex open domains, and let \(f,g\) be densities on them bounded above and below by positive constants, with equal mass. The Brenier potential has the transport boundary condition and satisfies \[ \int_{\partial u(E)}g(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_E f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x \quad(E\subset\Omega\text{ Borel}), \] with \(g\) extended by zero outside \(\Lambda\). This weighted subgradient-image identity is the weak meaning of \(g(\nabla u)\det D^2u=f\). Together with \(\partial u(\Omega)\subset\overline\Lambda\) and the full-mass image condition, it characterizes the corresponding convex transport potential up to constants.

The weighted identity avoids evaluating a density at a multivalued subgradient. Its justification uses almost-everywhere invertibility of the forward and reverse Brenier maps and the convex-target localization theory. For a rigorous treatment see Ambrosio–Gigli, §1.4. Under smoothness it reduces to the change-of-variables calculation above. The equation and the target boundary condition must be kept together: a local volume constraint alone does not choose the destination domain.

One-dimensional and radial examples

One dimension. The equation is \[ u''(x)=\frac{f(x)}{g(u'(x))}. \] Since \(T=u'\), integration gives \(F_\nu(T(x))=F_\mu(x)\), hence \(T=F_\nu^{-1}\circ F_\mu\). Thus the Monge--Ampère equation reduces exactly to monotone rearrangement.

Radial measures. If \(f,g\) are radial and \(T(x)=t(r)x/r\), mass conservation inside balls gives \[ \int_0^r f_0(s)s^{d-1}\,\mathrm{d}s = \int_0^{t(r)}g_0(s)s^{d-1}\,\mathrm{d}s. \] Differentiation yields \[ f_0(r)r^{d-1}=g_0(t(r))t(r)^{d-1}t'(r), \] which equals the determinant formula because the radial and tangential eigenvalues of \(DT\) are \(t'(r)\) and \(t(r)/r\).

Quadratic potentials. For \(u(x)=\tfrac12x^\top Ax+b\cdot x\) with \(A>0\), \(\det D^2u=\det A\). The gradient map sends ellipsoids to ellipsoids and recovers the Gaussian transport matrix from Quadratic transport and Brenier's theorem.

Regularity questions

The equation suggests that smooth positive \(f,g\) should produce a smooth potential, but this is false without geometry. Convexity of the domains, bounds away from zero and infinity, and boundary regularity are essential. The next page separates interior regularity, global regularity, and the additional curvature condition required by general costs.

Sources and proof details. Santambrogio, Optimal transport for applied mathematicians, Chapter 1, for the relation between Brenier potentials and the second boundary-value problem; the regularity chapter that follows explains its geometric hypotheses.

Practice: volume and boundary data

  1. For transport from uniform \([0,1]\) to uniform \([0,2]\), solve the one-dimensional Jacobian equation.

    Hint and answer

    Here \(f=1,g=1/2\), so \(u\,\!\prime\prime=2\). The image condition fixes \(u\,\!\prime(x)=2x\); adding a constant to \(u\) is irrelevant.

  2. Why does \(\det D^2u=1\) alone not specify a unique transport?

    Hint and answer

    In \(d\ge2\), many positive-definite matrices \(A\) have determinant one. The potentials \(x^\top Ax/2\) send a given source to different target shapes; the boundary image chooses the target.
