Learning goal. Connect endpoint cost to minimum kinetic action and derive the formal Wasserstein gradient.

The static problem minimizes endpoint displacement. The Benamou--Brenier formula gives the same distance by minimizing kinetic energy over every mass-preserving flow joining the endpoints. It is the bridge from transport plans to fluid mechanics and the source of the formal Riemannian structure of \(\mathcal P_2\).

Dynamic action

Definition. An admissible dynamic pair between \(\mu_0,\mu_1\in\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\) consists of a narrowly continuous curve \(\mu_t\) and a velocity field \(v_t\in L^2(\mu_t)\) satisfying \[ \partial_t\mu_t+\nabla\cdot(v_t\mu_t)=0, \qquad \mu_{t=0}=\mu_0,\quad\mu_{t=1}=\mu_1. \] Its action is \[ \mathcal A(\mu,v)=\int_0^1\int_{\mathbb R^d}|v_t(x)|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t(x)\,\mathrm{d}t. \]

The dynamic formula

Benamou--Brenier formula. For \(\mu_0,\mu_1\in\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\), \[ \boxed{ W_2^2(\mu_0,\mu_1) = \inf_{(\mu_t,v_t)} \int_0^1\int|v_t|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t\,\mathrm{d}t }, \] where the infimum is over all admissible dynamic pairs.

Proof. For any admissible pair, Absolutely continuous curves and the continuity equation gives \[ W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1) \le\int_0^1|\dot\mu_t|\,\mathrm{d}t \le\int_0^1\|v_t\|_{L^2(\mu_t)}\,\mathrm{d}t. \] Cauchy--Schwarz on the unit time interval yields \[ W_2^2(\mu_0,\mu_1) \le\int_0^1\|v_t\|_{L^2(\mu_t)}^2\,\mathrm{d}t =\mathcal A(\mu,v). \] Hence \(W_2^2\) is a lower bound on every action.

For the reverse inequality, take the displacement geodesic generated by an optimal plan. It is absolutely continuous with constant metric speed \(W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1)\). Its minimal velocity field satisfies \[ \|v_t\|_{L^2(\mu_t)}=|\dot\mu_t|=W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1) \] for almost every \(t\). Consequently \[ \inf\mathcal A\le\mathcal A(\mu,v) =\int_0^1W_2^2(\mu_0,\mu_1)\,\mathrm{d}t=W_2^2(\mu_0,\mu_1)\le\inf\mathcal A. \] This proves equality and attainment.

Convex momentum formulation

If \(\mu_t=\rho_t\,\mathrm{d}x\), introduce momentum \(m_t=\rho_tv_t\). The constraints become linear: \[ \partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot m=0. \] The action becomes \[ \int_0^1\int\frac{|m_t(x)|^2}{\rho_t(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t, \] with the lower-semicontinuous convention \(0/0=0\) and \(|m|^2/0=+\infty\) for \(m\ne0\). The perspective function \((\rho,m)\mapsto|m|^2/\rho\) is convex, so the dynamic transport problem is a convex optimization problem in \((\rho,m)\).

Convexity. Define \(f(\rho,m)=|m|^2/\rho\) for \(\rho>0\), \(f(0,0)=0\), and \(f(0,m)=+\infty\) if \(m\ne0\). An explicit representation is \[ f(\rho,m)=\sup_{z\in\mathbb R^d}\{2z\cdot m-\rho|z|^2\},\qquad\rho\ge0. \] For \(\rho>0\), completing the square proves it: \[ 2z\cdot m-\rho|z|^2=\frac{|m|^2}{\rho}-\rho\left|z-\frac m\rho\right|^2. \] For \(\rho=0\), the supremum is zero when \(m=0\), and tends to infinity along \(z=tm\) when \(m\ne0\). Each function inside the supremum is continuous affine in \((\rho,m)\); therefore the supremum is convex and lower semicontinuous, including at zero density. Explicitly, for \(0\le t\le1\), \[ f((1-t)a+tb)\le(1-t)f(a)+tf(b), \] by taking the supremum of the affine identity. Integrating this pointwise inequality proves convexity of the action in \((\rho,m)\).

Equations for a smooth geodesic

A minimal velocity belongs to the tangent space and is therefore a gradient, \(v_t=\nabla\phi_t\), under smoothness assumptions. First variations of the action subject to the continuity equation give \[ \partial_t\phi_t+\frac12|\nabla\phi_t|^2=0. \] Taking a gradient yields the inviscid Burgers equation \[ \partial_tv_t+(v_t\cdot\nabla)v_t=0. \] Together with \[ \partial_t\rho_t+\nabla\cdot(\rho_tv_t)=0, \] this is pressureless Euler flow: particles keep constant velocity along straight characteristics.

Smooth variational derivation. Take smooth positive \(\rho\) and smooth fields on a flat torus (or impose compactly supported variations and the integrability needed on \(\mathbb R^d\)). Fix the endpoint densities. The half-action Lagrangian, after integration by parts in time and space, is \[ \mathscr L(\rho,v,\phi)=\int_0^1\int\rho\left(\tfrac12|v|^2-\partial_t\phi-v\cdot\nabla\phi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t +\left[\int\phi\rho\,\mathrm{d}x\right]_0^1. \] The endpoint term has zero variation. For \(v_h=v+hw\), differentiation gives \[ \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}h}\mathscr L(\rho,v_h,\phi)\right|_{h=0} =\int_0^1\int\rho(v-\nabla\phi)\cdot w\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=0. \] Since \(w\) is arbitrary and \(\rho>0\), the fundamental lemma of variations yields \(v=\nabla\phi\). Varying \(\rho\) by smooth \(\sigma\) with \(\int\sigma_t=0\) gives \[ \int\sigma\left(-\partial_t\phi-\tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2\right)=0. \] The bracket is therefore a function of time alone. Write \(-\partial_t\phi-\tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2=a(t)\). Replacing \(\phi\) by \(\widetilde\phi=\phi+\int_0^t a(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\) preserves \(\nabla\widetilde\phi=v\) and gives \[ \partial_t\widetilde\phi+\tfrac12|\nabla\widetilde\phi|^2 =\partial_t\phi+a(t)+\tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2=0. \] For each coordinate \(i\), commutation of smooth second derivatives gives \[ \partial_i\left(\tfrac12|\nabla\widetilde\phi|^2\right) =\sum_j(\partial_j\widetilde\phi)(\partial_i\partial_j\widetilde\phi) =\sum_jv_j\partial_jv_i. \] Taking a spatial gradient of Hamilton–Jacobi therefore gives \(\partial_tv+(v\cdot\nabla)v=0\). This is a smooth stationarity calculation; the preceding dynamic transport theorem supplies the rigorous measure-valued minimization statement.

Otto's tangent metric

Definition. For a sufficiently regular \(\mu\), \[ T_\mu\mathcal P_2 =\overline{\{\nabla\phi:\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^d)\}}^{L^2(\mu)}. \] The formal Riemannian inner product is \[ \langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\psi\rangle_{T_\mu} =\int\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi\,\mathrm{d}\mu. \]

A tangent vector is represented by the distribution \(-\nabla\cdot(\mu\nabla\phi)\), but the gradient field is the useful minimal representative. The metric norm of the tangent vector is \(\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^2(\mu)}\). The Benamou--Brenier formula is therefore the usual energy formula for Riemannian distance, interpreted on an infinite-dimensional space.

First variations and Wasserstein gradients

Formal gradient formula. Suppose a functional \(\mathcal F\) has first variation \(\delta\mathcal F/\delta\rho\), meaning \[ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}\mathcal F(\rho+\varepsilon\sigma)\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0} =\int\frac{\delta\mathcal F}{\delta\rho}\,\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x. \] Along \(\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho v)=0\), \[ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal F(\rho_t) =\int\nabla\!\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal F}{\delta\rho}\right)\cdot v_t\,\rho_t\,\mathrm{d}x. \] Hence \[ \operatorname{grad}_{W_2}\mathcal F(\rho) =\nabla\!\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal F}{\delta\rho}\right). \]

Proof in the smooth setting. Put \(f_t=\delta\mathcal F/\delta\rho(\rho_t)\). Assume the chain rule is valid and work on a torus, or assume sufficient integrability and vanishing boundary flux on \(\mathbb R^d\). The continuity equation and integration by parts give \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal F(\rho_t) &=\int f_t\partial_t\rho_t\,\mathrm{d}x\\ &=-\int f_t\nabla\cdot(\rho_tv_t)\,\mathrm{d}x\\ &=\int\nabla f_t\cdot v_t\,\rho_t\,\mathrm{d}x =\langle\nabla f_t,v_t\rangle_{L^2(\rho_t)}. \end{aligned} \] When \(\nabla f_t\) belongs to the Wasserstein tangent space, it is the vector representing this directional derivative in that Hilbert space. This is the stated formal gradient; for rough densities the chain rule and tangent-space membership require separate justification.

Examples of the action

Translation. For \(\mu_t=(x\mapsto x+ta)_\#\mu_0\), \(v_t=a\), so \[ \mathcal A=\int_0^1|a|^2\,\mathrm{d}t=|a|^2=W_2^2(\mu_0,\mu_1). \]

General \(p\). The same argument gives \[ W_p^p(\mu_0,\mu_1) =\inf\int_0^1\int|v_t|^p\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t\,\mathrm{d}t \] for \(1<p<\infty\). Only \(p=2\) produces a quadratic inner-product geometry.

Sources and proof details. Ambrosio–Gigli, A user's guide to optimal transport, §2, for the rigorous dynamic representation; the smooth Hamilton–Jacobi and tangent calculations here are formal and require the stated regularity.

Practice: action and first variations

  1. A Dirac mass follows \(x(t)=t^2a\). Compare its quadratic action with the optimal action between its endpoints.

    Hint and answer

    Its action is \(\int_0^1|2ta|^2\,\mathrm{d}t=4|a|^2/3\). Constant-speed motion has action \(|a|^2\). The geometric path alone does not determine the action; timing matters.

  2. Compute the first variation of \(\mathcal F(\rho)=\int V\rho\,\mathrm{d}x\) and its formal gradient flow.

    Hint and answer

    The first variation is \(V\), up to a constant. The velocity is \(-\nabla V\), and \(\partial_t\rho=\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla V)\).
