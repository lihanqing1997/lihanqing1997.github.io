Learning goal. Test convexity along transport geodesics and understand the role of the Jacobian in entropy convexity.

Convexity depends on the interpolation used. Entropy is convex under ordinary mixtures, while interaction energies need not be; neither fact alone determines behavior along transport geodesics. The geometry relevant to transport follows displacement geodesics, and convexity along those curves controls uniqueness, contraction, inequalities, and evolution equations.

Geodesic \(\lambda\)-convexity

Definition. A functional \(\mathcal F:\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\to(-\infty,\infty]\) is displacement \(\lambda\)-convex if for every pair \(\mu_0,\mu_1\) in its effective domain there exists a constant-speed geodesic \(\mu_t\) such that \[ \mathcal F(\mu_t) \le(1-t)\mathcal F(\mu_0)+t\mathcal F(\mu_1) -\frac{\lambda}{2}t(1-t)W_2^2(\mu_0,\mu_1). \] When the inequality holds along every geodesic, one says strongly displacement convex.

Positive \(\lambda\) is strong convexity, \(\lambda=0\) is ordinary geodesic convexity, and negative \(\lambda\) allows controlled concavity. The correction term is the metric analogue of strong convexity in a Hilbert space.

Potential energy

Proposition. Let \[ \mathcal V(\mu)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}V(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x). \] If \(D^2V\ge\lambda I\), then \(\mathcal V\) is displacement \(\lambda\)-convex.

Proof. The Hessian bound means \(x\mapsto V(x)-\lambda|x|^2/2\) is convex. Its convexity inequality and the identity \[ |(1-t)x+ty|^2=(1-t)|x|^2+t|y|^2-t(1-t)|x-y|^2 \] give \[ V((1-t)x+ty)\le(1-t)V(x)+tV(y)-\tfrac\lambda2t(1-t)|x-y|^2. \] Let \(\pi\) be an optimal coupling and \(F_t(x,y)=(1-t)x+ty\). Since \(\mu_t=F_{t\#}\pi\), integration gives \[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal V(\mu_t)&=\int V(F_t(x,y))\,\mathrm{d}\pi\\ &\le(1-t)\int V(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0+t\int V(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_1 -\tfrac\lambda2t(1-t)\int|x-y|^2\,\mathrm{d}\pi\\ &=(1-t)\mathcal V(\mu_0)+t\mathcal V(\mu_1) -\tfrac\lambda2t(1-t)W_2^2(\mu_0,\mu_1). \end{aligned} \] The Hessian bound supplies a quadratic-affine lower bound for \(V\), so its negative part is integrable for \(\mathcal P_2\) laws. The inequality is therefore meaningful also with infinite positive energy; for finite endpoint energies all terms above are finite.

Interaction energy

Proposition. Let \(W:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\) be even and convex, and define \[ \mathcal W(\mu)=\frac12\iint W(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y). \] Then \(\mathcal W\) is displacement convex. If \(D^2W\ge\lambda I\), the corresponding convexity estimate holds modulo translations; the center-of-mass direction remains flat.

Proof. Use two independent copies \((x,y)\), \((x',y')\) of an optimal coupling. Along interpolation, \[ x_t-x_t'=(1-t)(x-x')+t(y-y'). \] Writing \(F_t(x,y)=(1-t)x+ty\), its product pushforward is \(\mu_t\otimes\mu_t\). Thus \[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal W(\mu_t) &=\tfrac12\iint W((1-t)(x-x')+t(y-y'))\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\pi(x',y')\\ &\le\tfrac{1-t}{2}\iint W(x-x')\,\mathrm{d}\pi\,\mathrm{d}\pi +\tfrac t2\iint W(y-y')\,\mathrm{d}\pi\,\mathrm{d}\pi\\ &=(1-t)\mathcal W(\mu_0)+t\mathcal W(\mu_1). \end{aligned} \] This proves \[ \mathcal W(\mu_t)\le(1-t)\mathcal W(\mu_0)+t\mathcal W(\mu_1). \] For \(D=y-x\), \(D'=y'-x'\), strong convexity contributes \(-\lambda t(1-t)\mathbb E|D-D'|^2/4\). Expanding and using independence gives \[ \mathbb E[D\cdot D']=\mathbb ED\cdot\mathbb ED'=|\mathbb ED|^2, \qquad \mathbb ED=m_1-m_0, \] and consequently \[ \mathbb E|D-D'|^2=2\mathbb E|D|^2-2|\mathbb ED|^2 =2\{W_2^2(\mu_0,\mu_1)-|m_1-m_0|^2\}, \] where \(m_i=\int x\,\mathrm{d}\mu_i\). Hence the correction is \(-\lambda t(1-t)[W_2^2-|m_1-m_0|^2]/2\). In particular the energy is displacement \(\lambda\)-convex on a fixed-mean class; common translations have zero correction.

Internal energy and McCann's condition

Definition. For \(\mu=\rho\,\mathrm{d}x\), define \[ \mathcal U(\mu)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}U(\rho(x))\,\mathrm{d}x, \] and set \(\mathcal U(\mu)=+\infty\) off densities for superlinear \(U\). For the theorem below, assume \(U:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\) is continuous and convex, \(U(0)=0\), \(U(r)/r\to\infty\) at infinity, and \(U(r)\ge-Cr^a\) for \(0\le r\le1\), with \(d/(d+2)<a<1\). This last bound controls the negative part on \(\mathcal P_2\). McCann's condition is that \[ s\longmapsto s^dU(s^{-d}) \] is convex and nonincreasing on \((0,\infty)\).

McCann displacement-convexity theorem (external result). Under the preceding assumptions, if \(U\) satisfies McCann's condition, then \(\mathcal U\) is displacement convex on \(\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\).

Proof in a precise smooth setting. We prove the conclusion when the endpoints have densities on bounded domains and their Brenier map \(T\) is a \(C^1\) diffeomorphism with \(DT\) positive definite and eigenvalues bounded above and away from zero; assume the densities are bounded above and away from zero on their domains. These conditions make every following integral finite. The general theorem stated above is the nonsmooth result in the cited source.

Set \(T_t=(1-t)\operatorname{id}+tT\) and \(J_t=\det((1-t)I+tDT)\). Strong monotonicity makes \(T_t\) injective, and its positive Jacobian makes it a diffeomorphism onto its image. The change-of-variables formula and pushforward identity imply \[ \int f(T_t(x))\rho_t(T_t(x))J_t(x)\,\mathrm{d}x =\int f(z)\rho_t(z)\,\mathrm{d}z =\int f(T_t(x))\rho_0(x)\,\mathrm{d}x. \] Testing arbitrary bounded Borel \(f\) and using invertibility gives \(\rho_t(T_t(x))J_t(x)=\rho_0(x)\) almost everywhere.

For a positive matrix with eigenvalues \(a_1,\ldots,a_d\), expand \(\prod_i((1-t)+ta_i)\). The sum of its \(k\)-fold products is at least \(\binom dk(\prod_i a_i)^{k/d}\): apply arithmetic–geometric mean to the \(\binom dk\) products, noting that each \(a_i\) occurs \(\binom{d-1}{k-1}\) times. More explicitly, for \(1\le k\le d\), \[ \begin{aligned} \frac1{\binom dk}\sum_{|I|=k}\prod_{i\in I}a_i &\ge\left(\prod_{|I|=k}\prod_{i\in I}a_i\right)^{1/\binom dk}\\ &=\left(\prod_{i=1}^d a_i^{\binom{d-1}{k-1}}\right)^{1/\binom dk} =\left(\prod_i a_i\right)^{k/d}. \end{aligned} \] Taking the \(k=0\) term to be one, \[ J_t=\sum_{k=0}^d(1-t)^{d-k}t^k\sum_{|I|=k}\prod_{i\in I}a_i \ge\sum_{k=0}^d\binom dk(1-t)^{d-k}t^kJ_1^{k/d} =((1-t)+tJ_1^{1/d})^d. \] Taking the positive \(d\)-th root proves \[ J_t^{1/d}\ge(1-t)+tJ_1^{1/d}. \] For \(r>0\), \(H_r(s)=s^dU(r/s^d)=rH_1(s/r^{1/d})\), so McCann's scalar assumption makes \(H_r\) convex and nonincreasing. Therefore \[ J_tU(r/J_t)=H_r(J_t^{1/d}) \le H_r((1-t)+tJ_1^{1/d}) \le(1-t)U(r)+tJ_1U(r/J_1). \] With \(\Omega_0\) the source domain, substitute \(r=\rho_0(x)\) and integrate: \[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal U(\mu_t) &=\int_{T_t(\Omega_0)}U(\rho_t(z))\,\mathrm{d}z =\int_{\Omega_0}J_tU(\rho_0/J_t)\,\mathrm{d}x\\ &\le(1-t)\int_{\Omega_0}U(\rho_0)\,\mathrm{d}x +t\int_{\Omega_0}J_1U(\rho_0/J_1)\,\mathrm{d}x\\ &=(1-t)\mathcal U(\mu_0)+t\mathcal U(\mu_1). \end{aligned} \] This proves \(\mathcal U(\mu_t)\le(1-t)\mathcal U(\mu_0)+t\mathcal U(\mu_1)\) in the stated setting. No smooth-to-rough limit is asserted by this calculation: the general version uses almost-everywhere Jacobians and energy approximation as part of McCann's theorem.

Entropy and porous-medium energies

Boltzmann entropy. For \(U(r)=r\log r\), \[ s^dU(s^{-d})=-d\log s, \] which is convex and decreasing. Hence \[ \operatorname{Ent}(\mu)=\int\rho\log\rho\,\mathrm{d}x \] is displacement convex.

Power energies. For \(m>0\), \(m\ne1\), and \(U(r)=r^m/(m-1)\), McCann's condition holds when \[ m\ge1-\frac1d. \] The superlinear theorem above directly covers \(m>1\). For \(0<m<1\), the scalar condition still gives the displayed threshold, but the lower-semicontinuous extension assigns the singular part the recession coefficient \(U\,\!\prime(\infty)=0\), rather than \(+\infty\). On all of \(\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\), further control of the negative part is needed, for example \(m>d/(d+2)\). The fast-diffusion extension is not an automatic consequence of the superlinear statement.

Free energies

The functional \[ \mathcal F(\mu) =\int U(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x +\int V\,\mathrm{d}\mu +\frac12\iint W(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y) \] combines internal, confining, and interaction energies. The previous results allow their displacement-convexity constants to be added. This single class generates the heat, Fokker--Planck, porous-medium, and aggregation--diffusion equations studied on the JKO construction and diffusion equations.

Consequences of strict convexity

Uniqueness of minimizers. If \(\mathcal F\) is displacement \(\lambda\)-convex with \(\lambda>0\), then it has at most one minimizer. If a minimizer \(\mu_\star\) exists, then \[ \mathcal F(\mu)-\mathcal F(\mu_\star) \ge\frac{\lambda}{2}W_2^2(\mu,\mu_\star). \]

Proof. If \(\mathcal F(\mu)=+\infty\), the bound is immediate. Otherwise take a geodesic \(\mu_t\) from the finite-energy minimizer \(\mu_*\) to \(\mu\). Minimality and displacement convexity imply, for \(0<t<1\), \[ \mathcal F(\mu_*)\le\mathcal F(\mu_t) \le(1-t)\mathcal F(\mu_*)+t\mathcal F(\mu)-\tfrac\lambda2t(1-t)W_2^2(\mu_*,\mu). \] Subtract \((1-t)\mathcal F(\mu_*)\) and divide by \(t>0\): \[ \mathcal F(\mu)-\mathcal F(\mu_*)\ge\tfrac\lambda2(1-t)W_2^2(\mu_*,\mu). \] The distance is finite and independent of \(t\), so \(t\downarrow0\) gives the asserted estimate. If \(\mu\) is another minimizer, then \[ 0=\mathcal F(\mu)-\mathcal F(\mu_*)\ge\tfrac\lambda2W_2^2(\mu_*,\mu)\ge0. \] Because \(\lambda>0\), this forces \(W_2(\mu_*,\mu)=0\), hence \(\mu=\mu_*\).

Generalized geodesics

The squared Wasserstein distance is not convex along every ordinary geodesic in dimensions \(d\ge2\). Many variational arguments use generalized geodesics with a common base measure: glue optimal maps from a base \(\omega\) to \(\mu_0,\mu_1\), then interpolate their images. Writing \(\mu_t=((1-t)T_0+tT_1)_\#\omega\), the natural coupling to \(\omega\) gives an upper bound for \(W_2^2(\omega,\mu_t)\) by the corresponding Hilbertian quadratic expression. The interpolated coupling need not itself be optimal. For absolutely continuous \(\omega\), both maps are convex gradients and so is their convex combination, which gives equality in this particular setting.

Sources and proof details. Santambrogio, Optimal transport for applied mathematicians, Chapter 7, for McCann's condition and the treatment of singular measures and internal energies.

Practice: which interpolation is convex?

  1. For \(V(x)=|x|^2/2\), show the displacement convexity inequality is an equality with \(\lambda=1\).

    Hint and answer

    Expand \(|(1-t)x+ty|^2\), integrate under an optimal plan, and identify \(\int|x-y|^2\,\mathrm{d}\pi=W_2^2\).

  2. Let \(\rho_a\) be uniform on an interval of length \(a>0\). Compute its entropy and test it along interpolation of lengths.

    Hint and answer

    \(\operatorname{Ent}(\rho_a)=-\log a\). The one-dimensional transport geodesic interpolates lengths linearly, and \(-\log\) is convex.
