Learning goal. Separate regular Gaussian inference, nonsmooth limits, and degenerate null limits before choosing a bootstrap.

Statistical optimal transport is not governed by one universal central limit theorem. Transport costs can be smooth away from equality and degenerate at equality; potentials may be unique or set-valued; maps require differentiation of convex potentials; barycenters are nonlinear Fréchet means. The inferential regime must be identified before a limit law or bootstrap is chosen.

Transport value as a statistical functional

Definition. For a fixed cost \(c\), write \[ \mathcal T_c(\mu,\nu) =\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int c\,\mathrm{d}\pi. \] Its plug-in estimator is \(\mathcal T_c(\widehat\mu_n,\widehat\nu_m)\), or \(\mathcal T_c(\widehat\mu_n,\nu)\) in the one-sample case.

Directional derivatives from duality

Envelope principle with a compact potential class. Suppose the dual value, throughout a neighborhood of the base marginals in a specified vector topology, is the maximum over one compact class \(K\) of normalized feasible potential pairs. Suppose potential integration is continuous on \(K\), and pairings with convergent perturbation directions converge uniformly on \(K\). These assumptions hold, for example, for finite cost matrices with strictly positive marginal weights, after restricting to bounded normalized transformed potentials. Let \(\Phi^\star(\mu,\nu)\) be the normalized set of optimal dual pairs. Then the Hadamard directional derivative along feasible perturbations in signed zero-mass directions \((\alpha,\beta)\) is \[ \mathcal T'_{c,(\mu,\nu)}(\alpha,\beta) = \sup_{(\varphi,\psi)\in\Phi^\star(\mu,\nu)} \left\{\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\alpha+\int\psi\,\mathrm{d}\beta\right\}. \] If the normalized dual pair is unique, the derivative is linear.

Proof. Write \(z=(\mu,\nu)\), \(h=(\alpha,\beta)\), \(\ell_k(z)=\mu\varphi_k+\nu\psi_k\), so \(\mathcal T(z)=\max_{k\in K}\ell_k(z)\). Let \(t_j\downarrow0\), \(h_j\to h\), and \(k_j\) maximize at \(z+t_jh_j\). For each base optimizer \(k\in K^*(z)\), \[ \frac{\mathcal T(z+t_jh_j)-\mathcal T(z)}{t_j}\ge\ell_k(h_j). \] Taking the liminf and then the supremum over \(k\) gives the lower bound. For the upper bound, choose a subsequence realizing the limsup and extract \(k_j\to k_*\in K\). Uniform convergence of the directional pairings on compact \(K\) gives \(M=\sup_{j,k}|\ell_k(h_j)|<\infty\). Fix a base optimizer \(k_0\). Maximality at \(z+t_jh_j\) yields \[ \ell_{k_j}(z)+t_j\ell_{k_j}(h_j)\ge\ell_{k_0}(z)+t_j\ell_{k_0}(h_j), \] and hence \[ 0\le\mathcal T(z)-\ell_{k_j}(z) \le t_j(\ell_{k_j}(h_j)-\ell_{k_0}(h_j))\le2Mt_j\to0. \] Continuity in \(k\) then gives \(\ell_{k_*}(z)=\mathcal T(z)\), so \(k_*\in K^*(z)\). Also \[ \frac{\mathcal T(z+t_jh_j)-\mathcal T(z)}{t_j} =\frac{\ell_{k_j}(z)-\mathcal T(z)}{t_j}+\ell_{k_j}(h_j) \le\ell_{k_j}(h_j)\longrightarrow\ell_{k_*}(h). \] The last convergence is justified by \[ |\ell_{k_j}(h_j)-\ell_{k_*}(h)| \le\sup_{k\in K}|\ell_k(h_j)-\ell_k(h)|+|\ell_{k_j}(h)-\ell_{k_*}(h)|\to0. \] The limiting value is at most \(\sup_{k\in K^*(z)}\ell_k(h)\), matching the lower bound. Uniform convergence in the direction assumption is precisely what makes this a Hadamard, rather than merely fixed-direction, conclusion.

Uniqueness of the transport plan does not by itself guarantee differentiability of the value. What matters for the envelope derivative is uniqueness of the normalized dual potentials in the relevant function space.

Central limit theorem in a regular regime

Two-sample delta method. Suppose the normalized optimal potentials \((\varphi,\psi)\) are unique, square integrable, and the transport functional is Hadamard differentiable tangentially to a space in which the two empirical processes converge, with derivative \((\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\alpha\varphi+\beta\psi\). These are additional hypotheses, not consequences of potential uniqueness alone. If \(n/(n+m)\to\lambda\in(0,1)\), then \[ \sqrt{\frac{nm}{n+m}} \{\mathcal T_c(\widehat\mu_n,\widehat\nu_m)-\mathcal T_c(\mu,\nu)\} \Rightarrow N(0,\sigma^2), \] where \[ \sigma^2 =(1-\lambda)\operatorname{Var}_\mu\{\varphi(X)\} +\lambda\operatorname{Var}_\nu\{\psi(Y)\}. \]

Proof. Linear differentiability gives \[ \mathcal T_c(\widehat\mu_n,\widehat\nu_m)-\mathcal T_c(\mu,\nu) = (\widehat\mu_n-\mu)\varphi+(\widehat\nu_m-\nu)\psi +o_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2}). \] The functional delta method (the external probability theorem for Hadamard differentiable functionals and tight empirical processes) supplies this stochastic remainder under the stated hypotheses. Put \(r_{n,m}=\sqrt{nm/(n+m)}\). Then \(r_{n,m}/\sqrt n\to\sqrt{1-\lambda}\), \(r_{n,m}/\sqrt m\to\sqrt\lambda\), and \(r_{n,m}(n^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2})\) is bounded. The scaled remainder is therefore \(o_{\mathbb P}(1)\). Writing \[ A_n=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n(\varphi(X_i)-\mu\varphi), \qquad B_m=\frac1{\sqrt m}\sum_{j=1}^m(\psi(Y_j)-\nu\psi), \] independence and the scalar CLT give \[ (A_n,B_m)\Rightarrow(Z_\mu,Z_\nu),\qquad Z_\mu\perp Z_\nu,\quad \operatorname{Var}Z_\mu=\operatorname{Var}_\mu\varphi,\quad \operatorname{Var}Z_\nu=\operatorname{Var}_\nu\psi. \] The scaled expansion is \[ r_{n,m}(\mathcal T(\widehat\mu_n,\widehat\nu_m)-\mathcal T(\mu,\nu)) =\sqrt{\frac m{n+m}}A_n+\sqrt{\frac n{n+m}}B_m+o_{\mathbb P}(1). \] Slutsky gives \(\sqrt{1-\lambda}Z_\mu+\sqrt\lambda Z_\nu\). Its covariance cross term is zero by independence, so its variance is exactly \((1-\lambda)\operatorname{Var}_\mu\varphi+\lambda\operatorname{Var}_\nu\psi\).

An explicit regular two-point model. Let \(\mu=\operatorname{Bern}(a)\), \(\nu=\operatorname{Bern}(b)\) on \(\{0,1\}\), with \(0<a,b<1\) and \(a\ne b\). For every \(p\ge1\), \[ W_p^p(\mu,\nu)=|a-b|. \] The common mass stays put and exactly \(|a-b|\) crosses the unit gap. If \(\widehat a,\widehat b\) are sample proportions and \(n/(n+m)\to\lambda\), the ordinary delta method gives \[ \sqrt{\frac{nm}{n+m}}\bigl(|\widehat a-\widehat b|-|a-b|\bigr) \Rightarrow N\bigl(0,(1-\lambda)a(1-a)+\lambda b(1-b)\bigr). \] At \(a=b\), the same scaling instead gives the absolute value of a centered normal variable. Thus “under equality” does not always mean a second-order limit: it may mean a first-order nonsmooth limit. The smooth one-dimensional \(W_2^2\) example below has a different, quadratic regime.

Nonunique potentials and directional limits

If \(\Phi^\star\) contains several normalized pairs, the derivative is a supremum of Gaussian linear functionals rather than a single linear functional. The functional delta method then yields a generally non-Gaussian limit \[ \sup_{(\varphi,\psi)\in\Phi^\star} \{\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,\mathbb G_\mu\varphi+\sqrt\lambda\,\mathbb G_\nu\psi\}. \] Here the independent Brownian bridges correspond to the two samples and the normalization is the same as above. Such limits can occur for discrete measures, symmetric configurations, and costs with flat optimal faces; valid functional convergence must still be proved.

Degeneracy under equality

First-order degeneracy. For \(c(x,y)=d(x,y)^p\), \(\mathcal T_c(\mu,\mu)=0\). The zero potential pair is optimal, and in many smooth settings the first directional derivative along probability perturbations vanishes. The empirical transport cost under the null is then governed by a second-order or nonsmooth limit and does not have the same scaling as under \(\mu\ne\nu\).

One-dimensional \(W_2\) null limit. Suppose \(\mu\) has density \(f\in C^1([a,b])\), \(0<c\le f\le C\), on \([a,b]\), and zero outside. Write \(Q=F^{-1}\). The quantile-process theorem, used here as an external consequence of the uniform empirical-process CLT and the derivative of inversion, gives convergence in \(L^2(0,1)\): \[ \sqrt n\{F_n^{-1}(u)-Q(u)\} \Rightarrow-\frac{B(u)}{f(Q(u))}, \] where \(B\) is a Brownian bridge. Therefore \[ nW_2^2(\widehat\mu_n,\mu) \Rightarrow \int_0^1\frac{B(u)^2}{f(Q(u))^2}\,\mathrm{d}u. \] The limit is quadratic and non-Gaussian, illustrating why a root-\(n\) Gaussian approximation to \(W_2^2\) fails at the null.

Justification of the final limit. Put \(Z_n=\sqrt n(Q_n-Q)\) and \(Z(u)=-B(u)/f(Q(u))\), the \(L^2(0,1)\)-valued limit given by the stated quantile-process theorem. The quantile identity gives the exact equality \[ nW_2^2(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)=n\int_0^1|Q_n-Q|^2\,\mathrm{d}u=\|Z_n\|_2^2. \] For \(z,w\in L^2\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives \[ |\|z\|_2^2-\|w\|_2^2| =|\langle z-w,z+w\rangle| \le\|z-w\|_2(\|z\|_2+\|w\|_2). \] Hence squared norm is continuous, and the continuous-mapping theorem yields \[ nW_2^2(\widehat\mu_n,\mu)\Rightarrow\|Z\|_2^2 =\int_0^1\frac{B(u)^2}{f(Q(u))^2}\,\mathrm{d}u. \] Since \(f\ge c>0\), Tonelli and \(\mathbb EB(u)^2=u(1-u)\) give \[ \mathbb E\|Z\|_2^2 =\int_0^1\frac{u(1-u)}{f(Q(u))^2}\,\mathrm{d}u \le c^{-2}\int_0^1u(1-u)\,\mathrm{d}u=\frac1{6c^2}<\infty. \] The convergence used here is in \(L^2\)-distribution, not merely pointwise in the quantile level.

Bootstrap validity

Bootstrap principle. The ordinary empirical bootstrap is typically valid when \(\mathcal T_c\) is fully Hadamard differentiable with a unique linear derivative and the potential class is empirically regular. It may fail when the derivative is only directional, when the null is degenerate, or when the empirical support changes the active optimal face.

Possible remedies include subsampling, an \(m\)-out-of-\(n\) bootstrap, estimating the directional derivative and simulating its Gaussian argument, or using entropic regularization to obtain a smooth functional. Each remedy targets a different source of nonregularity and must be justified for the regime at hand.

Estimating optimal potentials

Empirical dual potentials are \(M\)-estimators over a constrained function class. Consistency follows from compactness after normalization, uniform convergence of the empirical dual objective, and uniqueness of the population potential. Rates require local curvature of the dual criterion and control of the complexity of the potential class. Potentials are statistically important because they are influence functions for regular transport costs.

Estimating transport maps

Plug-in map principle. For quadratic cost, estimate densities or potentials, solve an empirical or regularized Monge--Ampère problem for \(\widehat u\), and set \(\widehat T=\nabla\widehat u\). Under strong convexity and regularity, \[ \|\widehat T-T\|_{L^2(\mu)} \] can be controlled by errors in the estimated potential, its gradient, or the source and target measures in stronger norms than weak convergence alone.

Raw empirical measures in dimension \(d\ge2\) usually yield a discrete plan rather than a globally defined map. A map estimator therefore requires interpolation, density smoothing, semi-discrete transport, or another structural regularization. Cost stability alone does not supply quantitative derivative error bounds or confidence statements for maps. Convexity does provide useful qualitative gradient stability, but rates require additional control of the potential class and the underlying distributions.

Statistical barycenters

A one-dimensional barycenter CLT. Let \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_N\) be independent identically distributed random laws on \(\mathbb R\), with quantile functions \(Q_i\), and suppose \[ \mathbb E\|Q_1\|_{L^2(0,1)}^2<\infty. \] Their sample \(W_2\)-barycenter has quantile \(\widehat Q_N=N^{-1}\sum_iQ_i\). The population barycenter has quantile \(\bar Q=\mathbb EQ_1\), and \[ \sqrt N(\widehat Q_N-\bar Q)\Rightarrow G \quad\text{in }L^2(0,1), \] where \(G\) is centered Gaussian with covariance operator \[ Ch=\mathbb E\bigl[\langle Q_1-\bar Q,h\rangle(Q_1-\bar Q)\bigr]. \]

Proof. The quantile isometry turns squared \(W_2\) distance into squared \(L^2\) distance. Bochner expectation exists because \(\mathbb E\|Q_1\|_2\le(\mathbb E\|Q_1\|_2^2)^{1/2}<\infty\). Let \(\mathcal C\subset L^2(0,1)\) be the cone of classes with nondecreasing representatives. It is convex. To check closedness, if \(q_n\in\mathcal C\) and \(q_n\to q\) in \(L^2\), a subsequence converges pointwise on a full-measure set \(E\). For \(u<v\) in \(E\), \[ q_n(u)\le q_n(v)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q(u)\le q(v). \] Extending these ordered values monotonically across the null complement gives a nondecreasing representative equal to \(q\) almost everywhere (the possible one-sided discrepancies are at most countably many jumps). Thus \(\mathcal C\) is closed. Bochner expectations of integrable random elements in a closed convex set stay in that set, by approximation with finite convex combinations. Hence \(\bar Q=\mathbb EQ_1\in\mathcal C\). For any candidate quantile \(q\), \[ \mathbb E\|Q_1-q\|_2^2=\mathbb E\|Q_1-\bar Q\|_2^2+\|q-\bar Q\|_2^2, \] Indeed, expansion produces the cross term \[ 2\mathbb E\langle Q_1-\bar Q,\bar Q-q\rangle =2\langle\mathbb E(Q_1-\bar Q),\bar Q-q\rangle=0. \] Interchanging pairing and expectation is valid because \(\mathbb E|\langle Q_1-\bar Q,\bar Q-q\rangle|\le\mathbb E\|Q_1-\bar Q\|_2\|\bar Q-q\|_2<\infty\). This proves the unique population minimizer. Replacing expectation by the finite sample average proves the sample formula. Set \(Y_i=Q_i-\bar Q\). Then \[ \mathbb EY_i=0,\qquad\mathbb E\|Y_i\|_2^2<\infty,\qquad \sqrt N(\widehat Q_N-\bar Q)=N^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^NY_i. \] The central limit theorem for i.i.d. variables in a separable Hilbert space is the probability input and applies with these hypotheses. Its covariance is characterized, for \(h,k\in L^2\), by \[ \mathbb E[\langle G,h\rangle\langle G,k\rangle] =\mathbb E[\langle Y_1,h\rangle\langle Y_1,k\rangle] =\langle Ch,k\rangle, \quad Ch=\mathbb E[\langle Y_1,h\rangle Y_1]. \] The defining expectation is finite by \(\mathbb E\|\langle Y_1,h\rangle Y_1\|_2\le\|h\|_2\mathbb E\|Y_1\|_2^2\).

This theorem observes each input distribution itself. If each \(\mu_i\) is instead estimated from a finite inner sample, an additional estimation layer appears. In higher dimensions there is no global quantile chart: a tangent-space expansion needs its own differentiability, invertibility, remainder, and tightness arguments. The scalar and Hilbert-space calculations cannot simply be relabeled as a general barycenter CLT.

Regularization changes the estimand

Entropic and other regularizations often restore differentiability and improve sample behavior, but they estimate a regularized transport functional. Valid inference must separate stochastic error from regularization bias and specify whether the regularization parameter is fixed or tends to zero. Entropic transport and Sinkhorn divergences make this tradeoff explicit.

Sources and proof details. Balakrishnan–Manole–Wasserman (2025), for map estimation and inferential regimes. The compact-class envelope theorem and one-dimensional barycenter CLT are proved above with their hypotheses; general empirical-process and quantile-process CLTs are external probability inputs. The two-point and one-dimensional examples specify concrete models.

Practice: choosing the inferential regime

  1. At equality of two Bernoulli laws, why is the ordinary centered bootstrap for \(|\widehat a-\widehat b|\) problematic?

    Hint and answer

    The derivative of \(|z|\) at zero is directional and nonlinear. The bootstrap sees a random local shift rather than reproducing the fixed null absolute-normal law automatically.

  2. If \(\varepsilon_n\to0\), what extra term must be controlled to use an entropic confidence interval for unregularized transport?

    Hint and answer

    The bias between the regularized and unregularized population functionals must be negligible on the confidence interval scale or explicitly corrected; numerical error must be controlled too.
