Learning goal. Compute barycenters on the line and separate their mixture-convex uniqueness proof from geodesic arguments.

A Wasserstein barycenter is an average of distributions formed in transport geometry rather than by mixing their densities. It is the Fréchet mean of probability measures and links geodesics, multi-marginal transport, convexity, and statistical estimation.

The Fréchet functional

Definition. For \(\mu_i\in\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\), positive weights \(\lambda_i\), and \(\sum_i\lambda_i=1\), a barycenter minimizes \[ \mathcal V(\nu)=\frac12\sum_i\lambda_iW_2^2(\nu,\mu_i). \] It averages locations through transport rather than averaging densities.

Existence and uniqueness. A barycenter exists. If at least one input \(\mu_j\) is absolutely continuous, it is unique.

Existence. Comparing a minimizing sequence \(\nu_n\) with a fixed competitor bounds \(W_2(\nu_n,\mu_1)\), hence bounds its second moments. This gives tightness and a weakly convergent subsequence, \(\nu_n\Rightarrow\nu\). Portmanteau gives \(\nu\in\mathcal P_2\). For each \(i\), optimal plans between \(\nu_n\) and \(\mu_i\) have weakly convergent subsequences with limiting marginals \(\nu,\mu_i\); lower semicontinuity of squared distance yields \[ W_2^2(\nu,\mu_i)\le\liminf_nW_2^2(\nu_n,\mu_i). \] The finite weighted sum is therefore lower semicontinuous, so \(\nu\) is a minimizer. We used weak compactness, not a false assertion of \(W_2\)-compactness from bounded moments.

Uniqueness. Each \(W_2^2(\mu_i,\cdot)\) is convex under ordinary mixtures: mix two optimal plans to obtain a feasible plan for the mixed target. If \(\mu_j\) is absolutely continuous, this convexity is strict between distinct targets \(\nu_0,\nu_1\). Indeed, equality would make \[ (1-t)(\operatorname{id},T_0)_\#\mu_j+t(\operatorname{id},T_1)_\#\mu_j \] optimal for the mixed target. Brenier's theorem forces its conditional distribution \((1-t)\delta_{T_0(x)}+t\delta_{T_1(x)}\) to be a Dirac mass. Thus \(T_0=T_1\) almost everywhere, implying \(\nu_0=\nu_1\), a contradiction. Since \(\lambda_j>0\), \(\mathcal V\) is strictly mixture-convex.

Absolute continuity of the barycenter itself is a further regularity result, not a consequence of this uniqueness proof. The first-order equation below explicitly assumes it. Ordinary mixture convexity here is compatible with the failure of general geodesic convexity of squared Wasserstein distance.

First-order optimality

Barycenter equation. Suppose the barycenter \(\bar\mu\) is absolutely continuous and let \(T_i\) be the Brenier map from \(\bar\mu\) to \(\mu_i\). Then \[ \sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_iT_i(x)=x \qquad\text{for }\bar\mu\text{-almost every }x. \]

Proof. The following argument uses only transport couplings and elementary differentiation, before the general continuity-equation calculus. Perturb \(\bar\mu\) by \(S_\varepsilon=\operatorname{id}+\varepsilon\xi\), with smooth compactly supported \(\xi\). Coupling \(S_\varepsilon(x)\) with \(T_i(x)\) gives \[ W_2^2(S_{\varepsilon\#}\bar\mu,\mu_i) \le\int|x+\varepsilon\xi(x)-T_i(x)|^2\,\mathrm{d}\bar\mu(x). \] Writing \(B=\int\xi\cdot\sum_i\lambda_i(x-T_i(x))\,\mathrm{d}\bar\mu\), expansion and minimality give \[ 0\le\mathcal V(S_{\varepsilon\#}\bar\mu)-\mathcal V(\bar\mu) \le\varepsilon B+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}\int|\xi|^2\,\mathrm{d}\bar\mu. \] Divide separately for \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(\varepsilon<0\) and let \(\varepsilon\to0\); this forces \(B=0\), namely \[ \int \xi(x)\cdot\sum_i\lambda_i(x-T_i(x))\,\mathrm{d}\bar\mu(x)=0 \] for every \(\xi\). Hence the vector field in parentheses vanishes almost surely.

Multi-marginal formulation

Multi-marginal representation. Let \[ \bar x_\lambda=\sum_i\lambda_ix_i. \] Then \[ \inf_{\nu}\sum_i\lambda_iW_2^2(\nu,\mu_i) = \inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_N)} \int\sum_i\lambda_i|x_i-\bar x_\lambda|^2\,\mathrm{d}\gamma. \] If \(\gamma^\star\) solves the right side, then \((\bar x_\lambda)_\#\gamma^\star\) is a barycenter.

Proof. Given a candidate \(\nu\), glue optimal couplings of \(\nu\) with all \(\mu_i\) conditionally on the barycenter variable \(z\). For every \(x_1,\ldots,x_N,z\), \[ \sum_i\lambda_i|x_i-z|^2 = \sum_i\lambda_i|x_i-\bar x_\lambda|^2+|z-\bar x_\lambda|^2. \] The minimum over \(z\) is attained at \(\bar x_\lambda\), giving one inequality. Conversely, any multi-marginal coupling and \(z=\bar x_\lambda\) induce couplings of its law with each \(\mu_i\), giving the other inequality. Both infima are attained by the direct method. If the starting \(\nu\) is a barycenter, the same identity and equality of the infima force \(\mathbb E|z-\bar x_\lambda|^2=0\) in the glued plan. Thus every barycenter is the weighted-sum law of an optimal input coupling, the fact used in the stability proof below.

One-dimensional barycenters

Quantile formula. On \(\mathbb R\), the unique \(W_2\)-barycenter satisfies \[ F_{\bar\mu}^{-1}(u)=\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_iF_{\mu_i}^{-1}(u) \quad\text{for almost every }u\in(0,1). \]

Proof. By the one-dimensional formula, \[ \mathcal V(\nu)=\frac12\int_0^1 \sum_i\lambda_i|F_\nu^{-1}(u)-F_{\mu_i}^{-1}(u)|^2\,\mathrm{d}u. \] Pointwise minimization in \(F_\nu^{-1}(u)\) gives the weighted average. It remains nondecreasing because it is a positive combination of nondecreasing functions, so it is a valid quantile.

Gaussian barycenters

Gaussian barycenter (external result). The barycenter of nondegenerate Gaussian laws \(N(m_i,\Sigma_i)\) is \(N(\bar m,\bar\Sigma)\), where \[ \bar m=\sum_i\lambda_im_i \] and \(\bar\Sigma\) is the unique positive-definite solution of \[ \bar\Sigma = \sum_i\lambda_i (\bar\Sigma^{1/2}\Sigma_i\bar\Sigma^{1/2})^{1/2}. \]

The equation is the matrix form of \(\sum_i\lambda_iT_i(x)=x\). If the covariance matrices commute, it simplifies to \[ \bar\Sigma^{1/2}=\sum_i\lambda_i\Sigma_i^{1/2}. \]

Proof in the commuting case. Simultaneously diagonalize the covariances, with coordinate standard deviations \(s_{i\ell}>0\). For any joint coupling of the Gaussian inputs, its multi-marginal objective is \[ \sum_i\lambda_i\mathbb E|X_i|^2-\mathbb E\left|\sum_i\lambda_iX_i\right|^2. \] The first term and all means are fixed. For each coordinate, Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\operatorname{Cov}(X_{i\ell},X_{j\ell})\le s_{i\ell}s_{j\ell}\), so the second term is maximized by \(X_{i\ell}=m_{i\ell}+s_{i\ell}Z_\ell\), with a common vector of independent standard normals \(Z\). This joint law therefore minimizes the objective. Its weighted sum is Gaussian with mean \(\sum_i\lambda_im_i\) and coordinate standard deviations \(\sum_i\lambda_i s_{i\ell}\), proving the formula among all candidate laws. The general matrix theorem above is the external Gaussian barycenter result in the source below.

Stability of barycenters

Stability. If \(\mu_{i,n}\to\mu_i\) in \(W_2\) with fixed positive weights, barycenters \(\bar\mu_n\) are relatively compact in \(W_2\), and every limit is a barycenter of the limiting inputs. A unique limiting barycenter gives convergence of the whole sequence.

Proof. Use the multi-marginal representation above to write each barycenter as the law of \(Z_n=\sum_i\lambda_iX_{i,n}\) under an optimal joint coupling of the inputs. The input \(W_2\)-convergence makes each family \(|X_{i,n}|^2\) uniformly integrable. Jensen gives \[ |Z_n|^2\le\sum_i\lambda_i|X_{i,n}|^2. \] A finite sum of uniformly integrable families remains uniformly integrable under arbitrary couplings: on a small-probability event, bound each term by its own tail plus a fixed cutoff times the event probability. Thus the barycenters have uniformly integrable second moments as well as tightness, giving \(W_2\)-compactness. The triangle inequality implies \(\mathcal V_n(\nu_n)\to\mathcal V(\nu)\) whenever \(\nu_n\to\nu\) in \(W_2\). Passing \(\mathcal V_n(\bar\mu_n)\le\mathcal V_n(\nu)\) to a subsequential limit proves optimality. Uniqueness identifies all limits.

Population Fréchet means

If \(\mathsf Q\) is a probability distribution on \(\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\), a population barycenter minimizes \[ \nu\longmapsto\frac12\int W_2^2(\nu,\mu)\,\mathrm{d}\mathsf Q(\mu). \] The empirical barycenter replaces \(\mathsf Q\) by sampled distributions. Existence, uniqueness, consistency, and limit distributions are therefore Fréchet-mean questions in a nonlinear metric space; Inference for transport costs, maps, and barycenters returns to their statistical theory.

Sources and proof details. Peyré–Cuturi, Computational optimal transport, §9.2, for barycenters, Gaussian examples, and computation. The multi-marginal representation above supplies the stability proof.

Practice: averaging distributions

  1. Find the equal-weight \(W_2\)-barycenter of \(\delta_{-1}\) and \(\delta_1\). Compare it with their mixture.

    Hint and answer

    The barycenter is \(\delta_0\), since \(\tfrac12(x+1)^2+\tfrac12(x-1)^2=x^2+1\). The mixture keeps its mass at the endpoints.

  2. Find the equal-weight barycenter of \(N(0,1)\) and \(N(2,9)\) on the line.

    Hint and answer

    Means and standard deviations average: the answer is \(N(1,4)\). Averaging the variances would give the wrong answer.
