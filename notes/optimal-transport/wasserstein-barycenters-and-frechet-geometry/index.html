Learning goal. Compute barycenters on the line and separate their mixture-convex uniqueness proof from geodesic arguments.

A Wasserstein barycenter is an average of distributions formed in transport geometry rather than by mixing their densities. It is the Fréchet mean of probability measures and links geodesics, multi-marginal transport, convexity, and statistical estimation.

The Fréchet functional

Definition 13.1 (The Fréchet functional). For \(\mu_i\in\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\), positive weights \(\lambda_i\), and \(\sum_i\lambda_i=1\), a barycenter minimizes \[ \mathcal V(\nu)=\frac12\sum_i\lambda_iW_2^2(\nu,\mu_i). \] It averages locations through transport rather than averaging densities.

Theorem 13.2 (Existence and uniqueness). A barycenter exists. If at least one input \(\mu_j\) is absolutely continuous, it is unique.

Existence. Recall \(\mathcal V(\nu)=\tfrac12\sum_i\lambda_iW_2^2(\nu,\mu_i)\), where \(\lambda_i>0\), \(\sum_i\lambda_i=1\). Choose a minimizing sequence with \(\mathcal V(\nu_n)\le C<\infty\). Then \[ W_2^2(\nu_n,\mu_1)\le2C/\lambda_1,\qquad \int|z|^2\,\mathrm{d}\nu_n(z) \le2W_2^2(\nu_n,\mu_1)+2\int|x|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_1(x)\le M. \] The moment bound follows by integrating \(|z|^2\le2|z-x|^2+2|x|^2\) against an optimal coupling. Markov gives \(\nu_n(|z|>R)\le M/R^2\), so Prokhorov gives a weakly convergent subsequence \(\nu_{n_k}\Rightarrow\nu\). Portmanteau yields \(\int|z|^2\,\mathrm{d}\nu\le M\).

For each \(i\), take a further subsequence realizing the liminf of \(W_2^2(\nu_{n_k},\mu_i)\), and then a weak limit \(\pi_i\) of the corresponding optimal couplings. Its marginals are \(\nu,\mu_i\), and \[ W_2^2(\nu,\mu_i)\le\int|z-x|^2\,\mathrm{d}\pi_i \le\liminf_kW_2^2(\nu_{n_k},\mu_i). \] For the finite nonnegative sum, \[ \inf\mathcal V\le\mathcal V(\nu) \le\tfrac12\sum_i\lambda_i\liminf_kW_2^2(\nu_{n_k},\mu_i) \le\liminf_k\mathcal V(\nu_{n_k})=\inf\mathcal V. \] Hence \(\nu\) is a barycenter.

Uniqueness. If \(\pi_{i,0},\pi_{i,1}\) optimally couple \(\mu_i\) to \(\nu_0,\nu_1\), then \((1-t)\pi_{i,0}+t\pi_{i,1}\) couples \(\mu_i\) to \(\nu_t=(1-t)\nu_0+t\nu_1\), giving \[ W_2^2(\mu_i,\nu_t)\le(1-t)W_2^2(\mu_i,\nu_0)+tW_2^2(\mu_i,\nu_1). \] For an absolutely continuous \(\mu_j\), write \(\pi_{j,k}=(\operatorname{id},T_k)_\#\mu_j\). If equality held for \(0<t<1\), the mixed plan would be optimal; Brenier forces its conditional laws to be Dirac: \[ (1-t)\delta_{T_0(x)}+t\delta_{T_1(x)}=\delta_{S(x)}\quad\mu_j\text{-a.e.} \] Evaluating the complement of \(\{S(x)\}\) gives \(T_0=T_1=S\) almost everywhere, hence \(\nu_0=\nu_1\). Thus distinct targets give strict inequality for the \(j\)-th term; since \(\lambda_j>0\), \(\mathcal V\) is strictly convex under mixtures. Two different minimizers would have a midpoint of smaller value, a contradiction.

Absolute continuity of the barycenter itself is a further regularity result, not a consequence of this uniqueness proof. The first-order equation below explicitly assumes it. Ordinary mixture convexity here is compatible with the failure of general geodesic convexity of squared Wasserstein distance.

First-order optimality

Theorem 13.3 (Barycenter equation). Suppose the barycenter \(\bar\mu\) is absolutely continuous and let \(T_i\) be the Brenier map from \(\bar\mu\) to \(\mu_i\). Then \[ \sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_iT_i(x)=x \qquad\text{for }\bar\mu\text{-almost every }x. \]

Proof. The following argument uses only transport couplings and elementary differentiation, before the general continuity-equation calculus. Perturb \(\bar\mu\) by \(S_\varepsilon=\operatorname{id}+\varepsilon\xi\), with smooth compactly supported \(\xi\). Coupling \(S_\varepsilon(x)\) with \(T_i(x)\) gives \[ W_2^2(S_{\varepsilon\#}\bar\mu,\mu_i) \le\int|x+\varepsilon\xi(x)-T_i(x)|^2\,\mathrm{d}\bar\mu(x). \] The expansion used here is \[ |x+\varepsilon\xi-T_i|^2=|x-T_i|^2+2\varepsilon(x-T_i)\cdot\xi+\varepsilon^2|\xi|^2. \] Writing \(B=\int\xi\cdot\sum_i\lambda_i(x-T_i(x))\,\mathrm{d}\bar\mu\), expansion and minimality give \[ 0\le\mathcal V(S_{\varepsilon\#}\bar\mu)-\mathcal V(\bar\mu) \le\varepsilon B+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}\int|\xi|^2\,\mathrm{d}\bar\mu. \] Put \(Q=\int|\xi|^2\,\mathrm{d}\bar\mu\). For \(\varepsilon>0\), \(B\ge-\varepsilon Q/2\); for \(\varepsilon<0\), \(B\le-\varepsilon Q/2\). Passing to zero from the respective sides gives \(B\ge0\) and \(B\le0\), namely \[ \int \xi(x)\cdot\sum_i\lambda_i(x-T_i(x))\,\mathrm{d}\bar\mu(x)=0 \] for every \(\xi\). Let \(G(x)=\sum_i\lambda_i(x-T_i(x))\in L^2(\bar\mu)\). Smooth compactly supported vector fields are dense in \(L^2(\bar\mu;\mathbb R^d)\). Choose \(\xi_n\to G\) in that space; then \[ \int|G|^2\,\mathrm{d}\bar\mu =\lim_n\int\xi_n\cdot G\,\mathrm{d}\bar\mu=0, \] where convergence follows from Cauchy–Schwarz. Thus \(G=0\) almost everywhere.

Multi-marginal formulation

Theorem 13.4 (Multi-marginal representation). Let \[ \bar x_\lambda=\sum_i\lambda_ix_i. \] Then \[ \inf_{\nu}\sum_i\lambda_iW_2^2(\nu,\mu_i) = \inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_N)} \int\sum_i\lambda_i|x_i-\bar x_\lambda|^2\,\mathrm{d}\gamma. \] If \(\gamma^\star\) solves the right side, then \((\bar x_\lambda)_\#\gamma^\star\) is a barycenter.

Proof. Write \(B(x_1,\ldots,x_N)=\sum_i\lambda_ix_i\). Expanding around \(B\) and using \(\sum_i\lambda_i(x_i-B)=0\) gives \[ \begin{aligned} \sum_i\lambda_i|x_i-z|^2 &=\sum_i\lambda_i|x_i-B|^2+2(B-z)\cdot\sum_i\lambda_i(x_i-B)+\sum_i\lambda_i|B-z|^2\\ &=\sum_i\lambda_i|x_i-B|^2+|B-z|^2. \end{aligned} \] Let \(I\) be the infimum on the left in the theorem, and \(J\) the multi-marginal infimum. Given \(\nu\), disintegrate optimal couplings as \(\pi_i(\mathrm{d}z,\mathrm{d}x_i)=\nu(\mathrm{d}z)K_i(z,\mathrm{d}x_i)\), and glue them by \[ P(\mathrm{d}z,\mathrm{d}x_1\cdots\mathrm{d}x_N) =\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\prod_iK_i(z,\mathrm{d}x_i). \] The input marginal \(\gamma=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)_\#P\) is feasible, and \[ \sum_i\lambda_iW_2^2(\nu,\mu_i) =\int\sum_i\lambda_i|x_i-z|^2\,\mathrm{d}P \ge\int\sum_i\lambda_i|x_i-B|^2\,\mathrm{d}\gamma\ge J. \] Taking the infimum in \(\nu\) gives \(I\ge J\).

Conversely, given \(\gamma\in\Pi(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_N)\), set \(\nu=B_\#\gamma\). Jensen gives \(\int|B|^2\,\mathrm{d}\gamma\le\sum_i\lambda_i\int|x_i|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_i<\infty\), so \(\nu\in\mathcal P_2\). The laws \((B,x_i)_\#\gamma\) are feasible couplings; therefore \[ I\le\sum_i\lambda_iW_2^2(\nu,\mu_i) \le\int\sum_i\lambda_i|B-x_i|^2\,\mathrm{d}\gamma. \] Taking the infimum gives \(I\le J\). The input coupling set is tight and weakly closed, hence compact; the nonnegative continuous cost is lower semicontinuous in its integral and finite on the product coupling. The direct method gives a minimizer \(\gamma_*\). The last inequalities are then equalities, making \(B_\#\gamma_*\) a barycenter.

If the starting \(\nu\) was a barycenter, its glued plan satisfies \[ I=\int\sum_i\lambda_i|x_i-B|^2\,\mathrm{d}\gamma+\int|B-z|^2\,\mathrm{d}P \ge J+\int|B-z|^2\,\mathrm{d}P=I+\int|B-z|^2\,\mathrm{d}P. \] Hence \(B=z\) almost surely and \(\gamma\) is optimal. Thus every barycenter has the asserted representation.

One-dimensional barycenters

Proposition 13.5 (Quantile formula). On \(\mathbb R\), the unique \(W_2\)-barycenter satisfies \[ F_{\bar\mu}^{-1}(u)=\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_iF_{\mu_i}^{-1}(u) \quad\text{for almost every }u\in(0,1). \]

Proof. Put \(Q_i=F_{\mu_i}^{-1}\), \(\bar Q=\sum_i\lambda_iQ_i\). For every real \(q\), expansion gives \[ \sum_i\lambda_i|q-Q_i(u)|^2 =|q-\bar Q(u)|^2+\sum_i\lambda_i|\bar Q(u)-Q_i(u)|^2. \] The cross term is zero because \(\sum_i\lambda_i(\bar Q-Q_i)=0\). Since \(\bar Q\) is nondecreasing and \(\|\bar Q\|_2\le\sum_i\lambda_i\|Q_i\|_2<\infty\), it is almost everywhere the quantile of \(\bar\mu=\bar Q_\#\lambda\in\mathcal P_2\). For any \(\nu\in\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R)\), integrating the identity gives \[ \mathcal V(\nu)=\tfrac12\|F_\nu^{-1}-\bar Q\|_{L^2(0,1)}^2+\mathcal V(\bar\mu). \] Thus \(\bar\mu\) minimizes. Equality forces \(F_\nu^{-1}=\bar Q\) almost everywhere, hence \(\nu=\bar\mu\), proving uniqueness.

Gaussian barycenters

Theorem 13.6 (Gaussian barycenter (external result)). The barycenter of nondegenerate Gaussian laws \(N(m_i,\Sigma_i)\) is \(N(\bar m,\bar\Sigma)\), where \[ \bar m=\sum_i\lambda_im_i \] and \(\bar\Sigma\) is the unique positive-definite solution of \[ \bar\Sigma = \sum_i\lambda_i (\bar\Sigma^{1/2}\Sigma_i\bar\Sigma^{1/2})^{1/2}. \]

The equation is the matrix form of \(\sum_i\lambda_iT_i(x)=x\). If the covariance matrices commute, it simplifies to \[ \bar\Sigma^{1/2}=\sum_i\lambda_i\Sigma_i^{1/2}. \]

Proof in the commuting case. Choose a common orthonormal basis diagonalizing all covariances, with diagonal entries \(s_{i\ell}^2>0\). For any joint law of the inputs, the variance identity gives \[ \mathbb E\sum_i\lambda_i|X_i-\bar X|^2 =\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbb E|X_i|^2-\mathbb E|\bar X|^2, \qquad\bar X=\sum_i\lambda_iX_i. \] The first term is fixed. With \(\bar m_\ell=\sum_i\lambda_im_{i\ell}\), \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb E|\bar X|^2 &=|\bar m|^2+\sum_{\ell=1}^d\sum_{i,j}\lambda_i\lambda_j \operatorname{Cov}(X_{i\ell},X_{j\ell})\\ &\le|\bar m|^2+\sum_{\ell=1}^d\sum_{i,j}\lambda_i\lambda_js_{i\ell}s_{j\ell} =|\bar m|^2+\sum_{\ell=1}^d\left(\sum_i\lambda_is_{i\ell}\right)^2. \end{aligned} \] The inequality is Cauchy–Schwarz for each pair of centered coordinates. Equality holds simultaneously for \[ X_{i\ell}=m_{i\ell}+s_{i\ell}Z_\ell,\qquad (Z_1,\ldots,Z_d)\sim N(0,I_d). \] These have the prescribed Gaussian marginals. Their joint law therefore minimizes the multi-marginal objective, and \[ \bar X_\ell=\bar m_\ell+\left(\sum_i\lambda_is_{i\ell}\right)Z_\ell. \] The representation theorem proves that this Gaussian law is a barycenter among all probability laws. Uniqueness follows from the absolutely continuous input. The noncommuting matrix theorem stated above is the cited external result.

Stability of barycenters

Theorem 13.7 (Stability). If \(\mu_{i,n}\to\mu_i\) in \(W_2\) with fixed positive weights, barycenters \(\bar\mu_n\) are relatively compact in \(W_2\), and every limit is a barycenter of the limiting inputs. A unique limiting barycenter gives convergence of the whole sequence.

Proof. Represent each barycenter as \(\bar\mu_n=\mathcal L(Z_n)\), \(Z_n=\sum_i\lambda_iX_{i,n}\), with \(X_{i,n}\sim\mu_{i,n}\) under an optimal input coupling. Put \(S_n=\sum_i\lambda_i|X_{i,n}|^2\); Jensen gives \(|Z_n|^2\le S_n\). Input \(W_2\)-convergence implies \[ M:=\sup_n\mathbb ES_n<\infty,\qquad \tau(L):=\sum_i\lambda_i\sup_n\mathbb E[|X_{i,n}|^2\mathbf1_{\{|X_{i,n}|^2>L\}}]\to0. \] For any event \(E\), \[ \mathbb E[S_n\mathbf1_E]\le L\mathbb P(E)+\tau(L). \] Taking \(E=\{|Z_n|^2>R\}\) and using \(\mathbb P(E)\le M/R\) gives \[ \sup_n\mathbb E[|Z_n|^2\mathbf1_{\{|Z_n|^2>R\}}]\le LM/R+\tau(L). \] Let \(R\to\infty\), then \(L\to\infty\). Thus the barycenters have uniformly integrable second moments; Markov also gives tightness on \(\mathbb R^d\). The Wasserstein compactness criterion supplies a subsequence \(\bar\mu_{n_k}\to\bar\mu\) in \(W_2\).

For any \(\nu_n\to\nu\) in \(W_2\), triangle inequality gives \[ |W_2(\nu_n,\mu_{i,n})-W_2(\nu,\mu_i)| \le W_2(\nu_n,\nu)+W_2(\mu_{i,n},\mu_i)\to0. \] Squaring convergent finite numbers and summing gives \(\mathcal V_n(\nu_n)\to\mathcal V(\nu)\). Hence for every fixed competitor \(\eta\), \[ \mathcal V(\bar\mu)=\lim_k\mathcal V_{n_k}(\bar\mu_{n_k}) \le\lim_k\mathcal V_{n_k}(\eta)=\mathcal V(\eta). \] Every limit is a barycenter. If the limiting barycenter is unique and the full sequence failed to converge to it, a subsequence at distance at least \(\varepsilon>0\) would have a convergent further subsequence, contradicting this identification.

Population Fréchet means

If \(\mathsf Q\) is a probability distribution on \(\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\), a population barycenter minimizes \[ \nu\longmapsto\frac12\int W_2^2(\nu,\mu)\,\mathrm{d}\mathsf Q(\mu). \] The empirical barycenter replaces \(\mathsf Q\) by sampled distributions. Existence, uniqueness, consistency, and limit distributions are therefore Fréchet-mean questions in a nonlinear metric space; Inference for transport costs, maps, and barycenters returns to their statistical theory.

Exercises

averaging distributions

  1. Exercise 13.1. Find the equal-weight \(W_2\)-barycenter of \(\delta_{-1}\) and \(\delta_1\). Compare it with their mixture.

    Hint

    Minimize the average squared distance to the two atoms over a candidate location.

    Solution

    The barycenter is \(\delta_0\), since \(\tfrac12(x+1)^2+\tfrac12(x-1)^2=x^2+1\). The mixture keeps its mass at the endpoints.

  2. Exercise 13.2. Find the equal-weight barycenter of \(N(0,1)\) and \(N(2,9)\) on the line.

    Hint

    Average the one-dimensional quantile functions, keeping the mean and standard deviation separate.

    Solution

    Means and standard deviations average: the answer is \(N(1,4)\). Averaging the variances would give the wrong answer.
