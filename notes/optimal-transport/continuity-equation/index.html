Learning goal. Relate particle motion, weak conservation of mass, and the minimal velocity representing a measure curve.

A curve of measures has no pointwise derivative in the usual vector-space sense. Its motion in the Wasserstein metric is described by a velocity field transporting mass through the continuity equation. Metric absolute continuity and this Eulerian description are equivalent.

The distributional continuity equation

Definition 14.1 (The distributional continuity equation). A narrowly continuous curve \(t\mapsto\mu_t\in\mathcal P(\mathbb R^d)\) and a Borel velocity field \(v_t(x)\) with \(\int_0^T\int|v_t|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty\) solve \[ \partial_t\mu_t+\nabla\cdot(v_t\mu_t)=0 \] on \((0,T)\) if, for every \(\zeta\in C_c^\infty((0,T)\times\mathbb R^d)\), \[ \int_0^T\int_{\mathbb R^d} \{\partial_t\zeta(t,x)+\nabla\zeta(t,x)\cdot v_t(x)\} \,\mathrm{d}\mu_t(x)\,\mathrm{d}t=0. \]

Equivalently, for every \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^d)\), the map \(t\mapsto\int\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t\) is absolutely continuous and \[ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t =\int\nabla\phi(x)\cdot v_t(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t(x) \] for almost every \(t\).

Flows of particles

Proposition 14.2 (Flows of particles). Suppose \(v\) is continuous in time, smooth in space, and globally Lipschitz in space with an integrable Lipschitz bound. Assume also \(\|v_t\|_\infty\le a(t)\) for some \(a\in L^1(0,T)\). If \(X_t(x)\) solves \[ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}X_t(x)=v_t(X_t(x)),\qquad X_0(x)=x. \] Then \(\mu_t=X_{t\#}\mu_0\) solves the continuity equation.

Proof. Fix \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^d)\). The chain rule along each particle gives \[ \phi(X_t(x))-\phi(X_s(x)) =\int_s^t\nabla\phi(X_r(x))\cdot v_r(X_r(x))\,\mathrm{d}r. \] The integrand satisfies \[ |\nabla\phi(X_r(x))\cdot v_r(X_r(x))| \le\|\nabla\phi\|_\infty a(r), \qquad\int_s^t\int\|\nabla\phi\|_\infty a(r)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0(x)\,\mathrm{d}r<\infty. \] Thus Fubini and the pushforward identity yield \[ \begin{aligned} \int\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t-\int\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_s &=\int\int_s^t\nabla\phi(X_r(x))\cdot v_r(X_r(x))\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0(x)\\ &=\int_s^t\int\nabla\phi(X_r(x))\cdot v_r(X_r(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0(x)\,\mathrm{d}r\\ &=\int_s^t\int\nabla\phi(y)\cdot v_r(y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_r(y)\,\mathrm{d}r. \end{aligned} \] The right integrand lies in \(L^1(s,t)\), so the left scalar function is absolutely continuous, with derivative \(\int\nabla\phi\cdot v_t\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t\) almost everywhere. For \(\eta\in C_c^\infty(0,T)\), integration by parts gives \[ \int_0^T\int[\eta'(t)\phi(x)+\eta(t)\nabla\phi(x)\cdot v_t(x)]\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t(x)\,\mathrm{d}t=0. \] Finite sums of these separated tests are dense in the compactly supported \(C^1\) test space. The integrable bound on \(v\) permits passage to the limit, giving the distributional continuity equation.

Metric absolute continuity

Definition 14.3 (Metric absolute continuity). A curve \(\mu:[0,T]\to(\mathcal P_p(\mathbb R^d),W_p)\) belongs to \(AC^p\) if there exists \(m\in L^p(0,T)\) such that \[ W_p(\mu_s,\mu_t)\le\int_s^t m(r)\,\mathrm{d}r \] whenever \(s\le t\). Its metric derivative is \[ |\dot\mu_t|=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{W_p(\mu_{t+h},\mu_t)}{|h|}, \] which exists for almost every \(t\), belongs to \(L^p\), and is the smallest admissible \(m\).

Velocity characterization

Theorem 14.4 (Eulerian characterization in the quadratic case). Let \(t\mapsto\mu_t\in\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\) be narrowly continuous on \([0,T]\). Then \(\mu\in AC^2([0,T];\mathcal P_2)\) if and only if there is a Borel field \(v\) solving the continuity equation with \[ \int_0^T\int|v_t(x)|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t(x)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty. \] Every such field satisfies \(|\dot\mu_t|\le\|v_t\|_{L^2(\mu_t)}\) almost everywhere. There is a unique field in \(T_{\mu_t}\mathcal P_2=\overline{\{\nabla\phi:\phi\in C_c^\infty\}}^{L^2(\mu_t)}\) with equality; it minimizes the norm among all representing fields.

Proof. 1. Construct a field from the curve. Set \(m(t)=|\dot\mu_t|\in L^2(0,T)\). For \(\phi\in C_c^\infty\), \(f_\phi(t)=\int\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t\) is absolutely continuous, since \[ |f_\phi(t)-f_\phi(s)|\le\operatorname{Lip}(\phi)W_2(\mu_t,\mu_s). \] At times where \(f_\phi'\) and the metric derivative exist, use an optimal coupling \(\pi_{t,h}\) of \(\mu_t,\mu_{t+h}\). Taylor's theorem gives \[ \phi(y)-\phi(x)=\nabla\phi(x)\cdot(y-x)+R_\phi(x,y), \qquad |R_\phi(x,y)|\le\tfrac12\|D^2\phi\|_\infty|y-x|^2. \] Integrating against \(\pi_{t,h}\), its marginals give the increment of \(f_\phi\), while \[ \left|\int\nabla\phi(x)\cdot(y-x)\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{t,h}\right| \le\left(\int|\nabla\phi|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t\right)^{1/2} \left(\int|y-x|^2\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{t,h}\right)^{1/2}. \] Dividing by \(|h|\) yields \[ \left|\frac{f_\phi(t+h)-f_\phi(t)}h\right| \le\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^2(\mu_t)}\frac{W_2(\mu_t,\mu_{t+h})}{|h|} +\frac{\|D^2\phi\|_\infty}{2|h|}W_2(\mu_t,\mu_{t+h})^2. \] The remainder tends to zero because \[ \frac{W_2(\mu_t,\mu_{t+h})^2}{|h|} =|h|\left(\frac{W_2(\mu_t,\mu_{t+h})}{|h|}\right)^2\longrightarrow0. \] Thus \[ |f_\phi'(t)|\le m(t)\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^2(\mu_t)}. \tag{1} \] Choose a countable rational vector space of tests dense in the \(C^2\)-norm on each compact support. Remove the union of their exceptional null sets. Inequality (1) then holds simultaneously for every finite real linear combination, by continuity in the coefficients.

Let \(q(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}x)=\mathrm{d}t\,\mu_t(\mathrm{d}x)\). On fields \(\xi(t,x)=\sum_{i=1}^N\eta_i(t)\nabla\phi_i(x)\), with bounded measurable \(\eta_i\), define \[ L(\xi)=\int_0^T\sum_i\eta_i(t)f_{\phi_i}'(t)\,\mathrm{d}t. \] Pointwise application of (1) to \(\sum_i\eta_i(t)\phi_i\) gives \[ |L(\xi)|\le\int_0^T m(t)\|\xi_t\|_{L^2(\mu_t)}\,\mathrm{d}t \le\|m\|_{L^2(0,T)}\|\xi\|_{L^2(q)}. \tag{2} \] In particular, \(L\) is independent of how \(\xi\) is represented. Let \(H\) be the closure of these fields in \(L^2(q;\mathbb R^d)\). Hilbert-space Riesz representation provides \(v\in H\) with \(L(\xi)=\int v\cdot\xi\,\mathrm{d}q\). An \(L^2(q)\) class has a Borel representative, which we use for \(v\).

The space \(H\) is stable under multiplication by bounded measurable functions of time. Restricting (2) to a measurable set \(A\subset(0,T)\) and approximating \(\mathbf1_Av\) by fields of this space gives \[ \|\mathbf1_Av\|_{L^2(q)}^2 =L(\mathbf1_Av) \le\|\mathbf1_Am\|_{L^2(0,T)}\|\mathbf1_Av\|_{L^2(q)}. \] Therefore \(\int_A\|v_t\|_{L^2(\mu_t)}^2\,\mathrm{d}t\le\int_A m(t)^2\,\mathrm{d}t\) for every \(A\), so \(\|v_t\|_{L^2(\mu_t)}\le m(t)\) almost everywhere. For \(\xi=\eta(t)\nabla\phi\), \(\eta\in C_c^\infty(0,T)\), the Riesz identity and time integration by parts give \[ \int_0^T\int\eta(t)\nabla\phi(x)\cdot v_t(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t(x)\,\mathrm{d}t =\int_0^T\eta(t)f_\phi'(t)\,\mathrm{d}t =-\int_0^T\eta'(t)f_\phi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t. \] Equivalently, \(\int(\partial_t(\eta\phi)+\nabla_x(\eta\phi)\cdot v)\,\mathrm{d}q=0\), the weak continuity equation for separated tests. Density extends the identity to all compactly supported smooth tests; finite sums of separated time and space tests are dense in the corresponding \(C^1\)-norm.

2. Recover metric speed from a field. Apply the superposition theorem stated below. If \(e_t(\gamma)=\gamma(t)\), then \((e_s,e_t)_\#\boldsymbol\eta\) is a coupling. Minkowski's integral inequality gives \[ \begin{aligned} W_2(\mu_s,\mu_t) &\le\left\|\int_s^t\dot\gamma_r\,\mathrm{d}r\right\|_{L^2(\boldsymbol\eta)} \le\int_s^t\|\dot\gamma_r\|_{L^2(\boldsymbol\eta)}\,\mathrm{d}r\\ &=\int_s^t\|v_r\|_{L^2(\mu_r)}\,\mathrm{d}r. \end{aligned} \] Writing \(g(r)=\|v_r\|_{L^2(\mu_r)}\in L^2(0,T)\), the bound proves \(\mu\in AC^2\). At Lebesgue points of \(g\) where the metric derivative exists, \[ |\dot\mu_t|=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{W_2(\mu_{t+h},\mu_t)}{|h|} \le\lim_{h\to0}\frac1{|h|}\int_{\min(t,t+h)}^{\max(t,t+h)}g(r)\,\mathrm{d}r=g(t). \] Combined with Step 1 this is equality for the constructed field.

3. Minimality and uniqueness. An approximating sequence in \(H\) has a subsequence converging in \(L^2(\mu_t)\) for almost every \(t\): choose \(\xi_n\to v\) with \(\sum_n\|\xi_n-v\|_{L^2(q)}^2<\infty\). Tonelli gives \[ \int_0^T\sum_n\|\xi_n(t,\cdot)-v_t\|_{L^2(\mu_t)}^2\,\mathrm{d}t =\sum_n\|\xi_n-v\|_{L^2(q)}^2<\infty, \] so the summands tend to zero for almost every \(t\). Thus \(v_t\in T_{\mu_t}\mathcal P_2\). If \(w\) is any other square-integrable representing field, testing the difference equation with \(\eta(t)\phi(x)\) shows \(w_t-v_t\) is orthogonal to every test gradient, hence to the tangent space, for almost every \(t\). More explicitly, the difference equation gives \[ \int_0^T\eta(t)\left[\int\nabla\phi\cdot(w_t-v_t)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t\right]\,\mathrm{d}t=0 \quad(\forall\eta\in C_c^\infty(0,T)). \] The bracket is integrable, hence zero almost everywhere. Use a countable dense test family to obtain one common full-measure set, and then extend by \(L^2(\mu_t)\)-continuity to the tangent space. Therefore \(\langle v_t,w_t-v_t\rangle_{L^2(\mu_t)}=0\), and expansion of the square gives \[ \|w_t\|_{L^2(\mu_t)}^2=\|v_t\|_{L^2(\mu_t)}^2+\|w_t-v_t\|_{L^2(\mu_t)}^2. \] This proves both assertions.

The general \(1<p<\infty\) characterization replaces squared norms by \(p\)-th powers. We use the proved quadratic version throughout the dynamic chapters; the general result is Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré, Theorem 8.3.1.

Remark 14.5 (Where a discrete velocity lives). A displacement \((y-x)/h\) is defined on \(\pi_{t,h}\), not on \(\mu_t\). Disintegrating \(\pi_{t,h}=\mu_t(\mathrm{d}x)\pi_{t,h,x}(\mathrm{d}y)\) gives the actual field \[ b_{t,h}(x)=\int\frac{y-x}{h}\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{t,h,x}(y),\qquad \int|b_{t,h}|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t\le\frac{W_2(\mu_t,\mu_{t+h})^2}{h^2}, \] by conditional Jensen. Taylor's remainder is bounded by \(\|D^2\phi\|_\infty W_2^2/(2|h|)\), exactly as above. The Hilbert-space construction avoids assuming that weak limits on changing marginal spaces automatically define a measurable field.

The superposition principle

Theorem 14.6 (Superposition principle (external theorem)). If \((\mu_t,v_t)\) solves the continuity equation and \(\int_0^T\int|v_t|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty\), then there exists a probability measure \(\boldsymbol\eta\) on \(AC([0,T];\mathbb R^d)\) such that \[ (e_t)_\#\boldsymbol\eta=\mu_t,\qquad \dot\gamma_t=v_t(\gamma_t) \] for \(\boldsymbol\eta\)-almost every path and almost every time.

This is the external input used in Step 2; see Ambrosio–Gigli–Savaré, Theorem 8.2.1. It is a measure-valued extension of the method of characteristics. Even when the vector field has no unique classical flow, the solution is still a probability distribution over integral curves.

Minimal velocities and tangent directions

Velocity fields are not unique. A field \(w_t\) satisfying \[ \nabla\cdot(w_t\mu_t)=0 \] may be added without changing the curve. For \(p=2\), the minimal velocity is the orthogonal projection onto \[ T_{\mu_t}\mathcal P_2 =\overline{\{\nabla\phi:\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^d)\}}^{L^2(\mu_t)}. \] Divergence-free components are orthogonal to all gradients and contribute kinetic energy without moving the measure at first order.

Translation, dilation, and displacement flows

Example 14.7 (Translation). If \(\mu_t=(x\mapsto x+ta)_\#\mu_0\), then \(v_t\equiv a\), the continuity equation holds, and \(|\dot\mu_t|=|a|\).

Example 14.8 (Dilation). If \(\mu_t=(x\mapsto e^t x)_\#\mu_0\), then \(v_t(x)=x\). Hence \[ |\dot\mu_t|^2=\int|x|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_t(x) \] when this field is the minimal \(L^2\) representative.

Example 14.9 (A displacement geodesic). If \(\mu_t=((1-t)\operatorname{id}+tT)_\#\mu_0\), then along the particle starting at \(x\), velocity is \(T(x)-x\). In Eulerian coordinates, \[ v_t=(T-\operatorname{id})\circ T_t^{-1} \] wherever \(T_t\) is invertible. Its kinetic norm is constant and equals \(W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1)\).

Exercises

velocities and conservation

  1. Exercise 14.1. If \(\mu_t=(x\mapsto(1+t)x)_\#\mu_0\), find its Eulerian velocity.

    Hint

    Differentiate the moving particle position, then express its initial position in terms of its current position.

    Solution

    A particle at \(z=(1+t)x\) has velocity \(x=z/(1+t)\). Hence \(v_t(z)=z/(1+t)\).

  2. Exercise 14.2. Why does adding \(w\) with \(\nabla\cdot(\mu w)=0\) leave the instantaneous curve unchanged?

    Hint

    Insert the added field into the weak continuity equation and use its zero weighted divergence.

    Solution

    Test against a smooth compactly supported function: \(\int\nabla\phi\cdot w\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0\). Such a component is invisible to the weak equation, but can increase kinetic energy.
