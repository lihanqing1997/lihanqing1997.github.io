Learning goal. Derive the PDE generated by internal, potential, and interaction energies and check dissipation signs.

Many nonlinear diffusion equations are steepest-descent equations in Wasserstein space. The same computation that identifies their velocity also reveals the correct Lyapunov functional, stationary measures, and dissipation rate.

The general free-energy equation

Proposition 18.1 (First variation). For \[ \mathcal F(\rho) =\int U(\rho(x))\,\mathrm{d}x +\int V(x)\rho(x)\,\mathrm{d}x +\frac12\iint W(x-y)\rho(x)\rho(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y, \] with \(W\) even, \[ \frac{\delta\mathcal F}{\delta\rho} =U'(\rho)+V+W*\rho \] up to an irrelevant additive constant.

Proof in a smooth setting. For a direct justification, work first on a flat torus with smooth \(\rho>0\), smooth \(U,V,W\), and even \(W\). A smooth zero-mass perturbation \(\sigma\) keeps \(\rho+h\sigma>0\) for small \(|h|\). For \(|h|\le h_0\) small enough, \(\rho+h\sigma\) takes values in a compact subinterval \([a,b]\subset(0,\infty)\). The mean-value theorem gives \[ \left|\frac{U(\rho+h\sigma)-U(\rho)}h\right|\le\sup_{r\in[a,b]}|U'(r)|\,|\sigma|, \] an integrable bound on the compact torus. Dominated convergence therefore gives the internal-energy derivative. The interaction expansion is exact: \[ (\rho(x)+h\sigma(x))(\rho(y)+h\sigma(y)) =\rho(x)\rho(y)+h[\sigma(x)\rho(y)+\rho(x)\sigma(y)]+h^2\sigma(x)\sigma(y). \] Its quadratic remainder divided by \(h\) tends to zero in the integral because \(W,\sigma\) are bounded. The interaction term has derivative \[ \frac12\iint W(x-y)[\sigma(x)\rho(y)+\rho(x)\sigma(y)]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y. \] Fubini and evenness give \[ \begin{aligned} \iint W(x-y)\rho(x)\sigma(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y &=\iint W(y-x)\rho(y)\sigma(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\ &=\int\sigma(x)\left[\int W(x-y)\rho(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right]\,\mathrm{d}x =\int(W*\rho)\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x. \end{aligned} \] The first term has this same value, cancelling the factor \(1/2\). The internal and potential derivatives are \(\int U'(\rho)\sigma\) and \(\int V\sigma\). Therefore \[ D\mathcal F(\rho)[\sigma]=\int[U'(\rho)+V+W*\rho]\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x. \] Since \(\int\sigma=0\), adding a constant to the bracket changes no variation. The same calculation on \(\mathbb R^d\) is valid whenever its difference quotients have integrable dominating functions; this must be checked for the chosen growth and tails.

Corollary 18.2 (Gradient-flow equation). The formal \(W_2\)-gradient flow is \[ \boxed{ \partial_t\rho =\nabla\cdot\left[ \rho\nabla\{U'(\rho)+V+W*\rho\} \right]. } \]

Energy dissipation

Proposition 18.3 (Dissipation identity). On the torus in the preceding smooth setting, or on \(\mathbb R^d\) when differentiation under the energy integral is valid and the boundary terms specified below vanish, \[ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal F(\rho_t) =-\int\rho_t \left|\nabla\!\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal F}{\delta\rho_t}\right)\right|^2\,\mathrm{d}x \le0. \]

Proof. Write \(F_t=U'(\rho_t)+V+W*\rho_t\). The chain rule just proved and \(\partial_t\rho=\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla F)\) give \[ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal F(\rho_t)=\int F_t\nabla\cdot(\rho_t\nabla F_t)\,\mathrm{d}x =-\int\rho_t|\nabla F_t|^2\,\mathrm{d}x \] on the torus, without boundary terms. For \(\mathbb R^d\), take cutoffs \(\chi_R\) equal to one on \(B_R\), supported in \(B_{2R}\), with \(|\nabla\chi_R|\le C/R\). The exact cutoff identity is \[ \int\chi_RF_t\nabla\cdot(\rho_t\nabla F_t)\,\mathrm{d}x =-\int\chi_R\rho_t|\nabla F_t|^2\,\mathrm{d}x -\int F_t\rho_t\nabla F_t\cdot\nabla\chi_R\,\mathrm{d}x. \] The final term is the error \[ -\int F_t\rho_t\nabla F_t\cdot\nabla\chi_R\,\mathrm{d}x, \qquad |\text{error}|\le\frac C R\int_{R<|x|<2R}|F_t|\rho_t|\nabla F_t|\,\mathrm{d}x. \] If this bound tends to zero (also integrably in time for a time-integrated identity), and both \(\rho|\nabla F|^2\) and \(F\partial_t\rho\) are integrable, dominated convergence gives \[ \lim_R\int\chi_R\rho|\nabla F|^2=\int\rho|\nabla F|^2, \qquad\lim_R\int\chi_RF\partial_t\rho=\int F\partial_t\rho. \] Together with the vanishing error, this proves the same identity. These are explicit sufficient decay conditions. When \(-\nabla F\) belongs to the tangent space, the velocity theorem identifies its norm with metric speed; identifying the metric slope additionally uses the energy subgradient theorem, not integration by parts alone.

The heat equation

Theorem 18.4 (Heat as entropy flow). For the Boltzmann entropy \[ \operatorname{Ent}(\rho)=\int\rho\log\rho\,\mathrm{d}x, \] the Wasserstein gradient flow is \[ \partial_t\rho=\Delta\rho. \]

Proof of the smooth equation. In the smooth positive-density setting above, \(U(r)=r\log r\) satisfies \(U'(r)=1+\log r\). Componentwise, \[ \partial_i(1+\log\rho)=\frac{\partial_i\rho}{\rho},\qquad \rho\nabla(1+\log\rho)=\nabla\rho. \] The velocity and continuity equation are therefore \[ v=-\nabla\log\rho,\qquad \partial_t\rho=-\nabla\cdot(\rho v) =\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\log\rho) =\nabla\cdot(\nabla\rho)=\Delta\rho. \] Under the preceding integrability and boundary assumptions, the dissipation identity becomes \[ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\operatorname{Ent}(\rho_t) =-\int\rho_t\left|\frac{\nabla\rho_t}{\rho_t}\right|^2\,\mathrm{d}x =-\int\frac{|\nabla\rho_t|^2}{\rho_t}\,\mathrm{d}x. \] The weak gradient-flow identification uses the evolution theory cited in the preceding chapter; this calculation explicitly identifies its smooth PDE and dissipation.

Fokker--Planck and Langevin dynamics

Theorem 18.5 (Fokker--Planck flow). For \[ \mathcal F(\rho)=\int\rho\log\rho\,\mathrm{d}x+\int V\rho\,\mathrm{d}x, \] the gradient flow is \[ \partial_t\rho=\Delta\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla V). \] If \(Z=\int e^{-V}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty\), its equilibrium is \(\gamma=Z^{-1}e^{-V}\,\mathrm{d}x\), and \[ \mathcal F(\rho)-\mathcal F(\gamma) =\operatorname{KL}(\rho\,\|\,\gamma). \]

This is the forward equation of the Langevin diffusion \[ \mathrm{d}X_t=-\nabla V(X_t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt2\,\mathrm{d}B_t. \] When \(D^2V\ge\lambda I\), displacement \(\lambda\)-convexity gives \(W_2\)-contraction at rate \(e^{-\lambda t}\).

Example 18.6 (Ornstein--Uhlenbeck flow). For \(V(x)=|x|^2/2\), \[ \mathrm{d}X_t=-X_t\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt2\,\mathrm{d}B_t, \] and \[ X_t=e^{-t}X_0+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}\,Z,\qquad Z\sim N(0,I). \] The invariant law is \(N(0,I)\), and the semigroup contracts \(W_2\) by \(e^{-t}\).

Porous-medium and fast-diffusion equations

Theorem 18.7 (Power internal energy). For \[ \mathcal U_m(\rho)=\frac{1}{m-1}\int\rho^m\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad m>0,\quad m\ne1, \] the Wasserstein gradient flow is \[ \partial_t\rho=\Delta(\rho^m). \]

Proof of the smooth equation. For \(m>0\), \(m\ne1\), differentiation of \(U_m(r)=r^m/(m-1)\) gives \[ U_m'(\rho)=\frac m{m-1}\rho^{m-1}, \qquad \nabla U_m'(\rho)=m\rho^{m-2}\nabla\rho. \] For a positive smooth density, \[ \rho\nabla U_m'(\rho)=m\rho^{m-1}\nabla\rho=\nabla(\rho^m). \] Substituting this flux into the continuity equation with \(v=-\nabla U_m'(\rho)\) gives \[ \partial_t\rho=\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla U_m'(\rho)) =\nabla\cdot\nabla(\rho^m)=\Delta(\rho^m). \] For \(m>1\) this is porous-medium diffusion; for \(0<m<1\) it is fast diffusion. Construction of global probability solutions requires the stated energy framework and the dimension-dependent integrability conditions; the algebraic identification alone does not establish that existence theorem.

Aggregation and interaction

Theorem 18.8 (Aggregation equation). For the interaction energy \[ \mathcal W(\rho)=\frac12\iint W(x-y)\rho(x)\rho(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y, \] the gradient flow is \[ \partial_t\rho=\nabla\cdot\{\rho\nabla(W*\rho)\}. \]

Attractive kernels may concentrate mass, while repulsive kernels spread it. Adding entropy or power internal energy gives the aggregation--diffusion equation \[ \partial_t\rho =\Delta(\rho^m)+\nabla\cdot\{\rho\nabla(V+W*\rho)\}. \] The competition between diffusion and attraction determines whether solutions disperse, approach equilibrium, or concentrate.

Weak solutions from JKO

Theorem 18.9 (Identification from a convergent JKO scheme). Suppose smooth JKO steps satisfy the first-order condition of the preceding chapter and, on every bounded time interval, \(\sum_kW_2^2(\mu^k,\mu^{k-1})/\tau\le A<\infty\) uniformly as \(\tau\downarrow0\). Suppose their piecewise constant interpolants satisfy \[ \mathrm{d}t\,\mu_\tau(t)\rightharpoonup\rho(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x, \qquad v_\tau\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mu_\tau(t)\rightharpoonup-\rho\nabla\{U'(\rho)+V+W*\rho\}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x \] against continuous compactly supported scalar and vector tests, respectively, with common initial law \(\rho_0\,\mathrm{d}x\). Then \(\rho\) solves the displayed diffusion equation weakly with that initial law. Existence of these convergences is supplied, for the uniformly convex Fokker–Planck model, by the concrete evolution theorem in the preceding chapter; other energies require their own compactness and flux-identification estimates.

Proof of the conditional limit statement. Let \(t_k=k\tau\), \(\mu^k=\rho^k\,\mathrm{d}x\), and let \(T_k\) optimally transport \(\mu^k\) to \(\mu^{k-1}\). The first-order JKO equation gives \(v^k=(\operatorname{id}-T_k)/\tau\). Thus \[ \tau\int|v^k|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu^k=\frac{W_2^2(\mu^k,\mu^{k-1})}{\tau}, \qquad \sum_k\tau\int|v^k|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu^k\le A. \] For \(\phi\in C_c^\infty([0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^d)\), Taylor expansion in space gives \[ \begin{aligned} &\int\phi(t_{k-1},x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^k(x)-\int\phi(t_{k-1},y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{k-1}(y)\\ &\quad=\tau\int\nabla\phi(t_{k-1},x)\cdot v^k(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^k(x)+r_k, \qquad |r_k|\le\tfrac12\|D_x^2\phi\|_\infty W_2^2(\mu^k,\mu^{k-1}). \end{aligned} \] Also \(\phi(t_k,x)-\phi(t_{k-1},x)=\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_k}\partial_t\phi(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}t\). Add this identity and sum over all steps meeting the time support of \(\phi\). The left side telescopes to \(-\int\phi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^0\). With the piecewise constant \(\mu_\tau(t)=\mu^k\), \(v_\tau(t)=v^k\) on \((t_{k-1},t_k]\), we obtain \[ \int\phi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^0 +\int_0^\infty\int(\partial_t\phi+\nabla\phi\cdot v_\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_\tau\,\mathrm{d}t=R_\tau. \] The summed spatial errors are at most \(\tfrac12\|D_x^2\phi\|_\infty\tau A\). Replacing \(\nabla\phi(t_{k-1})\) by \(\nabla\phi(t)\) costs at most \[ \tau\|\partial_t\nabla\phi\|_\infty\int_0^{T_\phi}\int|v_\tau|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_\tau\,\mathrm{d}t \le\tau\|\partial_t\nabla\phi\|_\infty\sqrt{T_\phi A}, \] where \(T_\phi\) contains the time support and the last bound is Cauchy–Schwarz. Hence \(|R_\tau|\le C_\phi\tau\to0\).

By the stated convergences of the space-time density and flux, both integrals pass to the limit, yielding \[ \int\phi(0,x)\rho_0(x)\,\mathrm{d}x +\int_0^\infty\int\left[\rho\partial_t\phi-\rho\nabla\{U'(\rho)+V+W*\rho\}\cdot\nabla\phi\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=0. \] This is the weak formulation of the claimed PDE. The flux-convergence assumption is essential: weak convergence of densities alone does not permit passage through the nonlinear expression \(\rho\nabla U'(\rho)\).

Exercises

energies behind diffusion

  1. Exercise 18.1. For \(U(r)=r^m/(m-1)\), \(m>1\), verify the gradient-flow equation is \(\partial_t\rho=\Delta(\rho^m)\).

    Hint

    Differentiate the internal-energy density, then expand the divergence of density times its gradient.

    Solution

    \(U\,\!\prime(r)=mr^{m-1}/(m-1)\), so \(\rho\nabla U\,\!\prime(\rho)=\nabla(\rho^m)\).

  2. Exercise 18.2. For \(V(x)=|x|^2/2\), identify the equilibrium of the entropy-plus-potential flow.

    Hint

    Set the sum of the entropy variation and confining potential equal to a constant.

    Solution

    It is the standard Gaussian. Relative entropy to this law is the free-energy difference and decreases along the Fokker–Planck equation.
