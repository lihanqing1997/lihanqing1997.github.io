Learning goal. Derive the PDE generated by internal, potential, and interaction energies and check dissipation signs.

Many nonlinear diffusion equations are steepest-descent equations in Wasserstein space. The same computation that identifies their velocity also reveals the correct Lyapunov functional, stationary measures, and dissipation rate.

The general free-energy equation

First variation. For \[ \mathcal F(\rho) =\int U(\rho(x))\,\mathrm{d}x +\int V(x)\rho(x)\,\mathrm{d}x +\frac12\iint W(x-y)\rho(x)\rho(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y, \] with \(W\) even, \[ \frac{\delta\mathcal F}{\delta\rho} =U'(\rho)+V+W*\rho \] up to an irrelevant additive constant.

Proof in a smooth setting. For a direct justification, work first on a flat torus with smooth \(\rho>0\), smooth \(U,V,W\), and even \(W\). A smooth zero-mass perturbation \(\sigma\) keeps \(\rho+h\sigma>0\) for small \(|h|\). Compactness bounds all difference quotients, so differentiation under the integrals is justified by dominated convergence. The interaction term has derivative \[ \frac12\iint W(x-y)[\sigma(x)\rho(y)+\rho(x)\sigma(y)]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y. \] Interchanging \(x,y\) in its second integral and using \(W(y-x)=W(x-y)\) makes the two terms equal. Their sum is \(\int(W*\rho)\sigma\). The internal and potential derivatives are \(\int U'(\rho)\sigma\) and \(\int V\sigma\). Therefore \[ D\mathcal F(\rho)[\sigma]=\int[U'(\rho)+V+W*\rho]\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x. \] Since \(\int\sigma=0\), adding a constant to the bracket changes no variation. The same calculation on \(\mathbb R^d\) is valid whenever its difference quotients have integrable dominating functions; this must be checked for the chosen growth and tails.

Gradient-flow equation. The formal \(W_2\)-gradient flow is \[ \boxed{ \partial_t\rho =\nabla\cdot\left[ \rho\nabla\{U'(\rho)+V+W*\rho\} \right]. } \]

Energy dissipation

Dissipation identity. On the torus in the preceding smooth setting, or on \(\mathbb R^d\) when differentiation under the energy integral is valid and the boundary terms specified below vanish, \[ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal F(\rho_t) =-\int\rho_t \left|\nabla\!\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal F}{\delta\rho_t}\right)\right|^2\,\mathrm{d}x \le0. \]

Proof. Write \(F_t=U'(\rho_t)+V+W*\rho_t\). The chain rule just proved and \(\partial_t\rho=\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla F)\) give \[ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal F(\rho_t)=\int F_t\nabla\cdot(\rho_t\nabla F_t)\,\mathrm{d}x =-\int\rho_t|\nabla F_t|^2\,\mathrm{d}x \] on the torus, without boundary terms. For \(\mathbb R^d\), take cutoffs \(\chi_R\) equal to one on \(B_R\), supported in \(B_{2R}\), with \(|\nabla\chi_R|\le C/R\). The integration by parts has error \[ -\int F_t\rho_t\nabla F_t\cdot\nabla\chi_R\,\mathrm{d}x, \qquad |\text{error}|\le\frac C R\int_{R<|x|<2R}|F_t|\rho_t|\nabla F_t|\,\mathrm{d}x. \] If this bound tends to zero (also integrably in time for a time-integrated identity), and \(\rho|\nabla F|^2\) is integrable, dominated convergence gives the same identity. These are explicit sufficient decay conditions. When \(-\nabla F\) belongs to the tangent space, the velocity theorem identifies its norm with metric speed; identifying the metric slope additionally uses the energy subgradient theorem, not integration by parts alone.

The heat equation

Heat as entropy flow. For the Boltzmann entropy \[ \operatorname{Ent}(\rho)=\int\rho\log\rho\,\mathrm{d}x, \] the Wasserstein gradient flow is \[ \partial_t\rho=\Delta\rho. \]

Proof. Since \(\delta\operatorname{Ent}/\delta\rho=1+\log\rho\), \[ \nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla\log\rho) =\nabla\cdot(\nabla\rho)=\Delta\rho. \] Moreover, \[ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\operatorname{Ent}(\rho_t) =-\int\frac{|\nabla\rho_t|^2}{\rho_t}\,\mathrm{d}x =-\int|\nabla\log\rho_t|^2\rho_t\,\mathrm{d}x, \] so entropy dissipation is the Fisher information.

Fokker--Planck and Langevin dynamics

Fokker--Planck flow. For \[ \mathcal F(\rho)=\int\rho\log\rho\,\mathrm{d}x+\int V\rho\,\mathrm{d}x, \] the gradient flow is \[ \partial_t\rho=\Delta\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\nabla V). \] If \(Z=\int e^{-V}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty\), its equilibrium is \(\gamma=Z^{-1}e^{-V}\,\mathrm{d}x\), and \[ \mathcal F(\rho)-\mathcal F(\gamma) =\operatorname{KL}(\rho\,\|\,\gamma). \]

This is the forward equation of the Langevin diffusion \[ \mathrm{d}X_t=-\nabla V(X_t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt2\,\mathrm{d}B_t. \] When \(D^2V\ge\lambda I\), displacement \(\lambda\)-convexity gives \(W_2\)-contraction at rate \(e^{-\lambda t}\).

Ornstein--Uhlenbeck flow. For \(V(x)=|x|^2/2\), \[ \mathrm{d}X_t=-X_t\,\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt2\,\mathrm{d}B_t, \] and \[ X_t=e^{-t}X_0+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}\,Z,\qquad Z\sim N(0,I). \] The invariant law is \(N(0,I)\), and the semigroup contracts \(W_2\) by \(e^{-t}\).

Porous-medium and fast-diffusion equations

Power internal energy. For \[ \mathcal U_m(\rho)=\frac{1}{m-1}\int\rho^m\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad m\ne1, \] the Wasserstein gradient flow is \[ \partial_t\rho=\Delta(\rho^m). \]

Proof. The first variation is \(\tfrac{m}{m-1}\rho^{m-1}\). Hence \[ \nabla\cdot\left(\rho\nabla\frac{m}{m-1}\rho^{m-1}\right) =\nabla\cdot(m\rho^{m-1}\nabla\rho) =\Delta(\rho^m). \] The case \(m>1\) is porous-medium diffusion with finite-speed propagation; \(m<1\) is fast diffusion, subject to the integrability range dictated by dimension.

Aggregation and interaction

Aggregation equation. For the interaction energy \[ \mathcal W(\rho)=\frac12\iint W(x-y)\rho(x)\rho(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y, \] the gradient flow is \[ \partial_t\rho=\nabla\cdot\{\rho\nabla(W*\rho)\}. \]

Attractive kernels may concentrate mass, while repulsive kernels spread it. Adding entropy or power internal energy gives the aggregation--diffusion equation \[ \partial_t\rho =\Delta(\rho^m)+\nabla\cdot\{\rho\nabla(V+W*\rho)\}. \] The competition between diffusion and attraction determines whether solutions disperse, approach equilibrium, or concentrate.

Weak solutions from JKO

Variational construction. Under lower semicontinuity, coercivity, and displacement-convexity assumptions on the free energy, the JKO scheme has a continuous-time limit that is a weak solution of the corresponding diffusion equation and satisfies the energy-dissipation inequality.

Identification of the PDE. The Euler equation of each JKO step gives the discrete velocity \[ v_\tau^{k+1} =-\nabla\{U'(\rho_\tau^{k+1})+V+W*\rho_\tau^{k+1}\}. \] Insert this field in the discrete weak continuity equation, sum over time steps, and use the action bound for compactness. Weak convergence of the densities and lower-semicontinuity of the dissipation identify the limiting continuity equation with the displayed PDE.

Sources and proof details. Santambrogio, Optimal transport for applied mathematicians, Chapter 8, for weak-solution constructions. The first-variation and dissipation calculations on this page assume smoothness and sufficient boundary decay.

Practice: energies behind diffusion

  1. For \(U(r)=r^m/(m-1)\), \(m>1\), verify the gradient-flow equation is \(\partial_t\rho=\Delta(\rho^m)\).

    Hint and answer

    \(U\,\!\prime(r)=mr^{m-1}/(m-1)\), so \(\rho\nabla U\,\!\prime(\rho)=\nabla(\rho^m)\).

  2. For \(V(x)=|x|^2/2\), identify the equilibrium of the entropy-plus-potential flow.

    Hint and answer

    It is the standard Gaussian. Relative entropy to this law is the free-energy difference and decreases along the Fokker–Planck equation.
