Adaptive integration spends computational effort where the integrand is difficult, rather than using a uniformly fine grid everywhere.

Local error estimate

Definition. An adaptive quadrature method estimates the local error on an interval by comparing two rules, often a coarse rule and a refined or embedded higher-order rule. Intervals with large estimated error are subdivided.

Adaptive Simpson principle

Proposition. If \(S(a,b)\) denotes Simpson's rule and \(S(a,m)+S(m,b)\) its split version, then their difference estimates the leading error term. Recursion continues until the estimated contribution meets the tolerance.

Reliability caveat

Remark. A posteriori estimates can miss narrow spikes, discontinuities, or cancellation patterns if the sampling never sees them. Adaptive methods are powerful but still depend on assumptions about the integrand.

Termination and tolerance

Proposition. Practical adaptive routines combine absolute and relative tolerances, recursion limits, and safeguards against roundoff stagnation. A returned tolerance is an algorithmic diagnostic, not a theorem unless the required regularity assumptions hold.

Higher-dimensional boundary

Remark. Adaptive deterministic integration scales poorly with dimension. High-dimensional integration often shifts toward sparse grids, quasi-Monte Carlo, or probabilistic methods, which belong partly to numerical analysis and partly to statistics.

Local error estimation

Adaptive quadrature compares nested rules on an interval \(I\), such as Simpson estimates \(S_I\) and the sum \(S_{I_L}+S_{I_R}\). If the leading error is \(Ch^p\), halving gives \[ \int_I f-(S_{I_L}+S_{I_R}) \approx\frac{(S_{I_L}+S_{I_R})-S_I}{2^p-1}. \] This is an asymptotic estimator; it can fail when the leading coefficient vanishes accidentally or the integrand is unresolved.

Recursive tolerance allocation

A standard strategy accepts \(I\) when the local estimate is below \(\tau_I\), otherwise bisects and assigns \(\tau_I/2\) to each child. Then the sum of accepted local tolerances is at most the global tolerance. Absolute and relative criteria are often combined: \[ \eta_I\le \tau_{\mathrm{abs},I} +\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}|Q_I|. \] Pure relative tolerance is meaningless when the true integral is near zero.

Proposition (complexity under local smoothness). Suppose a local rule has \[ |E_I|\le C|I|^{p+1}\sup_I|f^{(p)}|. \] If \(f^{(p)}\) is bounded and intervals are refined until \(|E_I|\lesssim\tau|I|/(b-a)\), a uniform worst-case mesh needs \[ N=O(\tau^{-1/p}) \] subintervals.

Proof. On width \(h\), the global sum of \(N=(b-a)/h\) local errors is \[ O(Nh^{p+1})=O(h^p). \] Setting this to \(\tau\) yields \(h=O(\tau^{1/p})\) and the claimed count. Adaptivity improves the constant and local allocation when derivatives are inhomogeneous, not this worst-case smoothness exponent.

Singularities and transformations

Endpoint algebraic singularities are better handled by a change of variables than by blind bisection. If \(f(x)\sim x^{-\alpha}\), \(0<\alpha<1\), set \(x=t^q\) with \(q(1-\alpha)\) large enough to smooth the transformed integrand. Infinite intervals can use rational maps; double- exponential transformations are effective when endpoint derivatives blow up.

Oscillatory integrals

For \(\int_a^bf(x)e^{i\omega g(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\), resolving every oscillation costs proportional to \(\omega\). Filon-type rules interpolate the slow amplitude and integrate against the oscillatory factor analytically. Steepest descent and stationary phase identify where asymptotic contribution is concentrated. Ordinary local-error estimators can mistake cancellation for accuracy.

Reliability failures

A narrow spike between sampled nodes, discontinuity, NaN, or highly oscillatory alias can make two nested rules agree while both are wrong. Robust software limits recursion depth, checks nonfinite values, samples endpoint and interior structure, and reports a diagnostic when the requested tolerance is not certified. An error estimate is data, not an oracle.
