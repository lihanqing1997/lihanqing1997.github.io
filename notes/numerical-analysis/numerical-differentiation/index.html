Numerical differentiation is delicate because differentiation amplifies high-frequency error and rounding noise.

Finite differences

Definition. The forward difference \((f(x+h)-f(x))/h\), backward difference \((f(x)-f(x-h))/h\), and centered difference \((f(x+h)-f(x-h))/(2h)\) approximate \(\mathrm{d}f/\mathrm{d}x\).

Truncation error

Proposition. If \(f\) is smooth, Taylor expansion gives first-order error for one-sided differences and second-order error for centered differences. Higher-order formulas are obtained by forcing more Taylor coefficients to cancel.

Roundoff barrier

Theorem. For finite precision data, the differentiation error has the typical form \(C_1h^p+C_2u/h\). The optimal step size balances truncation error against cancellation and is not obtained by taking \(h\) as small as possible.

Ill-posedness

Remark. Differentiation is not a continuous operation from uniform noisy data to uniform derivative estimates. Reliable numerical differentiation therefore needs smoothness assumptions, regularization, or model-based structure.

Complex-step derivative

Proposition. For analytic real-valued functions extendable to complex inputs, \(\operatorname{Im} f(x+ih)/h\) approximates \(f'(x)\) without subtractive cancellation. This is a useful computational trick when complex arithmetic is available.

Finite-difference formulas

Taylor expansion gives \[ \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}h =f'(x)+\frac h2f''(x)+O(h^2), \] and the centered formula \[ \frac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h} =f'(x)+\frac{h^2}{6}f^{(3)}(x)+O(h^4). \] Symmetry cancels even powers and raises the order.

Theorem (general weights). A formula \[ D_hf(x)=\frac1h\sum_{j=0}^m w_j f(x+c_jh) \] is exact for polynomials of degree at most \(p\) if and only if \[ \sum_jw_jc_j^k= \begin{cases} 1,&k=1,\\ 0,&0\le k\le p,\ k\ne1. \end{cases} \] If \(f\in C^{p+1}\), its truncation error is \(O(h^p)\), subject to the first nonvanishing moment.

Proof. Substitute Taylor expansions \[ f(x+c_jh)=\sum_k \frac{f^{(k)}(x)}{k!}c_j^kh^k. \] Coefficient matching gives the moment conditions and the leading remainder.

Roundoff versus truncation

If function values have relative errors of order \(u\), a first-difference roundoff contribution is roughly \(u|f(x)|/h\). For a centered formula, \[ \text{error}\lesssim C_1h^2+C_2u/h. \] Balancing terms gives \[ h_{\mathrm{opt}}\asymp u^{1/3}, \qquad \text{minimum error}\asymp u^{2/3}, \] after restoring problem scales. Taking \(h\) arbitrarily small makes the answer worse.

Richardson extrapolation

If \[ D_h=D+c h^p+O(h^{p+q}), \] then \[ D_{\mathrm{ext}} =\frac{2^pD_{h/2}-D_h}{2^p-1} =D+O(h^{p+q}). \] Repeated extrapolation produces high order, but also combines nearby noisy quantities with large coefficients. Smooth asymptotic expansions and roundoff control are required.

Complex-step differentiation

For a real-analytic function evaluated with complex arithmetic, \[ f(x+ih)=f(x)+ihf'(x)-\frac{h^2}{2}f''(x)+O(h^3), \] so \[ f'(x)=\frac{\operatorname{Im}f(x+ih)}h+O(h^2). \] There is no subtractive cancellation, permitting very small \(h\). The method fails for nonanalytic code paths such as absolute values, comparisons, or complex-unsafe library routines.

Automatic differentiation

Automatic differentiation applies the chain rule to the computational graph. Forward mode propagates directional derivatives at a cost proportional to the number of input directions; reverse mode propagates adjoints at a cost comparable to one function evaluation for scalar output, but stores or recomputes intermediates. It gives derivatives of the implemented algorithm, which may differ from derivatives of an ideal mathematical problem at nondifferentiable branches or unconverged iterative solvers.
