Euclidean distance is often the wrong geometry for a simplex, a positive cone, or a space with highly anisotropic coordinates. Mirror descent replaces the squared Euclidean distance in the gradient step by a divergence generated by a strongly convex potential.

Bregman divergence and its three-point identity

Definition. Let \(C\) be convex and let \(\psi\) be differentiable on \(\operatorname{int}C\). Its Bregman divergence is \[ D_\psi(x,y) =\psi(x)-\psi(y)-\langle\nabla\psi(y),x-y\rangle. \] If \(\psi\) is \(1\)-strongly convex with respect to a norm \(\|\cdot\|\), then \(D_\psi(x,y)\ge\frac12\|x-y\|^2\).

A Bregman divergence is nonnegative but generally asymmetric and does not satisfy the triangle inequality. Its orientation matters in both the algorithm and the proof.

Three-point identity. For points where the gradients exist, \[ \langle\nabla\psi(y)-\nabla\psi(z),x-y\rangle = D_\psi(x,z)-D_\psi(x,y)-D_\psi(y,z). \]

Proof. Expand the three divergences on the right. The values \(\psi(x),\psi(y),\psi(z)\) cancel, leaving \(\langle\nabla\psi(z),x-z\rangle -\langle\nabla\psi(y),x-y\rangle +\langle\nabla\psi(z),y-z\rangle\), which simplifies to the left side.

The mirror step in primal and dual coordinates

Definition. Given a subgradient \(g_k\in\partial f_k(x_k)\), mirror descent sets \[ x_{k+1} \in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{x\in C} \left\{\eta_k\langle g_k,x\rangle+D_\psi(x,x_k)\right\}. \]

Mirror optimality equation. The update satisfies \[ \left\langle \eta_kg_k+\nabla\psi(x_{k+1})-\nabla\psi(x_k), x-x_{k+1} \right\rangle\ge0 \quad(x\in C). \] If \(C\) imposes no active constraint, this reduces to \[ \nabla\psi(x_{k+1}) =\nabla\psi(x_k)-\eta_kg_k. \]

Proof. Differentiate the convex update objective and apply the variational inequality for minimization over \(C\). In the unconstrained interior, the normal cone is zero, so its gradient must vanish.

The map \(x\mapsto\nabla\psi(x)\) produces dual coordinates. Mirror descent takes an ordinary additive step there and maps back through \((\nabla\psi)^{-1}\).

The one-step inequality and regret

Mirror descent inequality. If \(\psi\) is \(1\)-strongly convex with respect to \(\|\cdot\|\), then for every \(x\in C\), \[ \eta_k\langle g_k,x_k-x\rangle \le D_\psi(x,x_k)-D_\psi(x,x_{k+1}) +\frac{\eta_k^2}{2}\|g_k\|_*^2. \]

Proof. Insert \(x\) into the mirror optimality equation and use the three-point identity: \[ \eta_k\langle g_k,x_{k+1}-x\rangle \le D_\psi(x,x_k)-D_\psi(x,x_{k+1}) -D_\psi(x_{k+1},x_k). \] Add \(\eta_k\langle g_k,x_k-x_{k+1}\rangle\). Strong convexity and Young's inequality give \[ \eta_k\langle g_k,x_k-x_{k+1}\rangle -D_\psi(x_{k+1},x_k) \le\eta_k\|g_k\|_*\|x_k-x_{k+1}\| -\frac12\|x_k-x_{k+1}\|^2 \le\frac{\eta_k^2}{2}\|g_k\|_*^2. \]

Online regret bound. Suppose the convex losses \(f_k\) have subgradients satisfying \(\|g_k\|_*\le G\), and suppose \(D_\psi(x,x_1)\le R^2\) for every comparator \(x\in C\). With constant step \(\eta=R/(G\sqrt n)\), \[ \sum_{k=1}^n[f_k(x_k)-f_k(x)] \le\frac32RG\sqrt n \qquad(x\in C). \]

Proof. Convexity gives \(f_k(x_k)-f_k(x)\le\langle g_k,x_k-x\rangle\). Sum the one-step inequality. The Bregman terms telescope and the final one is nonnegative: \[ \sum_{k=1}^n[f_k(x_k)-f_k(x)] \le\frac{R^2}{\eta}+\frac{\eta nG^2}{2}. \] The stated step gives \(\frac32RG\sqrt n\). Optimizing the bound instead gives \(\eta=\sqrt2R/(G\sqrt n)\) and the slightly smaller constant \(\sqrt2RG\sqrt n\).

Entropy geometry on the probability simplex

Exponentiated-gradient update. On \(\Delta_d=\{x_i\ge0,\sum_i x_i=1\}\), take \(\psi(x)=\sum_i x_i\log x_i\). Then \[ D_\psi(x,y)=\sum_i x_i\log\frac{x_i}{y_i} \] is relative entropy. Solving the mirror step with a multiplier for \(\sum_i x_i=1\) gives \[ x_{k+1,i} =\frac{x_{k,i}e^{-\eta_kg_{k,i}}} {\sum_jx_{k,j}e^{-\eta_kg_{k,j}}}. \] Positivity and normalization are preserved automatically, unlike an unconstrained Euclidean step.

The Hessian \(\nabla^2\psi\) defines a local Riemannian metric, while the Bregman divergence retains global affine information. This connects mirror descent to dually flat information geometry and to entropic variational inference.
