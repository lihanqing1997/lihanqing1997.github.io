Newton and quasi-Newton directions are local proposals. A globalization mechanism decides how far to move before the local model becomes unreliable. Line search controls step length along a chosen direction; a trust region controls the set over which the model is optimized.

Armijo backtracking

Definition. A direction \(p\) is a descent direction at \(x\) if \(\nabla f(x)^\top p<0\). For \(c_1\in(0,1)\), the Armijo condition is \[ f(x+\alpha p) \le f(x)+c_1\alpha\nabla f(x)^\top p. \] Backtracking starts from \(\alpha_0>0\) and replaces \(\alpha\) by \(\rho\alpha\), \(0<\rho<1\), until the inequality holds.

Finite termination. If \(f\) is differentiable at \(x\) and \(p\) is a descent direction, Armijo backtracking terminates after finitely many reductions.

Proof. Differentiability gives \[ f(x+\alpha p) =f(x)+\alpha\nabla f(x)^\top p+o(\alpha). \] Since \((1-c_1)\nabla f(x)^\top p<0\), the remainder is smaller in magnitude than \(-(1-c_1)\alpha\nabla f(x)^\top p\) for all sufficiently small \(\alpha\). The Armijo inequality then holds, and geometric backtracking eventually reaches such a step.

Wolfe curvature conditions

The weak Wolfe condition supplements Armijo by \[ \nabla f(x+\alpha p)^\top p \ge c_2\nabla f(x)^\top p,\qquad c_10, \] preserving positive definiteness.

Zoutendijk condition. Suppose \(f\) is bounded below on the initial level set and has an \(L\)-Lipschitz gradient there. If \(p_k\) are descent directions and \(\alpha_k\) satisfy the Wolfe conditions, then \[ \sum_{k=0}^\infty \cos^2\theta_k\,\|\nabla f(x_k)\|^2<\infty, \qquad \cos\theta_k= \frac{-\nabla f(x_k)^\top p_k} {\|\nabla f(x_k)\|\,\|p_k\|}. \] If \(\cos\theta_k\ge c>0\), then \(\|\nabla f(x_k)\|\to0\).

Proof. Lipschitz continuity and the Wolfe curvature condition imply the lower step bound \[ \alpha_k \ge\frac{(1-c_2)(-\nabla f(x_k)^\top p_k)} {L\|p_k\|^2}. \] Armijo then gives \[ f(x_k)-f(x_{k+1}) \ge\frac{c_1(1-c_2)}L \frac{(\nabla f(x_k)^\top p_k)^2}{\|p_k\|^2} =\frac{c_1(1-c_2)}L \cos^2\theta_k\|\nabla f(x_k)\|^2. \] Sum over \(k\); the left side is bounded by \(f(x_0)-\inf f\). A uniform angle bound then forces the gradient norms to zero.

Quadratic models inside a trust region

Definition. A trust-region method approximately solves \[ \min_{\|p\|\le\Delta_k} m_k(p)=f(x_k)+g_k^\top p+\frac12p^\top B_kp. \] After proposing \(p_k\), it compares actual and predicted decrease: \[ \rho_k= \frac{f(x_k)-f(x_k+p_k)} {m_k(0)-m_k(p_k)}. \] A poor ratio rejects the step and shrinks \(\Delta_k\); a ratio near one permits acceptance and possible expansion.

Cauchy decrease. Minimizing the model along \(-g_k\) within the trust region gives a step \(p_k^C\) with \[ m_k(0)-m_k(p_k^C) \ge\frac12\|g_k\| \min\left\{\Delta_k,\frac{\|g_k\|}{\|B_k\|}\right\}. \]

Proof. Put \(p=-tg_k\) with \(0\le t\le\Delta_k/\|g_k\|\). The predicted decrease is \(t\|g_k\|^2-\frac12t^2g_k^\top B_kg_k\). If the unconstrained minimizer along the ray occurs before the boundary, substitution gives at least \(\|g_k\|^2/(2\|B_k\|)\); otherwise the boundary step gives at least \(\Delta_k\|g_k\|/2\).

If every computed step achieves a fixed fraction of this Cauchy decrease, the model Hessians remain bounded, and \(f\) is smooth and bounded below, the acceptance-radius rules imply \(\liminf_k\|\nabla f(x_k)\|=0\), with standard variants giving full convergence to first-order stationarity. Trust regions remain meaningful when \(B_k\) is indefinite because the bounded subproblem can exploit negative curvature rather than treating it as an invalid Newton step.
