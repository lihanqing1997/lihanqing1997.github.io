Splitting introduces auxiliary variables so that incompatible structures become separate subproblems. ADMM combines augmented-Lagrangian stabilization with alternating minimization and a dual update.

The augmented Lagrangian

Consider the convex problem \[ \min_{x,z}\ f(x)+g(z) \quad\text{subject to}\quad Ax+Bz=c. \] For penalty \(\rho>0\), the augmented Lagrangian is \[ \mathcal L_\rho(x,z,y) =f(x)+g(z)+y^\top(Ax+Bz-c) +\frac\rho2\|Ax+Bz-c\|^2. \] The quadratic term changes the algorithm but not the feasible primal solutions or KKT equations.

Definition. With scaled dual variable \(u=y/\rho\), ADMM performs \[ \begin{aligned} x^{k+1}&\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_x \left\{f(x)+\frac\rho2 \|Ax+Bz^k-c+u^k\|^2\right\},\\ z^{k+1}&\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_z \left\{g(z)+\frac\rho2 \|Ax^{k+1}+Bz-c+u^k\|^2\right\},\\ u^{k+1}&=u^k+Ax^{k+1}+Bz^{k+1}-c. \end{aligned} \]

The primal residual \(r^{k+1}=Ax^{k+1}+Bz^{k+1}-c\) measures infeasibility. The dual residual \(s^{k+1}=\rho A^\top B(z^{k+1}-z^k)\) measures change in the stationarity condition induced by the second block.

KKT residuals from the subproblems

Approximate stationarity. Let \(y^{k+1}=\rho u^{k+1}\). The ADMM optimality equations imply \[ \begin{aligned} 0&\in\partial g(z^{k+1})+B^\top y^{k+1},\\ 0&\in\partial f(x^{k+1})+A^\top y^{k+1}+s^{k+1}. \end{aligned} \] Hence \(r^{k+1}\to0\) and \(s^{k+1}\to0\) imply the KKT equations asymptotically.

Proof. The \(z\)-subproblem condition is \[ 0\in\partial g(z^{k+1}) +\rho B^\top(Ax^{k+1}+Bz^{k+1}-c+u^k). \] Since \(u^{k+1}=u^k+r^{k+1}\), this is the first inclusion. The \(x\)-condition uses \(z^k\); add and subtract \(Bz^{k+1}\), then use the dual update to obtain the second inclusion and the stated \(s^{k+1}\).

Convex convergence

ADMM convergence. Suppose \(f\) and \(g\) are proper, closed, and convex; each ADMM subproblem has a solution; and the unaugmented Lagrangian has a saddle point \((x^\star,z^\star,y^\star)\). Then \[ r^k\to0,\qquad s^k\to0,\qquad f(x^k)+g(z^k)\to f(x^\star)+g(z^\star). \] The dual sequence converges to a dual solution. Under standard rank or strong-convexity conditions, the primal variables also converge.

Proof outline. Monotonicity of \(\partial f\) and \(\partial g\), applied to the subproblem optimality equations and the KKT equations at the saddle point, yields the Lyapunov inequality \[ \rho\|B(z^{k+1}-z^\star)\|^2 +\rho^{-1}\|y^{k+1}-y^\star\|^2 \le \rho\|B(z^k-z^\star)\|^2 +\rho^{-1}\|y^k-y^\star\|^2 -\rho\|r^{k+1}\|^2 -\rho\|B(z^{k+1}-z^k)\|^2. \] Summation proves square summability of primal and dual residuals and hence their convergence to zero. Weak duality bounds the objective error above and below by terms involving \(r^k\) and \(y^k\), proving value convergence. Cluster points satisfy KKT; additional identifiability conditions make the primal limit unique.

Consensus splitting

Example. To minimize \(\sum_{j=1}^Mf_j(x)+g(x)\), introduce local copies \(x_j\) and a consensus variable \(z\), with \(x_j=z\). The \(x_j\)-updates are independent: \[ x_j^{k+1} =\operatorname{prox}_{f_j/\rho}(z^k-u_j^k). \] The \(z\)-update combines the local proposals through the proximal map of \(g\), followed by dual consensus updates. This is useful when data, likelihood terms, or model components are distributed.

The penalty \(\rho\) changes numerical conditioning but not the target. Residual balancing adapts \(\rho\) when primal feasibility and dual stationarity progress at very different rates.
