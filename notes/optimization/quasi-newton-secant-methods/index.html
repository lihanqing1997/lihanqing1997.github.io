Quasi-Newton methods infer curvature from successive gradients. They retain Newton's linear-system form but update an approximate Hessian or inverse Hessian using only first-order information.

The secant equation as integrated curvature

Let \[ s_k=x_{k+1}-x_k,\qquad y_k=\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla f(x_k). \] The fundamental theorem of calculus gives \[ y_k=\left[\int_0^1\nabla^2f(x_k+ts_k)\,\mathrm{d}t\right]s_k. \] Thus a Hessian approximation \(B_{k+1}\) should satisfy the secant equation \[ B_{k+1}s_k=y_k. \]

In one dimension this determines the approximation completely: \(B_{k+1}=y_k/s_k\), and the resulting root update is the classical secant method. In several dimensions one vector equation leaves many degrees of freedom, so an update principle is needed.

The BFGS rank-two update

Definition. Given a symmetric positive-definite \(B_k\) and \(s_k^\top y_k>0\), BFGS sets \[ B_{k+1} =B_k-\frac{B_ks_ks_k^\top B_k}{s_k^\top B_ks_k} +\frac{y_ky_k^\top}{y_k^\top s_k}. \] For \(H_k=B_k^{-1}\), the inverse update is \[ H_{k+1} =(I-\rho_ks_ky_k^\top)H_k(I-\rho_ky_ks_k^\top) +\rho_ks_ks_k^\top, \qquad \rho_k=(y_k^\top s_k)^{-1}. \]

Secant and positive-definite preservation. The BFGS matrix is symmetric, \(B_{k+1}s_k=y_k\), and \(B_{k+1}\succ0\) whenever \(B_k\succ0\) and \(s_k^\top y_k>0\).

Proof. Symmetry is visible. Multiplying by \(s_k\) cancels the first correction against \(B_ks_k\) and leaves \(y_k\). For \(v\ne0\), \[ v^\top B_{k+1}v =v^\top B_kv-\frac{(v^\top B_ks_k)^2}{s_k^\top B_ks_k} +\frac{(v^\top y_k)^2}{y_k^\top s_k}. \] Cauchy-Schwarz in the \(B_k\)-inner product makes the first two terms nonnegative. Equality there occurs only when \(v\) is proportional to \(s_k\); then the last term is strictly positive because \(s_k^\top y_k>0\).

For a strongly convex \(f\), the curvature condition follows from \[ s_k^\top y_k =\int_0^1s_k^\top\nabla^2f(x_k+ts_k)s_k\,\mathrm{d}t \ge\mu\|s_k\|^2. \] In nonconvex problems, a Wolfe line search or a damped update is used to retain positive definiteness.

BFGS directions and line search

The search direction is \[ p_k=-H_k\nabla f(x_k), \] which is a descent direction whenever \(H_k\succ0\). One then chooses \(\alpha_k>0\) by line search and sets \(x_{k+1}=x_k+\alpha_kp_k\). The update incorporates the curvature observed over this accepted step.

Local superlinear convergence. Suppose \(f\) is twice continuously differentiable, strongly convex near \(x^\star\), and its Hessian is Lipschitz there. Under a line search that eventually accepts unit steps and the Dennis-More condition \[ \frac{\|(B_k-\nabla^2f(x^\star))s_k\|}{\|s_k\|}\to0, \] the quasi-Newton iterates satisfy \[ \frac{\|x_{k+1}-x^\star\|}{\|x_k-x^\star\|}\to0. \] BFGS with a Wolfe line search satisfies this condition under the stated local regularity.

Proof outline. Taylor expansion gives \(\nabla f(x_k)=\nabla^2f(x^\star)e_k+O(\|e_k\|^2)\). With a unit step, \[ e_{k+1} =e_k-B_k^{-1}\nabla f(x_k) =B_k^{-1}(B_k-\nabla^2f(x^\star))e_k+O(\|e_k\|^2). \] Uniform positive definiteness bounds \(B_k^{-1}\). The Dennis-More condition makes the first term \(o(\|e_k\|)\), and the remainder is also \(o(\|e_k\|)\). For BFGS, the rank-two variational update and Wolfe curvature condition imply that the accumulated secant errors vanish in the step directions, establishing Dennis-More.

Limited-memory BFGS

Storing \(H_k\) costs \(O(d^2)\). L-BFGS retains only the most recent \(m\) pairs \((s_j,y_j)\) and applies the inverse update implicitly through the two-loop recursion, costing \(O(md)\) memory and arithmetic. It is effective when gradients are available but Hessian-vector products or factorizations are too expensive.
