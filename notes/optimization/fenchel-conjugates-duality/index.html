Conjugacy describes a convex function through all of its affine lower bounds. It converts slopes into points, infimal problems into supremal ones, and regularization penalties into explicit dual constraints.

Affine minorants and the conjugate

Definition. For \(f:\mathbb R^d\to(-\infty,+\infty]\), its Fenchel conjugate is \[ f^*(y)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb R^d} \{\langle y,x\rangle-f(x)\}. \] The biconjugate is \(f^{**}(x)=\sup_y\{\langle y,x\rangle-f^*(y)\}\).

Regardless of whether \(f\) is convex, \(f^*\) is convex and lower semicontinuous because it is a supremum of affine continuous functions.

Fenchel-Young inequality and equality. For every \(x,y\), \[ f(x)+f^*(y)\ge\langle x,y\rangle. \] If \(f\) is proper and convex, equality holds if and only if \(y\in\partial f(x)\), equivalently \(x\in\partial f^*(y)\).

Proof. The definition of the supremum gives \(f^*(y)\ge\langle y,x\rangle-f(x)\). Equality means that \(x\) maximizes \(z\mapsto\langle y,z\rangle-f(z)\), which is equivalent to \[ f(z)\ge f(x)+\langle y,z-x\rangle\qquad(z\in\mathbb R^d). \] This is exactly \(y\in\partial f(x)\). Rewriting the same equality as \(f^*(y)+f(x)=\langle x,y\rangle\) gives \(x\in\partial f^*(y)\).

Conjugates worth recognizing

Quadratic and norm. For \(f(x)=\frac12x^\top Qx\), \(Q\succ0\), \[ f^*(y)=\frac12y^\top Q^{-1}y. \] For a norm \(\|\cdot\|\) with dual norm \(\|\cdot\|_*\), \[ (\lambda\|\cdot\|)^*(y) =\iota_{\{y:\|y\|_*\le\lambda\}}(y). \] The second formula follows because \(\sup_x\{\langle y,x\rangle-\lambda\|x\|\}\) is zero when \(\|y\|_*\le\lambda\) and is \(+\infty\) after scaling a direction that violates the dual-norm inequality.

Log-sum-exp and entropy. For \(f(x)=\log\sum_{i=1}^m e^{x_i}\), \[ f^*(p)= \begin{cases} \sum_i p_i\log p_i,&p_i\ge0,\ \sum_i p_i=1,\\ +\infty,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \] At an interior simplex point, stationarity in \(x\) gives \(p_i=e^{x_i}/\sum_j e^{x_j}\); substitution yields negative entropy. This dual pair underlies exponential families, softmax, and entropic mirror descent.

Recovering a closed convex function

Fenchel-Moreau theorem. A proper function \(f:\mathbb R^d\to(-\infty,+\infty]\) satisfies \(f=f^{**}\) if and only if it is convex and lower semicontinuous.

Proof. Fenchel-Young gives \(f^{**}\le f\). Suppose \(f\) is proper, closed, and convex and fix \(x_0\) and \(r<f(x_0)\). The point \((x_0,r)\) lies strictly below the closed convex epigraph of \(f\), so strict separation supplies \((y,\alpha)\ne0\) and \(c\) with \[ \langle y,x_0\rangle+\alpha r>c \ge\langle y,x\rangle+\alpha t \quad(t\ge f(x)). \] Necessarily \(\alpha<0\); normalize to \(\alpha=-1\). Then \(\langle y,x\rangle-f(x)\le c\), so \(f^*(y)\le c\), while \(\langle y,x_0\rangle-f^*(y)>r\). Hence \(f^{**}(x_0)>r\). Letting \(r\uparrow f(x_0)\) proves \(f^{**}(x_0)\ge f(x_0)\). Conversely, every biconjugate is convex and lower semicontinuous.

Dualizing a composite objective

Fenchel-Rockafellar duality. Let \(f:\mathbb R^m\to(-\infty,+\infty]\) and \(g:\mathbb R^d\to(-\infty,+\infty]\) be proper, closed, and convex, and let \(A:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^m\) be linear. The primal problem \[ \inf_x\{f(Ax)+g(x)\} \] has dual \[ \sup_y\{-f^*(y)-g^*(-A^\top y)\}. \] If \(A(\operatorname{dom}g)\) meets \(\operatorname{ri}(\operatorname{dom}f)\), the two values agree and the dual supremum is attained whenever finite.

Proof. Use \(f(Ax)=\sup_y\{\langle y,Ax\rangle-f^*(y)\}\). Interchanging infimum and supremum formally gives \[ \sup_y\left\{-f^*(y)+ \inf_x[\langle A^\top y,x\rangle+g(x)]\right\} =\sup_y\{-f^*(y)-g^*(-A^\top y)\}. \] This immediately proves weak duality. For strong duality, apply the supporting-hyperplane theorem to the closed perturbation value function \(u\mapsto\inf_x\{f(Ax+u)+g(x)\}\). The relative-interior condition makes this function continuous at \(u=0\), so it has a supporting slope there; that slope is a dual optimizer and closes the gap.

At primal-dual optimality, Fenchel-Young equality gives \[ y^\star\in\partial f(Ax^\star),\qquad -A^\top y^\star\in\partial g(x^\star). \] These inclusions are both an optimality certificate and the starting point for primal-dual splitting algorithms.
