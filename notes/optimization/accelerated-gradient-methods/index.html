Acceleration adds a carefully tuned extrapolation to the gradient step. The extra sequence is not merely physical momentum: its coefficients are chosen so that a weighted objective error and a weighted distance decrease together, producing a \(1/k^2\) rate.

Nesterov's convex iteration

Definition. Set \(x_0=y_0\) and \(t_0=1\). For \(k\ge0\), define \[ \begin{aligned} x_{k+1}&=y_k-\frac1L\nabla f(y_k),\\ t_{k+1}&=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4t_k^2}}2,\\ y_{k+1}&=x_{k+1} +\frac{t_k-1}{t_{k+1}}(x_{k+1}-x_k). \end{aligned} \] The recurrence implies \(t_{k+1}^2-t_{k+1}=t_k^2\) and \(t_k\ge(k+2)/2\).

One-step model inequality. If \(f\) is convex and \(L\)-smooth and \(p_L(y)=y-L^{-1}\nabla f(y)\), then for every \(x\), \[ f(p_L(y))-f(x) \le\frac L2\bigl(\|x-y\|^2-\|x-p_L(y)\|^2\bigr). \]

Proof. This is the three-point inequality for a gradient step, with the base point renamed \(y\). It follows by subtracting the convex lower model of \(f(x)\) from the smooth quadratic upper model of \(f(p_L(y))\), then substituting \(\nabla f(y)=L(y-p_L(y))\).

The accelerated potential

Accelerated \(1/k^2\) rate. If \(f\) is convex and \(L\)-smooth and \(x^\star\) is a minimizer, then the preceding iteration satisfies \[ f(x_k)-f^\star \le\frac{2L\|x_0-x^\star\|^2}{(k+1)^2} \qquad(k\ge1). \]

Proof. Define \(E_k=f(x_k)-f^\star\) and \[ z_k=x_k+(t_k-1)(x_k-x_{k-1}) \quad(k\ge1),\qquad z_0=x_0. \] Apply the one-step model inequality at \(y_k\), first with \(x=x_k\) and then with \(x=x^\star\). Multiply the second inequality by \(t_{k+1}-1\), add the first, and use convexity to combine the two comparison points. The identities defining \(y_k,t_k,z_k\) reduce the result to \[ t_{k+1}^2E_{k+1} +\frac L2\|z_{k+1}-x^\star\|^2 \le t_k^2E_k+\frac L2\|z_k-x^\star\|^2. \] Thus the potential telescopes. At \(k=0\), a gradient step and the three-point inequality bound the initial potential by \(\frac L2\|x_0-x^\star\|^2\). Since \(t_k\ge(k+2)/2\), division by \(t_k^2\) gives the claimed rate after reindexing.

The potential explains the extrapolation coefficient: it is precisely what makes the cross terms become \(\|z_{k+1}-x^\star\|^2\). Arbitrary momentum does not preserve this cancellation.

Acceleration with strong convexity

Square-root condition-number rate. Suppose \(f\) is \(\mu\)-strongly convex and \(L\)-smooth. With \[ \beta=\frac{\sqrt L-\sqrt\mu}{\sqrt L+\sqrt\mu},\qquad y_k=x_k+\beta(x_k-x_{k-1}),\qquad x_{k+1}=y_k-\frac1L\nabla f(y_k), \] an accelerated Lyapunov function decreases geometrically at rate \(1-\sqrt{\mu/L}\). Consequently \[ f(x_k)-f^\star \le C\left(1-\frac1{\sqrt\kappa}\right)^k, \qquad \kappa=L/\mu, \] where \(C\) depends on the initial point and momentum state.

Proof outline. Apply the smoothness upper model at the extrapolated point and the strong-convexity lower model at \(x^\star\). With \(q=\sqrt{\mu/L}\), introduce the shifted state \(z_k=x_k+q^{-1}(x_k-x_{k-1})\). Direct substitution of \(\beta=(1-q)/(1+q)\) cancels the mixed terms and gives a Lyapunov recursion of the form \[ f(x_{k+1})-f^\star+\frac\mu2\|z_{k+1}-x^\star\|^2 \le(1-q) \left[f(x_k)-f^\star+\frac\mu2\|z_k-x^\star\|^2\right]. \] Iteration yields the displayed rate. The square root enters because the momentum coefficient is chosen to equalize contraction over the extreme curvatures \(\mu\) and \(L\).

Ordinary gradient descent needs order \(\kappa\log(1/\varepsilon)\) iterations for relative accuracy \(\varepsilon\); acceleration reduces this to \(\sqrt\kappa\log(1/\varepsilon)\). This dependence is optimal among first-order black-box methods for smooth strongly convex functions.

Oscillation, restart, and noise

Quadratic modes. On \(f(x)=\frac12x^\top Qx\), every eigencomponent follows a second-order scalar recurrence. Tuning momentum to the spectral interval \([\mu,L]\) damps all modes much faster than a first-order recurrence, but an underestimated \(L\) can make high-curvature modes unstable.

In practice, backtracking estimates \(L\), and adaptive restart resets the momentum when the objective rises or the extrapolation points in an unproductive direction. Persistent stochastic-gradient noise can be amplified by momentum; acceleration of deterministic optimization error does not automatically accelerate statistical accuracy.
