An equality constraint restricts motion to a lower-dimensional surface. At a regular constrained minimum, the objective gradient must be normal to that surface. Lagrange multipliers are the coordinates of this normal vector in the basis supplied by the constraint gradients.

Regular level sets and feasible velocities

Consider \[ \min_{x\in\mathbb R^d} f(x) \quad\text{subject to}\quad h(x)=0, \] where \(h=(h_1,\ldots,h_m):\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^m\) and \(m\le d\). Assume \(f\) and \(h\) are continuously differentiable near a feasible point \(x^\star\).

Tangent space of a regular level set. If the Jacobian \(Dh(x^\star)\) has row rank \(m\), then \(M=\{x:h(x)=0\}\) is locally a \(C^1\) manifold of dimension \(d-m\), and \[ T_M(x^\star)=\ker Dh(x^\star). \]

Proof. The implicit function theorem gives local coordinates in which \(M\) is the graph of a \(C^1\) function, hence a \(C^1\) manifold of dimension \(d-m\). If \(\gamma:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to M\) is differentiable with \(\gamma(0)=x^\star\), differentiating \(h(\gamma(t))=0\) gives \(Dh(x^\star)\gamma'(0)=0\). Conversely, the graph coordinates construct a curve in \(M\) with any prescribed velocity in the kernel.

The multiplier equation

Definition. The Lagrangian is \[ \mathcal L(x,\lambda)=f(x)+\lambda^\top h(x), \qquad \lambda\in\mathbb R^m. \]

Lagrange multiplier theorem. If \(x^\star\) is a local constrained minimizer and \(\operatorname{rank}Dh(x^\star)=m\), then there is a unique \(\lambda^\star\in\mathbb R^m\) such that \[ h(x^\star)=0,\qquad \nabla f(x^\star)+Dh(x^\star)^\top\lambda^\star=0. \]

Proof. For every \(v\in T_M(x^\star)\), choose a feasible curve with velocity \(v\). Local minimality of \(f(\gamma(t))\) gives \(\langle\nabla f(x^\star),v\rangle=0\). Hence \(\nabla f(x^\star)\) lies in \[ (\ker Dh(x^\star))^\perp =\operatorname{range}Dh(x^\star)^\top. \] Thus \(-\nabla f(x^\star)=Dh(x^\star)^\top\lambda^\star\) for some multiplier. Full row rank makes \(Dh(x^\star)^\top\) injective, so the multiplier is unique.

Without the rank condition, a multiplier may fail to exist or may not be unique. For minimizing \(f(x)=x\) subject to \(h(x)=x^2=0\), the unique feasible point is a global minimizer, but \(h'(0)=0\) and the equation \(1+\lambda h'(0)=0\) has no solution.

Curvature along the constraint surface

Second-order necessary condition. Suppose \(f,h_1,\ldots,h_m\) are \(C^2\), the rank condition holds, and \((x^\star,\lambda^\star)\) satisfies the multiplier equation at a local constrained minimum. Then \[ v^\top\nabla_{xx}^2\mathcal L(x^\star,\lambda^\star)v\ge0 \qquad\text{for every }v\in\ker Dh(x^\star). \] If the inequality is strict for every nonzero tangent vector, then \(x^\star\) is a strict constrained local minimizer.

Proof. Let \(\gamma(t)\) be a feasible \(C^2\) curve with \(\gamma(0)=x^\star\) and \(\gamma'(0)=v\). Since \(h(\gamma(t))=0\), \(f(\gamma(t))=\mathcal L(\gamma(t),\lambda^\star)\). Differentiating twice and using \(\nabla_x\mathcal L(x^\star,\lambda^\star)=0\) gives \[ \frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}t^2} f(\gamma(t))\bigg|_{t=0} =v^\top\nabla_{xx}^2\mathcal L(x^\star,\lambda^\star)v. \] A one-dimensional local minimum has nonnegative second derivative. The strict condition, combined with a local parametrization of the level set and Taylor's theorem, gives quadratic increase along all feasible directions.

Rayleigh quotients as a constrained eigenvalue problem

Example. Let \(A=A^\top\). To minimize \(x^\top Ax\) subject to \(x^\top x=1\), use \[ \mathcal L(x,\lambda)=x^\top Ax-\lambda(x^\top x-1). \] Stationarity gives \(Ax=\lambda x\). At an eigenvector \(q_i\), tangent vectors satisfy \(q_i^\top v=0\), and \[ v^\top\nabla^2_{xx}\mathcal L(q_i,\lambda_i)v =2v^\top(A-\lambda_iI)v. \] This is nonnegative for every tangent \(v\) precisely at an eigenvector associated with the smallest eigenvalue. Thus the multiplier is the optimal value and the constrained curvature recovers the spectral ordering.

Multipliers therefore carry both geometry and sensitivity. In a perturbed problem \(h(x)=u\), the multiplier is, under regularity, the negative derivative of the value function with respect to \(u\).
