Coordinate descent replaces one expensive full-space step by a cheap one-dimensional or block update. It is effective when partial derivatives are cheap, the data matrix is sparse, or a separable penalty has a closed-form coordinate minimizer.

Coordinate-wise smoothness

Definition. A differentiable function has coordinate Lipschitz constants \(L_i>0\) if \[ |\partial_i f(x+te_i)-\partial_i f(x)|\le L_i|t| \qquad(x\in\mathbb R^d,t\in\mathbb R). \] Integration along the coordinate line gives \[ f(x+te_i)\le f(x)+t\partial_i f(x)+\frac{L_i}{2}t^2. \]

Minimizing the upper model in \(t\) gives the coordinate-gradient update \[ x^+=x-\frac{\partial_i f(x)}{L_i}e_i \] and the decrease \[ f(x^+)\le f(x)-\frac{(\partial_i f(x))^2}{2L_i}. \]

Randomized coordinate descent

Expected convergence. Let \(f\) be convex, differentiable, and coordinate-wise smooth. Put \(S=\sum_{i=1}^dL_i\), choose coordinate \(i\) with probability \(p_i=L_i/S\), and take the coordinate-gradient step above. If the initial sublevel set has radius \[ R=\sup\{\|x-x^\star\|:f(x)\le f(x_0)\}<\infty, \] then \[ \mathbb E[f(x_k)-f^\star]\le\frac{2SR^2}{k+4}. \] If \(f\) is \(\mu\)-strongly convex, then \[ \mathbb E[f(x_k)-f^\star] \le\left(1-\frac\mu S\right)^k(f(x_0)-f^\star). \]

Proof. Conditional on \(x\), the coordinate decrease and the sampling probabilities give \[ \mathbb E[f(x^+)\mid x] \le f(x)-\frac1{2S}\|\nabla f(x)\|^2. \] Convexity implies \(f(x)-f^\star\le\langle\nabla f(x),x-x^\star\rangle \le R\|\nabla f(x)\|\). Hence, writing \(\Delta_k=\mathbb E[f(x_k)-f^\star]\), \[ \Delta_{k+1}\le\Delta_k-\frac{\Delta_k^2}{2SR^2}, \] where Jensen's inequality handles the random gap. Taking reciprocals and telescoping gives the \(1/k\) bound. Under strong convexity, \(\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\ge2\mu(f(x)-f^\star)\), so the same one-step estimate directly yields the geometric recursion.

Exact block minimization

For a partition \(x=(x_{B_1},\ldots,x_{B_m})\), block coordinate descent replaces one block by \[ x_{B_i}^+\in \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{u} f(x_{B_1},\ldots,u,\ldots,x_{B_m}). \] For continuously differentiable convex \(f\), cyclic exact block minimization produces a decreasing objective. If the level set is compact and each block minimizer is unique, every cluster point is coordinatewise stationary, hence has zero full gradient and is globally optimal.

Uniqueness or a suitable selection rule matters. With nonsmooth, nonseparable coupling, coordinatewise optimality may fail to imply global stationarity.

Soft thresholding in lasso regression

Example. For \[ F(\beta)=\frac1{2n}\|y-X\beta\|^2+\lambda\|\beta\|_1, \] hold all coordinates except \(j\) fixed and let \(r_j=y-\sum_{\ell\ne j}X_\ell\beta_\ell\). The \(j\)-th subproblem is \[ \min_b\left\{ \frac{\|X_j\|^2}{2n}b^2 -\frac{X_j^\top r_j}{n}b+\lambda|b| \right\}. \] Its optimality condition gives \[ \beta_j^+ =\frac{n}{\|X_j\|^2} S_\lambda\!\left(\frac{X_j^\top r_j}{n}\right), \qquad S_\lambda(t)=\operatorname{sign}(t)(|t|-\lambda)_+. \] Maintaining the residual makes each update proportional to the number of nonzero entries in \(X_j\), which explains the method's efficiency for sparse designs.
