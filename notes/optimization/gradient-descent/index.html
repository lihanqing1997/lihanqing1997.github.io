Gradient descent repeatedly minimizes the smoothness-based quadratic upper model of the objective. Its analysis is a model case for optimization proofs: derive one-step descent, identify a quantity that telescopes, and convert that quantity into objective or parameter error.

The Euclidean steepest-descent direction

Steepest local decrease. If \(\nabla f(x)\ne0\), then \[ \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\|v\|\le1} \langle\nabla f(x),v\rangle =-\frac{\nabla f(x)}{\|\nabla f(x)\|}. \] Equivalently, minimizing \(\langle\nabla f(x),s\rangle+\frac1{2\eta}\|s\|^2\) gives \(s=-\eta\nabla f(x)\).

Proof. Cauchy-Schwarz gives \(\langle\nabla f(x),v\rangle\ge-\|\nabla f(x)\|\|v\|\), and equality holds at the displayed unit vector. The quadratic model has gradient \(\nabla f(x)+s/\eta\), whose unique zero is \(s=-\eta\nabla f(x)\).

Definition. Gradient descent with steps \(\eta_k>0\) is \[ x_{k+1}=x_k-\eta_k\nabla f(x_k). \]

Descent and nonconvex stationarity

Descent estimate. If \(f\) is \(L\)-smooth and \(0<\eta\le1/L\), then \[ f(x_{k+1}) \le f(x_k)-\eta\left(1-\frac{L\eta}{2}\right) \|\nabla f(x_k)\|^2 \le f(x_k)-\frac\eta2\|\nabla f(x_k)\|^2. \] If \(f\) is bounded below, then \[ \min_{0\le j<k}\|\nabla f(x_j)\|^2 \le\frac{2(f(x_0)-\inf f)}{\eta k}. \]

Proof. Apply the descent lemma with \(y=x-\eta\nabla f(x)\): \[ f(y)\le f(x)-\eta\|\nabla f(x)\|^2 +\frac{L\eta^2}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^2. \] Summing the resulting decrease from \(j=0\) to \(k-1\) gives \[ \frac\eta2\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|\nabla f(x_j)\|^2 \le f(x_0)-f(x_k)\le f(x_0)-\inf f. \] The smallest summand is no larger than the average.

This theorem guarantees approach to first-order stationarity, not to a global minimum. For a nonconvex objective, the limit can be a local minimum or, under exceptional initialization, a saddle point.

The \(1/k\) rate for smooth convex objectives

Three-point inequality. If \(f\) is convex and \(L\)-smooth and \(x^+=x-L^{-1}\nabla f(x)\), then for every \(z\), \[ f(x^+)-f(z) \le\frac L2\bigl(\|x-z\|^2-\|x^+-z\|^2\bigr). \]

Proof. Smoothness and convexity give, respectively, \[ f(x^+)\le f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),x^+-x\rangle +\frac L2\|x^+-x\|^2, \] \[ f(z)\ge f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),z-x\rangle. \] Subtract and use \(\nabla f(x)=L(x-x^+)\). Expanding \(2\langle x-x^+,x^+-z\rangle\) as a difference of three squared norms yields the claim.

Convex convergence rate. If \(x^\star\) minimizes a convex \(L\)-smooth function and gradient descent uses \(\eta=1/L\), then for \(k\ge1\), \[ f(x_k)-f^\star \le\frac{L\|x_0-x^\star\|^2}{2k}. \]

Proof. The three-point inequality with \(z=x^\star\) gives \[ f(x_{j+1})-f^\star \le\frac L2(\|x_j-x^\star\|^2-\|x_{j+1}-x^\star\|^2). \] Sum over \(j=0,\ldots,k-1\). Since the objective values decrease, \(k(f(x_k)-f^\star)\) is at most the sum of the \(k\) gaps. The squared distances telescope, leaving the displayed bound.

Linear convergence under strong convexity

Strongly convex rate. If \(f\) is \(\mu\)-strongly convex and \(L\)-smooth, then the step \(1/L\) satisfies \[ f(x_k)-f^\star \le\left(1-\frac\mu L\right)^k (f(x_0)-f^\star). \]

Proof. Strong convexity with \(y=x^\star\), followed by minimizing the resulting quadratic in \(\|x-x^\star\|\), gives the Polyak-?ojasiewicz inequality \[ \|\nabla f(x)\|^2\ge2\mu(f(x)-f^\star). \] The descent estimate at step \(1/L\) therefore yields \[ f(x_{k+1})-f^\star \le f(x_k)-f^\star-\frac1{2L}\|\nabla f(x_k)\|^2 \le\left(1-\frac\mu L\right)(f(x_k)-f^\star). \] Iterate the recursion.

Spectral behavior on a quadratic. For \(f(x)=\frac12x^\top Qx\), \(Q\succ0\), the error obeys \(e_{k+1}=(I-\eta Q)e_k\). In an eigenbasis, \(e_{k,i}=(1-\eta\lambda_i)^ke_{0,i}\). Thus convergence occurs exactly when \(0<\eta<2/\lambda_{\max}(Q)\), and the best constant step is \(2/(\lambda_{\min}+\lambda_{\max})\). Elongated curvature creates slow, alternating motion in the high-curvature directions.

The phrase “steepest descent” is metric-dependent. Changing the inner product changes the gradient vector representing the same differential. Mirror descent, natural gradient, and Newton's method exploit this fact by replacing Euclidean geometry with one adapted to the problem.
