Gauss-Newton and Fisher scoring are structured Newton methods. They discard a curvature term whose expectation or residual-weighted contribution is often small, leaving a positive-semidefinite matrix with a direct statistical interpretation.

Nonlinear least squares and residual curvature

Let \(r=(r_1,\ldots,r_m):\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^m\) and \[ F(\theta)=\frac12\|r(\theta)\|^2. \] Writing \(J(\theta)=Dr(\theta)\), differentiation gives \[ \nabla F(\theta)=J(\theta)^\top r(\theta), \qquad \nabla^2F(\theta) =J(\theta)^\top J(\theta) +\sum_{i=1}^m r_i(\theta)\nabla^2r_i(\theta). \]

Definition. The Gauss-Newton direction solves \[ J_k^\top J_kp_k=-J_k^\top r_k. \] Equivalently, it minimizes the linearized residual model \[ \min_p\frac12\|r_k+J_kp\|^2. \]

The omitted term is weighted by the residuals. Near an exact fit \(r(\theta^\star)=0\), Gauss-Newton and Newton have the same limiting Hessian. If \(J(\theta^\star)\) has full column rank, the local model is strictly convex.

Local convergence at a zero-residual solution. Suppose \(r\) is twice continuously differentiable, \(r(\theta^\star)=0\), and \(J(\theta^\star)\) has full column rank. Then undamped Gauss-Newton iterates started sufficiently near \(\theta^\star\) converge quadratically.

Proof. Taylor expansion gives \(r(\theta)=J_\star e+O(\|e\|^2)\) and \(J(\theta)=J_\star+O(\|e\|)\), where \(e=\theta-\theta^\star\). Hence \[ J(\theta)^\top r(\theta) =J_\star^\top J_\star e+O(\|e\|^2), \quad J(\theta)^\top J(\theta) =J_\star^\top J_\star+O(\|e\|). \] Since \(J_\star^\top J_\star\) is invertible, substituting into the Gauss-Newton update cancels \(e\) and leaves \(e^+=O(\|e\|^2)\).

At a nonzero-residual minimizer, the omitted Hessian term need not vanish; the typical local rate is linear unless that term is small relative to \(J^\top J\).

Observed curvature and Fisher information

For log-likelihood \(\ell(\theta)\), write the score and information as \[ S(\theta)=\nabla\ell(\theta),\qquad J_{\mathrm{obs}}(\theta)=-\nabla^2\ell(\theta),\qquad I(\theta)=\mathbb E_\theta[J_{\mathrm{obs}}(\theta)]. \] Newton maximization uses \(J_{\mathrm{obs}}^{-1}S\), whereas Fisher scoring uses \[ \theta_{k+1}=\theta_k+I(\theta_k)^{-1}S(\theta_k). \]

Under the regularity conditions that permit differentiating under the integral, \[ I(\theta) =\mathbb E_\theta[S_\theta(X)S_\theta(X)^\top] =-\mathbb E_\theta[\nabla^2\ell_\theta(X)]. \] Expected information is positive semidefinite and averages away sample-specific curvature fluctuations. The identities are developed from the statistical side in likelihood, score, and information.

IRWLS for generalized linear models

Weighted least-squares form. Let independent responses satisfy \(\mathbb E(Y_i)=\mu_i\), \(\operatorname{Var}(Y_i)=\phi V(\mu_i)\), and \(g(\mu_i)=\eta_i=x_i^\top\beta\). Define \[ z_i=\eta_i+(Y_i-\mu_i)\frac{\mathrm{d}\eta_i}{\mathrm{d}\mu_i}, \qquad w_i=\frac1{\phi V(\mu_i)} \left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_i}{\mathrm{d}\eta_i}\right)^2. \] A Fisher-scoring step solves \[ (X^\top W X)\beta^+=X^\top Wz. \]

Proof. The chain rule expresses the score as \[ S(\beta) =X^\top D(Y-\mu),\qquad D_{ii}=\frac1{\phi V(\mu_i)} \frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_i}{\mathrm{d}\eta_i}. \] Expected negative curvature is \(X^\top WX\). Fisher scoring therefore gives \(\beta^+=\beta+(X^\top WX)^{-1}X^\top D(Y-\mu)\). Since \(W(z-X\beta)=D(Y-\mu)\), rearrangement yields the weighted least-squares normal equations.

Logistic regression. With \(\mu_i=(1+e^{-x_i^\top\beta})^{-1}\), \(w_i=\mu_i(1-\mu_i)\) and \[ z_i=x_i^\top\beta+ \frac{Y_i-\mu_i}{\mu_i(1-\mu_i)}. \] IRWLS repeatedly fits \(z\) to \(X\) with weights \(w_i\). Near separation, some weights approach zero and the unregularized coefficients can diverge, so numerical convergence alone does not guarantee existence of a finite maximum-likelihood estimate.
