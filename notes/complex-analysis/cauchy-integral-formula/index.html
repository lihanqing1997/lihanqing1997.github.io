Derivative formula

Corollary. Under the same hypotheses, \[ f^{(n)}(z)=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta-a|=r}\frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{n+1}}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta. \] Thus holomorphic functions are automatically infinitely complex differentiable.

Proof mechanism

Proof. Subtract \(f(z)\) inside the numerator: \((f(\zeta)-f(z))/(\zeta-z)\) is holomorphic near \(z\), so its integral vanishes by Cauchy's theorem. The remaining integral is \(f(z)\int(\zeta-z)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta=2\pi i f(z)\).

Mean value property

Corollary. For a circle centered at \(a\), Cauchy's formula implies \(f(a)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}f(a+re^{it})\,\mathrm{d}t\). Holomorphic functions equal their circular averages.

Analyticity follows

Theorem. Expanding \((\zeta-z)^{-1}\) as a geometric series inside the contour gives a convergent power series for \(f\). Hence every holomorphic function is analytic.

Boundary dependence

Remark. The formula is local: the contour must lie in the domain. Singularities between the point and the contour change the representation by adding residue contributions.

Interior values from boundary data

Theorem (Cauchy integral formula). Let \(\Gamma\) be a positively oriented cycle null-homologous in \(\Omega\), and let \(f\) be holomorphic on \(\Omega\). For \(z\in\Omega\setminus\Gamma\), \[ \frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta =\operatorname{Ind}(\Gamma,z)f(z). \]

Proof. Define \[ g(\zeta)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{f(\zeta)-f(z)}{\zeta-z},&\zeta\ne z,\\ f'(z),&\zeta=z. \end{cases} \] The removable value makes \(g\) holomorphic. Cauchy's theorem gives \(\int_\Gamma g\,\mathrm{d}\zeta=0\), hence \[ \int_\Gamma\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta =f(z)\int_\Gamma\frac{\mathrm{d}\zeta}{\zeta-z} =2\pi i\,\operatorname{Ind}(\Gamma,z)f(z). \]

Mean value and boundary dependence

Parametrizing a circle gives \[ f(z_0)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} f(z_0+re^{it})\,\mathrm{d}t. \] Taking real parts yields the harmonic mean-value property. More broadly, the formula says a holomorphic function inside a contour is determined by its boundary values, a nonlocal rigidity that has no analogue for arbitrary smooth functions.
