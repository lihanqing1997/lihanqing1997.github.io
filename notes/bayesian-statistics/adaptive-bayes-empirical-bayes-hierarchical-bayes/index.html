Adaptive Bayes tries to learn unknown regularity or sparsity while still quantifying uncertainty. Empirical Bayes and hierarchical Bayes differ in whether the hyperparameter is estimated or integrated out.

Hierarchical adaptation

A hyperprior can spread mass over models with different effective complexity.

Definition. Let \(\lambda\) be a regularity, scale, bandwidth, or sparsity hyperparameter. A hierarchical prior has \[ \lambda\sim H,\qquad \theta\mid\lambda\sim\Pi_\lambda. \]

Proposition. If \(H\) assigns at least \(\exp(-C n\varepsilon_{n,\lambda}^2)\) mass near a near-optimal \(\lambda\), then the hierarchical prior can inherit the contraction rate associated with that \(\lambda\).

Proof. The prior mass condition in the general contraction theorem can be satisfied by integrating over a hyperparameter neighborhood. The likelihood tests are unchanged; the hyperprior contributes only to the denominator mass budget.

Empirical Bayes

Empirical Bayes estimates hyperparameters from data and then plugs them into the prior.

Definition. An empirical Bayes posterior has the form \[ \Pi_{\hat\lambda(X)}(\cdot\mid X), \] where \(\hat\lambda(X)\) is often chosen by marginal likelihood or a moment estimate.

Remark. Because \(\hat\lambda\) is data-dependent, frequentist analysis must control the posterior uniformly over the likely range of \(\hat\lambda\).

Marginal likelihood selection

Marginal likelihood balances fit and prior complexity.

Proposition. In many Gaussian sequence problems, marginal likelihood selects a scale or truncation level by trading off fit to observed coefficients with the prior penalty for model size.

Proof. The marginal likelihood factors over coefficients. Terms for included coefficients reward explaining signal; terms for excessive complexity pay a normalizing penalty. The maximizer balances these contributions.

Adaptive credible sets

Adaptive contraction does not automatically give adaptive honest coverage.

Remark. Credible sets may need inflation or self-similarity restrictions. Otherwise, functions with deceptive coefficient patterns can make the posterior radius too small for honest frequentist coverage.

Oracle hyperparameters

Definition. Let \(\{\Pi_\lambda:\lambda\in\Lambda\}\) be a family of priors. An oracle hyperparameter \(\lambda_n^\star\) minimizes a concentration criterion such as \[ \varepsilon_n(\lambda) =\inf\left\{ \varepsilon: -\log\Pi_\lambda \big(B_{\mathrm{KL}}(f_0,\varepsilon)\big) \le n\varepsilon^2 \right\}. \] Adaptation asks whether a data-driven or hierarchical posterior contracts near \(\inf_\lambda\varepsilon_n(\lambda)\) without knowing the truth's regularity.

Hierarchical Bayes mixture

Definition. Put a hyperprior \(\widetilde\pi(\mathrm{d}\lambda)\) and define \[ \Pi(\mathrm{d}f) =\int\Pi_\lambda(\mathrm{d}f) \widetilde\pi(\mathrm{d}\lambda). \] The hyperposterior is \[ \widetilde\pi(\mathrm{d}\lambda\mid X) \propto m_\lambda(X) \widetilde\pi(\mathrm{d}\lambda), \] where \[ m_\lambda(X)=\int p_f(X)\Pi_\lambda(\mathrm{d}f). \]

Theorem (hierarchical adaptation template). Suppose a set \[ \Lambda_n^\star =\{\lambda: \varepsilon_n(\lambda) \le M\varepsilon_{n,\star}\} \] has hyperprior mass at least \[ e^{-Cn\varepsilon_{n,\star}^2}, \] and the union of relevant \(\lambda\)-sieves has entropy and prior-tail bounds at scale \(\varepsilon_{n,\star}\). Then the hierarchical posterior contracts at rate \(\varepsilon_{n,\star}\), up to constants and any entropy cost of searching over \(\lambda\).

Proof outline. The mixture prior assigns enough KL mass by integrating over \(\Lambda_n^\star\). Cover the union sieve jointly in \(f\) and hyperparameter; hyperparameter discretization adds its search entropy. Apply the general contraction theorem to the marginal mixture prior. Posterior concentration of \(\lambda\) itself is unnecessary if all retained hyperparameters yield near-oracle \(f\)-contraction.

Empirical Bayes marginal likelihood

Definition. Type-II maximum likelihood chooses \[ \widehat\lambda_n \in\arg\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda} m_\lambda(X^{(n)}) \] and reports \[ \Pi_{\widehat\lambda_n}(\cdot\mid X^{(n)}). \]

Proposition. If, with high probability, \(\widehat\lambda_n\) lies in a set \[ \Lambda_n^{\mathrm{good}} \] on which prior concentration, sieve entropy, testing, and remainder bounds hold uniformly at rate \(\varepsilon_n\), then the empirical Bayes posterior contracts at \(\varepsilon_n\).

Proof. The contraction proof for fixed \(\lambda\) can be made uniform over the good set. On the event \(\widehat\lambda_n\in\Lambda_n^{\mathrm{good}}\), substitute the random hyperparameter into that bound. The complementary event has vanishing probability.

The substantive work is proving that marginal likelihood avoids bad scales and establishing uniformity. Treating \(\widehat\lambda_n\) as fixed without this step understates uncertainty and can invalidate rate claims.

Gaussian sequence empirical Bayes

Example. Let \[ Y_j=\theta_{0j}+n^{-1/2}\varepsilon_j, \qquad \theta_j\mid\alpha \sim N(0,j^{-1-2\alpha}). \] The marginal law is \[ Y_j\mid\alpha \sim N\left( 0,\, j^{-1-2\alpha}+n^{-1} \right), \] so \[ \ell_n(\alpha) =-\frac12\sum_j \left[ \log(j^{-1-2\alpha}+n^{-1}) +\frac{Y_j^2} {j^{-1-2\alpha}+n^{-1}} \right]+C. \] Maximizing this criterion estimates a regularity index. Its behavior depends on how signal energy is distributed across frequencies, not merely membership in a Sobolev ball.

Self-similarity and polished tails

Definition. A schematic self-similarity condition requires constants \(c,C>0\) and \(\beta>0\) such that for all large \(J\), \[ cJ^{-2\beta} \le\sum_{j=J}^{2J}\theta_{0j}^2 \le CJ^{-2\beta}. \] A polished-tail condition instead controls the entire tail by a nearby block: \[ \sum_{j>N}\theta_{0j}^2 \le L\sum_{j=N}^{\rho N}\theta_{0j}^2. \]

These conditions prevent long stretches of nearly zero coefficients followed by hidden high-frequency energy. Such deceptive signals can make a hyperparameter selector infer excessive smoothness, producing a posterior radius smaller than regularization bias.

Adaptive credible balls

Theorem (schematic coverage result). In conjugate Gaussian sequence models, under self-similarity or polished tails, an empirical or hierarchical Bayes credible ball \[ C_n =\{f:\|f-\widehat f\|_2\le Lr_{n,\alpha}\}, \] where \(r_{n,\alpha}\) is the posterior \(1-\alpha\) radius and \(L>1\) is a fixed inflation constant, can satisfy \[ \inf_{f_0\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{ss}}} P_{f_0}(f_0\in C_n)\to1 \] while its radius adapts at the minimax order over smoothness classes in \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{ss}}\).

Proof outline. Decompose \[ \|f_0-\widehat f\| \le \|\widehat f-E_{f_0}\widehat f\| +\|E_{f_0}\widehat f-f_0\|. \] The posterior radius and stochastic term are of the same oracle order. Self-similarity bounds bias by a constant multiple of that radius. Inflation absorbs both terms uniformly. Without the lower energy condition, bias can dominate while the radius remains small.

Empirical versus hierarchical uncertainty

Hierarchical Bayes averages over hyperparameter uncertainty; empirical Bayes plugs in one estimate. In large regular problems their first-order behavior may agree, but near weak identification or multimodal hyperposteriors the difference can be substantial. A hyperposterior credible interval is not automatically calibrated merely because it is wider; the same bias and topology issues remain.
