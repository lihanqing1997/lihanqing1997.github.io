Conjugacy is not merely a computational trick. It identifies families of priors that are stable under likelihood multiplication and therefore makes the update algebra explicit.

Conjugate priors in exponential families

The natural exponential-family form makes the source of conjugacy transparent.

Definition. Suppose \[ p_\eta(x)=\exp\{\eta^\top T(x)-A(\eta)\}h(x). \] A conjugate prior has density \[ \pi_{\alpha,\beta}(\eta)\propto \exp\{\eta^\top\alpha-\beta A(\eta)\} \] on the natural parameter space.

Proposition. After observing \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\), the posterior is in the same family with \[ \alpha_n=\alpha+\sum_{i=1}^n T(x_i),\qquad \beta_n=\beta+n. \]

Proof. Multiply the prior density by the likelihood. The exponent becomes \[ \eta^\top\alpha-\beta A(\eta)+\eta^\top\sum_iT(x_i)-nA(\eta), \] which is the same conjugate form with the updated hyperparameters.

Beta-binomial and gamma-Poisson calculations

The simplest conjugate pairs should be remembered as algebraic templates.

Example. If \(X\mid p\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)\) and \(p\sim\operatorname{Beta}(a,b)\), then \[ p\mid X=x\sim\operatorname{Beta}(a+x,b+n-x). \] If \(X_i\mid\lambda\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\) and \(\lambda\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(a,b)\) with rate \(b\), then \[ \lambda\mid X^{(n)}\sim\operatorname{Gamma}\left(a+\sum_iX_i,b+n\right). \]

Normal-normal shrinkage

Gaussian conjugacy shows the posterior mean as a precision-weighted average of prior information and data information.

Proposition. If \(X_i\mid\theta\sim N(\theta,\sigma^2)\) and \(\theta\sim N(m_0,\tau_0^2)\), then \[ \theta\mid X^{(n)}\sim N(m_n,\tau_n^2), \quad \tau_n^{-2}=\tau_0^{-2}+n\sigma^{-2}, \quad m_n=\tau_n^2(\tau_0^{-2}m_0+n\sigma^{-2}\bar X). \]

Proof. Complete the square in \[ -\frac{(\theta-m_0)^2}{2\tau_0^2}-\frac{n(\bar X-\theta)^2}{2\sigma^2}. \] The coefficient of \(\theta^2\) gives \(\tau_n^{-2}\), and the coefficient of \(\theta\) gives \(\tau_n^{-2}m_n\).

Computation beyond conjugacy

Conjugacy gives exact formulas, but the same posterior is well-defined without conjugacy. Computation then approximates integrals rather than changing the target distribution.

Remark. Laplace approximation, Monte Carlo, Markov chain Monte Carlo, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, and variational Bayes should all be read as different approximations to posterior expectations under the same Bayes formula.

Conjugate exponential-family theorem

Theorem. Suppose \[ p_\eta(x)=h(x) \exp\{\eta^\top T(x)-A(\eta)\} \] is a natural exponential family. The prior \[ \pi(\eta\mid\xi,\nu) \propto \exp\{\eta^\top\xi-\nu A(\eta)\} \] is conjugate. Given \(x_{1:n}\), \[ \eta\mid x_{1:n} \sim\pi\left( \eta\ \middle|\ \xi+\sum_{i=1}^nT(x_i),\,\nu+n \right). \]

Proof. Multiplying likelihood and prior kernels gives \[ \exp\left[ \eta^\top\left\{\xi+\sum_iT(x_i)\right\} -(\nu+n)A(\eta) \right], \] which is the same prior family with updated hyperparameters.

The pair \((\xi,\nu)\) behaves like prior sufficient statistics and prior sample size, but this interpretation is exact only when \(\xi/\nu\) lies in the mean-parameter space and the prior is proper.

Gamma-Poisson prediction

Example. Let \[ X_i\mid\lambda\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim} \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda), \qquad \lambda\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(a,b) \] in shape-rate form. Then \[ \lambda\mid x \sim\operatorname{Gamma} \left(a+\sum_ix_i,b+n\right). \] For one future count, \[ P(\widetilde X=k\mid x) =\frac{\Gamma(a^\star+k)} {\Gamma(a^\star)k!} \left(\frac{b^\star}{b^\star+1}\right)^{a^\star} \left(\frac1{b^\star+1}\right)^k, \] where \(a^\star=a+\sum_ix_i\) and \(b^\star=b+n\). This is negative binomial, with \[ E(\widetilde X\mid x)=\frac{a^\star}{b^\star}, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(\widetilde X\mid x) =\frac{a^\star}{b^\star} +\frac{a^\star}{(b^\star)^2}. \]

Normal model with unknown mean and variance

Theorem. Let \[ X_i\mid\mu,\sigma^2\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2), \] \[ \mu\mid\sigma^2\sim N(\mu_0,\sigma^2/\kappa_0), \qquad \sigma^2\sim\operatorname{InvGamma}(\alpha_0,\beta_0). \] Then the posterior has parameters \[ \kappa_n=\kappa_0+n,\qquad \mu_n=\frac{\kappa_0\mu_0+n\bar X}{\kappa_n}, \] \[ \alpha_n=\alpha_0+\frac n2, \] \[ \beta_n=\beta_0+\frac12\sum_i(X_i-\bar X)^2 +\frac{\kappa_0n}{2\kappa_n} (\bar X-\mu_0)^2. \] The marginal posterior of \(\mu\) is Student \(t_{2\alpha_n}\) with center \(\mu_n\) and scale squared \(\beta_n/(\alpha_n\kappa_n)\).

Proof. Complete the square: \[ \sum_i(X_i-\mu)^2 +\kappa_0(\mu-\mu_0)^2 =\sum_i(X_i-\bar X)^2 +\frac{\kappa_0n}{\kappa_n}(\bar X-\mu_0)^2 +\kappa_n(\mu-\mu_n)^2. \] The resulting kernel is normal in \(\mu\mid\sigma^2\) and inverse-gamma in \(\sigma^2\). Integrating \(\sigma^2\) produces the Student distribution.

Conditional conjugacy and Gibbs sampling

Proposition. Suppose a joint posterior has full conditional distributions \[ \pi(\theta_j\mid\theta_{-j},x). \] The systematic-scan Gibbs kernel that successively samples each full conditional leaves the joint posterior invariant.

Proof. Updating one coordinate from its full conditional leaves \(\pi\) invariant because \[ \int \pi(\mathrm{d}\theta) K_j(\theta,\mathrm{d}\theta') =\pi(\mathrm{d}\theta'). \] This follows by disintegrating \(\pi\) into \(\pi(\mathrm{d}\theta_j\mid\theta_{-j})\pi_{-j}(\mathrm{d}\theta_{-j})\). A composition of invariant kernels remains invariant.

Conjugate full conditionals can make a hierarchical posterior computationally convenient even when its marginal posterior has no closed form. Invariance alone does not guarantee rapid mixing; dependence between blocks may still make Gibbs updates move slowly.
