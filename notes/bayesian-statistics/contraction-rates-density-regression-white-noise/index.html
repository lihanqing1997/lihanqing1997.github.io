Contraction rates become concrete in density estimation, nonparametric regression, and Gaussian white noise. Each model has its own testing geometry but the same bias-complexity-prior-mass logic.

White-noise sequence model

The Gaussian sequence model makes the bias-variance calculation explicit.

Definition. In the sequence form of white noise, \[ Y_j=\theta_j+n^{-1/2}Z_j,\qquad Z_j\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(0,1). \]

Proposition. If \(\theta_0\) has Sobolev smoothness \(\beta\), the minimax \(L^2\)-rate is \(n^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}\).

Proof. Estimate the first \(J\) coefficients and ignore the rest. Variance is \(J/n\); squared bias is \(J^{-2\beta}\). Balancing \(J/n\asymp J^{-2\beta}\) gives \(J\asymp n^{1/(2\beta+1)}\) and squared error \(n^{-2\beta/(2\beta+1)}\).

Density estimation

For densities, Hellinger testing often drives the rate theorem.

Remark. Mixture priors and log-density priors require separate approximation estimates, entropy bounds, and prior small-ball estimates. Smoothness of the true density determines the approximation term.

Regression

Nonparametric regression rates resemble white-noise rates when the design is regular and errors are Gaussian.

Proposition. For fixed regular design and Gaussian errors, posterior contraction in empirical \(L^2\) norm follows from the same testing and entropy structure as Gaussian sequence models.

Proof. The likelihood ratio is Gaussian with squared empirical norm as its natural distance. Tests between regression functions are built from normal likelihood ratios, with errors controlled by \(\exp(-c n\|f-g\|_n^2)\).

Adaptive rates

Adaptation requires the prior to put enough mass near each smoothness class without knowing the smoothness in advance.

Remark. Hierarchical priors on truncation, bandwidth, or scale can adapt if their hyperprior mass matches the optimal bias-variance balance over a range of regularities.

Gaussian white-noise sequence model

Definition. The white-noise experiment \[ \mathrm{d}Y(t)=f_0(t)\,\mathrm{d}t +n^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}W(t) \] becomes, in an orthonormal basis, \[ Y_j=\theta_{0j}+n^{-1/2}\varepsilon_j, \qquad \varepsilon_j\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(0,1). \]

Theorem (minimax Sobolev rate). Over \[ \Theta_\beta(R) =\left\{\theta: \sum_jj^{2\beta}\theta_j^2\le R^2 \right\}, \] the minimax squared \(\ell^2\)-risk is of order \[ n^{-2\beta/(2\beta+1)}. \]

Upper-bound proof. Estimate the first \(J\) coefficients by \(Y_j\) and set the rest to zero. Then \[ E\|\widehat\theta-\theta_0\|_2^2 =\frac Jn+\sum_{j>J}\theta_{0j}^2 \le\frac Jn+R^2J^{-2\beta}. \] Choosing \[ J\asymp n^{1/(2\beta+1)} \] gives the rate. A matching Assouad or Fano construction on the first \(J\) coordinates gives the lower bound.

Conjugate Gaussian prior rate

Proposition. Assign \[ \theta_j\sim N(0,j^{-1-2\alpha}). \] The posterior mean has shrinkage \[ \widehat\theta_j =\frac{n}{n+j^{1+2\alpha}}Y_j, \] with effective cutoff \[ J_n\asymp n^{1/(1+2\alpha)}. \] If truth has Sobolev regularity \(\beta\), its squared error is governed by \[ J_n/n+J_n^{-2\beta}, \] up to shrinkage-transition terms. The optimal match is \(\alpha=\beta\), yielding \[ n^{-2\beta/(2\beta+1)}. \]

Proof. The posterior formula follows from Gaussian conjugacy. Below \(J_n\), likelihood precision dominates prior precision and variance contributes roughly \(J_n/n\). Above \(J_n\), strong shrinkage leaves truth-tail bias bounded by \(J_n^{-2\beta}\). Balance at \(\alpha=\beta\).

Fixed-design nonparametric regression

Definition. \[ Y_i=f_0(x_i)+\varepsilon_i, \qquad \varepsilon_i\sim N(0,\sigma^2). \] The empirical prediction norm is \[ \|f-f_0\|_n^2 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \{f(x_i)-f_0(x_i)\}^2. \]

Proposition. For Gaussian regression, the average KL divergence and variance between \(f_0\) and \(f\) are proportional to \[ \|f-f_0\|_n^2. \] Thus the general contraction theorem naturally yields prediction-norm contraction.

Proof. For one observation with equal variance, \[ K\{N(f_0(x_i),\sigma^2), N(f(x_i),\sigma^2)\} =\frac{\{f(x_i)-f_0(x_i)\}^2}{2\sigma^2}. \] Sum and divide by \(n\). The log likelihood ratio variance is likewise proportional to the squared mean difference.

Converting empirical prediction to \(L^2(P_X)\) or supremum norm requires design regularity and function-class control. Random-design regression directly induces the \(L^2(P_X)\) metric in expectation.

Density estimation rate calculation

Theorem (histogram heuristic made explicit). For a \(\beta\)-Hölder density on \([0,1]\), a regular histogram with \(J\) bins has approximation error \[ \|f_{0,J}-f_0\|_1\lesssim J^{-\beta} \] for \(0<\beta\le1\), while estimating \(J\) probabilities has stochastic Hellinger scale \[ \sqrt{J/n}. \] Balancing gives \[ J\asymp n^{1/(2\beta+1)}, \qquad \varepsilon_n\asymp n^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}. \]

Proof. Hölder continuity bounds within-bin variation by \(CJ^{-\beta}\), giving approximation error. A multinomial vector of dimension \(J\) has aggregate squared error order \(J/n\), and local Hellinger distance has the same scale under density bounds. Equate squared bias \(J^{-2\beta}\) and variance \(J/n\).

Classification and binary regression

For \[ Y\mid X=x\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}\{\eta(x)\}, \] KL divergence is locally equivalent to \[ \|\eta-\eta_0\|_{L^2(P_X)}^2 \] when \(\eta\) stays away from zero and one. A prior on a latent link \[ \eta(x)=\Psi\{f(x)\} \] inherits contraction from \(f\) if \(\Psi\) is Lipschitz on posterior-relevant ranges. Classification excess risk can be faster than regression-function error under a margin condition controlling \[ P_X(|\eta_0(X)-1/2|\le t). \]

Inverse problems

Definition. In a linear inverse problem, \[ Y_j=\kappa_j\theta_{0j}+n^{-1/2}\varepsilon_j, \qquad \kappa_j\asymp j^{-r}. \] The forward observation attenuates high-frequency coefficients.

Proposition. A truncated estimator has risk \[ \frac1n\sum_{j\le J}\kappa_j^{-2} +\sum_{j>J}\theta_{0j}^2 \asymp \frac{J^{2r+1}}n+J^{-2\beta}. \] Balancing gives \[ J\asymp n^{1/(2\beta+2r+1)}, \qquad \varepsilon_n \asymp n^{-\beta/(2\beta+2r+1)}. \]

Proof. Estimating \(\theta_j\) requires dividing noise by \(\kappa_j\), giving variance \(n^{-1}\kappa_j^{-2}\). Sum polynomial singular values and add Sobolev tail bias, then balance.

Logarithmic factors

Log factors arise from prior small-ball probabilities, random dimension penalties, tail truncation, adaptation over smoothness, or sup-norm testing. They are not always proof artifacts. A claimed minimax rate should specify whether it is exact, up to logarithms, pointwise in smoothness, or adaptive across a scale.
