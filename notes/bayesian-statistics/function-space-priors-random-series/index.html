Nonparametric Bayes places priors on functions or measures. Random series priors make this concrete by randomizing coefficients in a basis or frame.

Random series priors

A function-space prior is a probability law on an infinite-dimensional space.

Definition. Let \(\{\phi_j\}\) be basis functions and let \(\xi_j\) be random coefficients. A random series prior has the form \[ f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty} a_j\xi_j\phi_j \] whenever the series converges in the chosen function norm.

Proposition. If \(\sum_j |a_j|\|\phi_j\|<\infty\) almost surely after weighting by \(|\xi_j|\), then the series converges absolutely in the Banach space.

Proof. The Weierstrass criterion in a Banach space says that absolute summability of the norms implies convergence of the series, because the partial sums are Cauchy.

Support through approximation

Support is determined by which deterministic functions can be approximated by likely coefficient sequences.

Proposition. If every coefficient neighborhood around a target sequence has positive prior probability and the basis expansion of \(f_0\) is approximable by finite truncations, then \(f_0\) lies in the support of the prior.

Proof. Choose a finite truncation close to \(f_0\). Positive prior mass in a small neighborhood of the first finitely many coefficients and sufficiently small tail gives positive probability of a ball around \(f_0\).

Regularity and coefficient decay

Smoothness is encoded through coefficient decay.

Remark. Gaussian, Laplace, and spike-and-slab coefficient priors induce different regularity and sparsity behavior. The prior scale \(a_j\) controls how much mass is placed on smooth or rough functions.

Posterior contraction

Random series priors contract by balancing approximation error and stochastic estimation error.

Proposition. For a truncation level \(J\), a typical rate calculation balances bias from \(\sum_{j>J}a_j\xi_j\phi_j\) with variance of estimating \(J\) effective coefficients.

Proof. The posterior cannot contract faster than the prior can approximate the truth, and the likelihood cannot estimate more than the effective finite-dimensional complexity allowed by the sample size. Equating the two terms gives the usual bias-variance rate heuristic.

Sequence representations of smoothness

Definition. Let \((\phi_j)_{j\ge1}\) be an orthonormal basis of \(L^2[0,1]\), and write \[ f=\sum_{j\ge1}\theta_j\phi_j. \] A Sobolev ellipsoid of regularity \(\beta>0\) and radius \(R\) is \[ \mathcal H^\beta(R) =\left\{ f:\sum_{j\ge1}j^{2\beta}\theta_j^2\le R^2 \right\}. \] The tail satisfies \[ \sum_{j>J}\theta_j^2 \le J^{-2\beta}R^2. \]

Proof. For \(j>J\), \(j^{-2\beta}\le J^{-2\beta}\), so \[ \sum_{j>J}\theta_j^2 =\sum_{j>J}j^{-2\beta} (j^{2\beta}\theta_j^2) \le J^{-2\beta}R^2. \]

Gaussian series priors and sample regularity

Definition. A Gaussian series prior is \[ f=\sum_{j\ge1}\tau_jZ_j\phi_j, \qquad Z_j\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(0,1). \] A common polynomial scale is \[ \tau_j^2\asymp j^{-1-2\alpha}. \]

Proposition. If \(\tau_j^2\asymp j^{-1-2\alpha}\), then \[ f\in H^s \quad\text{almost surely for every }s<\alpha, \] while \[ P(f\in H^\alpha)=0. \]

Proof. \[ E\|f\|_{H^s}^2 =\sum_jj^{2s}\tau_j^2 \asymp\sum_jj^{2s-1-2\alpha}, \] which is finite exactly when \(s<\alpha\); finite expectation implies almost-sure finiteness. At \(s=\alpha\), the norm is comparable to \(\sum_jj^{-1}Z_j^2\), which diverges almost surely by independence and the strong law on dyadic blocks.

The prior's reproducing kernel Hilbert space is smoother than a typical draw: it consists of coefficient sequences \(h_j\) satisfying \[ \sum_j\frac{h_j^2}{\tau_j^2}<\infty. \] Prior support, approximation, and sample regularity are therefore three distinct notions.

Truncated random-series prior

Definition. Let \[ J\sim\pi_J,\qquad f=\sum_{j=1}^J\theta_j\phi_j, \qquad \theta_j\mid J\sim g_j. \] The prior adapts through the random effective dimension \(J\).

Theorem (Gaussian sequence contraction). Observe \[ Y_j=\theta_{0j}+n^{-1/2}\varepsilon_j, \qquad \varepsilon_j\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(0,1), \] and suppose \(\theta_0\in\mathcal H^\beta(R)\). If a random truncation prior assigns at least \[ \exp\{-C J\log n\} \] mass to \(J\) near \[ J_n\asymp(n/\log n)^{1/(2\beta+1)} \] and coefficient densities are positive near the truth, then a typical contraction rate is \[ \varepsilon_n \asymp \left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)} \] in \(\ell^2\), up to prior-dependent logarithmic factors.

Proof outline. Truncation bias is \[ \|\theta_0-\theta_{0,1:J}\|_2 \lesssim J^{-\beta}. \] A \(J\)-dimensional coefficient ball of radius \(\varepsilon\) has prior concentration cost of order \(J\log(1/\varepsilon)\), while likelihood testing allows cost \(n\varepsilon^2\). Balance \[ J^{-2\beta}\asymp\frac{J\log n}{n}, \] giving \(J_n\) and \(\varepsilon_n\). Sieve entropy and the tail of \(\pi_J\) verify the general contraction theorem.

Conjugate white-noise posterior

Theorem. In the Gaussian sequence model, assign independent \[ \theta_j\sim N(0,\tau_j^2). \] Then independently over \(j\), \[ \theta_j\mid Y_j \sim N\left( \frac{n\tau_j^2}{1+n\tau_j^2}Y_j,\, \frac{\tau_j^2}{1+n\tau_j^2} \right). \]

Proof. Combine prior precision \(\tau_j^{-2}\) with likelihood precision \(n\). The posterior precision is \(n+\tau_j^{-2}\), and the precision-weighted mean is \[ \frac{nY_j}{n+\tau_j^{-2}} =\frac{n\tau_j^2}{1+n\tau_j^2}Y_j. \]

The shrinkage factor is near one when \(n\tau_j^2\gg1\) and near zero when \(n\tau_j^2\ll1\). The transition index acts as an effective dimension. Posterior variance describes stochastic uncertainty around the shrunk center; frequentist coverage additionally depends on the omitted high-frequency bias.

Prior support through coefficient support

Proposition. Suppose finite linear combinations of \((\phi_j)\) are dense in a Banach space \(\mathbb B\), every finite-dimensional coefficient prior has positive density everywhere, and the series converges in \(\mathbb B\) almost surely. Then the prior has full support in the closure of all convergent coefficient sequences: \[ \Pi(\|f-f_0\|_{\mathbb B}<\varepsilon)>0 \] for every supported \(f_0\) and \(\varepsilon>0\).

Proof. Approximate \(f_0\) by a finite expansion within \(\varepsilon/3\). Positive finite-dimensional density gives positive probability that the first \(J\) coefficients approximate its coefficients closely enough for another \(\varepsilon/3\). Almost-sure tail convergence implies that, for some deterministic tail bound with positive probability, the remaining series contributes less than \(\varepsilon/3\). Intersect the events.

Full support is qualitative. Contraction rates depend on how much, not merely whether, prior mass enters a shrinking neighborhood.
