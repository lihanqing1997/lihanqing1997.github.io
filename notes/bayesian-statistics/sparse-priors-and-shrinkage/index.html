Coordinate sparsity

Sparse Bayesian inference encodes the belief that the signal has many exactly or effectively zero coordinates. The prior must both search over supports and estimate coefficients on a selected support.

Definition. A spike-and-slab prior has indicators \(\gamma_j\in\{0,1\}\) and coefficients \[ \beta_j\mid\gamma_j\sim (1-\gamma_j)\delta_0+\gamma_jG_j,\qquad \gamma_j\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q). \] The induced support is \(S_\gamma=\{j:\gamma_j=1\}\).

Proposition. If the indicators are independent, the prior mass of a support \(S\) is \(q^{|S|}(1-q)^{p-|S|}\). Choosing \(q\asymp p^{-a}\) penalizes large models by approximately \(a|S|\log p\).

Proof. The first identity is the Bernoulli product probability. Taking negative logarithms gives \(-|S|\log q-(p-|S|)\log(1-q)\). When \(q\) is small, the second term is lower order for sparse \(S\), while \(-\log q\asymp \log p\).

Continuous shrinkage

Definition. A global-local shrinkage prior has \(\beta_j\mid\lambda_j,\tau\sim N(0,\tau^2\lambda_j^2)\), where \(\tau\) is global shrinkage and \(\lambda_j\) is local shrinkage. The horseshoe prior takes \(\lambda_j\sim C^+(0,1)\) and often \(\tau\sim C^+(0,\tau_0)\).

Remark. Spike-and-slab priors are natural for support recovery. Horseshoe-type priors are natural for adaptive shrinkage and uncertainty quantification when exact zeros are less central.

Model-size priors and beta-min separation

Definition. A complexity prior assigns \(\Pi(|S|=s)\propto e^{-a s\log(ep/s)}\). It matches the combinatorial size \({p\choose s}\) of the sparse model class.

Theorem. Exact support recovery generally requires posterior concentration near the truth and a beta-min condition \(\min_{j\in S_0}|\beta_{0j}|\) larger than the estimation noise and shrinkage bias.

Proof. If an active coefficient is below the local uncertainty scale, the likelihood cannot reliably distinguish it from zero. Conversely, once likelihood separation exceeds the prior penalty for adding the variable, posterior odds favor the true support over supports missing that coordinate.

Spike-and-slab prior

Definition. A model-selection prior is \[ s\sim\pi_p,\qquad S\mid s\sim\operatorname{Unif}\{A\subset[p]:|A|=s\}, \] \[ \beta_S\sim g_S,\qquad \beta_{S^c}=0. \] A complexity prior often satisfies \[ \pi_p(s)\asymp \exp\{-a s\log(bp/s)\}. \]

Proposition. For a fixed support \(S\) of size \(s\), \[ -\log\Pi(S) =-\log\pi_p(s)+\log\binom ps \asymp s\log(p/s) \] under a compatible complexity prior.

Proof. Conditional uniformity gives \(\Pi(S)=\pi_p(s)/\binom ps\). Stirling bounds \[ s\log(p/s)\le\log\binom ps \le s\log(ep/s). \]

Normal means posterior odds

Example. Observe \[ Y_j\mid\beta_j\sim N(\beta_j,\sigma^2), \] with prior \[ \beta_j\sim(1-w)\delta_0+wN(0,\tau^2). \] The posterior inclusion odds are \[ \frac{P(z_j=1\mid Y_j)} {P(z_j=0\mid Y_j)} =\frac{w}{1-w} \sqrt{\frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma^2+\tau^2}} \exp\left[ \frac{\tau^2Y_j^2} {2\sigma^2(\sigma^2+\tau^2)} \right]. \] Conditional on inclusion, \[ \beta_j\mid Y_j,z_j=1 \sim N\left( \frac{\tau^2}{\sigma^2+\tau^2}Y_j,\, \frac{\sigma^2\tau^2}{\sigma^2+\tau^2} \right). \]

Proof. Under the spike, \(Y_j\sim N(0,\sigma^2)\); under the slab, \(Y_j\sim N(0,\sigma^2+\tau^2)\). Their density ratio times prior odds gives inclusion odds. Gaussian conjugacy gives the conditional slab posterior.

Global-local shrinkage

Definition. A global-local prior has \[ \beta_j\mid\lambda_j,\tau \sim N(0,\tau^2\lambda_j^2), \qquad \lambda_j\sim\pi_{\mathrm{loc}}, \qquad \tau\sim\pi_{\mathrm{glob}}. \] For the horseshoe, \[ \lambda_j,\tau\sim C^+(0,1). \]

Proposition (shrinkage coefficient). In unit-variance normal means, conditional on \((\lambda_j,\tau)\), \[ E(\beta_j\mid Y_j,\lambda_j,\tau) =(1-\kappa_j)Y_j, \qquad \kappa_j=\frac1{1+\tau^2\lambda_j^2}. \]

Proof. Gaussian posterior precision is \[ 1+\frac1{\tau^2\lambda_j^2}. \] The posterior mean coefficient multiplying \(Y_j\) is \[ \frac{\tau^2\lambda_j^2} {1+\tau^2\lambda_j^2}=1-\kappa_j. \]

The global scale \(\tau\) pushes most \(\kappa_j\) toward one, while heavy-tailed local scales allow signals to have \(\kappa_j\) near zero. Continuous shrinkage never sets a coefficient exactly to zero; support summaries require an explicit threshold or decision loss.

Horseshoe singularity and tails

Proposition. After integrating a half-Cauchy local scale, the horseshoe marginal density has an infinite spike at zero and Cauchy-like polynomial tails. Consequently it strongly shrinks noise while exerting bounded relative shrinkage on very large signals.

Proof outline. The marginal density is a normal scale mixture \[ p(\beta\mid\tau) =\int_0^\infty \varphi(\beta;0,\tau^2\lambda^2) \frac{2}{\pi(1+\lambda^2)} \,\mathrm{d}\lambda. \] Near \(\beta=0\), contributions from small scales produce logarithmic divergence. For large \(|\beta|\), large \(\lambda\) dominates and the half-Cauchy mixing tail yields polynomial decay. Exact constants depend on parameterization.

Posterior contraction in sparse normal means

Theorem (rate template). If \(\beta_0\) has at most \(s_0\) nonzero coordinates and a spike-and-slab or suitably scaled global-local prior assigns enough mass near sparse vectors while controlling effective model size, then \[ \Pi\left( \|\beta-\beta_0\|_2 >M\sqrt{s_0\log(p/s_0)} \mid Y \right)\to0 \] in a unit-noise normal-means experiment. With regression noise scale \(n^{-1/2}\), divide the rate by \(\sqrt n\).

Proof outline. Sparse testing separates Euclidean shells at exponential rate in squared distance. A complexity prior pays order \(s_0\log(p/s_0)\) to locate the support, and the slab supplies local mass around active coefficients. Sieve model-counting entropy has the same order. The contraction theorem gives the rate.

Beta-min and uncertainty near zero

Proposition. Uniform exact support recovery is impossible if nonzero signals may satisfy \[ |\beta_{0j}|=o\left( \sqrt{\frac{\log p}{n}} \right) \] in an orthogonal regression with \(p\) candidate coordinates.

Proof. Locating one active coordinate among \(p\) alternatives requires distinguishing \(p\) normal laws. Their pairwise KL divergences are order \(n\beta^2\). If this is \(o(\log p)\), Fano's inequality bounds the probability of correct support identification away from one.

This is not a failure of a particular prior. It is an information limit. A posterior that reports near-certain exact inclusion below this scale is overconfident or using strong structural information beyond generic sparsity.

Structured shrinkage

Group, fused, tree, matrix, and low-rank priors replace coordinate count by the complexity of a structured class. Examples include:

  • group spike-and-slab for all-or-none factors;
  • horseshoe differences for piecewise-constant signals;
  • matrix-normal shrinkage for coefficient arrays;
  • factorized singular-value priors for low rank;
  • heredity priors requiring interactions to include main effects.

The contraction rate combines parameter count within the chosen structure and logarithmic cost of locating that structure. A prior should penalize the actual combinatorial model class, not reuse an independent-coordinate penalty blindly.
