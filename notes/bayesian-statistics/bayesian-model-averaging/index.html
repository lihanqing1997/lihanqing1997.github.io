Model index

Bayesian model averaging treats the model itself as an unknown quantity. Predictions and summaries should average over posterior model uncertainty rather than condition on a single selected model.

Definition. Let \(M\in\mathcal M\) be a model index with prior \(\pi(M)\), parameter prior \(\pi_M(\theta_M)\), and marginal likelihood \[ m_M(x)=\int p_M(x\mid\theta_M)\pi_M(\theta_M)\,\mathrm{d}\theta_M. \] Then \[ \Pi(M\mid x)={\pi(M)m_M(x)\over\sum_{M'}\pi(M')m_{M'}(x)}. \]

Proposition. For a future observation \(Y\), the BMA predictive distribution is \[ p(y\mid x)=\sum_{M\in\mathcal M}\Pi(M\mid x)\int p_M(y\mid x,\theta_M)\Pi_M(\mathrm{d}\theta_M\mid x). \]

Proof. Apply the law of total probability with respect to the posterior distribution of \(M\), then integrate over the posterior of parameters inside each model.

Occam factor

Remark. The marginal likelihood averages likelihood over prior volume. A complex model is rewarded only when enough of its prior mass predicts the observed data well, producing the Bayesian Occam effect.

Bayes factors and posterior odds

Proposition. For two models \(M_1,M_0\), \[ {\Pi(M_1\mid x)\over\Pi(M_0\mid x)} ={\pi(M_1)\over\pi(M_0)}\,{m_1(x)\over m_0(x)}. \] The second factor is the Bayes factor.

Proof. Divide the two posterior model probabilities. The common normalizing denominator over all models cancels.

Prediction versus selection

Remark. BMA can have excellent predictive behavior even when posterior mass does not concentrate on a single model. This distinction matters when predictors are redundant or when several models approximate the truth equally well.

Posterior predictive mixture

Theorem. For models \(M_1,\ldots,M_K\), \[ p(\widetilde y\mid y) =\sum_{k=1}^K P(M_k\mid y) p(\widetilde y\mid y,M_k). \]

Proof. Apply total probability over the posterior model index: \[ p(\widetilde y\mid y) =\sum_k \int p(\widetilde y\mid\theta_k,M_k) \Pi(\mathrm{d}\theta_k,M_k\mid y). \]

Predictive variance decomposition

Proposition. Let \[ \mu_k=E(\widetilde Y\mid y,M_k), \qquad v_k=\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde Y\mid y,M_k), \qquad w_k=P(M_k\mid y). \] Then \[ E(\widetilde Y\mid y)=\sum_kw_k\mu_k, \] \[ \operatorname{Var}(\widetilde Y\mid y) =\sum_kw_kv_k +\sum_kw_k(\mu_k-\bar\mu)^2. \]

Proof. Apply total expectation and total variance conditional on the model index. The second term is between-model uncertainty and disappears after selecting one model.

Posterior inclusion probabilities

Definition. In variable selection, \[ \operatorname{PIP}_j =P(j\in S\mid y) =\sum_{S:j\in S}P(S\mid y). \] Under additive utility for inclusion decisions, thresholding PIPs can be Bayes optimal, with threshold determined by false-inclusion and false-exclusion costs.

Proposition. If loss is \[ L(S_0,\widehat S) =c_{\mathrm{FP}}|\widehat S\setminus S_0| +c_{\mathrm{FN}}|S_0\setminus\widehat S|, \] the Bayes action includes variable \(j\) exactly when \[ \operatorname{PIP}_j >\frac{c_{\mathrm{FP}}} {c_{\mathrm{FP}}+c_{\mathrm{FN}}}. \]

Proof. Posterior expected loss separates over coordinates. Inclusion costs \(c_{\mathrm{FP}}P(j\notin S_0\mid y)\); exclusion costs \(c_{\mathrm{FN}}P(j\in S_0\mid y)\). Compare them.

Median probability model

Theorem (orthogonal prediction setting). In Gaussian linear regression with orthogonal design, common shrinkage structure, and squared-error prediction for future responses with matching design covariance, the model containing all variables with \[ \operatorname{PIP}_j\ge1/2 \] minimizes posterior expected predictive loss among single submodels.

Proof outline. Orthogonality makes predictive risk decompose coordinatewise. For coordinate \(j\), including its posterior conditional estimate is preferable exactly when posterior inclusion probability exceeds one half under symmetric inclusion/exclusion predictive costs. Summing independent coordinate decisions gives the median probability model.

The theorem is not universal. Correlated predictors create joint predictive effects, and the optimal submodel need not be formed by marginal PIP thresholding.

Redundant predictors and dilution

Example. Suppose \(m\) nearly duplicate predictors produce \(m\) models with almost identical likelihood and prior mass, while one distinct predictor produces one comparable model. A uniform prior over models assigns the redundant predictive direction roughly \(m\) times as much total prior mass. Posterior odds can therefore reflect representation multiplicity rather than distinct predictive evidence.

Dilution priors allocate mass to neighborhoods of model space or use determinant/geometry penalties so redundant models share rather than multiply prior support.

Model-selection consistency

Theorem (finite model list). If one model \(M_{k_0}\) contains the true distribution, every other model is at positive KL distance or is a regular overfitted extension penalized by dimension, and all model priors are positive, then \[ P(M_{k_0}\mid Y^{(n)})\to1 \] under standard identifiability and proper-prior conditions.

When several models contain truth, posterior mass can favor the smallest regular true model through Occam factors. Under misspecification, it tends toward models with best KL approximation plus complexity effects. With expanding model spaces, multiplicity priors and signal conditions become essential.

BMA versus stacking

BMA weights are posterior probabilities derived from joint model priors and marginal likelihoods. Stacking weights solve \[ \max_{w\in\Delta} \sum_i\log\sum_kw_kp_{k,-i}(y_i) \] and target out-of-sample prediction. If no candidate model is true, stacking can outperform BMA by mixing complementary misspecifications. The two methods answer different questions and should not be evaluated by whether their weights agree.

Computation over large model spaces

Exact enumeration is impossible for \(2^p\) subsets. Stochastic search variable selection, MC3, birth-death chains, shotgun search, variational model distributions, and dynamic programming exploit different structure. Posterior model probability estimates must account for unvisited mass; normalizing probabilities only over visited models can create severe overconfidence.

Reporting model uncertainty

Report posterior predictive distributions, inclusion probabilities or grouped inclusion probabilities, sensitivity to model and slab priors, and the distribution of model size. A single selected formula conceals between-model uncertainty and can make subsequent standard errors conditional on a decision that the posterior itself regarded as uncertain.
