Posterior consistency means that the posterior eventually concentrates near the data-generating parameter. In parametric models, the key ingredients are identifiability, prior mass near the truth, and likelihood separation away from it.

Consistency neighborhoods

A topology on the parameter or model space determines what it means to learn the truth.

Definition. For data \(X^{(n)}\sim P_{\theta_0}^{(n)}\), the posterior is consistent at \(\theta_0\) if for every neighborhood \(U\) of \(\theta_0\), \[ \Pi(U^c\mid X^{(n)})\to0 \] in \(P_{\theta_0}^{(n)}\)-probability or almost surely.

Definition. A prior has Kullback-Leibler support at \(\theta_0\) if every set \(\{\theta:K(P_{\theta_0},P_\theta)<\varepsilon\}\) has positive prior mass.

Likelihood separation

Away from the truth, likelihood ratios should decay exponentially.

Proposition. In an iid dominated model, if \(K(P_{\theta_0},P_\theta)>0\), then \[ \frac1n\log\frac{p_\theta^{(n)}(X^{(n)})}{p_{\theta_0}^{(n)}(X^{(n)})} \to -K(P_{\theta_0},P_\theta) \] almost surely under \(P_{\theta_0}\).

Proof. The left side is the empirical average of \(\log\{p_\theta(X_i)/p_{\theta_0}(X_i)\}\). The strong law of large numbers gives its expectation, which is \(-K(P_{\theta_0},P_\theta)\).

Finite-cover consistency argument

Compactness can turn pointwise likelihood separation into uniform separation.

Theorem. If every closed set away from \(\theta_0\) can be covered by finitely many likelihood-separated neighborhoods, and the prior puts positive mass in every KL neighborhood of \(\theta_0\), then the posterior is consistent at \(\theta_0\).

Proof. The posterior numerator over the bad set is bounded by a finite sum of exponentially small likelihood ratios. The denominator is bounded below by the likelihood integrated over a KL neighborhood, which decays no faster than \(\exp\{-n\varepsilon\}\) with high probability. Choosing the separation larger than \(\varepsilon\) forces the posterior bad mass to vanish.

Nonidentifiability

If two parameters generate the same distribution, consistency can only hold for equivalence classes.

Remark. Bayesian consistency is a statement about the sampling distributions supported by the prior. If the map \(\theta\mapsto P_\theta\) is not injective, the posterior may concentrate on the set of observationally equivalent parameters rather than on a single point.

Kullback-Leibler neighborhoods

Definition. For iid data from \(P_{\theta_0}\), a Kullback-Leibler neighborhood is \[ B_{\mathrm{KL}}(\theta_0,\varepsilon) =\left\{ \theta: K(P_{\theta_0},P_\theta)<\varepsilon,\quad V(P_{\theta_0},P_\theta)<\varepsilon \right\}, \] where \[ K(P_0,P)=E_0\log\frac{p_0}{p}, \qquad V(P_0,P)=E_0\left( \log\frac{p_0}{p}-K(P_0,P) \right)^2. \]

The KL condition keeps the likelihood from being exponentially too small near truth; the variance condition controls fluctuations of its log.

A compact-parametric consistency theorem

Theorem. Let \(\Theta\) be compact, let \(\theta\mapsto p_\theta(x)\) be suitably continuous, and suppose:

  1. \(P_{\theta_0}\) is identifiable;
  2. the prior assigns positive mass to every neighborhood of \(\theta_0\);
  3. for every neighborhood \(U\) of \(\theta_0\), there exist tests \(\phi_n\) such that \[ P_{\theta_0}^{n}\phi_n\to0, \qquad \sup_{\theta\in U^c}P_\theta^n(1-\phi_n) \le e^{-c_Un}. \]

Then \[ \Pi(U^c\mid X_{1:n})\xrightarrow{P_{\theta_0}}0 \] for every neighborhood \(U\).

Proof. Write the posterior numerator outside \(U\) as \[ N_n=\int_{U^c} \prod_{i=1}^n\frac{p_\theta(X_i)}{p_{\theta_0}(X_i)} \Pi(\mathrm{d}\theta). \] Multiplying by \(1-\phi_n\) and taking \(P_{\theta_0}\)-expectation, Fubini and change of measure give \[ E_{\theta_0}\{(1-\phi_n)N_n\} =\int_{U^c}P_\theta^n(1-\phi_n)\Pi(\mathrm{d}\theta) \le e^{-c_Un}. \] The test contribution \(\phi_n\) vanishes under truth. A positive-prior KL neighborhood gives, with high probability, a denominator lower bound \(e^{-o(n)}\). Therefore the posterior ratio outside \(U\) vanishes.

Tests from likelihood separation

Proposition. In a dominated finite-dimensional model with compact \(\Theta\), if Hellinger distance satisfies \[ \inf_{\theta\in U^c} h(P_{\theta_0},P_\theta)>0, \] then exponentially consistent tests of \(\theta_0\) against \(U^c\) exist.

Proof outline. For one fixed alternative, Neyman-Pearson tests have total error bounded by a power of the Hellinger affinity \[ \rho(P_{\theta_0},P_\theta)^n =\{1-h^2(P_{\theta_0},P_\theta)\}^n. \] Continuity and compactness cover \(U^c\) by finitely many Hellinger balls whose centers remain separated from truth. The maximum of the finitely many tests retains exponential error bounds.

Bernoulli consistency directly

Example. Let \[ X_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_0), \qquad p\sim\operatorname{Beta}(a,b), \quad a,b>0. \] Then \[ p\mid X_{1:n} \sim\operatorname{Beta}(a+S_n,b+n-S_n). \] Its mean converges almost surely to \(p_0\), because \[ \frac{a+S_n}{a+b+n}\to p_0, \] and its variance is \(O(n^{-1})\). Chebyshev's inequality therefore gives \[ \Pi(|p-p_0|>\varepsilon\mid X_{1:n}) \to0 \quad\text{almost surely}. \]

Consistency of distributions versus parameters

Proposition. If the model is nonidentifiable, define \[ [\theta_0]=\{\theta:P_\theta=P_{\theta_0}\}. \] No posterior based only on observations can consistently distinguish points within \([\theta_0]\), but it may consistently concentrate on every open set containing \([\theta_0]\).

Proof. Likelihoods are identical throughout the equivalence class, so posterior density ratios within it equal prior density ratios. Tests can separate the equivalence class from distributions at positive statistical distance, but no test has power exceeding size between identical laws.

Doob and model-wide consistency

Remark. Doob's theorem gives posterior consistency for \(\Pi\)-almost every parameter under broad identifiability assumptions. Its exceptional set can contain scientifically important fixed truths and depends on the prior. A frequentist consistency theorem instead fixes every \(\theta_0\) in a specified class and verifies prior mass plus testing conditions there. The two statements should not be conflated.

Prior support failure

Proposition. If an open neighborhood \(U\) of \(\theta_0\) has prior probability zero, then \[ \Pi(U\mid X_{1:n})=0 \] for every sample and every \(n\).

Proof. The posterior is absolutely continuous with respect to the prior whenever it is defined. Prior-null sets remain posterior-null.

Data reweight prior support; they do not create support that the model excluded. In misspecified models, consistency is instead toward a Kullback-Leibler minimizing set, treated on page 20.
